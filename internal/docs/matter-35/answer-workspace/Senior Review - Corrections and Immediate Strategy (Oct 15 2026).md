# Senior Review: Ross Answer, Discovery, Deadlines, and Immediate Strategy

**Case:** *Brendan Ngwa Nforbi v. Ross Stores, Inc., et al.*, Santa Clara County Superior Court No. 26CV501995  
**Review date:** October 15, 2026  
**Status:** Second-pass litigation review of PR #1008. This memorandum controls where it conflicts with the first-pass memorandum.

## Executive conclusion

The existing workspace correctly identifies three high-value facts: Ross skipped material complaint allegations, admitted complaint paragraph 57, and admitted that surveillance cameras captured the incident. Those points should be locked down through discovery before Ross is shown how to cure them.

The first-pass memorandum nevertheless contains two material errors:

1. Code of Civil Procedure section 431.30(d) was misread. Subdivision (d), not subdivision (e), contains the verified-complaint rule requiring denials to be made positively or according to information and belief. Subdivision (e) permits a denial based on insufficient information or belief.
2. The pleading-motion calendar is too early and the automatic-extension date is wrong. Because Ross's proofs state service by California mail as well as electronic service, the mail-based outside date for a demurrer or motion to strike is October 30, 2026. A statutory 30-day extension leads to November 30, 2026 after weekends and court holidays. November 17 remains the conservative operating date for plaintiff's discovery responses; November 19 is the mail-based outside date.

The highest-value move is not a broad demurrer. It is rapid preservation and discovery directed at the video, employee identities, Ross's factual basis for each defense, and admissions already embedded in the answer.

## 1. Severity-ranked corrections

### Critical

#### A. Section 431.30(d) and (e)

The original memorandum incorrectly states that subdivision (d) is limited to general denials of unverified complaints and that subdivision (e) governs verified complaints.

Correct rule:

- Section 431.30(d) permits a general denial in an unverified case, subject to the limited-civil exception, and also states that allegations of a verified complaint must be denied positively or according to information and belief.
- Section 431.30(e) permits a party who lacks sufficient information or belief to answer an allegation to state that deficiency and deny on that ground.

Ross therefore did not necessarily cite the wrong subsection. The defect worth raising is narrower: repeated statements that defendants “can neither admit nor deny” do not expressly state lack of sufficient information or belief and do not clearly place a denial on that statutory ground.

#### B. Pleading-motion dates

Ross's proofs check both United States mail and electronic service on October 15, 2026.

- Demurrer to answer: section 430.40(b), 10 days after service.
- Motion to strike: section 435(b)(1), same 10-day period.
- California mail: section 1013(a), add 5 calendar days.
- Electronic service, if valid against a self-represented plaintiff: section 1010.6, add 2 court days.

Mail calculation: October 15 + 10 + 5 = Friday, October 30.  
Electronic-only calculation: October 15 + 10 reaches Sunday, October 25; applying the electronic extension yields Tuesday, October 27.

The legally supportable outside date is October 30 because the papers were served by California mail. Operationally, complete the conference by October 22–23 and preserve rights no later than October 27 unless counsel confirms the computation.

#### C. Automatic extension

Sections 430.41(a)(2) and 435.5(a)(2) provide a 30-day extension from the date the pleading motion would otherwise be due when the required declaration is timely filed and served.

- October 30 + 30 days = Sunday, November 29, rolling to Monday, November 30.
- Even using October 27, the thirtieth day is Thanksgiving, November 26; Santa Clara is also closed Friday, November 27, followed by the weekend. The resulting date is again Monday, November 30.

November 25 is incorrect.

#### D. Pro se work-product assumption

Do not assume plaintiff's personal litigation notes, AI drafts, damages worksheets, or strategy documents are protected by Code of Civil Procedure section 2018.030. The California statutory protection is framed as the work product of an attorney, and the attorney ordinarily holds it.

For plaintiff-generated materials, use precise relevance, overbreadth, privacy, burden, trade-secret, protective-order, or document-definition objections where genuinely applicable. Produce discoverable factual material and segregate strategy commentary where a defensible basis exists. Do not assert attorney-client privilege over communications with nonlawyers or an AI system.

#### E. Civil Code section 43.5

Delete the citation. Civil Code section 43.5 concerns abolished heart-balm causes of action. The merchant-detention privilege is Penal Code section 490.5(f), with related common-law authority including *Fermino v. Fedco, Inc.* (1994) 7 Cal.4th 701.

### High

#### F. Merits assessment

**False imprisonment:** presently moderate and video-dependent, not “strong.” Ross will make non-detention its primary theory: no touching, no search, no police, no explicit theft accusation, no demand for a receipt, and a short interaction. Plaintiff must establish intentional restraint by obstruction, threat, menace, unreasonable duress, or another recognized means.

**Merchant privilege:** paragraph 57 is valuable but does not itself prove detention. Ross can argue that a beverage resembling store merchandise supplied reasonable cause for a brief inquiry. Plaintiff needs the entrance and exit video, the employee's observations, the store's product records, and Ross's stop criteria.

**Unruh:** presently challenging but discovery-sensitive. Intentional discrimination may be proved circumstantially, but the current record contains no explicit racial statement and no identified similarly situated comparator. Stop records, policy, training, employee history, and video are critical.

**Negligent hiring, training, or supervision:** presently weak or unknown. Plaintiff needs proof of employee unfitness or a particular risk plus Ross's actual or constructive knowledge. Prior complaints, training failures, supervisor knowledge, and policy departures matter more than the isolated encounter.

#### G. Affirmative defenses

The defenses are generic, but the original review overstates how many are legally irrelevant.

- Lack of notice can bear on negligent supervision.
- Reasonable care and comparative fault can bear on the negligence claim.
- Mitigation can bear on damages.
- Proposition 51 can matter to the negligence theory and unidentified third parties.
- Conduct of others could matter if the employee or security function involved a contractor.
- Failure to state a claim is largely surplusage but is not worth substantial motion practice.
- Uncertainty, lack of particularity, and the reservation clause are weak, but striking them has little merits value.

*FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima* (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367 is real and useful. It states that defenses should be pleaded carefully and with factual detail. But it arose in a summary-judgment setting and observed that the plaintiffs had not demurred to the answer. It does not guarantee that a court will strike every concise defense.

#### H. Verification

Section 446 permits a corporate officer to verify and permits another qualified person in specified circumstances. Rachel De la Cruz's verification does not state her title, but that omission alone does not prove invalidity. Do not infer from the Sunnyvale signature that she is a store-level employee, and do not characterize the October 8 execution date as fraudulent or invalid.

Seek:

1. her title and employer;
2. whether she is an officer of either corporation;
3. her authority to verify for both entities;
4. the reasonable investigation performed;
5. whether the text reviewed was the filed answer.

#### I. Fee prayer

The defense fee prayer is low-value motion practice. Ross can characterize it as preserving any fee or sanctions remedy otherwise authorized. Ask for the basis if desired, but do not center a motion on it.

### Medium

- Special Interrogatory Instruction 13 is objectionable under section 2030.060(d), which prohibits unapproved prefatory instructions. It is not merely a “minor” drafting issue.
- Many special interrogatories are compound or contain prohibited subparts. Object specifically and answer each separable portion to the extent understood.
- Special Interrogatory 17 is internally inconsistent because the defined term INCIDENT means the present occurrence.
- RFP 21 refers to nonexistent RFP 26. Object as vague and unintelligible, request clarification, and reserve supplementation.
- Filed lawsuits are public. Resist unlimited prior-claim discovery through relevance, overbreadth, proportionality, time limits, and injury overlap—not a blanket assertion that the cases themselves are private.
- *Vinson v. Superior Court* (1987) 43 Cal.3d 833 and *Britt v. Superior Court* (1978) 20 Cal.3d 844 support narrow tailoring of private medical discovery. They do not bar discovery of medical or psychological conditions plaintiff places in controversy.
- *Williams v. Superior Court* (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531 is a privacy-balancing case arising from PAGA employee-contact discovery. It is not a categorical shield for prior claims.

## 2. Admissions and pleading leverage

Ross's answer begins with complaint paragraph 22 and contains no valid blanket general denial to the verified complaint. Complaint paragraphs 1–21, 85–86, and 129 are not answered. Section 431.20(a) provides that material allegations not controverted are taken as true for purposes of the action.

The strongest omitted factual allegations appear to include:

- paragraph 10: operation of the store and employment;
- paragraph 12: DOE 1's loss-prevention or security role;
- paragraph 15: course and scope.

Legal conclusions and the jury demand do not become established facts merely because they were omitted.

Ross separately admitted complaint paragraph 57, which alleges that the sole asserted basis for stopping plaintiff was the openly carried beverage bought next door. Ross also admitted that the cashier witnessed the incident and that surveillance cameras captured it.

Strategy:

1. preserve the filed answer and proof of service;
2. do not identify the omissions in the pleading meet-and-confer;
3. serve RFAs directed to the underlying facts;
4. serve Form Interrogatory 15.1 and special interrogatories directed to every denial and defense;
5. expect Ross to seek leave to amend once the issue surfaces.

## 4. Defense-side outlook

Ross is likely to respond to the pleading letter by:

1. pointing out that section 431.30(d) was correctly cited;
2. agreeing to clarify “can neither admit nor deny” as a curable wording issue;
3. stating that De la Cruz was authorized and offering an amended verification;
4. declining to identify DOE 1 through a pleading conference;
5. maintaining that the defenses give adequate notice;
6. amending rather than litigating.

On the merits, Ross will argue:

- the employee asked a question but did not detain plaintiff;
- the exit remained open and plaintiff voluntarily engaged;
- no force, threat, search, accusation, receipt demand, or police contact occurred;
- if a detention occurred, the beverage created reasonable cause for a brief inquiry;
- no racial statement or identified comparator supports intentional discrimination;
- Ross lacked notice of employee unfitness;
- claimed damages are disproportionate to a short, nonphysical encounter;
- prior claims, inconsistent accounts, social media, medical history, and convictions may impeach credibility or show alternative causes.

Plaintiff's most exposed points are confinement, discriminatory intent, comparator proof, Ross's prior knowledge, prospective injunctive standing, and compensatory damages.

## 5. Discovery-response strategy

Serve complete, signed, verified responses by November 17 unless counsel confirms a different date in writing.

### Interrogatories

- State each objection specifically; avoid generic incorporated objections.
- For compound interrogatories, object and answer separable portions.
- Answer FROG 2.8 accurately and only to the requested categories.
- Narrow FROGs 2.12–2.13 to conditions or substances bearing on perception, memory, conduct, or claimed injury.
- Disclose incident-related symptoms and treatment while objecting to unrestricted lifetime medical history.
- Answer FROGs 7.x, 8.x, and 4.x simply if no property, wage, or insurance claim exists.
- FROG 15.1 is likely inapplicable because plaintiff's complaint contains no denials or affirmative defenses.

### Prior proceedings

- Identify responsive public filed matters accurately.
- Object to unlimited “every claim ever” language.
- Offer a reasonable limitation such as ten years and claims involving similar injuries, retail detention, or public-accommodation discrimination.
- Review settlement confidentiality provisions before producing agreements, while recognizing that confidentiality alone is not an evidentiary privilege.

### Production requests

- Do not assume personal notes or an internal damages worksheet are attorney work product.
- Produce responsive factual portions where required; preserve any legitimate objection to strategy material.
- Attorney communications may be privileged; communications with nonlawyer advocates, media, friends, or AI are not automatically privileged.
- Object to RFP 21 because the referenced RFP 26 does not exist.
- For each request, state compliance, partial compliance, inability to comply with reasons, or a specific objection.
- Produce native recordings and metadata where relevant, Bates-number document productions, and maintain an index.

## 7. Recommended pleading-motion decision

Do not file a broad demurrer now.

A broad motion will likely:

- reveal the omitted-paragraph theory;
- cause Ross to amend paragraph 57;
- allow Ross to add facts supporting its defenses;
- consume time without eliminating the shopkeeper defense.

Preserve the option through the statutory conference and extension declaration. Consider a later narrow motion only if Ross refuses to clarify materially defective denials or insists on nondefenses that create concrete prejudice.
