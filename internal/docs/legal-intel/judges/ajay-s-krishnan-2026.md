(510) 637-1296, askcrd@cand.uscourts.org; proposed orders askpo@cand.uscourts.org.

## (b) Notable 2026 orders

- **Hendrix v. Apple discovery letter brief (Aug. 21, 2026):** split-the-difference ruling on class
  scope — Apple right that discovery at that stage was limited to named plaintiffs, plaintiffs
  right that Apple had to identify all datasets and language models containing the asserted
  works, not only those named in the complaint. Set a Zoom hearing, then invited the parties to
  resolve on their own if the findings gave enough guidance.
  https://app.midpage.ai/document/hendrix-v-apple-inc--6f3d6f0b-dd4c-4a56-9fc1-c16ec9eef564
- **Azar v. Trump TRO (June 1, 2026):** ordered the government to respond by noon June 3 and set
  hearing June 4 via Zoom. Case mooted by a D.R.I. vacatur before a merits ruling.
- **Morris v. Evans R&R (4:26-cv-01563):** in a § 1915(e)(2) pro se screening he recommended
  granting the IFP motion; the plaintiff amended rather than objecting, mooting the R&R. District
  Judge Tigar then ordered a venue show cause. Per the district-court recitation, Krishnan screened
  the original complaint under § 1915(e)(2) — the standard pattern where a magistrate judges the
  pleading before a district judge takes over.
  https://app.midpage.ai/document/morris-v-evans--81699360-e72c-487c-9503-3fadc98fdb43
- **Young v. Stenger, 4:21-cv-08131:** § 1983 prisoner civil rights (Antioch Police) — consent
  case carried to trial. Krishnan's July 30, 2026 pretrial order set pretrial brief and MILs
  Aug. 17, final pretrial conference Aug. 27, jury selection Sept. 8. A pro se-originating § 1983
  matter that survived to jury trial on his docket.
  https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-cand-4_21-cv-08131/USCOURTS-cand-4_21-cv-08131-7
- **Stewart Title (Oct. 5-6, 2026):** granted ex parte application; modified order adjusting
  case-scheduling dates.

## (c) Observable patterns

1. **Keker, Van Nest litigator through and through.** Twenty years of trying cases — patent,
   copyright, trademark, trade secrets, business torts, securities, class actions, and a
   civil-rights pro bono practice with ACLU roots. The profile is a trial lawyer who took the
   bench, not an academic. Expect doctrinal depth written in a practitioner's register.
2. **Discovery-first docket.** The dominant share of his work is discovery referrals — Roblox MDL,
   Arizona v. Kennedy, Faust v. Paramount, Hendrix v. Apple. His Civil Standing Order devotes
   substantial space to discovery procedure: joint letter briefs capped at 5 pages (2.5 per side),
   mandatory in-person/video/phone meet-and-confer ("A mere exchange of letters, emails, or
   messages does not satisfy the requirement"), and a specific search-and-collection disclosure
   expectation.
3. **The judge's standing orders are unusually specific and practitioner-facing.** Read the
   current revision before filing anything:
   https://cand.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/standing-orders/ASK-CivilStandingOrder_8.20.26.pdf
   Key points for portfolio matters:
   - **Motions (non-discovery):** first, third, fifth Wednesdays, 1:30 p.m., Ct. 4, Oakland,
     **in person**. No advance reservation required. Zoom only on good cause, requested at least
     **3 business days** before the hearing.
   - **Amended pleadings require an attached redline** against the last pleading. This is not
     optional and matters for any Matter 29 amended complaint.
   - **One SJ motion per party.** Exceeding the limit requires leave and good cause.
   - **Proposed orders** emailed in Microsoft Word to askpo@cand.uscourts.org.
   - **Summary judgment and discovery** are the two areas with the most prescriptive rules.
4. **Pro se handling is procedural, not substantive.** His standing order routes pro se litigants
   to the Legal Help Center (Oakland, Room 470S; Mon/Wed 9-5; (415) 782-8982) and the Pro Se
   Handbook. Pro se litigants may file manually (original plus three copies) unless granted ECF
   permission under Civil L.R. 5-1(b). No sua sponte screening provision — a contrast with Cisneros's
   docket. The *Morris* R&R shows the standard § 1915(e)(2) screening path.
5. **The *Young v. Stenger* data point is the most relevant in the corpus.** A § 1983 claim by a
   self-represented prisoner reached a full jury-trial calendar on Krishnan's docket. He did not
   screen it out; he ran it to trial. That is a favorable signal for a pro se § 1983 plaintiff —
   the *Matter 29* posture is a different species (consumer/FCRA), but the pro se-civil-rights
   pattern is the one worth watching.
6. **Hendrix shows his working style on contested discovery: split the difference, then invite
   settlement.** He found each side partly right, then told the parties to resolve it themselves
   if the findings were guidance enough, and set a hearing only if they could not. Practical and
   efficiency-minded rather than ideology-driven.
7. **Reputation signal:** No public reputation quotes surfaced in this pass. His two standing
   orders (Civil, Pretrial) and the court's own announcements are the primary record. His Berzon
   clerkship is the most cited credential in secondary coverage.

## Caveats

1. Sampled, not comprehensive — a full -ASK docket census would need PACER/RECAP bulk access.
2. He has been on the bench only since January 2026. Every observation above is from his first
   year. Pattern claims are correspondingly weaker than for a judge with a decade of output.
3. No published MTD ruling by Krishnan surfaced in the sampled sources. His dispositive-motion
   track record is essentially unobserved — the *Morris* R&R is screening, not a contested MTD.
   Any prediction about how he rules on the Amazon motion is inference, not pattern-matching.
4. The Roblox MDL and Faust v. Paramount Section 230 motions belong to Seeborg and
   Martinez-Olguin respectively. Do not attribute those rulings to Krishnan.
