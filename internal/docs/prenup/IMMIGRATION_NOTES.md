## Preliminary analysis (for counsel to confirm)

1. **Marriage alone very likely does not change the outcome.**
   - Because he was admitted and she is a U.S. citizen, he is an "immediate relative." Overstay doesn't bar adjustment.
   - But both convictions are crimes involving moral turpitude (§ 212(a)(2)(A)), so he needs a **§ 212(h) waiver**.

     confirm).
   - **But** forcible sexual penetration is a "violent or dangerous" crime. Under 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(d), the waiver requires
     **exceptional and extremely unusual hardship**, and even that may not be enough given how serious the crime is.
     Hardship to a spouse who married knowing the record usually carries less weight.
   - The **final order** must first be reopened. The 90-day motion deadline passed in 2021, so reopening would need a joint
     motion with ICE or the immigration judge reopening on their own. Both are unlikely on this record.

| Fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Toyosi's status: U.S. citizen, green card holder, or neither | Only a U.S. citizen or green card holder can petition for a spouse (I-130). If she is neither, marriage gives the client no path. |

## Main barriers

- **Inadmissibility for the conviction** (INA § 212(a)(2)). A waiver may exist under § 212(h) based on hardship to a U.S. citizen
  or green card spouse. For violent or dangerous crimes, the standard rises to "exceptional and extremely unusual
  hardship" (8 C.F.R. § 212.7(d)). That is a very high bar.
- **Heightened marriage scrutiny.** Marrying while removal proceedings are pending triggers § 245(e). Even after a final
  order, expect close review of whether the marriage is genuine. A finding of marriage fraud is a **permanent bar**
  (§ 204(c)), so the record has to be clean.
- **Adam Walsh Act** (§ 204(a)(1)(A)(viii)) bars a U.S. citizen or green card holder convicted of certain offenses against minors from
  petitioning for anyone. It applies to the **petitioner**. It matters only if the client were ever the one petitioning.

## How the prenup interacts with immigration

1. **Proof the marriage is genuine.** USCIS looks for shared life: joint accounts, joint lease or title, shared bills,
   insurance, and both names on documents. A prenup that keeps everything separate is legal and common. It is not
   evidence of fraud. But the joint account (Article 7.2) should be **actually used** for household expenses, and the couple
   should build ordinary shared records. Consider adding Toyosi to the lease, insurance, and household bills even if Cat Tail
   Drive stays in the client's name.
2. **Affidavit of Support (I-864).** If Toyosi sponsors the client, she signs a federal promise to support him at 125% of
   the poverty line. **The prenup cannot waive it,** and it survives divorce. Her counsel will raise this. Add a recital
   acknowledging it so the agreement doesn't look like it is trying to override federal law.
3. **Surveillance terms.** Extensive recording and tracking terms are not an immigration issue in themselves. But if the
   marriage fails and either side alleges abuse, they become evidence on both sides. Keep everything mutual and revocable.
4. **Never tie the marriage to immigration in writing.** No clause should make anything conditional on a green card.
