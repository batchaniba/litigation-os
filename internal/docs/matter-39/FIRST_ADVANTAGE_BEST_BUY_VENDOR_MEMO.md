# Matter 39 — Best Buy / First Advantage Vendor Record

## Transaction-specific evidence in the portfolio

The vendor question is substantially pinned without relying on public anecdotes.

### October 17, 2023 — Best Buy offer
Best Buy's General Manager sent Plaintiff a congratulations offer for General Warehouse Worker / 2nd Shift, requisition 928556BR, at $22.60/hour. Best Buy required Candidate Zone acceptance, background-check information, and background-check authorization.

### October 17, 2023 — Background Authorization Consent Confirmation
Best Buy's onboarding record identifies **First Advantage Consumer Center** in Plaintiff's background-authorization process.

### October 17, 2023 — First Advantage Best Buy order
Minutes later, First Advantage generated a Best Buy drug-screen order concerning Plaintiff:
- order: Q09078711
- account: BEST BUY 11759586
- Best Buy employer workflow

These records are transaction-specific. They support alleging that First Advantage was directly participating in Best Buy's employment-screening process for Plaintiff.

## Public corroboration

Publicly available First Advantage consumer-report material from 2021 identifies **BEST BUY** at 7601 Penn Avenue South as the requesting client and First Advantage Background Services Corp. as the CRA.

Treat the public report as corroborative only. The complaint should rely first on Plaintiff's own Best Buy/First Advantage records.

Best Buy's current SEC filings identify Best Buy Co., Inc. as a Minnesota corporation with principal executive offices at 7601 Penn Avenue South, Richfield, Minnesota 55423.

## Pleading consequence

It is appropriate to allege:

> Best Buy used First Advantage Background Services Corp. as its consumer reporting agency and employment-screening vendor in connection with Plaintiff's October 2023 onboarding.

If counsel attacks that statement at Rule 12, the transaction-specific authorization confirmation and Best Buy First Advantage order provide the factual basis.
