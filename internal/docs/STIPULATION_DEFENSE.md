# Stipulation Defense — Plaintiff-First Standing Policy

Effective September 9, 2026. Applies to every current and future matter in the Interactive Litigation Portfolio, in every forum, and to drafting, reviewing, redlining, recommending, and recording any stipulation or agreement with defense.

## Governing instruction

Draft every stipulation in the terms most favorable to Plaintiff that the applicable procedure and record support. Optimize timing, substantive rights, procedural position, enforceability, and litigation leverage. Defense's proposed wording is a proposal to scrutinize, not the default template. This policy continues until Plaintiff changes it; a case-specific exception does not change the portfolio-wide default.

## Timelines

- Default to maintaining existing defense deadlines or shortening them where procedurally available and advantageous to Plaintiff. Do not routinely grant additional defense time for convenience, courtesy, settlement discussions, or preparation.
- Protect Plaintiff's preparation and response time. "Shorter" is not inherently plaintiff-favorable: do not compress Plaintiff's opposition, discovery, amendment, or compliance period merely to accelerate the case.
- Preserve favorable hearing dates, discovery progress, CMCs, trial dates, service deadlines, and settlement-demand expirations. No automatic downstream continuances, stays, tolling, or cross-defendant extensions.
- If additional defense time is proposed, first draft the strongest workable maintained-date or shortened-date alternative. Identify the specific reason for the extension, the smallest justified period, and the concrete benefit Plaintiff receives. If the court's requirements or the record make refusal disadvantageous, explain that specific reason.
- Specify the affected party, operative pleading or obligation, existing due date, proposed date, and exact scope. Use fixed dates when possible; make any triggering event unambiguous. Check calendar versus court days, service rules, holidays, time of day, and the court's time zone.
- Do not describe an expired deadline as extended by agreement, or treat an anticipated court order as entered. Identify any request for retroactive relief expressly.

## Terms and preserved rights

- Limit relief to the expressly identified issue, party, and obligation. No blanket "all deadlines," "all defendants," or indefinite "pending settlement" provisions.
- Identify every concession and any reciprocal benefit. Do not add factual admissions, concede good cause, validate disputed service, or characterize an untimely filing as timely without record support and Plaintiff's informed authorization.
- Scrutinize clauses preserving or expanding defense rights: new motions, renewed challenges, amendment opportunities, jurisdiction or service objections, arbitration, removal, or other procedural relief. Do not confer new rights or revive waived defenses as boilerplate.
- Preserve Plaintiff's claims, remedies, objections, discovery rights, and enforcement options to the extent consistent with the specific agreement. State the precise effect on any pending default request, motion, objection, or demand; do not promise to preserve a right that the agreed relief necessarily relinquishes.
- No incidental release, dismissal, prejudice, confidentiality, non-disparagement, fee/cost waiver, admission, or restriction on lawful use of evidence. Where such terms are the subject of the requested stipulation, negotiate their scope for Plaintiff's benefit and identify their consequences explicitly.
- For protective orders, examine designation scope, challenges, burdens, access, permitted use, cross-matter use where lawful, filing/sealing, and return/destruction terms. Do not assume a stipulation overrides an existing protective order.
- For settlement or dismissal stipulations, identify payment and performance dates, the sequence of payment and dismissal, enforcement provisions, release scope, and treatment of fees/costs. Prefer terms that protect Plaintiff until promised performance occurs.
- Include only accurate recitals supported by the record. Do not turn disputed defense assertions into agreed facts.
- No permission to affix Plaintiff's signature, use "/s/", certify consent, or file on Plaintiff's behalf unless Plaintiff has actually authorized the exact final version. Drafting or negotiating alone is not execution authority.

## Required review output

For every proposed stipulation, provide:

1. **Recommendation:** accept, counter, or decline, with the concrete effect on Plaintiff.
2. **Deadline comparison:** a table of affected obligation/party, current date and source, defense proposal, Plaintiff's counter, days gained/lost, and downstream effects. For a non-timing stipulation, state that no deadlines change after checking.
3. **Concession audit:** each waiver, admission, defense benefit, Plaintiff benefit, and right affected. Flag ambiguous language and supply replacement wording.
4. **Plaintiff-favorable draft or redline:** complete operative language, with unresolved facts clearly marked rather than invented.
5. **Exception note, if needed:** identify each departure from this policy and why it benefits Plaintiff or is procedurally required. Use existing case-specific authorization; otherwise present the concrete exception for Plaintiff's decision before accepting it.
6. **Effectiveness check:** distinguish proposed, agreed, signed, filed, and court-ordered status; identify whether court approval or another prerequisite is required.

## Source and calendar discipline

Read the operative pleading, controlling scheduling orders, relevant correspondence, existing agreements, and current applicable rules before supplying case-specific deadlines or legal conclusions. Verify current rules through authoritative sources. If sources conflict or are missing, say what is unresolved; do not invent a date or claim a reservation is legally effective.

A proposed or signed stipulation is not automatically an operative court deadline. Record its status accurately. Change the portfolio's operative calendar only when the applicable rule, effective agreement, or entered order supports the change; retain the original date and source in the history and synchronize affected matter/timeline records using existing repository workflows.

Existing executed stipulations, court orders, and historical records remain factual history. This policy governs future work and does not retroactively rewrite them.

## Reusable drafting starting point

Adapt and verify; do not insert as unreviewed boilerplate:

> The parties stipulate solely that [identified party]'s deadline to [specific obligation concerning the identified operative pleading] shall be [fixed date], [subject to Court approval, if required]. Except for that expressly stated change, no other deadline, hearing, discovery obligation, or trial date is modified. This stipulation does not authorize any additional relief or constitute an admission concerning disputed facts. [State accurately the rights preserved and the precise effect on pending motions, defaults, objections, and demands.]

Do not retain bracketed alternatives in an execution-ready document.
