# Matter 1 — Dismiss-and-Refile vs. Transfer to N.D. Cal.

**Question presented:** Once District Judge Coggins rules on the pending F&R in
*Newanforbi v. CHP et al.*, No. 2:25-cv-01460-DC-CSK (E.D. Cal.), is Plaintiff
free to file a voluntary dismissal in the E.D. Cal. and refile the same claims in
the N.D. Cal. — the proper forum in the first place — so long as the refiling
lands before November 1, 2026?

**Short answer: No. Waiting for the ruling is the single act that forecloses the
plan.** Three conditions have to hold at the same time for dismiss-and-refile to
work. All three hold today. At least two of them stop holding the moment Coggins
signs an order adopting the F&R. And the November 1, 2026 date is not the only
constraint — it is the least of them.

**This memo is portfolio analysis of the record in this repository, not legal
advice.** The preclusion and limitations calls below are the kind where being
wrong is not recoverable. Confirm each against the current docket and, if at all
possible, with counsel before acting.

---

## 1. Why "once Coggins rules" is the wrong trigger

### 1.1 The Rule 41(a)(1) window is open now and closes at judgment

Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) lets a plaintiff dismiss "without a court order" by filing a
notice "before the opposing party serves either an answer or a motion for summary
judgment." The State has served neither. It filed a Rule 12(b)(6) motion on
December 10, 2025 — and a Rule 12(b)(6) motion does not cut off the right. See
*Commercial Space Mgmt. Co. v. Boeing Co.*, 193 F.3d 1074, 1077–78 (9th Cir. 1999)
(notice is self-executing and effective on filing; no court order, and no judicial
discretion to refuse it); *Concha v. London*, 62 F.3d 1493, 1506 (9th Cir. 1995).

So the right exists **right now**. What it does not survive is entry of judgment.
Once Coggins adopts the F&R and the action is dismissed, there is no pending
action left to dismiss. The notice-of-dismissal route is not something being held
in reserve until the ruling — it is a window that the ruling closes.

### 1.2 The ruling itself is preclusive

Rule 41(b) provides that a dismissal — other than one for lack of jurisdiction,
improper venue, or failure to join a party under Rule 19 — "operates as an
adjudication on the merits" unless the order says otherwise. A dismissal under
Rule 12(b)(6) for failure to state a claim is a merits dismissal for
claim-preclusion purposes.

The record here makes that concrete: the Attorney General's January 5, 2026 reply
asked for **full dismissal with prejudice**. The magistrate judge's April 20, 2026
F&R recommends the motion be granted and the action dismissed. If Coggins adopts
it, a refiled N.D. Cal. complaint on the same November 1, 2024 stop is met with
res judicata on day one — and the November 1, 2026 filing date does nothing about
that. A timely-filed barred claim is still barred.

Note the one carve-out that would help and almost certainly will not apply: a
dismissal for **improper venue** is expressly non-merits under Rule 41(b). Nobody
has raised venue in this case. It is not going to appear in the order on its own.

### 1.3 The limitations clock gives no credit for the 14 months already served

This is the part of the question that carries a hidden assumption. A voluntary
dismissal without prejudice leaves the parties as though the action had never been
filed; the original May 27, 2025 filing does **not** toll the limitations period
for the refiled case. The refiled complaint has to be independently timely on its
own filing date.

The borrowed period is California's two-year personal-injury statute, CCP § 335.1,
applied to § 1983. Running from the November 1, 2024 incident:

| Item | Date |
|---|---|
| Incident / accrual (Fourth Amendment stop, search, arrest) | Nov. 1, 2024 |
| CCP § 335.1 two-year expiry | **Sun., Nov. 1, 2026** |
| Days remaining as of this memo (Aug. 7, 2026) | **86** |

Two wrinkles, one in each direction:

- **In Plaintiff's favor.** *Wallace v. Kato*, 549 U.S. 384, 389–90 (2007), holds a
  false-imprisonment claim accrues when the detention without legal process ends —
  here, on release, which the Dublin CHP Detention Certificate places around
  November 5, 2024. The Fourteenth Amendment theories tied to the CDL suspension
  accrue later still (suspension effective December 1, 2024). So not every count
  dies on the same day. But the core Fourth Amendment stop-and-search claim is
  measured from November 1, 2024.
- **Against.** November 1, 2026 is a **Sunday**. CCP § 12a would carry a state
  deadline to the next court day; do not build a plan that depends on borrowing
  that one extra day in federal court.

Also worth knowing what is *not* a lifeline. CCP § 352.1 imprisonment tolling is
contested on these facts — California authority reads it not to reach pretrial
detainees, see *Austin v. Medicis*, 21 Cal. App. 5th 577 (2018), and the five-day
hold here was a parole hold, not post-conviction custody on this charge.
Equitable tolling under *Addison v. State of California*, 21 Cal. 3d 313 (1978),
is available in principle for a plaintiff who pursued an alternate forum in good
faith — but it is discretionary, fact-bound, and materially harder to win when the
"alternate forum" move followed an adverse recommendation. Neither is something to
bet the claim on.

**Note a portfolio gap this exposes:** `DEADLINE_AUDIT.md` does not track the
November 1, 2026 § 335.1 expiry for the Matter 1 claims at all. It is invisible
today because the 2025 filing stopped the clock — but it becomes live and binding
the instant a voluntary dismissal is filed. It should be entered as a tracked
deadline regardless of which route is chosen.

## 2. The route that actually gets the N.D. Cal. — transfer, not dismissal

Everything the dismiss-and-refile plan is trying to accomplish is available
through **28 U.S.C. § 1404(a)** (convenience) or **§ 1406(a)** (wrong district),
with none of the downside:

- **The filing date is preserved.** A transferred case keeps its May 27, 2025
  filing date. The entire § 335.1 problem in § 1.3 above simply does not arise.
- **There is no second filing.** Nothing to preclude, no Rule 41(a)(1)(B) two-
  dismissal question, no Rule 41(d) costs.
- **It can be filed now, before the ruling.** A district court may resolve a
  threshold non-merits question ahead of the merits. See *Sinochem Int'l Co. v.
  Malaysia Int'l Shipping Corp.*, 549 U.S. 422, 431–32 (2007). A pending F&R does
  not have to be resolved first.

### 2.1 The venue argument is strong, and it got stronger by concession

Under § 1391(b)(2), venue lies where "a substantial part of the events or
omissions giving rise to the claim occurred." Every operative event occurred in
**Livermore, Alameda County** — I-580 East shoulder, 00:55 hours, November 1,
2024. The officers are **CHP Dublin Area**, also Alameda County. Alameda County is
in the **Northern District**.

The only thing anchoring this case in the Eastern District was the § 1391(b)(1)
residence hook for the State of California / CHP headquarters in Sacramento. And
Plaintiff's own December 22, 2025 opposition **conceded dismissal of the State and
all official-capacity claims**. Once that concession is given effect, the only
remaining defendants are Officers Meza-Gonzalez and Melano in their individual
capacities — two Dublin Area officers, sued over conduct in Alameda County. On the
posture Plaintiff himself created, E.D. Cal. venue is arguably improper as to
every remaining defendant, which puts § 1406(a) squarely in play alongside
§ 1404(a).

### 2.2 The relatedness argument is already on the E.D. Cal. docket

The July 7, 2026 Notice of Supplemental Authority already told Judge Coggins that
Magistrate Judge Krishnan, in *Newanforbi v. Serrato*, No. 4:26-cv-05953-ASK
(N.D. Cal.) (Matter 24), found a complaint arising from **this same November 1,
2024 incident** sufficient to survive § 1915 screening. That filing was framed as
persuasive authority against the F&R. It doubles as a transfer argument: a
related, live case on the same operative facts is already pending in the
transferee district, before a judge who has read these facts. See N.D. Cal. Civil
L.R. 3-12.

One caution: that order is not portable. Krishnan's screening ruling is not
preclusive, not binding on any other N.D. Cal. judge, and does not attach itself
to a case that arrives by refiling. Relation under L.R. 3-12 is a request, not an
entitlement.

## 3. Landmines the question does not account for

### 3.1 A dismissal in Matter 1 lifts the stay in Matter 2

The Alameda tort action, *Newanforbi v. Meza-Gonzalez*, No. 26CV168913 (Matter 2),
is **stayed in its entirety pending final resolution of Matter 1**. Plaintiff's own
counter-draft, which DAG Li accepted verbatim on April 14, 2026, defines "final
resolution" as entry of final judgment **or dispositive order**, plus expiration of
any appeal period.

A Rule 41(a)(1) notice is a dispositive event. Filing one in Matter 1 likely trips
that clause, lifts the Alameda stay, and revives DAG Li's noticed demurrer under
CCP § 430.10(c) — which was premised on "another action pending," namely Matter 1.
Whether that demurrer evaporates (no federal action pending) or gets re-aimed at
the new N.D. Cal. case depends on sequencing. Either way, the Alameda posture has
to be planned *before* anything is filed in Matter 1, not after.

The stipulation's tolling provision covers deadlines in the stayed Alameda action.
It is a contract between the parties. Do not read it as tolling the federal § 1983
limitations period for a new federal complaint — the AG would contest that
reading, and it is not the natural one.

### 3.2 The two-dismissal rule needs to be cleared first

Rule 41(a)(1)(B): a notice dismissal is without prejudice **unless** the plaintiff
"previously dismissed any federal- or state-court action based on or including the
same claim" — in which case the second notice operates as an adjudication on the
merits.

This portfolio has six prior Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) notices: Matter 6 (May 21, 2026)
and Matters 11, 13, 17, 18, and 21 (all June 29, 2026). Most are plainly different
claims. **Matter 13 is the one that needs a hard look.**

*Newanforbi v. Urrea et al.*, No. 2:26-cv-00190-DC-SCR, pleaded Fourth and
Fourteenth Amendment "unconstitutional detention" arising from a pretextual
Halloween-curfew hold that detained Plaintiff for five days — and it was dismissed
**with prejudice** on June 29, 2026. That is the same five-day parole hold pleaded
in Matter 1's own key facts as a damages element ("held five days on a parole
violation hold without arraignment"). Same transaction, same week, same custody.

Two distinct exposures follow, and both exist independent of any forum decision:

1. **Two-dismissal.** The AG can argue a Matter 1 notice is a second dismissal on
   a claim "based on or including" Matter 13's claim, converting it into a merits
   adjudication — which would destroy the refiling on its own terms. The better
   argument is that Matter 1's claim is the *stop, search, and arrest by CHP*,
   which has never been voluntarily dismissed, so the first-notice safe harbor
   holds. But it is an argument, not a given.
2. **Existing preclusion.** A with-prejudice dismissal is a merits judgment. The
   five-day-hold damages theory in Matter 1 may already be claim-precluded by
   Matter 13, right now, whatever happens with venue. Different agencies (CDCR
   parole vs. CHP) cuts against privity; both being State of California employees
   represented by the same Attorney General's office cuts for it.

Neither point argues for or against transfer. Both should be resolved before any
notice of dismissal is filed anywhere.

### 3.3 Rule 41(d) costs

If the same claim is refiled against the same defendants, Rule 41(d) lets the new
court order Plaintiff to pay the costs of the previously dismissed action and stay
the new case until they are paid. Transfer avoids this entirely.

### 3.4 Forum-shopping optics

Dismissing after an adverse F&R and refiling in the district whose magistrate
judge issued a favorable screening order on the same incident is the textbook
pattern. Rule 41(a)(1) is a matter of right and no court can refuse the notice —
but the optic travels with the case into the N.D. Cal., and it is precisely the
fact pattern that makes a discretionary *Addison* equitable-tolling request harder
to win if the limitations question ever becomes contested. A § 1404(a) motion
grounded in where the events occurred carries none of that.

## 4. Decision matrix

### If Coggins has not yet ruled (the situation as of this memo)

**Recommended:** file a motion to transfer under § 1404(a), pled in the
alternative under § 1406(a), **now**, while the F&R is pending. Ground it in
§ 1391(b)(2) (all events in Alameda County), the December 22, 2025 concession that
removed the only Sacramento defendant, and the pendency of Matter 24 in the
transferee district. Preserves the 2025 filing date; no preclusion risk; no
limitations risk; no Rule 41(d) exposure; no effect on the Matter 2 stay.

Reserve the Rule 41(a)(1) notice as a fallback only, and only after clearing
§ 3.2 and planning the Matter 2 consequence in § 3.1.

### If Coggins declines the F&R or grants leave to amend

File the amended complaint together with the § 1404(a) motion. The case stays
alive with its May 27, 2025 filing date.

### If Coggins adopts the F&R and dismisses

The move is **not** a refiling. It is:

- Rule 59(e) motion to alter or amend — **28 days** from entry of judgment; or
- Notice of appeal to the Ninth Circuit — **30 days**, FRAP 4(a)(1)(A).

Refiling the same claims in the N.D. Cal. after a merits dismissal invites a res
judicata motion, and against a portfolio of this size, a vexatious-litigant
reference. The refile option survives only if the order is **expressly without
prejudice and with leave to refile** — and even then the November 1, 2026 clock
binds, with no credit for the 14 months Matter 1 has been on file.

---

## 5. Summary

The premise that filing before November 1, 2026 makes the plan safe is the part to
discard. The limitations date is a floor, not a permission slip: it does not
survive a preclusive judgment, and the judgment is what arrives when Coggins rules.
The window in which dismiss-and-refile is even mechanically available is open
today and shuts at entry of judgment.

But the better instrument was always available and is still available: the case
does not need to be dismissed to end up in the Northern District. It needs to be
transferred there — with its filing date intact.
