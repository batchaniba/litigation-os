# Dossier: U.S. District Judge Dale A. Drozd — Hand-Pulled Objections Playbook

**Who:** Dale Alan Drozd, U.S. District Judge, Eastern District of California, Sacramento.
Full bio, structural context, and 2026 case-type breakdown are in the companion sampled audit —
read that first: [Dale A. Drozd — 2026 docket audit](dale-a-drozd-2026.md). This hand pull does
not repeat it. It answers one question: **what persuades Drozd when a magistrate judge's
findings and recommendations on a Rule 12 motion come up for de novo review on objections?**

**Why now:** He is the district judge on *Newanforbi v. UPS*, No. 2:26-cv-02847-DAD-CKD
(Matter 26). The MTD hearing is October 7, 2026 before Magistrate Judge Delaney, who will
issue findings and recommendations; 14-day objections follow; Drozd decides. UPS's reply
(Dkt. 14, filed September 14, 2026) asks for with-prejudice disposition and doubles down on
its Exhibit A "California Sex Offender Registry" source-of-record contradiction. The
objections brief is the last paper Drozd will read before ruling — this dossier is the
playbook for writing it.

**Method honesty:** CourtListener's full-text index for recent E.D. Cal. district-court
opinions is thin, so this is not a corpus census. It is built from twelve verified signed
orders (Justia/govinfo, 2021–2026) read in full or in substantial excerpt, plus the
CourtListener opinion search used as a finding aid. Outcome counts below are an observed
sample, not a rate. Anything not verified against a primary source is labeled unverified.

## 1. The F&R pipeline — how Drozd actually decides

The structural fact from the audit holds and deepens: **the magistrate judge writes the
decision; Drozd reviews it.** Every adoption order in the sample opens with the same
boilerplate, and the boilerplate is the tell — it is short, and it means what it says:

> "In accordance with the provisions of 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1)(C), this court has conducted a
> de novo review of the case. Having carefully reviewed the entire file, [including
> plaintiff's objections,] the court concludes that the findings and recommendations are
> supported by the record and by proper analysis. Accordingly: 1. The findings and
> recommendations . . . are adopted in full . . . ."

Observed F&R dispositions (sample of six, 2021–2026):

| Date | Action | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 6/10/26 | Adopted in full | Objections filed; called "lack[ing] support" and "meritless" — "provide no basis upon which to reject the pending findings and recommendations." Motion for reconsideration denied; matter referred back to the MJ. |
| 2/16/26 | Adopted in full | No objections filed; de novo review of the file; adopted. |
| 5/7/25 | **Declined to adopt** | F&R recommended dismissal for failure to prosecute. Drozd credited the pro se plaintiff's specific factual representations (law-library/mail disruptions at Kern Valley State Prison, corroborated by docket entries) and found the defendant's reading of the objections was a misconstruction. Referred back to the MJ. |
| 4/30/24 | Adopted in part, declined in part | Social-security appeal: adopted the F&R's "specific and legitimate reasons" analysis on the medical-opinion issue, rejected its "clear and convincing" analysis on the symptom-testimony issue, granted the appeal, remanded. A genuinely split decision on the merits. |
| 6/15/21 | Adopted in full | Employment case (IKEA): adopted F&R granting 12(b)(6) with leave to amend. |
| 2/14/26 | Adopted in full | Prisoner §1983: adopted F&R, dismissed SAC with prejudice. |

The pattern: adoption in full is the default, but the two non-adoptions prove the review is
real — **he reads the file, and he will break with the MJ when the record contradicts the
recommendation.** Both breaks turned on concrete record facts, not legal re-argument. In the
5/7/25 decline, the fulcrum was a specific factual showing (dates of law-library denial,
corroborating docket entries, a prior extension order) that undercut the F&R's premise. In
the 4/30/24 split, it was a line-by-line engagement with the ALJ-error analysis.

**What this means for objections:** the objections brief is not a second opposition brief.
Drozd's own words on the purpose of objections (2009, from his magistrate-judge years, and
nothing in his district-judge output contradicts it):

> "The purpose of filing objections to a magistrate judge's findings and recommendations is
> merely to identify the portions of the findings and recommendations to which objection is
> made and the basis of the objection. The district judge will conduct a de novo review of
> all portions of the findings and recommendations to which objections are made. The purpose
> of such objections is not to provide the parties an opportunity to conduct additional
> research and present new arguments that were not included in the motion heard by the
> assigned magistrate judge."

Tactical consequences:

1. **Object to specific passages, with record citations.** "The F&R states X (p. __); the
   record shows Y (Ex. __)." The 6/10/26 order is the model of what fails: objections that
   "lack support" and re-argue a discovery ruling without a showing.
2. **No new arguments, no new research.** If it wasn't before Delaney, it doesn't go in
   the objections. New authority should have been in the opposition.
3. **Lead with the record contradiction, not the legal theory.** The single most
   objection-friendly fact pattern in the sample is "the F&R's premise is factually wrong on
   this record." For UPS, that is Exhibit A itself: the F&R (if it credits UPS's national-
   registry reframing) must be confronted with the document's own words — "SOURCE OF
   RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY," components marked "Record Found"/"Decisional,"
   "INELIGIBLE" result. That is a 5/7/25-style record correction, not a re-argument.
4. **Expect no hearing before Drozd.** Motions are taken under submission on the papers
   (L.R. 230(g)); the October 7 Delaney hearing is the only oral argument in the pipeline.
   The objections brief must be self-sufficient.
5. **Fourteen days means fourteen days.** Every F&R in the sample carries a 14-day
   objections notice, and Drozd enforces the clock (the 2/16/26 adoption notes the time
   "has now passed"). Calendar it from service, not from the hearing.

## 2. Rule 12(b)(6) + leave to amend — the observed pattern

Drozd's 12(b)(6) orders follow a consistent architecture: Twombly/Iqbal plausibility
recitation (via *N. Star*, *Balistreri*), claim-by-claim analysis, then a binary
leave-to-amend call with a 21-day amend-or-notice deadline. Observed 2025–2026 civil
dispositions:

- **Granted with leave (the norm on first MTD):** *Altman v. Hartford*, 2:26-cv-00365-
  DAD-CKD (8/6/26) — MTD granted with leave to amend; 21 days to file FAC or notice of
  intent not to; motion taken under submission per L.R. 230(g). Note the case number:
  same DAD-CKD judge pair as *Newanforbi v. UPS*. **·** 7/19/25 order — MTD granted with
  leave as to all causes; 21 days; scheduling conference reset before Drozd by Zoom;
  defendant "encouraged to take heed of the court's analysis . . . in formulating its
  response to any amended complaint" — he writes MTD orders as roadmaps for the next
  round. **·** 4/9/26 CHP/State order — MTD granted in part: §1983 claims vs. the State
  dismissed **without** leave; state-tort claims dismissed **with** leave; 21 days.
- **Granted without further leave (only after failure to cure):** 7/18/25 order —
  second amended complaint dismissed; SAC "failed to [cure] the deficiencies the court
  had identified in detail in its prior order." Cites *Foman v. Davis* (failure to cure
  justifies denial) and *Steckman* ("exercise in futility"). The revealing passage is
  from the *prior* order in that case: the court had been "skeptical that plaintiff
  could remedy the pleading deficiencies" **but granted leave anyway** "due to the lack
  of a clear showing that amendment would be futile at that time."
- **Denied:** SJ for the employer under *McDonnell Douglas* in the CDCR Title VII case
  (9/29/25) — but that is summary judgment, not 12(b)(6); at the pleading stage he
  almost always gives one amendment.

The leave standard, in his own words: leave is denied only on a **clear showing of
futility**. Skepticism is not enough. For Matter 26, this cuts both ways and favors the
plaintiff: UPS's with-prejudice ask runs against his strong presumption of one free
amendment, and the 7/19/25 "roadmap" habit suggests that even a partial grant would come
with detailed guidance rather than a trap. But the corollary is strict — **the amendment,
if one is granted, must actually cure what the order identifies.** The 7/18/25 dismissal
is what happens when it doesn't.

Two procedural tripwires from the 4/9/26 order, both live in Matter 26:

- **L.R. 230(c) — always oppose.** Failure to file a timely opposition "may be construed
  as nonopposition," and Drozd has granted dismissal partly on that basis. (Not at issue
  in UPS — opposition was filed — but it governs any future motion.)
- **The 21-day notice alternative.** His orders routinely offer "file an amended
  complaint, or alternatively, a notice of intent not to do so" — and warn that silence
  "will result in the dismissal of those claims without further leave to amend." If an
  F&R recommends dismissal with leave and the plaintiff wants to stand on the pleading
  for appeal, the notice is the safe harbor; silence is not.

## 3. FCRA / background-check record — the centerpiece

*Smith v. General Information Services, Inc.*, No. 1:17-cv-00542-DAD-SAB (fee order
May 14, 2019) is the closest case in the federal reporter system to Matter 26's facts,
and Drozd was the district judge:

- An employment background-check company (GIS, a CRA) furnished a report to Walmart;
  **Walmart had delegated to the CRA the decision whether the applicant met its hiring
  standards** — a "Non Competitive" adjudication by the CRA disqualified the applicant.
  (The complaint alleged the report contained San Luis Obispo County criminal cases
  belonging to a different person, Robert Gregory Smith, Jr., not the plaintiff
  Robert Glenn Smith.)
- The case settled on plaintiff's acceptance of a **Rule 68 offer of $105,000**.
- On the fee motion, Drozd **twice rejected the defendant's "simple FCRA case"
  framing**: "multiple district courts have recognized that the FCRA 'is a complex and
  challenging area of law.'" He wrote: "The court takes plaintiff's allegations — that
  he was denied employment because defendant incorrectly determined that he had a
  criminal record — **to be a serious matter**," adding that "it is precisely because
  such claims may not yield a large monetary recovery that attorneys may be less willing
  to prosecute them in the first place. This court is certainly not inclined to reduce
  an attorney's lodestar recovery on that basis."
- He awarded $103,840 in fees plus $31,592.14 in costs ($135,432.14 total), and
  **refused to penalize counsel for rejecting an earlier $66,000 Rule 68 offer** to
  obtain the $105,000 result: "By turning down defendant's first Rule 68 offer,
  plaintiff ultimately increased his recovery by more than a third . . . The court will
  not penalize plaintiff's counsel for working diligently to maximize his client's
  recovery."

Why this matters for the UPS objections: Drozd has already, on the record, (a) treated
a background-check company's inaccurate criminal-record reporting as a serious matter
rather than a technical pleading dispute; (b) rejected defense minimization of FCRA
litigation as "simple"; and (c) rewarded, not punished, a plaintiff who held out past
an early settlement number. The objections brief should quote the "serious matter"
language and frame the Exhibit A contradiction in its terms: this is not a dispute
about registry taxonomy, it is a dispute about a man denied work because of what a
background check said about him.

His magistrate-judge-era FCRA orders (2012–2013) are less useful — granted 12(b)(6)s
with 30 days' leave in debt-collection FCRA/FDCPA cases, and one F&R recommending FCRA
claims dismissed with prejudice — but they confirm the through-line: **he grants leave
freely at the pleading stage and reserves with-prejudice disposition for claims that
cannot be cured.**

## 4. Pro se treatment

The audit's "liberal amendment, strict futility cutoff" holds, with two refinements:

- He **credits specific factual representations from pro se litigants** even against a
  represented defendant's characterization — the 5/7/25 decline-to-adopt turned on
  crediting the plaintiff's account of prison law-library disruptions over defense
  counsel's misconstruction of the objections. Precision and record-corroboration earn
  credibility; conclusory assertions do not.
- The futility cutoff is real and final: *Kononov* (2/14/26) adopted an F&R dismissing
  a pro se SAC **with prejudice**; the 7/18/25 order closed a case after the SAC failed
  to cure. Liberal amendment is one full chance, not an indefinite one.

## 5. Case management, scheduling, settlement posture

- **No oral argument before Drozd; papers decide.** L.R. 230(g) submission is the norm
  (observed in the 8/6/26 *Altman* order); his 2020 emergency procedures (all motions
  on the papers, MJ referrals for F&Rs) remain his operating pattern in 2026 output.
- **He manages through the MJ.** Referral for F&Rs under 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1)(B) and
  L.R. 302 is routine; after ruling on objections he refers matters back to the MJ
  "for further proceedings."
- **Settlement runs through magistrate judges.** In *New Directions Program v. Sierra
  Health*, No. 2:22-cv-01090-DAD-JDP, the settlement conference was held by MJ Peterson
  on Drozd's case — the standard pipeline. His scheduling orders push parties toward
  MJ-led settlement conferences, and the lapsed $250,000 demand's offer of a conference
  before Delaney fit that institutional preference. With the demand lapsed and Dkt. 14
  filed, the next natural settlement window is **after** the F&R/MTD ruling, when both
  sides have new information — consistent with his practice of letting motion practice
  mature before settlement pressure builds.
- **Timing realism:** F&R-to-order lags of weeks to months are normal. The October 7
  hearing produces a recommendation; Drozd's ruling follows on his own docket's time.

## 6. Matter 26 posture and the objections playbook

Portfolio facts (from the matter record, not independently re-verified for this
dossier): UPS's Exhibit A states "SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY,"
marks registry components "Record Found"/"Decisional," and returns "INELIGIBLE." The
Dkt. 14 reply seeks with-prejudice dismissal, reframes the data as national-registry
sourcing, and — per the matter record — leaves Count II and negligence per se
undefended as pleaded. The $250,000 demand lapsed September 14, 2026 with no engagement.

The playbook, in order:

1. **Win the F&R.** Drozd adopts in full as the default. The October 7 hearing before
   Delaney is the dispositive event; the objections brief is damage control or
   confirmation, not a fresh start.
2. **If objections are needed, make them a record-correction brief.** Pin each
   objection to a specific F&R passage and the exhibit or docket entry that contradicts
   it. Exhibit A vs. the national-registry reframing is the lead exhibit — it is the
   5/7/25 pattern (F&R premise factually wrong on the record), and it speaks in the
   "serious matter" register of *Smith v. GIS*.
3. **Quote his own standards back to him:** the "clear showing of futility" leave
   standard against the with-prejudice ask; the "simple FCRA case" rejection against
   any minimization; the objections-purpose language to keep the brief disciplined and
   specific (which itself signals credibility).
4. **Do not ask him for a hearing.** He won't hold one. Do not file new evidence or
   new arguments with the objections — his rule is explicit.
5. **Protect the amendment.** If the F&R recommends dismissal with leave, the 21-day
   amend-or-notice deadline is jurisdictional in effect: file the FAC or the notice of
   intent not to. Silence forfeits. And the FAC must cure what the order identifies —
   the 7/18/25 dismissal is the warning.
6. **Settlement timing:** let the MTD ruling land first. A post-ruling settlement
   posture — ideally via an MJ-led conference, his preferred channel — carries more
   leverage than the lapsed pre-ruling demand did.

## Quotable Drozd (verified from signed orders)

- On F&R review: "this court has conducted a de novo review of the case. Having
  carefully reviewed the entire file . . . the court concludes that the findings and
  recommendations are supported by the record and by proper analysis." → "adopted in
  full."
- On failed objections: "Plaintiff's objections lack support, are meritless and provide
  no basis upon which to reject the pending findings and recommendations." (6/10/26)
- On the purpose of objections: objections exist "merely to identify the portions of
  the findings and recommendations to which objection is made and the basis of the
  objection" — "not . . . an opportunity to conduct additional research and present new
  arguments." (2009)
- On leave to amend: granted despite skepticism, "due to the lack of a clear showing
  that amendment would be futile at that time"; denied only where amendment would be an
  "exercise in futility" after failure to cure. (7/18/25)
- On background-check cases: "The court takes plaintiff's allegations — that he was
  denied employment because defendant incorrectly determined that he had a criminal
  record — to be a serious matter." / the FCRA is a "complex and challenging area of
  law," not a "simple" case. (*Smith v. GIS*, 5/14/19)
- On holding out in settlement: "The court will not penalize plaintiff's counsel for
  working diligently to maximize his client's recovery." (*Smith v. GIS*)
