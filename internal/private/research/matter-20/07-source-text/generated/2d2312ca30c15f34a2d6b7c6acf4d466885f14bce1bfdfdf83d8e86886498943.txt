the above-referenced matter. The following outlines the facts of the case, relevant legal challenges,
and supporting case law demonstrating why the suspension of my driving privileges is unwarranted.




1. Welfare Check: Improper Escalation Without Probable Cause


On November 1, 2024, at approximately 12:30 a.m., | was safely parked on the shoulder of the
freeway. My vehicle was off, the key not in the ignition, and | was asleep in the driver's seat. Officer
Santiago Meza-Gonzalez approached my vehicle as part of a welfare check, a legitimate community
caretaking function. However, this welfare check was escalated into a DUI investigation without
specific, articulable facts or probable cause.

2. Context of the Holiday Enforcement Period


This incident occurred immediately after Halloween, during a period of heightened law enforcement
targeting alcohol-related offenses. While public safety initiatives during holidays are understandable,
systemic pressures during such times can lead to overly aggressive enforcement and unnecessary
escalation.


Relevant Case Law:


          1. People v. Reyes (2011) 195 Cal.App.4th 946:

               ¢« Law enforcement actions influenced by quotas or systemic pressures risk

              constitutional protections.



Challenges:

          The timing of this encounter raises legitimate concerns about whether the officer's actions
     were influenced by misplaced motivations or systemic pressures associated with heightened
      holiday enforcement campaigns.

          This context supports reasonable skepticism about the fairness and motivations behind the
     investigation.

 3. Lack of Probable Cause for the DUI Investigation


 The officer cited observations such as "red and watery eyes," "slurred speech," and an odor of
 alcohol. However, these subjective observations were not corroborated by any objective evidence of
 impairment, such as erratic driving or unsafe behavior.


 Relevant Case Law:


          1. Mercer v. DMV (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753:

               ¢« DUI investigations must be based on objective evidence of volitional movement or

               impairment.

          2. People v. Torres (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 977:

                      Physical symptoms like red eyes or slurred speech, without corroborating evidence
               of driving or impairment, are insufficient to justify a DUI arrest.



Challenges:

           The officer failed to account for alternative explanations for these observations, such as the
      effects of being abruptly awakened from sleep.

           There was no evidence of unsafe driving or recent operation of the vehicle to substantiate
      the officer's claims.



4. Ancillary Findings Are Fruit of the Poisonous Tree


Any subsequent observations or evidence, such as alleged poor performance on Field Sobriety
Tests (FSTs), were obtained following an improper escalation and are inadmissible under the fruit of
the poisonous tree doctrine.

              the poisonous tree.

         2. People v. Medina (1972) 6 Cal.3d 484:

                  - Findings derived from an unlawful search or detention cannot retroactively justify the
              initial actions.
