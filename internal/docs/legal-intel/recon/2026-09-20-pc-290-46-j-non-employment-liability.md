# Penal Code § 290.46(j) — liability outside employment

**Compiled 2026-09-20.** Doctrinal research memorandum; not legal advice; requester is pro se.

**Question:** Where does civil or criminal liability attach for use of California registry / Megan's Law website information **other than employment**?

**Answer in one line:** The same private-money statute that covers employment — § 290.46(j)(4)(A) — covers **seven other prohibited purposes** in (j)(2)(A)–(D) and (F)–(H). The field outside employment is even thinner: housing has an Attorney General opinion and one published anti-SLAPP case; insurance, credit, loans, education, and ordinary business-service denials have the statutory text and almost no case law.

This memo does **not** reopen the employment matrix in `internal/docs/PC_29046_EMPLOYMENT_CASES.md`. It maps the rest of subdivision (j).

---

## 0. What "use of registry information" actually means

Liability is **source-specific**, not status-specific.

- The civil bar is use of information **disclosed pursuant to § 290.46** (the DOJ Megan's Law internet website) or, in parallel, information **disclosed pursuant to § 290.4** (the DOJ public-inquiry service).
- Independent court records, self-disclosure, a lawful rap sheet under Penal Code § 11105, or a vendor report sourced to ordinary criminal indexes are **not** automatically a § 290.46 use. That is the *Donaleski* / *Lerma* source rule, and nothing in (j)(2) confines it to employment.
- Registrants are **not** a FEHA housing protected class merely because (j)(2)(G) exists. 89 Ops.Cal.Atty.Gen. 31 (2006) (Op. No. 05-301). **PRIMARY VERIFIED** (official PDF).

The current (j) lettering is the operative text (Stats. 2021, ch. 615; operative version of § 290.46). Older opinions say **(l)** for the same use restrictions. Do not cite (l) in a 2026 filing.

## 1. The eight prohibited purposes — employment is only one letter

Penal Code § 290.46(j)(1)–(2), current text (**PRIMARY VERIFIED**, leginfo 2026-09-20):

> A person is authorized to use information disclosed pursuant to this section **only to protect a person at risk**.
>
> Except as authorized under paragraph (1) or any other provision of law, use of any information that is disclosed pursuant to this section for purposes relating to any of the following is prohibited:

| Letter | Prohibited purpose | Employment? |
|---|---|---|
| (A) | Health insurance | No |
| (B) | Insurance | No |
| (C) | Loans | No |
| (D) | Credit | No |
| (E) | **Employment** | Yes — portfolio cluster 5 |
| (F) | Education, scholarships, or fellowships | No |
| (G) | Housing or accommodations | No |
| (H) | Benefits, privileges, or services provided by any business establishment | No |

The parallel public-inquiry statute, § 290.4(d)(1)–(2), copies the same eight letters and the same money schedule. **PRIMARY VERIFIED**, leginfo 2026-09-20.

(j)(3) then **preserves** independently authorized uses, including:

- Penal Code §§ 11105 and 11105.3 (official criminal-history access)
- Family Code § 8808
- Financial Code §§ 777.5 and 14409.2
- Health & Safety Code §§ 1522.01 and 1596.871 (community care / child care)
- Labor Code § 432.7

Those carve-outs are not employment-only. Child-care licensing and bank/credit-union access are the non-employment examples the Legislature actually named.

## 2. The money statute does not change with the letter

§ 290.46(j)(4)(A) is one sentence. It does not give employment a different dollar schedule from housing, insurance, or a gym membership.

Current text (**PRIMARY VERIFIED**):

> Any use of information disclosed pursuant to this section for purposes other than those provided by paragraph (1) or in violation of paragraph (2) shall make the user liable for the actual damages, and any amount that may be determined by a jury or a court sitting without a jury, not exceeding three times the amount of actual damage, and not less than two hundred fifty dollars ($250), and attorney's fees, exemplary damages, or a civil penalty not exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000).

Read it as written:

1. **Actual damages** — lost rent, lost loan, lost coverage, emotional distress according to proof. Not a statutory minimum.
2. **Additional amount** — trier of fact, **floor $250**, **ceiling 3× actuals**. This is the only true statutory floor, and it is $250, not $25,000.
3. **Attorney's fees.**
4. **Exemplary damages *or* a civil penalty not exceeding $25,000.** The $25,000 figure is a **cap on the civil-penalty option**, not a substitute floor for actuals. Cluster 5 lock.

(j)(4)(B) is **preventive** only (injunction / restraining order) for a **pattern or practice** of misuse of paragraph (2). The Attorney General, a district attorney, a city attorney, **or any person aggrieved** may bring it. Remedies are independent of Civil Code Part 2 (commencing with § 43).

*Towner v. County of Ventura*, Cal. Ct. App. 2d Dist. No. B306283 (Apr. 28, 2021) (published): § 290.46 "**expressly prescribes civil remedies — including actual and exemplary damages, and a civil penalty — for a violation of its provisions**," and a violation is therefore **not** a misdemeanor/felony for *Flatley* anti-SLAPP illegality. That characterization is not employment-limited. Do not describe *Towner* as a merits win on a § 290.46 claim.

The official Megan's Law site restates the same civil schedule under "Penalties for Misuse" (Pen. Code, § 290.46, subd. (j)).

## 3. Where non-employment liability actually has authority

### 3.1 Housing / accommodations — (j)(2)(G) — the only developed non-employment field

**Statute.** Using website-disclosed information to deny, terminate, or condition housing, except to protect a person at risk or as authorized by another provision of law, is a (j)(4)(A) civil wrong.

**Attorney General Opinion No. 05-301 (Apr. 27, 2006), 89 Ops.Cal.Atty.Gen. 31. PRIMARY VERIFIED (oag.ca.gov PDF).**

- Question asked: does (j)(2)(G) make registrants a FEHA "protected class"?
- Answer: **No.**
- Housing holding that **does** matter: subdivision (j) is "unequivocal." A registrant may be denied housing "**to protect a person at risk**" or for a reason "**authorized by a specific provision of law**." Use of Megan's Law website identifying information to deny housing **for any other reason** "will subject the person making such improper use of the information to civil liability under subdivision (j)(4)."
- Independent of FEHA. A landlord who uses the **website** to refuse a unit has Penal Code exposure even though DFEH will not treat the applicant as a FEHA class member.

**Los Angeles City Attorney report (Nov. 29, 2007)** to Council File 07-0976 (SECONDARY, city clerk PDF): same reading; adds that § 290.46 "limits a landlord's ability to use information obtained from the DOJ web site, but **does not restrict the use of information about an applicant's criminal history obtained from other sources**." Unruh does not, in that office's view, bar a refusal based on a conviction that reflects ability to satisfy a tenancy. Do not cite the City Attorney memo as a holding.

**Civil Code § 2079.10a** is the **opposite** of a 290.46 claim. Residential leases and 1–4 unit sale contracts must contain the statutory Megan's Law notice pointing tenants/buyers to `www.meganslaw.ca.gov`. After that notice, the lessor/seller/broker "is not required to provide information in addition" about nearby registrants, and "**[t]he information in the notice shall not give rise to any cause of action against the disclosing party by a registered sex offender.**" A required database notice is not a prohibited housing **use** against the registrant.

***Cross v. Cooper*, 197 Cal.App.4th 357 (6th Dist. 2011). PRIMARY VERIFIED (opinion text).**

Not a landlord-denial case. A tenant told a prospective buyer's agent that a registrant lived across the street. The seller sued for interference; the tenant brought anti-SLAPP. Holdings that matter outside employment:

- The **propriety of a § 290.46 use depends on whether it was intended to protect a person at risk.**
- A **specific, identifiable person actually at risk is not required.** The statute authorizes disclosure "not only when one provides the information to a mother who is standing in front of her house with a small child in her arms but also where one provides information with a **reasonable and good faith belief** that doing so will help protect another person and intending that the information do so."
- Intent is a **contested fact** (protective purpose vs. making the house unsellable).
- Even a proven unauthorized-but-noncriminal disclosure would not strip anti-SLAPP protection (*Mendoza* / *Flatley*: "illegal" means **criminal**).

*Cross* is therefore **defense-useful on (j)(1)** in housing-adjacent speech, and **not** a judgment that a landlord who used the website to refuse a tenant is liable. No located published judgment imposes (j)(4)(A) damages on a landlord.

Jessica's Law residency restrictions (Pen. Code § 3003.5 and related parole conditions) are a **different** statute. They are not § 290.46(j) private liability.

### 3.2 Business-establishment services — (j)(2)(H)

This is the catch-all: stores, banks, gyms, hotels, insurers-as-businesses, schools-as-businesses. The money statute is identical.

No located published or unpublished merits decision holds a business liable under (j)(2)(H) for refusing service because of a Megan's Law website lookup.

Unruh (Civ. Code § 51) occupies similar geography ("business establishments") but does **not** supply a shortcut. Op. 05-301 walked through *Harris v. Capital Growth Investors XIV*, 52 Cal.3d 1142 (1991), and *Koebke*: registrant status is not an enumerated Unruh class, and adding it would have to survive the *Harris* three-prong test. Do not plead Unruh as if (j)(2)(H) automatically converted the registrant into a protected class. Plead (j)(4)(A) on the **source** (website use), not on status.

### 3.3 Insurance / health insurance / loans / credit — (j)(2)(A)–(D)

Statutory prohibition is clear. **No located case** applies (j)(4)(A) to an underwriting, lending, or credit decision.

(j)(3)'s Financial Code §§ 777.5 and 14409.2 are the Legislature's reminder that **official** criminal-history access for certain financial institutions is preserved. That is the *Pastrana* structure (specific authorizing statute), not a license to scrape meganslaw.ca.gov for a credit file.

ICRAA / CCRAA / FCRA may independently restrict how a CRA **reports** criminal history for credit or insurance. Those are different statutes with different money (ICRAA § 1786.50: actuals or $10,000). Do not collapse them into (j)(4).

### 3.4 Education, scholarships, fellowships — (j)(2)(F)

Same: statutory bar, **no located merits case**. (j)(3) preserves Health & Safety Code child-care / community-care access. Penal Code § 290.45(d)(2) separately immunizes a school or day-care that in good faith disseminates **law-enforcement community-notification** information authorized under § 290.45 — a different information stream from the public website.

## 4. Criminal money that is not the (j)(4) civil action

These are **not** the private (j)(4)(A) schedule. They are additional, and they are not employment-specific.

| Provision | Conduct | Money / custody |
|---|---|---|
| § 290.46(h)(1) | Use of **website-disclosed** information to commit a **misdemeanor** | Additional fine **$10,000–$50,000** |
| § 290.46(h)(2) | Use of website-disclosed information to commit a **felony** | Consecutive **five-year** term (Pen. Code § 1170(h)) |
| § 290.46(i) | A **§ 290 registrant** who **enters** the Megan's Law internet website | Fine ≤ $1,000 and/or county jail ≤ 6 months |
| § 290.4(c)(1)–(2) | Same felony/misdemeanor misuse of the **inquiry service** | Felony: +5 years. Misdemeanor: additional fine **$500–$1,000** (narrower than (h)(1)) |
| § 290.45(e) | Misuse of **law-enforcement community-notification** information | Felony: +5 years. Misdemeanor: $500–$1,000 |

*Towner* is about the **civil** use-restriction in (j), not about (h) or (i). Harassment or a crime committed with website information is the (h) path. A housing or insurance denial, without more, is the (j)(4) civil path.

The website splash language quoted in Op. 05-301 tracks this split: "Anyone who uses this information to commit a crime or to harass an offender or his or her family is subject to criminal prosecution and civil liability."

## 5. Independent civil remedies the statute itself preserves

(j)(4)(B) last sentence: pattern/practice injunctive remedies are "**independent of any other remedies or procedures** that may be available to an aggrieved party under other provisions of law, including Part 2 (commencing with Section 43) of Division 1 of the Civil Code."

So, outside employment, a website-based denial can theoretically stack:

- (j)(4)(A) money
- (j)(4)(B) injunction if a pattern is pleaded
- ordinary torts (Civ. Code §§ 43 et seq.) — defamation, privacy, interference — on their own elements
- Unruh / FEHA **only** if an independent protected-class theory exists (Op. 05-301: registrant status alone is not enough)

Do not invent a FEHA or Unruh count from (j)(2)(G) or (H).

## 6. What is **not** non-employment § 290.46 liability

| Theory | Why it fails or is a different statute |
|---|---|
| "They used my conviction, so (j) applies" | Source must be information disclosed **pursuant to § 290.46 / § 290.4**. Court records and self-disclosure are the *Donaleski* / *Lerma* hole in every letter, not just (E). |
| FEHA housing discrimination because of registration | Op. 05-301: (j)(2)(G) does not create a protected class. |
| Unruh solely because of registration | Same opinion; *Harris* test not satisfied by the Penal Code bar alone. |
| Required Megan's Law lease/sale notice | Civ. Code § 2079.10a; the notice **cannot** be sued on by the registrant. |
| Neighbor / buyer warning with a protective purpose | *Cross*: (j)(1) is purpose-based; no named child required; anti-SLAPP may attach. |
| Child-care / community-care official checks | (j)(3) + Health & Saf. Code §§ 1522.01, 1596.871. |
| Official bank/credit-union criminal-history access | (j)(3) + Fin. Code §§ 777.5, 14409.2. |
| Law-enforcement community notification | § 290.45, with its own immunity and smaller criminal fines — not (j)(4). |
| Jessica's Law 2,000-foot residency rules | Pen. Code § 3003.5 etc., not a (j)(4) private action. |
| A CRA that only republishes website data to a landlord or insurer | *Mendoza*: the statutory "user" is the actor who **acts** on the information. Same limit as employment. |

Current § 290.45 **does not** define "person at risk." Do not cite a former § 290.45(a)(8) definition. That correction in `PC_29046_EMPLOYMENT_CASES.md` still holds. *Cross* supplies the working judicial test: reasonable, good-faith protective purpose; specific identifiable victim not required.

## 7. Practical map for this portfolio

The live screening matters (25, 26, 27, 29, 36–41) are **(j)(2)(E) employment** files. This memo is not a new cause of action in those cases.

Non-employment (j) liability would matter only if a **separate** actor used the website for a **different letter**:

- a **landlord** who pulled meganslaw.ca.gov and denied a unit → (G) + (j)(4)(A), with Op. 05-301 as the construction authority and *Cross* as the person-at-risk fight
- an **insurer, lender, or school** that used the website to deny coverage, credit, or admission → (A)–(D) or (F), statute only, no located case
- a **business** that used the website to refuse service → (H), statute only; do not convert it into Unruh
- **harassment / a crime** committed with website information → (h), criminal, not the civil money schedule
- a **pattern** of website misuse (same landlord, same insurer) → (j)(4)(B) injunction, including a private aggrieved-person action

There is still **no located final judgment** awarding (j)(4)(A) dollars against anyone — employer, landlord, insurer, or store. Outside employment, the **strongest citable liability statement** is not a damages judgment. It is the Attorney General's 2006 construction of (j)(2)(G): website-based housing denial, unless (j)(1) or another specific statute authorizes it, "will subject the person making such improper use … to civil liability under subdivision (j)(4)."
