 1                                      JURISDICTION & SERVICE
 2          Plaintiff’s Position:
 3          The Court has federal-question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. section 1331 over Plaintiff's
 4   claims under 42 U.S.C. section 1983 and 18 U.S.C. sections 1962 and 1964, and supplemental
 5   jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. section 1367 over the related California claims. Venue is proper in this
 6   District. Defendants accepted service effective August 31, 2026, and no party remains to be served.
 7          Defendants’ Position:
 8          Jurisdiction is not disputed. The Court has jurisdiction over this matter pursuant to 28 U.S.C.
 9   § 1331. Defendants have been served with a copy of the summons and Complaint.
10                                                   FACTS
11          Plaintiff’s Position:
12          Plaintiff was required, as a condition of California parole, to participate in sex-offender
13   treatment provided by HOPE under its state contractual and regulatory role. Plaintiff alleges that
14   HOPE and its chief executive officer and policymaker, Shannon Smith, maintained a blanket
15   retention policy linking treatment completion to the full parole term rather than an individualized
16   clinical determination; used false or retaliatory attendance and treatment-status reporting in the parole
17   process; and continued those practices after receiving notice of Plaintiff's objections. Defendants
18   dispute liability and the material factual allegations.
19          Defendants’ Position:
20          Defendants deny the Complaint's allegations and deny that Defendants engaged in any
21   wrongful conduct.
22                                              LEGAL ISSUES
23          Plaintiff’s Position:
24          The principal legal issues include whether Defendants acted under color of state law under 42
25   U.S.C. section 1983; whether the challenged policy and reporting practices support individual and
26   entity liability under West v. Atkins, 487 U.S. 42 (1988), and Monell v. Department of Social Services,
27   436 U.S. 658 (1978); whether the alleged conduct violated the First and Fourteenth Amendments and
28   California Civil Code section 52.1; whether fraud, negligent-misrepresentation, and RICO elements

15                                                   RELIEF
16           Plaintiff’s Position:
17           Plaintiff seeks compensatory damages for economic loss, emotional distress, loss of liberty,
18   and reputational harm in an amount according to proof; treble damages under 18 U.S.C. section
19   1964(c); punitive damages against Smith to the extent permitted by law; declaratory and injunctive
20   relief concerning the challenged retention and reporting practices; costs; and all other authorized
21   relief. The amount is not yet fully calculable before discovery because it depends on the duration and
22   causal effect of the challenged practices, lost earning capacity, and proof of noneconomic harm.
23   Plaintiff does not seek the public-enforcement-only $25,000 civil penalty under California Civil Code
24   section 52.1(b).
