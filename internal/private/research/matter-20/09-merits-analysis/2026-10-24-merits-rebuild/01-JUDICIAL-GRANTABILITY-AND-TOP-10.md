## Judicial decision test—before advocacy
**Direct certification.** The court would need to find the agency/official authorized (§679.10(a)-(b), CHP Answer ¶¶13–14); applicant direct victim of qualifying **criminal** unlawful restraint (§236 or substantially similar criminal activity, not merely unconstitutional search); deliberate restraint and absence of lawful privilege; statutory helpfulness under §679.10(g)-(h); and that only certification is legally permissible. Physical restraint, request, response and absence of charges are documented/admitted (M pp. 2–13). **Unlawfulness, element-by-element criminal qualification and exhausted discretion remain disputed.** No automatic entitlement.

**Vacatur/remand.** The court must identify an *actual* material misapplication of the qualifying offense definition, a demonstrably incorrect factual premise that infected the denial, prohibited basis used, or reviewable abuse in Serrato's substantive conclusion. The five-sentence denial rejects qualifying and substantially similar activity; it does **not** claim no charges, criminal history, or unhelpfulness. The letter's brevity cannot alone win Count 1; independent explanation claim was dismissed by R01. A material mistake in a component of the arrest report is not automatically proof that Serrato relied on that component. **This missing nexus is the principal obstacle.**

**Focused trial/proof procedure.** R01 p.4 says the pleaded theory could prevail **if proved at trial**, not that it did prevail. CHP's verified Answer contests the sensory findings, false reporting and probable cause. Under CCP §§1090–1091 and *Lassen v. City of Alameda* (1957) 150 Cal.App.2d 44, 47–48, disputed dispositive facts can be countervailed with proof; §1094 provides for noticed-motion judgment when no triable material fact remains or the case rests solely on an administrative record. This is the most procedurally defensible immediate objective if no stipulation narrows the factual disputes.
