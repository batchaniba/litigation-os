**Opening**

Good morning, Your Honor. Brendan Newanforbi, Petitioner, appearing for myself.

I submit on the Court's tentative as to the first and fourth causes of action, and I ask the Court to adopt it. The tentative is correct that this is a classic factual dispute that cannot be resolved on demurrer. Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1), uses the word "shall": a certifying official shall certify victim helpfulness on Form I-918 Supplement B when the victim was a victim of qualifying criminal activity and has been helpful. Whether those conditions are met turns on disputed facts. That is a merits question, not a pleading question.

I am not asking the Court today to order the Form B signed. I am asking the Court to overrule the demurrer on counts one and four so the Petition can proceed to an answer and a hearing on the merits.

---

**If the Attorney General contests count one**

The AG wants the dashcam, the empty can, and the PAS refusal treated as probable cause as a matter of law. The tentative already rejects that.

The Court must take the pleaded facts as true (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318). Exhibit 5: no odor, no slurred speech, no confusion. Exhibit 6: the arrest report says otherwise. Exhibit 7: CHP's own Penal Code section 851.6 certificate, November 5, 2024 — detention only, insufficient grounds for a complaint. No charges were filed. Those are CHP's documents. They create a conflict. They do not knock the Petition out.

The tentative cites People v. Thompson (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, then holds a finder of fact could still find probable cause lacking (People v. Wolterman (1992) 11 Cal.App.4th Supp. 15; Espinoza v. Shiomoto (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 85). An empty can in a parked car, and a declined PAS the officer said I was not required to take, do not as a matter of law establish DUI.

I do not have to prove false imprisonment today. The Petition alleges it. Section 679.10 does not require a charge or a conviction.

---

**If they say certification is "discretionary"**

Mandamus cannot compel a particular exercise of discretion — I said that in the opposition — but section 679.10(g)(1) says "shall certify" when the two conditions are met. Whether they are met is the factual dispute the tentative identifies. Count one should survive so that question can be tried.

---

**If the Attorney General contests count four**

Count four is Code of Civil Procedure section 1060. It asks the Court to declare the parties' rights under Penal Code section 679.10. It does not ask the Court to declare that the November 1, 2024 stop was qualifying criminal activity. Paragraph 94 of the Petition says that expressly.

Counsel calls the claim duplicative and retrospective. The tentative already answers that. The claim may later become duplicative of the surviving first cause of action. The Court cannot make that determination on demurrer. I may plead cumulative theories, even inconsistent ones. There is no election of remedies at the pleading stage.

Count four is not a rerun of counts two and three. One of the live controversies is Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (k): whether the absence of charges or a prosecution is a legitimate basis for denial. That question travels with count one. That is prospective. Griset held that declaratory relief was resolved after the writ claims were adjudicated. We are at demurrer. Griset does not require dismissal now.
