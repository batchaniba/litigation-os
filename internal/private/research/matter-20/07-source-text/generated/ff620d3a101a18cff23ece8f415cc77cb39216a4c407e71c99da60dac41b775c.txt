IV. LEGAL STANDARD
Code of Civil Procedure section 1085 authorizes mandate to compel performance of an act
specially enjoined by law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1085, subd. (a).) Mandamus generally does not
control how an official exercises genuine discretion, but it may compel the official to exercise
discretion under the correct legal standard and may correct an abuse of administrative discretion.
(Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442-443.) Section 1088 further
permits issuance of a peremptory writ in the first instance when the application is made upon
due notice; the notice must be at least ten days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1088.) If a return raises a
factual question essential to determination of the motion and affecting the parties' substantial
rights, Code of Civil Procedure section 1090 permits the Court, in its discretion, to order that
question tried; Petitioner requests a nonjury merits determination and does not request a jury.
(Code Civ. Proc., § 1090.) No plain, speedy, and adequate remedy exists in the ordinary course of
law: Penal Code section 679.10 provides no administrative appeal from CHP's certification denial,
and an award of damages would not supply the certification, which must come from an
authorized certifying official or entity. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1086.)

Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1) provides that, upon the victim's request, a certifying
official "shall certify victim helpfulness on the Form I-918 Supplement B certification" when the
victim was a victim of qualifying criminal activity and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful to
detection, investigation, or prosecution. Section 679.10 expressly includes false imprisonment
and unlawful criminal restraint among qualifying criminal activity and extends to state offenses
whose nature and elements are substantially similar to the enumerated activity. (Pen. Code, §
679.10, subds. (c), (d); 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U)(iii).) Subdivision (h) supplies a rebuttable
presumption of helpfulness where the victim has not refused or failed to provide information and
assistance reasonably requested by law enforcement. Subdivision (k) provides that certification
does not require an investigation, charge, apprehension, prosecution, or conviction, and that
separate-case cooperation cannot be a denial reason. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)(1), (k)(3)(H).)
[Quote § 679.10, subds. (b), (h), and (k) from the official text in the filed version.]

The review standard is abuse of discretion. (Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547, 562.) A
particular result may be compelled where the governmental entity has no discretion left to
exercise because the duty has become ministerial under the governing law. (Common Cause,
supra, 49 Cal.3d at pp. 442-443; California Correctional Supervisors, 96 Cal.App.4th at 827.) Direct
certification is therefore available only if the proven record establishes the statutory predicates
and leaves no lawful reasonable alternative; otherwise the remedy is vacatur and remand for
lawful reconsideration.


V. ARGUMENT

A. The existing record permits a finding that probable cause for the DUI arrest was lacking.
Probable cause exists when the facts known to the arresting officer would persuade a person of
reasonable caution that the person arrested committed a crime. (People v. Thompson (2006) 38
Cal.4th 811, 818; Order at p. 3.) Here, the Court has already held that if it credits Petitioner's
factual showing concerning the absence of alcohol odor, the absence of impairment in speech
and field sobriety testing, and false statements in the report, it could reasonably find probable
cause lacking. (Order at p. 4.)

The recording is objective evidence bearing on that question. It contains no statement by an
officer that Petitioner's breath or person smelled of alcohol; the liquid examined in the vehicle is
identified on the recording as urine; and Petitioner's speech is recorded throughout the
encounter. (Pet. Ex. 5; Decl. ¶¶ 7-10.) Petitioner performed the field sobriety tests the officers
administered. The preliminary alcohol screening test was described to him by the officer as not
required, and arrest followed refusal within about a minute. (Pet. Ex. 5 at 01:10:54, 01:11:15–
01:11:33; Order at p. 3.) The strongest focused discrepancy in the filled record is the walk-and-
turn: the report attributes the choice/difficulty to the subject, while the recording has Officer
Melano identifying uneven freeway ground and saying "I wouldn't do that one." (Pet. Ex. 6 at p. 3,
line 18; Pet. Ex. 5 at 01:10:23–01:10:30.) On the record as framed by the Court, Petitioner asks the
Court to find the arrest lacked probable cause.

CHP's section 851.6 certificate supplies additional contemporaneous evidence. CHP documented
that there were "insufficient grounds for making a criminal complaint," and no criminal charge
followed. (Pet. Ex. 7; Decl. ¶ 11.) Petitioner offers that record as corroborative evidence, not as a
substitute for the Court's independent determination of the probable-cause question, and not as
an adjudication of it.

B. If the detention lacked lawful authority, the alleged conduct falls within or is
substantially similar to qualifying criminal activity. False imprisonment is the unlawful
violation of the personal liberty of another. (Pen. Code, § 236.) Penal Code section 146, subdivision
(a) criminalizes a public officer's arrest or detention of a person against that person's will under
color of legal authority but without lawful authority — an offense substantially similar in nature
and elements to false imprisonment. Penal Code section 679.10 expressly identifies false
imprisonment and unlawful criminal restraint as qualifying activity and extends to offenses
substantially similar in nature and elements. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subds. (c), (d).)

Petitioner does not ask this Court to convict any officer of a crime. He asks the Court to determine
whether the conduct shown by the record constitutes qualifying criminal activity for the limited
certification question. Section 679.10 does not require an investigation, charge, apprehension,
prosecution, or conviction as a condition of obtaining certification. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)
(1).) Petitioner deliberately does not plead perjury or any officer-false-statement theory as an
independent qualifying crime: the knowledge element of such a theory is not pinned in this
record, and the report-vs-recording discrepancies are offered as evidence for the false-
imprisonment showing, not as a standalone crime. No felonious-assault or obstruction theory is
pleaded.

C. The existing record establishes helpfulness for purposes of the certification request.
Petitioner reported the incident through a citizen complaint and a POST complaint, gave CHP a
transcript of its own recording keyed by timestamp to the asserted contradictions in its report,
and declares that no one from CHP or any other agency has ever asked him for information or
assistance concerning this incident that he refused or failed to provide. (Pet. ¶¶ 7, 87; Pet. Ex. 3;
Decl. ¶ 12.) CHP's April 20 denial did not identify helpfulness as a reason for denial. (Pet. Ex. 1;
Order at p. 4.) And Respondents' Answer denies that any investigating entity ever requested
information from Petitioner. (Answer ¶ 57.)

That denial helps, not hurts, the helpfulness predicate. Subdivision (h) presumes helpfulness
where the victim has not refused or failed to provide information and assistance reasonably
requested; it does not expressly make an actual prior request a prerequisite, and a reasonable
assertion of unawareness prevents failure to cooperate from rebutting the presumption. (See
Attorney General Information Bulletin DLE-2024-05, Pet. Ex. 14, at pp. 2, 5.) Where no request was
made at all — as the Answer states — there can have been no refusal of one, and the
presumption stands unrebutted. CHP's demurrer argument that the presumption never triggers
absent an actual request is a preserved legal argument, but the statutory text and the Attorney
General's bulletin can also be read to treat absence of refusal as satisfying the condition.
Petitioner's refusals of the voluntary PAS and post-arrest chemical testing concern DUI testing
during the incident, not a later request for assistance in detecting, investigating, or prosecuting
alleged officer criminal activity, and are not automatically noncooperation. Separate-case
cooperation cannot be a denial reason. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)(3)(H).) Helpfulness is
pleaded here only as an element of Count 1, not as a revived standalone claim, and only because
CHP has put cooperation at issue.

E. CHP's best case, stated fairly and answered. CHP's totality case is not frivolous, and this
motion does not pretend otherwise. The report records red, watery eyes; HGN clues (lack of
smooth pursuit, distinct and sustained nystagmus at maximum deviation, onset before 45
degrees, lack of convergence); a Romberg estimate of 30 seconds against 43 actual with eyelid
flutters; a 21-and-over wristband; a party admission and admitted driving from Oakland; an
empty alcohol can; a vehicle seat adjusted to Petitioner's stature; pre-FST answers; and refusals of
the PAS and chemical tests; with a later blood result of 0.073. Taken together, CHP will argue,
those facts support reasonable cause notwithstanding the disputed facts.

The answer is the totality, correctly weighed. No officer observed driving or a moving vehicle; the
driving element is admission-based and declared on information and belief. The odor and speech
claims are paper-only and were never verbalized on the recording. The walk-and-turn account is
contradicted by an officer's own recorded words. The blood result is below the per se level and
was drawn nearly three hours after the stop. No charge was ever filed, and CHP's own certificate
reclassified the event as a detention for insufficient grounds. A court may credit the report's
observations and still find that the arrest lacked probable cause on this record — which is exactly
the finding the Court's demurrer order contemplated. Petitioner asks the Court to make it.

A.1. Arrest authority under Vehicle Code section 40300.5 is independently in issue. The arrest
is recorded as made for Vehicle Code section 23152(a) per section 40300.5. That statute permits a
warrantless DUI arrest without observed driving only where there is reasonable cause of
preceding impaired driving AND an enumerated condition: a crash, a person in or about a vehicle
obstructing a roadway, immediate apprehension necessary, immediate danger absent immediate
arrest, or destruction or concealment of evidence absent immediate arrest. (Veh. Code § 40300.5.)
No officer observed driving or a moving vehicle, so the arrest cannot rest on observation; it must
rest on an enumerated condition. The only condition CHP could plausibly invoke on this record is
obstruction of a roadway — and a freeway shoulder is not automatically a "roadway" (Veh. Code §
530: the portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel).
Whether any part of the Tesla intruded on live lanes, posed an obstruction, or created immediate
danger is genuinely unresolved on the produced record; it requires the native forward video,
location frames and road markings, and the officers' exact factual allegations, none of which
establish obstruction on the current paper. Petitioner acknowledges CHP's best authority:
Villalobos v. Zolin (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 556 upheld a section 40300.5 arrest where a DUI-
symptomatic person occupied a vehicle obstructing an interstate highway — direct defense
precedent IF CHP shows obstruction of the traveled roadway, which must be distinguished on
verified actual position, not rhetorical description. Petitioner's authorities are Mercer v.
Department of Motor Vehicles (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753 and Music v. Department of Motor Vehicles
(1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 841 (no observed movement and no statutory exception can invalidate a
warrantless DUI arrest), subject to the modern section 40300.5(c)-(e) exceptions those cases did
not address. Petitioner does not assert section 40300.5 is categorically inapplicable because the
vehicle was stopped; he asks the Court to find the enumerated condition unproved on this record,
which is a further failure of lawful authority for the restraint — and therefore of the false-
imprisonment predicate's "without lawful privilege" element — not a standalone claim.

F. Remedy. The primary rule is that mandate will not compel an official to exercise genuine
discretion in a particular manner. But a particular result may be compelled where the
governmental entity has no discretion left to exercise because the duty has become ministerial
under the governing law. (Common Cause, supra, 49 Cal.3d at pp. 442-443.) Here, section 679.10,
subdivision (g)(1) states that the certifying official "shall certify" when the statutory predicates are
met. If the Court finds those predicates established on the record and no lawful reasonable
alternative remaining, direct certification is the appropriate writ relief. (California Correctional
Supervisors, 96 Cal.App.4th at 827.)

If the Court concludes that CHP retains a determination that must be made in the first instance,
Petitioner alternatively requests a writ commanding CHP to set aside the denial and reconsider
the April 9 request within 30 days in conformity with the Court's findings and Penal Code section
679.10 — including subdivision (h)'s helpfulness presumption and subdivision (k)'s rule that no
