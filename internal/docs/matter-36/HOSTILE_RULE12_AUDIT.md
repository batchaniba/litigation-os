
# Matter 36 — Hostile Rule 12 / Summary-Judgment Audit

## Purpose

Attack the proposed federal complaint as defense counsel would.

This memo is intentionally adversarial.

---

# 1. FCRA — report existence before 12:23 p.m.

## Defense attack

The complaint has a confirmed First Advantage **task invitation**, not a confirmed completed or furnished report.

AT&T will argue:
- screening began at 10:19;
- rescission occurred at 12:23;
- no evidence shows a report existed by then;
- therefore no adverse action could have been “based in whole or in part” on a report.

## Plaintiff response

Plead report receipt/use on information and belief only if there is a factual basis.

The timing plus First Advantage workflow may support discovery, but it cannot substitute for the report forever.

## Risk

**High until report/transmission logs are obtained.**

If First Advantage records show no report or partial result was furnished before 12:23, Count One likely fails.

---

# 2. FCRA — Spanish assessment as independent cause / Dutta standing

## Defense attack

AT&T will argue:
- R-106742 was a bilingual Spanish role;
- Plaintiff failed the Spanish assessment;
- failure independently disqualified him;
- even perfect FCRA notice could not have changed the outcome;
- therefore no concrete injury is traceable to any pre-adverse notice violation.

This is the core **Dutta** attack.

## Plaintiff response

The chronology permits a competing inference:
- language assessment occurred before May 28;
- rating was to be sent to AT&T;
- AT&T nevertheless issued a formal offer on May 28;
- offer did not identify Spanish passage as an outstanding contingency;
- onboarding followed;
- First Advantage screening then launched;
- rescission followed about two hours later.

Plead that the report was at least a partial cause, not necessarily sole cause.

Also plead concrete lost-opportunity injury:
- accepted job withdrawn;
- no chance to review source;
- no chance to identify any California-registry source and object to unlawful employment use.

## Risk

**Medium-high.**

If discovery shows AT&T did not receive the Spanish score until after the offer and the score automatically required rejection, Dutta becomes much stronger.

If AT&T had the failing score before issuing the offer, the defense narrative weakens significantly.

# 3. FCRA § 1681b(b)(3)(A) — adverse action timing

## Defense attack

AT&T may argue:
- Patterson's text was not final adverse action;
- position remained contingent;
- manager declined to proceed before background adjudication;
- no consumer-report-based decision occurred.

## Plaintiff response

FCRA defines adverse action in employment broadly to include denial of employment or another employment decision adversely affecting a prospective employee.

An accepted offer withdrawn at 12:23 is plausibly adverse.

## Risk

**Low on adverse-action character; high on report causation.**

# 7. Penal Code § 290.46 — source problem

## Defense attack

AT&T's strongest merits argument:

> Even if we learned Plaintiff had a registerable conviction, we did not obtain that fact from California's Megan's Law website.

Under **Donaleski**, employer knowledge plus an independent investigation is not enough.

If the AT&T report shows:
- county criminal record;
- court record;
- other lawful criminal-history source;
- registry component clear or unused;

the § 290.46 theory may fail.

## Plaintiff response

The claim is source-specific.

Use the actual First Advantage source field when obtained.

UPS comparator supports plausibility but is not proof of AT&T's report.

## Risk

**High until source record is obtained.**

# 12. At-will doctrine

## Defense attack

California presumes at-will employment absent contrary agreement.

Even if an employment relationship formed, AT&T could terminate immediately for a lawful reason.

## Plaintiff response

At-will status does not authorize:
- FCRA violations;
- ICRAA violations;
- prohibited registry use;
- breach of a distinct promise if contract language supports one.

But at-will doctrine can sharply limit contract expectancy theories.

## Risk

**High for contract damages; low as a defense to independent statutes.**

# 16. Damages inflation

## Defense attack

## Recommendation

- reliance loss;
- other concrete economic harm.

## Risk

**Manageable if damages are pleaded conservatively.**
