Plaintiff BRENDAN NGWA NFORBI, appearing in propria persona, alleges:

# I. NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. On May 28, 2026, AT&T issued Plaintiff an official written offer for a Retail Sales Consultant position at 2439 West Kettleman Lane, Lodi, California.

2. The offer specified $24.95 per hour, targeted variable compensation of $13,700 annually prorated for part-time hours, reporting manager Orville R. Bell, and a tentative June 15, 2026 start date.

3. Plaintiff accepted through Workday and began onboarding.

4. At approximately 10:19 a.m., First Advantage sent Plaintiff the employment-screening task AT&T had ordered for “AT&T Non Management,” account 132156.

5. At approximately 12:23 p.m., AT&T recruiter Richard Patterson texted that the manager no longer wanted to proceed with Plaintiff's offer because Plaintiff did not pass Spanish and a Spanish speaker was needed.

6. Plaintiff had not received a copy of any consumer report or Summary of Rights before the 12:23 p.m. rescission.

7. Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that, before the rescission, First Advantage furnished AT&T a consumer-report result concerning Plaintiff and that AT&T based its decision in whole or in part on that report.

8. Plaintiff further alleges on information and belief that the report contained information disclosed pursuant to California Penal Code § 290.46, including Plaintiff's California registry information, and that AT&T used that information for an employment purpose.

9. Plaintiff does not allege that the Spanish explanation was necessarily false. The timing and sequence create competing explanations that can be resolved through AT&T's, LTI's, and First Advantage's records.

# II. JURISDICTION, STANDING, AND VENUE

10. This Court has federal-question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 because Count One arises under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

11. This Court has supplemental jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1367(a) over Counts Two and Three because they arise from the same offer, screening, and employment decision.

12. Plaintiff does not presently invoke diversity jurisdiction. AT&T Mobility Services LLC is a limited liability company, and the citizenship of every member has not been established.

13. Plaintiff has Article III standing. He alleges a concrete economic injury—the loss of the accepted Lodi employment and its documented $24.95 hourly compensation—rather than a bare procedural injury.

14. Plaintiff also alleges loss of the pre-adverse review-and-response opportunity protected by § 1681b(b)(3)(A). Had AT&T furnished the report before acting, Plaintiff could have identified and challenged any California-registry source and advised AT&T of the restriction in Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

15. These injuries are fairly traceable to the report-based employment action alleged and redressable by damages. See *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021).

16. Venue is proper under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2) because a substantial part of the events occurred in Lodi, San Joaquin County, California.

17. Under Eastern District of California Local Rule 120(d), San Joaquin County civil actions are commenced on the Sacramento side of the District.

35. At approximately 10:19 a.m., First Advantage sent Plaintiff an employment-screening task on behalf of “AT&T Non Management,” account 132156.

36. This transaction-specific communication confirms AT&T had initiated a First Advantage employment-screening workflow.

# VI. FACTUAL BASIS FOR REPORT RECEIPT AND USE

42. Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that First Advantage furnished AT&T a consumer-report result concerning Plaintiff before the 12:23 p.m. rescission and that AT&T based the rescission in whole or in part on that report.

43. The factual basis includes:
   a. the formal offer and acceptance;
   b. immediate onboarding;
   c. AT&T's initiation of a First Advantage screening at 10:19 a.m.;

45. Plaintiff does not plead the mere screening invitation itself as a “consumer report” or as an adverse result.

# VII. FACTUAL BASIS FOR REGISTRY SOURCE AND USE

46. At the time of the AT&T screening, Plaintiff was publicly listed on California's Megan's Law internet website.

47. Plaintiff possesses a First Advantage employment background report concerning himself generated for United Parcel Service during the surrounding period.

48. In that report, ordinary criminal-search components returned non-adverse results while the sex-offender-registry component was adverse or decisional and expressly identified the source of record as the California Sex Offender Registry.

51. On information and belief, the AT&T-specific report contained information concerning Plaintiff disclosed pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46.

52. On information and belief, AT&T received, accessed, considered, and used that information for a purpose relating to employment.

53. The source wording, component status, adjudication code, transmission time, access logs, and AT&T reason codes are within AT&T's and First Advantage's systems.

# VIII. THE SPANISH EXPLANATION DOES NOT ELIMINATE THE PLEADED REPORT THEORY

54. Plaintiff acknowledges the Spanish explanation and does not ask the Court to assume it was pretext merely because screening also occurred.

55. The following facts remain unresolved: the actual LTI score, the passing standard, when AT&T received the score, whether the score was known before the 10:13 offer, whether failure automatically required disqualification, whether First Advantage furnished a report before 12:23, and every reason actually considered by the decisionmaker.

56. The FCRA does not require a consumer report to be the sole reason for adverse action; § 1681b(b)(3)(A) applies when the action is based in whole or in part on the report.

57. If AT&T proves that the Spanish result independently and inevitably disqualified Plaintiff before any report result was furnished or used, that evidence may defeat causation and may implicate the standing analysis discussed in *Dutta v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co.* The present complaint does not conceal that defense.

# IX. SOURCE-SPECIFIC § 290.46 DEFENSES

58. Plaintiff does not allege that AT&T was barred from considering lawfully and independently obtained conviction information.

59. The § 290.46 claim is based on alleged use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 itself.

60. *Lerma v. Amazon.com Services, LLC*, No. 8:22-cv-01727 (C.D. Cal.), does not resolve the source issue on the present record. There, the transaction-specific report was before the court and showed ordinary court-record sourcing and no relevant registry hit.

61. AT&T's transaction-specific report is not presently before Plaintiff. General public conviction records do not establish which source First Advantage used in AT&T's report.

62. If the AT&T-specific report is properly considered and contradicts Plaintiff's information-and-belief source allegation, the claim must conform to that document.

# X. COUNT ONE
## Fair Credit Reporting Act — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A)

63. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 62.

64. Plaintiff was a consumer, AT&T procured or caused a consumer report to be procured for employment purposes, and AT&T was a person using the report within the meaning of the FCRA.

65. Before taking adverse action based in whole or in part on an employment consumer report, § 1681b(b)(3)(A) requires the person intending to take the action to provide the consumer a copy of the report and a written description of FCRA rights.

66. On information and belief, AT&T's rescission was based in whole or in part on a consumer report furnished by First Advantage.

67. AT&T did not provide Plaintiff a copy of that report or the written Summary of Rights before the 12:23 p.m. adverse action.

68. AT&T thereby violated § 1681b(b)(3)(A).

69. The count is timely without delayed discovery because the alleged violation occurred May 28, 2026.

70. AT&T's noncompliance was negligent and, on proof, willful. Willfulness under § 1681n includes reckless disregard. See *Safeco Insurance Co. of America v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47 (2007).

71. AT&T's own First Advantage workflow expressly referenced FCRA disclosure and authorization immediately before the alleged adverse action, supporting an inference that AT&T knew it was administering a regulated employment consumer-report process.

72. Plaintiff seeks relief authorized by 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681n and 1681o according to proof.

# XI. COUNT TWO
## California Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act
### Civil Code §§ 1786.40, 1786.50

73. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 62.

74. On information and belief, the First Advantage employment background report was an investigative consumer report under ICRAA.

75. Civil Code § 1786.40 requires the user, when employment is denied under circumstances in which an investigative consumer report was obtained, to advise the consumer and supply the name and address of the investigative consumer reporting agency.

76. On information and belief, AT&T denied the employment under circumstances in which it had obtained the First Advantage investigative consumer report.

77. AT&T did not provide the § 1786.40 advice at the time of the rescission.

78. This alleged omission is distinct from Count One's federal pre-adverse omission.

79. The count is timely without delayed discovery because the alleged conduct occurred May 28, 2026.

80. Plaintiff seeks the remedies authorized by Civil Code § 1786.50 according to proof.

# XII. COUNT THREE
## Unauthorized Employment Use of Information Disclosed Pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46
### Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A)

81. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 62.

82. Plaintiff is a person as to whom information is disclosed through California's Megan's Law internet website.

83. Section 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for purposes relating to employment except where statutorily authorized.

84. On information and belief, First Advantage furnished AT&T information concerning Plaintiff disclosed pursuant to § 290.46.

85. On information and belief, AT&T received, accessed, considered, and used that information for an employment purpose.

86. Plaintiff alleges the information was a substantial factor in rescinding the accepted Lodi offer.

87. Plaintiff is unaware of another law authorizing use of § 290.46-disclosed information for this retail-sales position.

88. The alleged use occurred May 28, 2026. A September 2026 filing is timely under either a potentially applicable one-year penalty characterization or a three-year statutory-liability characterization. Plaintiff therefore need not ask the Court to resolve that characterization at the pleading stage.

89. AT&T thereby violated Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

90. Plaintiff seeks the remedies authorized by § 290.46(j)(4)(A), without duplicative recovery.

91. Plaintiff does not presently seek preventive relief under § 290.46(j)(4)(B).
