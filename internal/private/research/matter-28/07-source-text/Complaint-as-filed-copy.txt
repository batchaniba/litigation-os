 8                                     IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

 9

10                            A. CDCR's Sex Offender Management Program

11

12          14. Under California Penal Code Section 3008, CDCR requires parolees to participate in a

13   sex offender management program for a period determined on an individualized basis between

14   one year and the parole term, "as determined by the certified sex offender management

15   professional in consultation with the parole officer." Section 3008(d)(2) presupposes a

16   meaningful possibility of completion before discharge from parole.

17

18          15. Title 15, California Code of Regulations, Section 3574 (effective April 17, 2025)

19   codifies SOMP standards. Section 3574(f)(1) expressly provides that treatment completion and

20   parole discharge are legally separate decisions. Section 3574(e)(2) establishes that the second

21   CTM is "the first review to determine whether the supervised person's sex offender management

22   program requirements have been satisfied." The regulation was adopted to implement the

23   settlement in Alliance for Constitutional Sex Offense Laws, Inc. v. Macomber (L.A. Super. Ct.

24   Case No. 23STCP02378), which was filed in July 2023 challenging an unwritten CDCR policy

25   requiring all registrants to remain in treatment for the entire parole term. NCR 25-01 confirms the

26   regulatory history.

27

28          16. CASOMB's Treatment Completion Guidelines (2018) require structured clinical

                                                     Page 5
                     COMPLAINT FOR DAMAGES AND INJUNCTIVE RELIEF — NEWANFORBI v. SMITH, ET AL.
 1   judgment and direct that the determination be a yes-or-no conclusion about whether treatment has

 2   been completed. CASOMB's Maintenance and Aftercare Guidelines (September 2024) state:

 3   "Discharge planning starts at intake. The final determination regarding whether an individual has

 4   'completed' treatment should never come as a surprise to the client."

 5

 6          17. In the Stockton region, HOPE was the sole CASOMB-certified SOMP provider during

 7   the period relevant to this action, holding an exclusive monopoly over mandatory treatment for

 8   sex offender parolees.

11

13   HOPE contracts with CDCR to provide mandatory sex offender treatment, a function CDCR is

14   statutorily required to administer under Penal Code Section 3008. HOPE admitted in verified

15   discovery responses that it is a CASOMB-certified provider contracting with CDCR's Division of

24   submission of clinical reports to CDCR that directly affect parole decisions, and its performance

25   of mandatory state-ordered treatment makes HOPE a state actor.

 7          21. HOPE's Clinical Operational Manual (September 2024 edition) confirms this policy in

15   clients. Clients can reach out via parole to obtain this documentation if they wish."

16

17          22. The juxtaposition of these provisions reveals the fraudulent architecture: HOPE

18   created a detailed graduation pathway to maintain the appearance that completion is achievable,

19   while simultaneously maintaining a blanket prohibition on discharge that ensures no client can

20   ever reach the end of that pathway during the period of mandatory participation.

23   SC-080355A (San Mateo County Super. Ct., Mar. 20, 2023) (Hill, J.). HOPE's own Executive

25   continuous treatment for all parolees until discharge. The court found that HOPE's policy "vitiates
