21                   THE COURT MAY DIRECT THIS MANNER OF SERVICE

22


23          Rule 4(e)(1) permits service by following California law. Section 413.30 provides that
24   the court "may direct that summons be served in a manner which is reasonably calculated to give
25   actual notice to the party to be served and that proofof such service be made as prescribed by the
26   court." That is the constitutional floor as well. Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.,

27   339 U.S. 306, 314(1950). The Ninth Circuit approves alternative service when ordinary methods
28   have not finished the work, so long as the method is reasonably calculated to give notice. Rio

3           Directing Li to accept the summons and the operative First Amended Complaint, and
4    treating delivery to him at the Spring Street address and email he uses as counsel for these
5    officers, is reasonably calculated to give Meza-Gonzalez actual notice. Li has been this officer's
6    lawyer on this incident since at least December 2025. Plaintiff is not asking the Court to treat Li
7    as an already-appointed Rule 4(e)(2)(C) agent. Li has not appeared in this action, because
8    Meza-Gonzalez has not been served. Plaintiff asks the Court to direct that service be completed

9    by delivery to the lawyer who has represented this defendant throughout. That is the least
10   intrusive way to close a gap duty-station service has not closed.
11


12          Civil Local Rule 7-11 is the right vehicle. Completing Rule 4 as to one defendant so the
13   related cases can move on the same calendar is a miscellaneous administrative matter. A 35-day

14   noticed motion would add a month of delay the Court can avoid on the papers.

15


16          A stipulation could not be obtained before filing because Li has not appeared here.

18   and invites a stipulation that he will accept service. If he stipulates, the proposed order can enter
19   as agreed. If he does not, the Court should grant the motion.
