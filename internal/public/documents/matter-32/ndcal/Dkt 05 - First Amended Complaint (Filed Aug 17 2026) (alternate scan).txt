8           49. The Fourth Amendment requires probable cause for a warrantless arrest. Probable

9    cause exists when the facts and circumstances known to the officer at the time are sufficient for a

10   prudent person to believe that the suspect has committed a crime. Devenpeck v. Alford,543 U.S.

11   146,152(2004).

12


13          50. At the moment of arrest(01:11:15-01:11:33), the facts known to the officers, as

14   reflected on the recording, included: Plaintiff was parked and asleep when contacted; the only

15   suspected "beverage" had been identified as urine; Plaintiff had spoken coherently and identified

16   his location; no officer had verbalized an alcohol odor; Officer Melano had dechned to administer

17   certain FSTs due to ground conditions; and Plaintiff had exercised his statutory right under

18   Vehicle Code section 23612(i) to decline a voluntary PAS test. No new observation or evidence

19   intervened between the PAS refiisal and the arrest. Plaintiffs exercise ofthe statutory right to

20   refuse the PAS test could not itself supply the probable cause that was otherwise absent. Thomas

21   V. Dillard, 818 F.3d 864,884-85 (9th Cir. 2016)(refusal to consent to a search cannot create

22   reasonable suspicion or establish probable cause). Moreover, officers evaluating probable cause
