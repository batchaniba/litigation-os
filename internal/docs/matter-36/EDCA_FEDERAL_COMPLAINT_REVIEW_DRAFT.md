Plaintiff alleges:

# I. INTRODUCTION

1. On May 28, 2026, after interviews and a Spanish-language assessment process, AT&T formally offered Plaintiff employment as a Retail Sales Consultant at its Lodi, California retail location.

2. The written offer specified a position, manager, store location, hourly wage of $24.95, annual target variable compensation of $13,700 prorated for part-time hours, and a tentative June 15, 2026 start date.

3. Plaintiff accepted the offer through AT&T's Workday system and immediately began onboarding.

4. Within minutes, AT&T assigned Plaintiff a First Advantage background-screening task under the account or package identified to Plaintiff as “AT&T Non Management.”

5. Approximately two hours after the offer and onboarding sequence began, AT&T recruiter Richard Patterson informed Plaintiff by text that “the manager” would not proceed because Plaintiff had not passed Spanish and a Spanish speaker was needed.

6. Plaintiff had not received a copy of any consumer report, Summary of Rights, pre-adverse action notice, or California investigative-report adverse-action advice before AT&T withdrew the employment opportunity.

7. Plaintiff alleges on information and belief, based on the timing, the First Advantage screening workflow, AT&T's immediate background-check initiation, Plaintiff's contemporaneous public California registry status, and First Advantage's documented screening of the same Plaintiff in other employment transactions, that AT&T procured and received one or more consumer-report results before the rescission and that information contained in the screening was a factor, in whole or in part, in AT&T's decision.

8. Plaintiff further alleges on information and belief that the screening included or furnished information disclosed through California's Megan's Law internet website and that AT&T used that information for an employment purpose prohibited by Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

9. Plaintiff does not allege that the First Advantage task invitation alone proves what the report contained. The report, source fields, transmission logs, adjudication matrix, access logs, and decision records are in Defendant's and First Advantage's possession.

10. AT&T has articulated a contemporaneous alternative explanation: failure of the Spanish assessment. That explanation presents a merits question rather than a basis to erase the pleaded background-screening chronology. The FCRA applies where adverse action is based “in whole or in part” on a consumer report.

# IV. THE APPLICATION, ASSESSMENT, OFFER, AND ONBOARDING

18. On May 16, 2026, Plaintiff submitted an application for AT&T requisition R-106742, Bilingual Spanish Retail Sales Consultant.

19. Plaintiff also applied for requisition R-107776, Retail Sales Consultant at Stockton Pacific.

20. Both applications advanced through AT&T's interview process.

21. On May 22, 2026, Language Testing International (“LTI”) sent Plaintiff instructions for an ACTFL Telephone OPIc Spanish-speaking assessment.

22. LTI represented that Plaintiff's rating would be supplied to AT&T's recruiting system.

23. The record presently available to Plaintiff does not contain:
   a. the Spanish score;
   b. the recording;
   c. AT&T's required threshold;
   d. the date/time AT&T received the result; or
   e. AT&T's internal assessment notes.

24. On May 28, 2026 at approximately 10:13 a.m., AT&T's Workday system sent Plaintiff a “Welcome to the team” message and formal offer for requisition R-106742.

25. The written offer identified:
   a. position: Retail Sales Consultant;
   b. manager: Orville R. Bell;
   c. work location: 2439 W. Kettleman Lane, Lodi, California;
   d. hourly wage: $24.95;
   e. biweekly pay;
   f. annual target variable compensation: $13,700, prorated for part-time hours; and
   g. tentative start date: June 15, 2026.

26. The offer listed multiple contingencies, including satisfactory standardized background checks.

27. The written offer did not identify Spanish proficiency as an unfulfilled post-offer contingency.

28. Plaintiff accepted the offer through Workday.

29. At approximately 10:15 through 10:17 a.m., Workday issued onboarding tasks, including review of documents, government identifiers, and personal-information tasks.

30. Plaintiff began completing the onboarding process in reliance on the accepted offer.

# V. FIRST ADVANTAGE SCREENING

31. At approximately 10:19 a.m. on May 28, 2026, First Advantage sent Plaintiff an employment-screening task on behalf of “AT&T Non Management.”

32. First Advantage was therefore the confirmed consumer-report/background-screening vendor used in Plaintiff's AT&T transaction.

33. The precise First Advantage legal furnishing entity, package contents, order identifier, report creation time, transmission time, access time, adjudication status, and source fields are presently in Defendant's and First Advantage's possession.

34. Plaintiff received no copy of a First Advantage consumer report before AT&T withdrew the employment opportunity.

35. Plaintiff received no Summary of Rights under the FCRA before AT&T withdrew the opportunity.

36. Plaintiff received no pre-adverse action notice stating that AT&T was considering action based in whole or in part on a consumer report.

37. Plaintiff received no ICRAA adverse-action advice identifying the investigative consumer reporting agency whose report was involved in the decision.

# VI. RESCISSION APPROXIMATELY TWO HOURS LATER

38. At approximately 12:23 p.m. on May 28, 2026, recruiter Richard Patterson texted Plaintiff that “the manager” no longer wanted to proceed because Plaintiff did not pass Spanish and a Spanish speaker was needed.

39. AT&T thereby withdrew or rescinded the accepted employment opportunity.

40. Plaintiff had received the formal offer only about two hours earlier.

41. The First Advantage screening task had been initiated within minutes of Plaintiff's acceptance and onboarding.

42. Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that, by the time of the rescission, AT&T had procured, received, accessed, or been furnished one or more First Advantage consumer-report results concerning Plaintiff.

43. Plaintiff further alleges on information and belief that the First Advantage information was a factor in whole or in part in the adverse decision.

# VII. FACTUAL BASIS FOR INFORMATION-AND-BELIEF REPORT ALLEGATIONS

44. Plaintiff does not rely solely on temporal proximity.

45. The factual basis supporting the inference includes:
   a. a formal employment offer followed immediately by First Advantage screening;
   b. AT&T onboarding tasks before the rescission;
   c. AT&T's own offer identifying background screening as a condition of employment;
   d. rescission approximately two hours after First Advantage screening commenced;
   e. the absence of any pre-adverse consumer-report materials;
   f. Plaintiff's California registry information being publicly available during the transaction;
   g. First Advantage's documented use as the CRA for the same Plaintiff in other employment transactions;
   h. First Advantage's documented ability in another same-consumer employment screen to identify a sex-offender component as adverse/decisional and expressly identify “CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY” as the source.

46. The comparator evidence does not establish the contents of AT&T's report and is not pleaded as a substitute for AT&T-specific proof.

47. It supplies context supporting the plausibility of Plaintiff's allegation while the AT&T report and transmission records remain exclusively in Defendant's and First Advantage's possession.

48. The Ninth Circuit permits information-and-belief pleading where relevant facts are peculiarly within a defendant's possession and the pleaded factual context makes the inference plausible.

# VIII. THE SPANISH-ASSESSMENT EXPLANATION

49. Plaintiff acknowledges that AT&T recruiter Patterson contemporaneously attributed the rescission to Plaintiff's Spanish assessment.

50. Plaintiff does not ask the Court at the pleading stage to infer that the Spanish explanation was necessarily false.

51. Plaintiff alleges that discovery is required to determine:
   a. when LTI completed the rating;
   b. when AT&T received it;
   c. the score;
   d. the minimum score required;
   e. whether AT&T knew the score before issuing the offer;
   f. whether the score alone required disqualification;
   g. whether background-screening results were also received;
   h. who made the final rescission decision; and
   i. every reason code or criterion applied.

52. The fact that AT&T issued a formal offer after the assessment period and did not identify Spanish passage as an outstanding offer contingency is relevant to whether the later explanation was the sole cause.

53. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A), Plaintiff need not prove that a consumer report was the sole cause; the statute applies when adverse action is based “in whole or in part” on a report.

# IX. COUNT ONE
## Fair Credit Reporting Act — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A)

54. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1–53.

55. Defendant procured or caused First Advantage to procure a consumer report concerning Plaintiff for employment purposes.

56. Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that, before rescinding the accepted offer, Defendant received or used information from that report and based its decision in whole or in part on the report.

57. Before taking adverse action, Defendant did not provide Plaintiff:
   a. a copy of the report; and
   b. the required written Summary of Rights.

58. The rescission of an accepted employment offer was an adverse employment decision affecting a prospective employee.

59. The violation caused concrete harm. Plaintiff lost the accepted employment opportunity and, because no report was furnished beforehand, lost the opportunity to identify and challenge the report's source, completeness, accuracy, or legal usability before AT&T acted.

60. In particular, had AT&T furnished the report before acting, Plaintiff could have identified any California-registry source and informed AT&T that Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits employment use of information disclosed through California's Megan's Law website.

61. This distinguishes the pleaded injury from a bare procedural violation where an applicant was independently and unavoidably disqualified regardless of anything that could have been raised during the pre-adverse process.

62. Plaintiff seeks remedies under 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681n and/or 1681o according to proof.

# X. COUNT TWO
## California Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act
## Civ. Code §§ 1786.40, 1786.50

63. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1–53.

64. On information and belief, the First Advantage product obtained and used by AT&T qualified as an investigative consumer report under California law because it contained information concerning Plaintiff's character, general reputation, personal characteristics, mode of living, criminal-history information, registry information, or other covered information used for employment purposes.

65. Defendant was a user of information within ICRAA.

66. Defendant took adverse employment action under circumstances in which an investigative consumer report had been obtained.

67. Defendant failed to provide the adverse-action advice required by Civil Code § 1786.40.

68. Under Civil Code § 1786.50, a user that fails to comply with ICRAA is liable for actual damages or, outside a class action, $10,000, whichever is greater, together with other relief authorized by statute.

69. Plaintiff reserves any claim under § 1786.16 until the exact First Advantage disclosure/authorization package is obtained and audited. He does not plead a standalone-document violation from the task invitation alone.

# XI. COUNT THREE
## Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) and (j)(4)(A)

70. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1–53.

71. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits, absent statutory authorization, use of information disclosed through California's Megan's Law internet website for purposes relating to employment.

72. Section 290.46(j)(4)(A) provides a civil remedy against a user who misuses that information.

73. Plaintiff's information was publicly disclosed through California's Megan's Law internet website at the time of the AT&T screening.

74. On information and belief, First Advantage furnished AT&T information disclosed through that website, including Plaintiff's California registry information.

75. On information and belief, AT&T received, accessed, considered, caused consideration of, or acted upon that information in determining whether Plaintiff could begin the accepted Retail Sales Consultant position.

76. The use was for an employment purpose.

77. The information was a substantial factor in the withdrawal of the employment opportunity.

78. The exact source field, report component, transmission, access, decision matrix, and internal reason codes are presently within Defendant's and First Advantage's possession.

79. Plaintiff does not allege that AT&T was prohibited from considering independently and lawfully obtained conviction information. His claim concerns use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46.

80. If the AT&T-specific report instead shows that the relevant information came solely from independent court records and that any sex-offender-registry component returned no record or was not used, Plaintiff will conform the claim to the actual evidence.

81. Plaintiff seeks the remedies authorized by § 290.46(j)(4)(A), without duplicative recovery.
