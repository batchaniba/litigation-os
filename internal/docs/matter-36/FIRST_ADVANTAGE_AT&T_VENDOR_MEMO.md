That makes First Advantage the confirmed background-screening vendor for this hiring transaction.

## What the invitation proves

It proves:
- AT&T initiated a First Advantage employment-screening workflow;
- the screening was connected to Plaintiff's AT&T application;
- the screening started before the rescission.

It does **not** by itself prove:
- that First Advantage completed a report before 12:23;
- what any report contained;
- that AT&T received a report before 12:23;
- that the report included California-registry information;
- that report information contributed to the decision.

Those are the central discovery / prefiling questions.

## Cross-matter comparator value

First Advantage also screened the same Plaintiff in other employment matters in the portfolio.

The most important comparator is Matter 26 / UPS, where the actual First Advantage report contains a sex-offender component described in the portfolio as:
- adverse / decisional;
- ineligible;
- source expressly identified as the California Sex Offender Registry.

Matter 25 / FedEx also involves First Advantage screening of the same Plaintiff, and FedEx's answer reportedly admits that the report identified Plaintiff as a California registrant.

### Proper pleading use

These comparators support plausibility because they involve:
- the same consumer;
- the same CRA;
- similar employment-screening use;
- the same underlying California registry status.

They are **not substitutes for AT&T's report**.

The AT&T complaint should expressly say that.

## Standalone disclosure issue

FCRA § 1681b(b)(2)(A) requires a clear and conspicuous written disclosure in a document consisting solely of the disclosure before procurement, plus written authorization.

Ninth Circuit authority including **Walker v. Fred Meyer** treats the standalone-document requirement as a real, independent FCRA rule.

However, the repo presently establishes only the First Advantage task invitation and references the FCRA disclosure/authorization workflow.

**Do not plead a § 1681b(b)(2)(A) violation until the actual authorization/disclosure is inspected.**

The same caution applies to ICRAA § 1786.16. California law imposes detailed standalone and agency-identification requirements, but the exact AT&T/First Advantage form controls.

## First Advantage defendant status

Do not name First Advantage merely because it furnished a report.

A viable CRA claim needs an independent violation attributable to First Advantage, such as:
- inaccurate reporting;
- failure to use reasonable procedures;
- unlawful furnishing;
- file-disclosure failures;
- reinvestigation violations;
- other CRA-specific statutory misconduct.

If the AT&T report shows a repeat California-registry furnishing pattern, that may strengthen Matter 31 and justify a separate First Advantage analysis, but it should not be bolted onto Matter 36 without an independent cause of action.

## Bottom line

The vendor question is solved.

The remaining factual question is **what First Advantage produced and what AT&T received before 12:23 p.m.**

If no consumer-report result was furnished before rescission, the FCRA adverse-action, ICRAA adverse-action, and § 290.46 counts become materially weaker or fail.

If a report was furnished before rescission and it reproduces the UPS source architecture, Matter 36 becomes substantially stronger.
