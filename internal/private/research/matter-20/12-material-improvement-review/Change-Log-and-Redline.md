# Matter 20: Material Change Log and Redline

The revised memorandum is a substantive replacement of the working memorandum, not a claim that every new sentence was already in the record. This change log supplies reviewable before/after passages and identifies the reason for each material change; the complete baseline remains in `10-working-motion/`.

## Material before/after changes

### No-observed-driving argument

**Before, verbatim, working memorandum p. 5:** “The record identifies a stationary vehicle on a freeway shoulder; it does not, simply by those words, establish roadway obstruction or another statutory exception.”

**After, revised section “Qualifying restraint requires absence of lawful authority”:** “No observed driving is not a complete answer to the arrest question. Mercer addressed the statutes then in force; Troppman explains the Legislature's later expansion of warrantless DUI arrest authority, including evidence dissipating through metabolism.”

The revision supplies the actual later authority rather than merely asking CHP to identify an exception. ([Troppman (2007) 40 Cal.4th 1121, 1137 fn. 11](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2586143/troppman-v-valverde/); [Schofield (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 968, 973–975](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2256187/people-v-schofield/).)

### Materiality of the walk-and-turn entry

**Before, working memorandum p. 7:** “If the original recording corroborates the exchange, the report materially misattributes the reason for nonperformance and cannot fairly count that nonperformance as Petitioner's failure.”

**After, revised section “Test the discrepancy and the remaining evidence together”:** “If the authenticated recording confirms that exchange, the Court should determine whether attributing nonperformance to Petitioner was false, whether nonperformance actually supported the report's ‘poor performance’ assessment, and whether correcting it materially changes the totality.”

The revision separates confirmation of a discrepancy from an established falsehood, its actual use and its legal materiality. It expressly preserves the other observations at AGO 000030–000032 instead of treating all field-test evidence as disproved.

### Adverse authority and residual facts

**Before:** The draft identifies adverse facts but does not engage the adverse aspect of Espinoza in a sustained way.

**After:** “The Court's cited Espinoza decision is adverse as well as instructive: coherent speech did not negate the other indicators there, and objective facts, not the officer's asserted subjective reason alone, determined probable cause.”

This is not a concession that all officers' observations are true; it requires the argument to test the actual totality. ([Espinoza (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 85, 101–103](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4378115/espinoza-v-shiomoto/).)

### PAS and chemical-test distinctions

**Before, verbatim, working memorandum p. 8:** “The holding concerned evidentiary use at trial and did not itself decide every possible prearrest reasonable-cause application.” The existing draft already recognizes this important limitation; the revision does not claim to have discovered an omitted qualification.

**After:** The revised draft retains that distinction and adds: “Petitioner does not enlarge that holding into an adjudication that this arrest lacked probable cause; nor does timing alone eliminate other known facts.”

Jackson's actual issue was trial use of voluntary PAS refusal, not a merits adjudication of this arrest; the existing limit is made consistent throughout. ([Jackson (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461, 1466–1469](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2010/a128782/).)

### Discovery and review timing: preserve an existing safeguard

**Before, verbatim, working memorandum p. 10:** “No finding of legal error is sought merely because the denial letter is brief or an internal memorandum bears a later date.”

**After:** “Discovery Nos. 6–8 do not identify every April review step and include production commitments; that incompleteness is not an admission that no review occurred.”

The revised paragraph preserves the existing limitation and adds the exact production-response foundation for a targeted completeness inquiry; it does not fix an inference the working packet already rejected. RFP Nos. 6–8 / merged source pp. 29–31 and privilege log entries 9–14 control.

### Burden and criminal qualification

**Before:** No equally explicit distinction between the ordinary civil burden in this writ and prosecution of the officers.

**After, two excerpts with the intervening citation omitted:** “This is not an officer's criminal prosecution requiring proof beyond a reasonable doubt.” And: “Nor does the existence of a factual dispute itself establish an abuse of discretion.”

The revision adds the actual general burden rules without shifting an essential element to CHP or implying that all possible special burdens have been decided. ([Evidence Code §§ 115](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=115.), [500](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=500.).)

### Remedy and pleading limits

**Before:** Conditional direct certification appears in the working notice, but the original petition ¶ 8 disclaims a particular result and its Tier 3 prayer permits a lawful renewed denial.

**After:** Direct relief is expressly subject to proof, a single lawful choice, operative pleading and adequate notice. The principal remand remedy is preserved; no conclusion that direct relief is either automatically barred or automatically available is invented.

The only-one-reasonable-choice rule supports preserving a properly available conditional remedy, not bypassing pleadings or findings. ([California Correctional Supervisors Organization (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/96/824.html).)

### Factual procedure

**Before:** The draft requests a focused evidentiary proceeding but leaves the essential questions relatively broad.

**After:** The revised draft identifies the exchange, residual observations, lawful custody authority, resulting qualifying activity and material certification error as the issues that must actually be resolved if they prevent paper determination.

A jury reference remains discretionary, and noticed-motion adjudication remains subject to section 1094's conditions. ([CCP §§ 1090](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1090.), [1091](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1091.), [1094](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1094.).)

### Federal screening order

**Before:** A full separate memorandum page, with three paragraphs, discusses the federal order.

**After:** One short paragraph preserves the assumed-true qualification, screening context, nonbinding/nonpreclusive purpose, no new retaliation claim and separate California conditional duty. ECF 5 pp. 3–5 remain the source; later federal history is still a prefiling hold.

This reduces the risk that the federal order becomes the brief's centerpiece while retaining the specific limited argument the user requested. No factual merits finding, new exhibit or granted judicial-notice request is represented.

### Answer title and record status

**Before:** “Respondents' Verified Answer,” including in the working notice and declaration.

**After:** “Answer” or “Answer to Verified Petition,” as appropriate. The regenerated moving-papers compilation makes only that neutral title correction to the affected original notice/declaration lines; it does not invent testimony or complete signatures.

The state/AG verification exemption is a separate reason not to characterize an unverified response as an automatic default. ([CCP § 446](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=446.).)

### Format and packet assembly

**Before:** Twelve-page memorandum without contents/authorities; proposed orders inside the working review compilation; referenced exhibits A–E not reproduced.

**After:** New memorandum PDF includes caption, contents and a linked table of authorities; moving papers and proposed orders are separate outputs. Exhibits remain a disclosed completion task with an exact assembly crosswalk rather than a false certification that they are attached.

This addresses the tables and separate-proposed-order requirements without asserting the compilation is a compliant filing in every other respect. ([CRC 3.1113](https://courts.ca.gov/cms/rules/index/three/rule3_1113).)
