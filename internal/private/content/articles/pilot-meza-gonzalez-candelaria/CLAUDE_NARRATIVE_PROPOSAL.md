# Narrative proposal: the night of November 1, 2024

This file proposes text for `ARTICLE.md`. It does not change `ARTICLE.md` or `FACT_CHECK.md`. Line numbers refer to `ARTICLE.md` at commit `84b77c0`. Each insertion point also quotes the exact text it sits next to, so it can still be placed if another agent shifts the lines.

**Bottom line.** The article currently tells the night in four abstract paragraphs of pleading summary (lines 43–53). The controlling transcript holds a tight, dramatic, fully timestamped hour: the knock, "Open the window," the door opening three seconds later, the cup, the PAS advisement, "I don't consent to that," and the order to turn around one second later. The officer's own account, as recorded in the DMV decision, is also available to set beside it. Adding that hour, attributed line by line, makes the article much stronger. Five corrections come first. They are cases where filed pleadings or the existing article differ from the controlling Exhibit A, and the narrative below follows Exhibit A (see Part 4, items 1–9):

1. The empty can was brought to Meza-Gonzalez at **01:00:06, before the arrest**, after a rear-door search at 00:59:46. The Alameda complaint says the can was found only after the arrest (Ala. Compl. 7:2–8). The transcript contradicts that.
2. "There's not pass or fail" was said by **Melano** (01:09:52), not Meza-Gonzalez. The Alameda complaint attributes it to Meza-Gonzalez at 01:08:20–40 (Ala. Compl. 6:3–5).
3. The encounter on the transcript runs **about 62 minutes** (00:54:36–01:57:10). Both FACs say "over two hours" or "more than two hours" (M-FAC 2:3, 6:22; C-FAC 11:12).
4. Meza-Gonzalez, not Melano, **administered** the field sobriety tests (01:05:07–01:10:30). M-FAC ¶53 (16:10–11) says Melano administered them.
5. The transcript does **not** say Newanforbi was still asleep when the door opened. It records knocking "to wake Plaintiff," "Open the window," and the door opening three seconds later. M-FAC ¶¶16, 56 say "still asleep." The Matter 20 research memo records Plaintiff's later correction that the knocking woke him.

## Part 1 — Proposed narrative passages (ready to drop in)

### Passage A — Cold open

**Where:** Insert as a new opening paragraph **immediately after line 33** (`# One Stop, Two Cases: Meza and Candelaria`) and **before line 35** (`A November 2024 California Highway Patrol contact on Interstate 580 produced two civil stories.`).

> At 12:55 on the morning of November 1, 2024, a California Highway Patrol cruiser pulled onto the dirt shoulder of eastbound Interstate 580 in Livermore and stopped behind a gray Tesla Model Y. **[N1][N2][N3]** Inside, a commercial truck driver had pulled off the freeway to sleep. **[N4]** "It's a rental. Huh," one of the two officers said, before either had stepped out. **[N5]** Within twenty minutes the driver, Brendan Newanforbi, was handcuffed in the back seat of that cruiser. **[N6]** Within four months, a one-year suspension of his Class A license had begun. He alleges it ended his work as a professional driver. **[N7][N8]** The cruiser's camera recorded the hour in between. **[N9]**

*Note on line 35:* Once Passage A is in, the sentence at line 35 can begin "That contact produced two civil stories." This avoids repeating "A November 2024 California Highway Patrol contact."

### Passage B — What the dashcam recorded (new section)

**Where:** Insert as a **new H2 section between line 41 and line 43**. That is after `What follows is a record of the filed papers and the public orders. It is not a finding that anyone violated the Constitution, and it does not forecast either case.` and before `## What the pleadings say happened that night`.

> ## What the dashcam recorded
>
> The account below follows a timestamped transcript of the CHP recording in Case No. RJ68845. Newanforbi prepared it himself and signed it under penalty of perjury, and it was filed as Exhibit A in his cases. **[N10][N11]** It is his transcription, not a stipulated or official CHP transcript. The speaker labels and the bracketed descriptions of what the video shows are his. **[N10]** Times are the recording's clock. **[N12]**
>
> **The knock.** At 00:55:37 the cruiser stopped behind the Tesla and its audio switched on. **[N2]** Officers Santiago Meza-Gonzalez and Jose Melano walked up the passenger side on the dirt shoulder, flashlights in hand, and shone them through the windows. **[N13]** "I think it's booze," Melano said. Then: "It wasn't here earlier when we passed by." **[N14]** Meza-Gonzalez knocked on the front passenger window and kept knocking. **[N15]** "Oh look at this," Melano said. At 00:56:40 he said, "Open the window." **[N16]** At 00:56:43, three seconds later, the transcript records that the passenger door was opened by Officer Melano. **[N17]**
>
> "Hello." "Uh-huh." "Are you okay?" "Yeah, we good." **[N18]** Asked where he was coming from, Newanforbi said, "We're coming from uh, uh, a party." **[N19]** Meza-Gonzalez noted that the Tesla's dash clock read 12:57 a.m. and told him the shoulder "is not a parking lot." Newanforbi said he had been stopped there "like 5-7 minutes." **[N20]** Asked how much he had had to drink, he answered, "Zero." **[N21]**
>
> **The cup.** "What's in that cup right there?" Meza-Gonzalez asked. "Don't drink it," Newanforbi said. "Yeah don't drink it." **[N22]** Melano took the cup. "Is it water? No this is piss. This is pee." Newanforbi: "I don't know what that is." Melano: "I'm telling you it's piss. It smells like piss." **[N23]** When Melano held the cup out, Meza-Gonzalez leaned back: "No I'm not gonna smell that." **[N24]** "OK. Leave it there. Put it back," Melano said. Five seconds later, Meza-Gonzalez asked Newanforbi to step out of the car. **[N25]**
>
> **The can.** Newanforbi walked to the patrol car, gave his name, and spelled it: "NEW-WAN-FOR-BI." **[N26]** While Meza-Gonzalez walked him there, the transcript records, Melano opened a rear passenger door of the Tesla and searched. **[N27]** Melano then came over holding an empty can. "Did you drink that?" Meza-Gonzalez asked. "No." "You haven't drank anything since you've been parked?" "No." **[N28]**
>
> **Where he was.** "You know where you're at right now?" "Right now, Livermore." Newanforbi read the exit sign about fifty yards ahead, "Isabella Avenue, Portola Avenue," and Meza-Gonzalez said, "Correct." **[N29]** Asked whether he had been driving, he said, "I was in the driver's seat, yes sir." **[N30]** He explained why he had stopped: "You know I'm a truck driver. So when I feel tired, when I feel fatigued, whenever I am unable to continue to drive that truck, I'm gonna pull over to the side of the road, try to get a nap. … How quickly can I move over to the side of the road, catch some shut-eye, wake up and continue on to finish the route?" **[N31]**
>
> **The tests.** Meza-Gonzalez ran an eye test, then a test in which Newanforbi tilted his head back, closed his eyes, and estimated thirty seconds. **[N32]** When Meza-Gonzalez described a one-leg stand, Newanforbi pushed back: "It seems as though I'm passing them and then the level of difficulty just keeps increasing and increasing and increasing. I just wanna know if I've passed your test." **[N33]** "Yeah there's not pass or fail," Melano said. Meza-Gonzalez: "I go through all of them regardless if it's a pass or what you think is a pass or fail." **[N34]** Newanforbi said he did not know if he could balance on one leg. Meza-Gonzalez told him, "It's fine if you don't. You don't have to." **[N35]** Newanforbi agreed to try a walking test: "I mean I think we can do that." Melano cut in: "It's kind of sketch here bro. … it's on the side of the freeway and it's like uneven ground. I wouldn't do that one." "Alright then," said Meza-Gonzalez. **[N36]**
>
> **"I don't consent to that."** At 01:10:33 Meza-Gonzalez asked him to take a preliminary alcohol screening test. "You may refuse to take this test," he said. **[N37]** "So you're saying that I am required or not required?" "You're not required to do this." "And if I choose not to consent to it?" "If you choose to not consent to it then it'll be up to my discretion whether I put you under arrest for DUI or not." **[N38]** The officers readied the device. "Yes or no?" At 01:11:15 Newanforbi said, "I don't consent to that." **[N39]** At 01:11:16 Meza-Gonzalez said, "OK. Brendan. Alright then. I'm gonna have you just turn around please. Place your hands behind your back." By 01:11:33 he was putting on the handcuffs. **[N40]**
>
> **Breath or blood.** Meza-Gonzalez searched him, put him in the back seat of the cruiser, and attached the handcuffs to the vehicle frame. **[N41]** He then asked whether Newanforbi would take a breath test or a blood test at the jail. "I don't know. It seems like torture." **[N42]** "Right now you're not refusing," Meza-Gonzalez told him, "you're just saying that basically you don't want to choose." He read the chemical-test admonition and explained that a refusal would bring an administrative suspension of at least a year, "even if" Newanforbi was sober. **[N43]** "Even as a truck commercial driver, that's what you're willing to do, force the DMV's hand?" Newanforbi asked. "I'm not forcing the DMV to do it, that's their rule." **[N44]** At 01:22:15 Meza-Gonzalez asked, "So you're refusing to take a test?" The transcript records no answer before his "OK." **[N45]** The officers compared notes. Melano: "We just have to write what time we last passed him here because he wasn't here earlier." Meza-Gonzalez: "But he said he was here for seven minutes." Then Melano: "Yeah you want to start your warrant?" **[N46]**
>
> At 01:36:47 Melano returned to read the implied-consent advisement. Newanforbi asked if it was Miranda. "No, no, no," Melano said. "Miranda rights we only give when we still wanna ask you questions about the investigation. … We already have everything we need right?" **[N47]** If he did not choose a test, Melano told him, "we're gonna get it anyways. It says it's going to be with a warrant and you're gonna automatically lose your license." "So which one are we doing sir?" … "Neither?" "Yep." "OK so we're getting a warrant." **[N48]**
>
> **The last conversation.** Before the cruiser pulled away, the officers wondered aloud whether the Tesla had an alternator. Newanforbi explained: "I don't think electric vehicles have alternators. … Just like your phone gets charged." **[N49]** At 01:52:05 the cruiser left the shoulder. The recording ends at 01:57:10 with its arrival at the Dublin CHP transfer. **[N50]**

### Passage C — The paperwork (officer's account, then what the complaint alleges)

**Where:** Insert **after line 49** (which ends `…and that a later administrative form nonetheless designated a chemical-test refusal.`) and **before line 51** (`Four days later, the complaints allege, Dublin CHP issued a Detention Certificate…`).

> The officer's account survives in the DMV decision that later suspended the license. It records that Meza-Gonzalez found Newanforbi "asleep" on the shoulder, knocked on the passenger window, and made contact "through the open right passenger side door". It records that he observed "Slurred Speech, Red and/or Watery Eyes, and Odor of Alcohol", that he "smelled the strong odor of an alcoholic beverage emitting from Respondent's breath and person while speaking to him in front of the patrol vehicle", and that Newanforbi performed "unsatisfactorily" on the eye test and the modified Romberg. It also records an arrest at 1:12 a.m. and Newanforbi's "admission to driving." **[N51]**
>
> The Meza-Gonzalez complaint alleges that after the arrest, Meza-Gonzalez signed a DS-367 Officer's Statement and a CHP Arrest-Investigation Report under penalty of perjury. It alleges those papers were used to obtain the blood-draw warrant, to begin the license suspension, and to refer charges to the District Attorney. **[N52]** It alleges four entries that the recording does not support: a "strong distinct odor of an alcoholic beverage," which neither officer says aloud on the recording; speech described as "slurred"; a statement that he "seemed very confused and disoriented"; and a checked box reading "Beverage Present: Yes." **[N53]** The complaint concedes that odor, speech, and orientation are "experiential observations" an officer need not voice. It alleges that the recording, which captures coherent speech, correct orientation, and a cup identified as urine, contradicts them. **[N54]** A magistrate issued the warrant, and the complaint alleges that blood was drawn at the CHP Dublin Area office. **[N55]** The officers have not admitted these allegations, and their motion to dismiss (Dkt. 22) is pending. **[N56]**

### Passage D — The hearing (Candelaria)

**Where:** Insert **after line 69** (which ends `…plus a reissue fee and an SR-22 financial-responsibility requirement.`) and **before line 71** (`The live First Amended Complaint names Diana Candelaria individually…`).

> Before any hearing, the complaint alleges, DMV issued a temporary license valid "through the outcome of hearing" that barred him from "driving commercially". **[N57]** On December 9, 2024, he filed a five-page written response identifying the dashcam evidence and the Detention Certificate. He also offered the certificate and a transcript of the recording into the administrative record. **[N58]** The DMV's own notice described the hearing officer's job in one sentence: "The Hearing Officer will develop the facts and render a final decision on your matter." **[N59]**
>
> The hearing was held over Microsoft Teams at 9:00 a.m. on January 24, 2025. Newanforbi testified under penalty of perjury. **[N60]** The complaint alleges that Candelaria marshaled and offered the Department's exhibits, ruled on his objections to them, and then decided the case. In the complaint's words, that crossed "from neutral fact development into advocacy." **[N61]** Her written decision summarizes his contention that the officer's claimed odor "from the passenger side of the vehicle" was "highly implausible." It then holds that, because the officer had certified the DS-367 under penalty of perjury, "It is presumed official duty has been regularly performed. No evidence was presented to the contrary." **[N62]** The complaint alleges that the decision does not mention the Detention Certificate or the dashcam contradictions. **[N63]** The suspension ran from February 20, 2025, through February 19, 2026. **[N64]** Defendants have answered the amended complaint (Dkt. 26). That answer was not in the portfolio when this draft was prepared. **[N65]**
