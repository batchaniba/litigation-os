## Bottom line

Maryland has no separate crime called “spousal rape.” Forcible sexual assault of a spouse is first- or second-degree rape under Criminal Law §§ 3-303 and 3-304. Marriage is not an element and, since October 1, 2023, is not a defense.

**Criminal felony rape has no statute of limitations.** A 2012 act can still be charged if the State can prove it. Delay is an evidence problem, not a filing-deadline problem.

**Civil damages for an adult survivor are still on a short clock.** Three years generally (Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 5-101); one year if the civil theory is assault (§ 5-105). The Child Victims Act “file at any time” rule in § 5-117 is for sexual abuse that occurred while the victim was a **minor**. Marriage does not toll the adult civil periods.

## Criminal clock

Maryland puts time limits on misdemeanors in Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 5-106. It does not put a time limit on felonies. *Massey v. State*, 320 Md. 605, 610 (1990); *State v. Renfro*, 223 Md. App. 779 (2015) (“Maryland has no statute of limitations on felonies.”).

| Offense | Grade | Criminal clock |
|---|---|---|
| First-degree rape, § 3-303 | Felony (up to life) | None |
| Second-degree rape, § 3-304 | Felony (up to 20 years) | None |
| Third-degree sexual offense, § 3-307 | Felony | None |
| Fourth-degree sexual offense, § 3-308 | Misdemeanor | Generally 1 year under § 5-106(a), with listed exceptions |

## Spousal defense — repealed, with a historical caveat

Effective October 1, 2023, Chapters 728 and 729 of 2023 repealed former Criminal Law § 3-318. A current spouse is not exempt from §§ 3-303, 3-304, 3-307, or 3-308.

Even before repeal, § 3-318 already allowed prosecution of a spouse for first-degree rape, specified second-degree rape, and specified third-degree sexual offense when the act was by force or threat of force and without consent, or when the spouses had been living apart under a written separation or for three months, or under a limited-divorce decree. Forcible marital rape was not a free pass under the old statute. What 2023 removed was the remaining gap for cohabiting spouses on the narrower theories.

Which version applies to a pre–October 1, 2023 incident is a counsel question (law at the time of the act versus the 2023 repeal). That question does not create a limitations period that does not exist.

Consent after 2024 Chapter 761 (Criminal Law § 3-301.1) means a clear and voluntary agreement. Presence or absence of consent is a totality-of-the-circumstances question. Consent can be withdrawn. Being married is not consent.

## Civil clock

| Claim | Clock | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| General tort (battery, IIED, and most other adult theories) | 3 years from accrual | Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 5-101 |
| Civil assault | 1 year | § 5-105 |
| Sexual abuse that occurred while the victim was a minor | No civil deadline | § 5-117 (Child Victims Act of 2023) |
| Interspousal immunity | Abolished; not a bar | *Bozman v. Bozman*, 376 Md. 461 (2003) |

Historic acts can still support fear, pattern, and Family Law § 8-205 divorce-equity factors after a standalone damages claim is time-barred. Admissibility remains a court question.

## Protective order

Rape or another listed sexual offense is a Family Law § 4-501(b)(1) act of “abuse.” A protective-order petition is not a damages case and is not governed by the civil three-year clock. It still needs a qualifying act and a fact pattern a court will act on. Final-order proof is by a preponderance of the evidence.

## What proof constitutes

“Proof” is not one thing. It depends on the room.

| Forum | Question | Burden |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal rape prosecution | Did this person commit this felony? | Beyond a reasonable doubt. State's Attorney controls the case. |
| Final protective order | Did a listed § 4-501 act occur? | Preponderance |
| Civil damages | Did this tort happen, and did it cause this harm? | Preponderance |

The same facts can clear a protective-order hearing and still not be a charge the State will file. An old felony can still be charged with no limitations clock and still lose a civil case if the adult damages window is gone.

### Elements the State must prove (force theories)

- A sexual act or vaginal intercourse occurred.
- It was without consent as defined in § 3-301.1.
- Force or threat of force, unless another statutory theory in § 3-304 applies.
- For first degree: a listed aggravator (weapon, strangulation or serious injury, threat of death or kidnapping, aided by another, or tied to a listed burglary).

Maryland does **not** require a second witness, a hospital kit, or a same-night police report. A complainant's testimony, if the fact-finder believes it, can be legally sufficient. That is different from “the State will charge on testimony alone.”

### What can constitute proof, when it exists and is admissible

- The complainant's account of the act, force or fear, and lack of agreement.
- A contemporaneous statement to a friend, doctor, advocate, or officer. Timing goes to credibility, not to a magic corroboration rule.
- Medical or forensic findings if an exam happened. Absence of injury does not disprove rape.
- Messages, voicemail, or writings already in the complainant's possession.
- A third person who saw force, heard a threat, saw her immediately after, or heard an admission.
- Pattern evidence only if a judge rules it admissible. Pattern is not a substitute for proving the charged act.

### What is not proof by itself

- A family member's second-hand report.
- “They have been married since 2012.”
- Emotional or psychological mistreatment with no listed sexual act.
- A demand letter, a repo note, or a settlement worksheet.
- An inference drawn in this workspace.

### Evidence hygiene that still applies

Maryland is an all-party-consent recording state. Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 10-402(c)(3). A secret new recording of a private conversation is usually a felony plus a § 10-410 civil action, not a proof plan. Preserve what already exists. Do not intercept, track, impersonate, or access shared accounts.
