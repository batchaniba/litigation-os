13                                      FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION

14

15   Fourth Amendment -- Judicial Deception and Unlawful

16   Bodily Intrusion Under a Warrant Obtained Through

17   Materially False or Misleading Statements

18   (42 U.S.C. § 1983 -- Against Defendant Meza-Gonzalez and DOES 1-10)

19

20          37. Plaintiff re-alleges paragraphs 1 through 36.

21

22          38. An officer violates the Fourth Amendment by submitting a warrant application

23   containing statements that are deliberately or recklessly false, or that omit material facts, if the

24   application -- stripped of the false statements and supplemented with the omitted facts -- would

25   not support probable cause. Franks v. Delaware, 438 U.S. 154, 155-56 (1978); Chism v.

26   Washington State, 661 F.3d 380, 386 (9th Cir. 2011).

27

28          39. Defendant Meza-Gonzalez executed under penalty of perjury the DS-367 and arrest

                                                   Page 11
               COMPLAINT FOR DAMAGES -- NGWA NFORBI v. MEZA-GONZALEZ, ET AL. (N.D. Cal.)
 1   report containing the four contradicted representations identified in paragraph 25 and the material

 2   omissions identified in paragraph 26. Those documents formed the basis of the warrant

 3   authorizing the involuntary extraction of Plaintiff's blood. When the contradicted representations

 4   are set aside and the omitted facts are restored, the remaining application does not plausibly

 5   support probable cause to believe Plaintiff was driving under the influence.

 6

 7          40. Defendant Meza-Gonzalez acted with at least reckless disregard for the truth. An

 8   officer who executes a sworn statement while a contemporaneous recording of the same

 9   encounter -- stored on his own patrol vehicle -- contains information materially inconsistent with

10   that sworn statement acts with reckless disregard, at minimum. The right to be free from a

11   warrant obtained through materially false or misleading statements was clearly established before

12   November 1, 2024. Franks, 438 U.S. at 155-56; Chism, 661 F.3d at 386; Galbraith v. County of

13   Santa Clara, 307 F.3d 1119, 1126 (9th Cir. 2002).

14

15          41. As a direct and proximate result, Plaintiff suffered a compelled blood extraction

16   without valid probable cause, and the further damages alleged in paragraphs 35-36.

 6           54. A warrantless entry into a constitutionally protected area is presumptively

15   an observable medical emergency. Even if a welfare rationale had been present, the community

16   caretaking exception does not authorize warrantless entry absent an imminent threat to safety.

17   Caniglia v. Strom, 593 U.S. 194, 197-99 (2021).
