## 3. The formal-order discrepancy — the defense's best "on the merits" exhibit

The signed formal order states the generic CCP §430.10(e) ground ("fails to allege facts sufficient to state any cause of action"), while the tentative ruling and minute order — the court's own words — state the specific ground: exhaustion of administrative remedies as a **jurisdictional precondition**, unmet **"prior to the filing of"** the FAC.

These are consistent, not contradictory: an exhaustion demurrer in California is brought under §430.10(e), because the complaint must allege exhaustion to state a claim. But the distinction matters for preclusion briefing:

- **Expect Van Dine to quote the formal order's "fails to state any cause of action" language** for a *Goddard*-style argument that the demurrer was sustained on the merits.
- **Answer:** courts look to the actual basis of decision, not the caption of the demurrer ground. The court's stated basis — in both the tentative ruling it affirmed and the minute order — was failure to satisfy a jurisdictional precondition to suit, framed temporally ("prior to the filing of his FAC"), i.e., prematurity. Under *Perez* and *Heath* (9th Cir. predicting California law), dismissal for failure to exhaust administrative remedies is not a decision on the merits, and under Restatement (Second) of Judgments §20(2) (via *Costello*), dismissal for failure to satisfy a precondition to suit does not bar a later action once the precondition is satisfied. The generic §430.10(e) recital in a defense-drafted form order does not convert that jurisdictional dismissal into a merits adjudication.

## 4. Revised element-by-element assessment (California law via 28 U.S.C. §1738)

**Element 3a — final judgment: FAILS for the defense.** No judgment of dismissal exists. Neither the minute order nor the signed formal order contains dismissal language, and the docket has no "Judgment" entry. The portal's "Case Disposed" flag and the vacated CMC are administrative closure, not a judgment. Independently, the 60-day appeal window from July 15, 2026 runs through Monday, September 14, 2026 (Cal. Rules of Court 8.104(a)(1)(A); weekend roll under CCP §12a), and under *Kay* there is no finality while appeal time runs.

**Element 3b — "on the merits": FAILS for the defense.** The court's reasoning is exhaustion as a jurisdictional precondition, unmet before the FAC was filed — a curable prematurity defect, not a merits determination (*Perez*; *Heath*; Restatement §20). Caveat per §3 above: brief around the formal order's generic phrasing; anchor on the tentative ruling and minute order.

**Element 2 — same parties / privity as to Smith: FAILS for the defense.** Smith was not a Matter 5 party. CEO status does not create privity (*DKN Holdings*; *Dillard*); *Taylor v. Sturgell* forecloses virtual representation. The only live factual question is *Taylor* category 4 (control of the Matter 5 defense) — not resolvable on the pleadings.

**Element 1 — same cause of action: the defense's strongest element.** Under *Boeken*'s harm-centered primary-rights test, the federal claims seek redress for the same course of conduct and injuries; the §1983/RICO labels and the federal-right distinction (*Gonzales*; *Furnace*) do not supply an escape. The Bane-on-Bane overlap is the most exposed. Do not lead the opposition here; the defense still loses on the other three elements, each independently.

**Issue-preclusion fallback:** the exhaustion finding was actually litigated, but the estopped issue — whether plaintiff exhausted *before filing the state FAC* — is immaterial to federal claims carrying no exhaustion requirement (*Patsy* — cite-check before filing), and the issue is not identical to any issue in the federal case.
