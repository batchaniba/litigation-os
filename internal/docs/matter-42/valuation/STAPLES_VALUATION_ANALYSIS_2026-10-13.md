# Matter 42 — Staples damages valuation

**Case:** Nforbi v. Staples Retail et al.  
**Matter:** 42  
**Valuation date:** October 13, 2026  
**Scope:** Staples only. First Advantage is reserved.  
**Purpose:** Private case valuation and independent model review. This is not a settlement demand or an assertion that every modeled remedy is presently recoverable.

## Executive result

The workbook distinguishes current proof from a complete-case scenario. On the presently documented record, liability and damages are not yet established: the $22 hourly rate is documented, but the causal start blockage or denial, weekly schedule, offsets, report source/use, emotional harm, and corporate punitive proof remain incomplete.

## What is documented

- A conditional Staples Retail Sales Supervisor offer was accepted.
- The offered rate is $22 per hour.
- The position was described as full-time.
- First Advantage issued a public-record reporting notice on October 7, 2026.
- The employment sequence, screening documents, offer materials, job posting, completed forms, and communications have been preserved.

## What remains assumed or unproven

- Forty paid hours per week. “Full-time” alone does not prove a 40-hour guarantee.
- October 5, 2026 as the causal wage-loss start date.
- A Staples decision delaying, rescinding, or denying employment because of the report.
- The exact source and contents of the public-record information.
- That Staples used California registry information in a manner covered by the selected statutory remedy.
- No qualifying interim earnings or other mitigation offsets.
- Two years of back-pay litigation time and two years of post-judgment front pay.
- A 15% benefits load.
- $100,000 in Staples-specific emotional distress.
- A discretionary additional award equal to compensatory damages.
- Exemplary damages equal to compensatory damages, including the necessary corporate knowledge, authorization, or ratification proof.

## Items intentionally excluded

- First Advantage liability or damages.
- Attorney fees for the plaintiff's own pro se time.
- Litigation costs, prejudgment interest, tax gross-up, future medical costs, overtime, raises, commissions, and unsupported out-of-pocket expenses.
- Duplicate recovery across FCRA, ICRAA, registry, privacy, or employment theories.
- Any company-valuation or revenue multiplier.
- Mutually inconsistent civil-penalty and exemplary remedies.

## Independent-review questions

1. Does the model overstate back pay by assuming loss began October 5, 2026 and continued through October 13, 2028?
2. Is two years of front pay defensible for a conditional offer that had not yet produced worked shifts?
3. Is the 15% benefits proxy too high or too low for this position?
4. Is $100,000 emotional distress supportable without medical treatment records, or should testimony-only distress be valued lower?
5. Can the statutory additional award properly be modeled as a multiple of the full compensatory pool, including future wages and distress?
6. Are the registry civil-penalty and exemplary branches correctly treated as alternatives?
7. Does any FCRA or ICRAA recovery overlap with the compensatory pool or statutory routes shown here?
8. What evidence-weighted probability should be applied to liability, causation, damages proof, mitigation, and collection?
9. Is the $300,000–$500,000 settlement-value band supported at the present proof stage, or is it only supportable after source/use and employer-decision evidence arrives?
10. Identify every arithmetic, legal-election, double-counting, mitigation, present-value, or proof defect and provide a corrected range.
