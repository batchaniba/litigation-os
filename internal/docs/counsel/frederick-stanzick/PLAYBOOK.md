### The *Carrillo* CRA playbook (Dkt. 57) — what she will actually write

Read this before forecasting Accurate's September 22 paper. These are the arguments she has already signed:

1. **Certification-only § 1681b(b)(1).** The CRA's duty is to obtain the employer's certification that the report will not be used in violation of EEO law. Obtaining it "dooms" the claim. The statute "governs the interaction between the employer and the CRA at the time of furnishing—not the employer's subsequent interaction with the applicant." Cites *Juster v. Workday*, 617 F. Supp. 3d 1128, 1137 (N.D. Cal. 2022).
2. ***Mendoza* wall.** Penal Code § 290.46 regulates the **user**, not a CRA that republishes public records at the employer's request. *Mendoza v. ADP Screening & Selection Servs.*, 182 Cal. App. 4th 1644, 1648–55 (2010); *McGroarty v. Am. Background Info. Servs.*, 2009 WL 980417 (C.D. Cal. Apr. 8, 2009); *Robles v. AMPAM Parks Mech.*, 2015 WL 1952311 (C.D. Cal. Apr. 28, 2015).
3. **"We do not use."** Asurint compiles and transmits. The employer picks the searches, initiates pre-adverse / adverse notices through a portal, and makes the decision. The notices say so on their face.
4. **No duty to police.** No authority requires a CRA to monitor, predict, or know how every state employment law applies to each candidate.
5. **Fail-safe / unascertainable class.** Membership turns on whether Vons *unlawfully used* registry information — a merits question about the employer, not the CRA. Ninth Circuit "ascertainability" is weaker (*Briseno*), but they still run the argument.
6. **Individualized mini-trials.** For each applicant: report contents, self-disclosure vs. registry vs. court records, what the employer reviewed, mitigation, whether any use was "to protect a person at risk," and what the CRA knew at furnishing.
7. **Adequacy / typicality attack on the named plaintiff.** Supervised-release violations, alcohol, *Dunford v. American DataBank*, 64 F. Supp. 3d 1378 (N.D. Cal. 2014); *Porath v. Logitech*, 2019 WL 6134936 (N.D. Cal. Nov. 18, 2019); *Meyer v. Portfolio Recovery*. Inflammatory framing of the underlying offense is part of the brief, not a sideshow.
8. **No injunctive standing.** Reporting SOR information is lawful (*Mendoza*); the proposed injunction is not tied to § 1681b(b)(1).

Drury is first chair on the signature block. Stanzick is the California name. Treat Dkt. 57 as **her** paper for Matter 29 purposes — she signed it — but do not assume she is the sole drafter.

## The seam: where the two defenses can conflict

| | Accurate (Stanzick) | Amazon (Frederick) |
|---|---|---|
| Best fact | "Our report never contained registry info" | "Our decision never used registry info" |
| Best law | *Carrillo* Dkt. 57: certification-only FCRA; *Mendoza*; *Roman* UCL | Fair Chance compliance; (j)(1) person-at-risk; employer-decision separation |
| Worst day | The report *did* flag the registry | The decision workflow *did* ingest the flag |

If Accurate denies the report contained registry information while Amazon's decision documents reference a registry-derived flag (or vice versa), one desk's position breaks the other's. **The transmission chain is the key evidence**: Accurate report + coding/status + transmission to Amazon + Amazon decision workflow, anchored to screening ID Y94611404.

Watch for:

- **Separate vs. joint motions.** If they file separately, compare factual premises line by line.
- **The entity-identity attack cutting both ways.** If Stanzick argues the wrong Accurate affiliate, that admission hands you the discovery target.
- **Settlement asymmetry.** The $250k demand went to Amazon only.
