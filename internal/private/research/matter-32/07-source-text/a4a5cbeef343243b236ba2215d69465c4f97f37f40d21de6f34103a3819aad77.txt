16          Plaintiff Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi, proceeding pro se, respectfully submits these

17   Objections to the Magistrate Judge’s Findings and Recommendations (“F&R”) issued April 20,

19   California, Monell claims against CHP, and official-capacity claims). Plaintiff objects, however,

22

23          These Objections are timely under 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1) and Local Rule 302(c)(1).

24   Plaintiff requests de novo review and denial of the motion to dismiss (ECF No. 12) or,

25   alternatively, leave to amend.

27                                      I. SPECIFIC OBJECTIONS

28

                                                  Page 1
                              PLAINTIFF'S OBJECTIONS TO MAGISTRATE JUDGE'S F&R
 1   Objection No. 1: The F&R errs by weighing disputed facts and crediting the CHP report over

 2   Plaintiff’s well-pleaded allegations (F&R at 5–6).

 3

 4           The F&R concludes that probable cause existed based on the late hour, shoulder location,

 5   and cup remark, while dismissing Plaintiff’s express allegations that (1) the officers had no

 6   probable cause, (2) the cup contained no alcohol, (3) the search found no evidence of intoxication

 7   or illegal activity, and (4) Plaintiff showed no signs of impairment. (Compl. ¶¶ 12–17.) It does so

 8   by crediting the CHP report’s contrary details (odor of alcohol, red/watery eyes, slurred speech,

 9   poor field sobriety tests).

10

11           This is reversible error. At the Rule 12(b)(6) stage the Court must accept all well-pleaded

12   factual allegations as true and draw all reasonable inferences in Plaintiff’s favor. Ashcroft v.

13   Iqbal, 556 U.S. 678, 678 (2009); Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 570 (2007). Pro se

14   complaints receive especially liberal construction. Chambers v. Herrera, 78 F.4th 1100, 1104 (9th

15   Cir. 2023). The Court may not weigh the CHP report’s version against Plaintiff’s version.

16

17           Two additional facts alleged in the Complaint make probable cause implausible on its

18   face: (a) the District Attorney declined to file any charges, and (b) CHP itself issued a certificate

19   under Penal Code § 851.6 characterizing the encounter as a mere detention (not an arrest) because

20   no accusatory pleading was filed. (Compl. ¶ 18.) These facts must be taken as true. The F&R’s

21   probable-cause ruling should be rejected.

22

23   Objection No. 2: The F&R errs in holding that the Fourteenth Amendment due-process claim

24   fails to state a claim (F&R at 7).

25

26           The Complaint alleges that Defendants arbitrarily escalated a welfare check into a full

27   custodial seizure (handcuffs, transport, chemical testing) without probable cause. (Compl. ¶¶

28   17–19, 22–23.) These facts state a plausible claim of arbitrary deprivation of liberty. The F&R

                                                   Page 2
                            PLAINTIFF'S OBJECTIONS TO MAGISTRATE JUDGE'S F&R
 1   offers no analysis explaining why they are insufficient. The claim should not be dismissed.

 2

 3   Objection No. 3: The F&R errs in granting qualified immunity on the pleadings (F&R at 7).

 4

 5          Qualified immunity is an affirmative defense that is rarely resolved at the 12(b)(6) stage

 6   when the complaint alleges a clear constitutional violation. Pearson v. Callahan, 555 U.S. 223,

 7   236 (2009). Plaintiff’s allegations — a welfare check escalated, on nothing more than a cup

 8   speculation, into a warrantless vehicle search and custodial arrest with no evidence found — state

 9   a Fourth Amendment violation. The right at issue has been clearly established for decades:

10   officers may not prolong a stop or conduct a warrantless search/arrest without probable cause.

11   Rodriguez v. United States, 575 U.S. 348 (2015) (prolonged stops); Dunaway v. New York, 442

12   U.S. 200 (1979); Arizona v. Gant, 556 U.S. 332 (2009). On the facts alleged in the Complaint,

13   any reasonable officer would have known the conduct was unlawful. The F&R’s

14   qualified-immunity ruling is premature.

15

16   Objection No. 4: The F&R errs in recommending dismissal without leave to amend (F&R at 7–8).

17

18          Leave to amend must be granted liberally to pro se plaintiffs unless it is clear that no

19   amendment can cure the defects. Garity v. APWU Nat’l Lab. Org., 828 F.3d 848, 854 (9th Cir.

20   2016). Plaintiff has already narrowed the claims in good faith, and the DA’s decision and Penal

21   Code § 851.6 certificate provide concrete factual support for a refined Fourth Amendment theory.

22   Amendment is not futile. Dismissal with prejudice at the pleading stage is an abuse of discretion.
