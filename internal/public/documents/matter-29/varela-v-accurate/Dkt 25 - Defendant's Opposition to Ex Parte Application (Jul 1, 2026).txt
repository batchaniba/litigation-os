 1         Defendant ACCURATE BACKGROUND, LLC, by its attorneys, hereby opposes
 2   Plaintiff’s Ex Parte Application to: Substitute Attorney of Record and Proceed Pro Se
 3   and Vacate the Voluntary Dismissal Pursuant to FRCP 60(b)(3) and (6) (ECF No. 23 (the
 4   “Application”)).1
 5                                       INTRODUCTION
 6         Plaintiff seeks to reopen a properly dismissed case to litigate a separate dispute
 7   between himself and his now-former counsel, FCRA Attorneys, over the disbursement of
 8   settlement funds – a dispute in which Accurate plays no part. Plaintiff does not contest
 9   that he authorized, signed, and entered into a binding settlement agreement with
10   Accurate. Nor does Plaintiff contest that Accurate satisfied all of its obligations under
11   the settlement agreement, including the timely transmittal of the full settlement amount to
12   his former counsel, FCRA Attorneys. By Plaintiff’s own admission, there are no
13   remaining disputes between Plaintiff and Accurate.
14         Plaintiff’s grievance – that his former counsel will not release his share of the
15   settlement proceeds – provides no basis to vacate the dismissal. Relief under Federal
16   Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b)(3) reaches only fraud or misconduct by an opposing party,
17   not a movant’s own lawyer, and Plaintiff alleges none by Accurate. Relief under Rule
18   60(b)(6) is reserved for extraordinary circumstances, which a post-settlement dispute
19   with one’s own counsel is not. Accordingly, Plaintiff’s application to vacate the
20   dismissal should be denied.2

 1                                   RELEVANT BACKGROUND
 2             Plaintiff filed this action on January 21, 2026. (ECF No. 1.) The parties
 3   subsequently reached a settlement, and Plaintiff signed a binding settlement agreement.
 4   Consistent with the terms of the parties’ settlement agreement, Accurate timely
 5   transmitted the full settlement amount to Plaintiff’s counsel of record, FCRA Attorneys,
 6   on May 28, 2026. The parties then filed a stipulated dismissal of the action with
 7   prejudice (ECF No. 21), which the Court entered on June 2, 2026 (ECF No. 22).
 8             On June 19, 2026, Plaintiff filed the present Application, seeking to substitute
 9   himself pro se in place of his former counsel and to vacate the dismissal under Rule
10   60(b)(3) and (6). (ECF No. 23.) Plaintiff’s stated basis is that his former counsel –
11   FCRA Attorneys – has refused to release his share of the settlement proceeds, allegedly
12   conditioning release on a video confirmation of his identity. (Id.)
13                                            ARGUMENT
14             Relief under Rule 60(b) is “an extraordinary remedy” available “only … upon a
15   showing of exceptional circumstances,” and the party seeking relief bears the burden.
16   See Latshaw v. Trainer Wortham & Co., 452 F.3d 1097, 1103 (9th Cir. 2006). Plaintiff
17   invokes both Rules 60(b)(3) and (b)(6). Plaintiff cannot satisfy the requirements of
18   either.
19   A.        Plaintiff Cannot Obtain Relief Under Rule 60(b)(3) Because He Alleges No
               Fraud or Misconduct by Accurate.
20
               Rule 60(b)(3) permits relief for “fraud, … misrepresentation, or misconduct by an
21
     adverse party.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(3) (emphasis added). To prevail, the movant “must
22
     prove by clear and convincing evidence that the verdict was obtained through fraud,
23
     misrepresentation, or other misconduct and the conduct complained of prevented the
24
     losing party from fully and fairly presenting the defense.” De Saracho v. Custom Food
25
     Mach., Inc., 206 F.3d 874, 880 (9th Cir. 2000) (citations omitted).
26
               Plaintiff’s allegations of misconduct run exclusively against his own former
27
     counsel, not against Accurate, the opposing party. His motion does not identify any
28

 1   misrepresentation or misconduct by Accurate; indeed, he concedes that Accurate fully
 2   performed when it transmitted the full settlement payment to FCRA Attorneys on May
 3   28, 2026. (ECF No. 23, p. 2.) Instead, Plaintiff contends that his former counsel is
 4   unreasonably refusing to transmit Plaintiff’s portion of the settlement proceeds to him by
 5   requiring Plaintiff to confirm his identity by video before it will release any part of the
 6   settlement proceeds to him. (Id., p. 3.) That is a dispute between Plaintiff and FCRA
 7   Attorneys that has nothing to do with Accurate and provides no basis to vacate a
 8   dismissal under Rule 60(b)(3). Nor could Plaintiff show that any conduct “prevented”
 9   him from presenting a case: He did not lose on the merits, but voluntarily settled and
10   stipulated to dismissal.
11         To the extent Plaintiff suggests the dismissal was premature because he has not
12   personally received his share, that misreads the settlement agreement: receipt by
13   Plaintiff’s counsel constituted receipt, and Accurate had no obligation to police his
14   counsel’s onward distribution of the funds. Under the terms of the parties’ settlement
15   agreement, Accurate was not required to ensure transmittal of any portion of the
16   settlement payment to Plaintiff personally. Rather, Section 2 of the settlement agreement
17   states that the settlement payment “will be sent to Plaintiff’s counsel no later than thirty
18   (30) days after” receipt of the signed agreement, necessary tax documents, and
19   “wire/ACH instructions from Plaintiff’s Counsel.” (ECF No. 23, Ex. A, ¶ 2 p. 3,
20   emphases added). Plaintiff’s counsel returned those documents to Accurate’s counsel on
21   May 5, 2026 (less than 30 days before Accurate’s timely transmittal of the settlement
22   payment). The Agreement in turn provided that, within five business days after receipt of
23   the settlement proceeds, the parties would file a stipulated dismissal of the lawsuit, with
24   prejudice, pursuant to Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(ii). (Id. at ¶ 3, p. 3) Those conditions were
25   satisfied when Plaintiff’s counsel received the funds, and the parties filed the dismissal on
26   June 2, 2026, which the Court then entered (ECF No. 22). Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Rule
27   60(b)(3) theory fails.

 1   B.     Plaintiff Cannot Obtain Relief Under Rule 60(b)(6) Because a Disbursement
            Dispute With His Own Counsel Is Not an Extraordinary Circumstance.
 2
            Rule 60(b)(6) is “used sparingly as an equitable remedy to prevent manifest
 3
     injustice and is to be utilized only where extraordinary circumstances prevented a party
 4
     from taking timely action to prevent or correct an erroneous judgment.” Latshaw v.
 5
     Trainer Wortham & Co., Inc., 452 F.3d 1097, 1103 (9th Cir. 2006). The movant “must
 6
     demonstrate both injury and circumstances beyond his control that prevented him from
 7
     proceeding .. with the action in a proper fashion.” Latshaw, 452 F.3d at 1103 (quoting
 8
     Community Dental Services v. Tani, 282 F.3d 1164, 1168 (9th Cir. 2002)).
 9
            Plaintiff can show neither. Plaintiff made a free and deliberate choice to settle his
10
     claims and to stipulate to a dismissal with prejudice, and “free, calculated, deliberate
11
     choices are not to be relieved from” under Rule 60(b). Ackermann v. United States, 340
12
     U.S. 193, 198 (1950); see Latshaw, 452 F.3d at 1099 (“[g]enerally speaking, Rule 60(b)
13
     is not intended to remedy the effects of a deliberate and independent litigation decision
14
     that a party later comes to regret through second thoughts or subsequently gained
15
     knowledge that corrects prior erroneous legal advice of counsel.”). A post-settlement
16
     dispute over Plaintiff’s own counsel’s disbursement of the settlement funds is not an
17
     “extraordinary circumstance,” and nothing prevented Plaintiff from taking timely action
18
     to protect his interests. Plaintiff received the full benefit of his bargain – a binding
19
     settlement, fully funded by Accurate – and his only complaint is that his own counsel will
20
     not pay him.
21
            That grievance has no connection to the dismissal. Plaintiff’s asserted injury arises
22
     entirely from his counsel’s conduct after the settlement was performed, and vacating the
23
     dismissal would do nothing to remedy it. Regardless of the reasons for FCRA Attorneys’
24
     apparent refusal to pay its own (now former) client, Accurate fulfilled all of its own
25
     obligations under the settlement agreement. To the extent FCRA Attorneys continues to
26
     withhold the settlement proceeds from Plaintiff, Plaintiff is entitled to seek relief directly
27

 3         In any event, even if Plaintiff could identify extraordinary circumstances, the
 4   equities foreclose relief. Reopening this action could not compel FCRA Attorneys – a
 5   non-party – to release Plaintiff’s funds, so vacatur would do nothing to redress the
 6   grievance Plaintiff actually asserts. At the same time, depriving Accurate of the finality

 8   would impose precisely the kind of prejudice Rule 60(b) is meant to prevent.
 9         Accordingly, Plaintiff provides no basis to reopen this case under either Rules
10   60(b)(3) or (6).

12         For the foregoing reasons, Accurate respectfully requests that the Court deny
13   Plaintiff’s motion to vacate the voluntary dismissal.
