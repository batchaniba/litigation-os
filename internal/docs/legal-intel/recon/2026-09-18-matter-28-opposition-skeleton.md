## 0. Standing orders before drafting

1. **Never concede or "not oppose" any count.** Cisneros converts concessions into with-prejudice
   dismissals (*K.J. v. United States*, 25-cv-07161-LJC, Doc. 34 — citing *Quisenberry*). Oppose
   every count, even weakly; argue leave to amend in the alternative on each.
2. **Treat the opposition as a leave-to-amend audition.** Facts first alleged in the opposition
   can't save the claim, but *can* win leave to amend (*Orion Tire Corp. v. Goodyear Tire & Rubber
   Co.*, 268 F.3d 1133, 1137–38 (9th Cir. 2001) — cited in her own *Gold Club* order, Doc. 45 at
   22). **Attach a proposed amended complaint** (or a detailed proffer of curative facts) — that
   is the single highest-leverage move in this filing.
3. **Cite clean.** Standing Order § E.4: failure to verify citations "may be grounds for sanctions
   and/or striking a filing." Every cite below is verification-tagged in the research memo.
   Re-verify pins before filing (three pins are SECONDARY: *Zetz* 713, *Schreiber* 1400–01,
   *DZ Reserve* 1233).
4. **Calendar:** opposition due **14 days after the motion is filed** (Civil L.R. 7-3). Her
   Standing Order § E.2: failure to timely oppose "may be construed as consent" to granting the
   motion. File nothing outside the briefing without leave — she strikes sur-replies and
   "supplemental authority" notices under L.R. 7-3(c) (*Gold Club*).
5. **Matter 6 disclosure:** the undisclosed overlapping habeas matter must be disclosed on the
   record; do not voluntarily dismiss Matter 28 (*Semtek* nullity rule per base memo).

## I. Standard of review (open the opposition here)

- *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009) / *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544
  (2007): plausibility; facts taken as true; reasonable inferences in plaintiff's favor.
- **Pro se liberal construction — quote her own words back to her** (*K.J.*, Doc. 34 at 4–5):
  "pleadings must be construed liberally and leave to amend must be given unless the pleading
  deficiency cannot be cured," *Lopez v. Smith*, 203 F.3d 1122, 1130–31 (9th Cir. 2000);
  *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89, 94 (2007).
- **Leave-to-amend default — quote her boilerplate** (*Gold Club*, Doc. 45 at 22): "Courts
  generally do not dismiss a complaint with prejudice under Rule 12(b)(6) unless it is clear the
  complaint cannot be saved by amendment," *United States v. Corinthian Colls.*, 655 F.3d 984,
  995 (9th Cir. 2011); leave "shall be freely given when justice so requires," Fed. R. Civ. P.
  15(a). Make defendants carry the **futility burden on each count**.
- Scope of review limited to complaint, incorporated materials, and judicial-notice materials
  (*Khoja v. Orexigen Therapeutics*, per her *Gold Club* order); *Starr v. Baca* two-part test.

## II. Res judicata — lead with this; it is the defense's headline ground

### A. The three independent defects (use all three; any one is fatal)

1. **No final judgment exists.** Matter 5 ended in the July 15, 2026 minute order sustaining the
   demurrer **without leave to amend** — with **no decretal dismissal language** and **no
   "Judgment" or "Judgment of Dismissal" entry** on the San Joaquin register (verified Sept 12;
   **re-pull the morning of filing** — Van Dine can cure this by getting a judgment entered).
   Demand judicial notice of the *actual* prior-judgment documents (minute order, register),
   not defendants' characterization of them.
2. **Not on the merits.** The demurrer was sustained on **failure to exhaust administrative
   remedies**, which the court itself called a **"jurisdictional requirement"** to filing.
   - *Heath v. Cleary*, 708 F.2d 1376, 1380 n.4 (9th Cir. 1983) (PRIMARY): an exhaustion-based
     state ruling precludes nothing — "the only issue necessarily decided by the court was the
     propriety of granting mandamus relief in light of Heath's failure to pursue administrative
     remedies."
   - *Perez v. City of San Bruno* (state exhaustion as jurisdictional precondition) — see base
     memo for the precise holding.
   - BAP memorandum (2012, SECONDARY): a demurrer sustained WOLA is a judgment on the merits
     **"as long as the order was based on the merits (or lack thereof) of the claims asserted
     and not on some formal defect"** (citing *Goddard*, 14 Cal.2d 47, 52; *Pollock v. Univ. of
     S. California*, 112 Cal.App.4th 1416 (2003)) — the formal-defect line.
   - *Costello v. United States*, 365 U.S. 265 (1961) (Restatement § 20 analogy): dismissals
     resting on a threshold defect are not on the merits.
3. **Smith was not a party to Matter 5.** The state defendants were HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc.,
   Katie Palacios, and Does — not Shannon Smith. Party-identity element fails as to her.

### B. Anticipated counter-authority — neutralize preemptively

- ***Blackwell v. Carrington Mortgage Servs.*, No. 24-cv-06494-BLF (N.D. Cal. Jan. 22, 2025)
  (Freeman, J.)** — pro se, res judicata 12(b)(6) granted **without leave**. **Two clean
  distinguishers:** (a) *Blackwell* anchored preclusion on a **separate Order of Dismissal With
  Prejudice** — no such order exists in Matter 5; (b) the *Blackwell* demurrer was for **failure
  to state a claim**, while Matter 5's was for **exhaustion** ("jurisdictional requirement").
  Pair with binding *Heath* n.4.
- ***Meinhardt v. City of Sunnyvale* (2024) 16 Cal.5th 643, 654–55** — the deeming doctrine
  (demurrer WOLA treated as "final judgment" when sufficiently final). **One paragraph, not
  more:** the doctrine was articulated for **appealability, not preclusion** — and even a
  deemed judgment on an exhaustion ground is still not **on the merits** under *Perez* /
  *Heath* n.4 / *Costello*. Address it before they spring it.
- If a judgment **has** been entered since Sept 12: fall back to the merits-element argument
  (defects 2 and 3 are judgment-independent) plus the **privity point** — Smith was not a
  party and is not in privity with the state defendants on an exhaustion-based dismissal.

### C. Affirmative alternative asks (end the res judicata section with these)

- In the alternative, **defer ruling on preclusion until summary judgment** — judicial economy
  counsels against deciding claim/issue preclusion on a 12(b)(6) where the prior record is
  incomplete and the state register is in flux. Cisneros has never decided preclusion on a
  12(b)(6) on the public record; in *this* case Dkt. 11 left preclusion to the defendants.
- Request leave to amend to plead the Matter 5 record with precision.

## III. State action — the § 1983/Bane threshold

### A. The affirmative case (lead with *West*, then the scheme, then joint action)

1. **Public function:** *West v. Atkins*, 487 U.S. 42 (1988) — a private contractor performing a
   function the state delegated is a state actor. Here the delegation is statutory, not merely
   contractual: Cal. Penal Code **§ 3008(d)(2)** (verbatim verified on leginfo) — a parolee
   "shall **successfully complete** a sex offender management program… as a condition of parole,"
   with program length "**as determined by the certified sex offender management professional**."
   The **completion determination is a state-conferred power over liberty** — not mere therapy.
2. **Joint action / nexus:** the Form 3043 reporting pipeline — HOPE's determinations feed parole
   discharge decisions, i.e., joint participation in the state's action. (See base memo for the
   Form 3043 detail.)
3. *Chavez v. Robinson*, 12 F.4th 978 (9th Cir. 2021) — state-actor status **assumed** for an
   individual sex-offender therapist; tension point for any defense cite. **Do not cite *Chavez*
   as a QI denial** — it *granted* QI to the therapist.

### B. If they cite *Gross v. Samudio*, No. 14-1248 (10th Cir. Oct. 27, 2015) (nonprecedential)

Answer in three moves: (a) **nonprecedential, Tenth Circuit, 2015** — persuasive only
(FRAP 32.1); (b) it turned on **pleading insufficiency** ("conclusory allegations… simply
because Colorado had contracts with the treatment providers"), not a categorical rule that
private vendors can never be state actors; (c) it did not involve a CEO sued for a **blanket
corporate policy** or a statute vesting the completion determination in the vendor.
Distinguish also on *Wittner v. Banner Health* (relied-on in *Gross*) — no liberty-vesting
statute there.

### C. Qualified immunity (if raised — peripheral unless they claim state action)

- Private contractors' QI is the *Richardson v. McKnight* / *Filarsky v. Delia* line; *Filarsky*
  limits QI to those "retained on a temporary or occasional basis" — not a systematic
  government vendor. No Cisneros 12(b)(6) QI ruling found (UNVERIFIED); QI is fact-bound and
  rarely resolved at the pleading stage.

## IV. First Amendment retaliation

- Elements (verified, *Arizona Students' Ass'n v. Ariz. Bd. of Regents*, 824 F.3d 858, 867
  (9th Cir. 2016) (PRIMARY; pin 867 SECONDARY)): (1) constitutionally protected activity;
  (2) defendants' actions would **"chill a person of ordinary firmness"**; (3) protected
  activity was a substantial motivating factor. *Arizona Students' Ass'n* **reversed** a
  12(b)(1)/12(b)(6) dismissal and held the district court erred in denying leave to amend.
- Plead/allege the protected activity (grievance/writ/challenge to treatment), the adverse
  action, and temporal or evidentiary nexus; argue in the alternative for leave to amend to
  strengthen the nexus.

## V. Due process

- *Kildare* is a poor fit for the defense (per the Sept 12 survey — re-check the exact holding

  parole-liberty line (verify before citing).

## VI. Bane Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1)

- Track the § 1983 state-action argument: private actors exercising a state-delegated power over
  liberty. Bane requires interference **by threats, intimidation, or coercion** — plead the
  specific coercive acts (e.g., conditioning liberty on compliance with the blanket policy).
- Keep Bane tethered to the § 1983 counts; it rises or falls with state action.

## VII. Fraud — the most exposed count; armor it

**This is the genuinely vulnerable count** (reliance + Rule 9(b) particularity). The strategy is
defense-in-depth: oppose the dismissal **and** make the leave-to-amend proffer so strong it
becomes the example of why dismissal must be without prejudice.

1. **Rule 9(b) proffer — give her exactly what *Zetz* demands.** *Zetz v. Boston Scientific
   Corp.*, 398 F. Supp. 3d 700, 713 (E.D. Cal. 2019) (PRIMARY; pin 713 SECONDARY): fraud must be
   pleaded with **who, what, when, where, how**; the counts there were dismissed **with leave to
   amend**. In the opposition (and the attached proposed FAC), name for each alleged
   misrepresentation: the **speaker**, the **document or communication**, the **date**, and the
   **specific representation** — the who/what/when/where/how grid, one row per representation.
2. **Reliance inference:** *DZ Reserve v. Meta Platforms, Inc.*, 96 F.4th 1223, 1233 (9th Cir.
   2024) (PRIMARY; pin 1233 SECONDARY) — a uniform misrepresentation communicated to all class
   members supports an inference of reliance; a defendant may not defeat reliance by arguing the
   victim "should not have relied on statements made with fraudulent intent to induce reliance."
3. **Intent:** plead facts supporting scienter from the blanket-policy evidence (*Dwyer*).
4. **Preclusion exposure:** fraud is also the count most exposed to a "could have been raised"
   argument — answer with the merits-defect line from § II above; a non-merits dismissal
   precludes nothing, including fraud.

## VIII. RICO — keep it lean and secondary

1. **Person/enterprise distinctness:** *Cedric Kushner Promotions, Ltd. v. King*, 533 U.S. 158,
   163 (2001) (PRIMARY) — "The corporate owner/employee, a natural person, is distinct from the
   corporation itself, a legally different entity with different rights and responsibilities due
   to its different legal status." Smith (person) / HOPE (enterprise) satisfies § 1962(c)
   distinctness.
2. **One dry footnote on *Schreiber*:** defense cited *Schreiber Distrib. Co. v. Serv-Well
   Furniture Co.*, 806 F.2d 1393 as "(9th Cir. 2004)"; it is **1986** (decided Dec. 24, 1986) —
   and it **reversed** a RICO dismissal with instructions to **grant leave to amend**
   (806 F.2d at 1400–01; pins SECONDARY). Use it affirmatively for leave to amend on the RICO
   count; the footnote signals recycled briefing.
3. Keep RICO lean per the base memo's standing guidance — do not let it become the
   credibility sink of the opposition.

## IX. Global leave-to-amend ask (close every count with it; close the brief with it)

1. For **each count**, plead the opposition in the alternative: the count survives; but if the
   Court finds a deficiency, **leave to amend** under Rule 15(a) — *Corinthian Colleges*,
   *Leadsinger*, her own *Gold Club* boilerplate, and the pro se *Lopez*/*Erickson* standard.
2. **Attach the proposed amended complaint** (or, at minimum, a detailed proffer of curative
   facts count by count — the *Zetz* grid for fraud, the Form 3043 joint-action detail for
   state action, the nexus facts for retaliation). Under her *Orion Tire* cite, this is what
   converts a dismissal into a without-prejudice one.
3. **PLRA exhaustion closer:** 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(h) (verbatim verified) — "prisoner" means one
   "incarcerated or detained in any facility"; *Kerr v. Puckett*, 138 F.3d 321, 322 (7th Cir.
   1998); *Talamantes v. Leyva*, 575 F.3d 1021, 1024 (9th Cir. 2009) — a parolee at liberty is
   not a "prisoner," so PLRA exhaustion is inapplicable to the § 1983 counts even if the state
   exhaustion ruling were given full effect.
