## 3. Strong law for the entry issue—plus the strongest opposing cases
- **US v Ngumezi**, 980 F.3d 1285, 1289–91 (9th Cir. 2020): opening door AND **entering/leaning into vehicle's interior** constitutes a Fourth Amendment search; a routine traffic investigation with reasonable suspicion but no particularized reason for physical entry cannot justify it. Lowered privacy expectation in cars is not no privacy. This is strong for a **privacy-search violation** if the alleged physical entry is objectively proved and no exception fits.
- **Texas v Brown**, 460 U.S. 730, 739–40 (1983): a flashlight inspection through car windows from lawful location is ordinarily **not** a search. The quoted pre-entry cup inspection is not automatically unlawful; it becomes problematic when officers cross into the protected interior without legal authority.
- **Mardis v Superior Court**, 218 Cal.App.2d 70, 73–75 (1963): **adverse, very close facts**. CHP officer on highway saw a sleeping driver, opened passenger door, discovered odor of alcohol; court upheld entry/investigation. Critical distinctions to verify: *Mardis* involved an **official radio report** of similar vehicle driving in manner suggesting intoxication, occupant asleep at night well off paved highway, and its pre-*Ngumezi* approach gave broader latitude to opening. It is not overruled here or rendered factually irrelevant by merely citing a newer federal case. Analyze its continuing persuasiveness and any material differences.
- **People v Superior Court (Poole)**, 267 Cal.App.2d 363, 365 (1968): police may investigate asleep motorists, including waking and asking driver to exit, and cites *Mardis*. Validity of **initial approach** does not itself equal unlimited interior search authority.
- **People v Madrid**, 168 Cal.App.4th 1050 (2008): vehicle-based welfare detention still must be objectively reasonable and limited to facts. Its precise facts concern stopping a moving car over concerns about a passenger; do not misapply it as a categorical door-opening case.
- **People v Ovieda**, 7 Cal.5th 1034 (2019), and **Caniglia v Strom**, 593 U.S. 194 (2021), disallow freestanding nonemergency **residential** entry. Critical caveat: **THEY CONCERN HOMES, NOT THIS VEHICLE**; neither abolishes noncriminal vehicle caretaking or all emergency-aid exceptions.
- **Whren v United States**, 517 U.S. 806 (1996), and **Brigham City v Stuart**, 547 U.S. 398 (2006): objective legal justification ordinarily controls notwithstanding mixed/subjective investigative intent. Therefore "I think it's booze" is evidence of chronology/observation and possibly credibility, **not by itself proof the welfare justification was pretextually unconstitutional**.
- **Vehicle Code §23223**: prohibits driver having open container containing actual alcoholic beverage in possession on highway; §23226 addresses open alcohol container in passenger compartment. But a **cup merely suspected** of containing booze is weaker than a visibly identifiable open alcoholic container. Proof of what was visible and perceived before door opening is indispensable.

## 4. The decisive legal bridge: *unlawful search is not automatically criminal false imprisonment*
**Stronger federal theory (Matter 32):** if no warrant, consent, objectively reasonable emergency aid or other exception, touching/opening door and searching inside may violate Fourth Amendment; physical inspection of cup may be related fruit for appropriate federal constitutional remedy.

**But state Matter 20 is NOT an ordinary Fourth Amendment suppression or damages case.** Penal Code §679.10(c) lists **false imprisonment** and **unlawful criminal restraint** as qualifying activities, not mere unconstitutional inspection/entry of a vehicle. An unlawful vehicle search alone is a privacy invasion, and does not necessarily mean a person was unlawfully confined. Need show:
1. The point at which the officer **restrained, confined or detained the person's liberty** against will (e.g., commands preventing departure / exit orders / handcuffing).
2. Why restraint at that point **lacked lawful authority** (distinguish investigative reasonable suspicion, DUI probable cause, VC §40300.5 exception); evaluate independently at each stage.
3. Satisfying the **criminal** false-imprisonment elements under PC §236 / CALCRIM 1242, not equating a civil constitutional violation or short-lived search with a criminal offense.
4. That this theory was properly presented to CHP in **April 9 request**, so an error in the April 20 no-qualifying-crime determination is a **Count One agency issue**, not a newly added cause of action beyond operative petition.
5. That the actual April decision applied an incorrect standard, demonstrably false material premise or prohibited denial reason, OR that the statutory predicates are now clearly proved.

### The "even if DUI arrest had probable cause" counterfactual
**If CHP establishes objective probable cause for the 01:12 arrest** (even independent of questioned cup/entry), that does not automatically retroactively legalize an unjustified interior search at 00:56:43. Thus the early-entry privacy claim could survive in **federal Matter 32**.

**But it does NOT automatically win the state U-visa certification writ.** An unlawful entry/search may exist alongside a later lawful arrest. If all confinement was privileged, the separate early privacy intrusion may fail to establish victimhood of PC §236 criminal false imprisonment. The special state writ only concerns CHP's Apr 20 certification decision on surviving Count One. **The necessary winning bridge is an unlawful coercive restraint qualifying as criminal false imprisonment, or another specifically supported qualifying activity already encompassed by the operative petition, plus demonstrated statutory error.**

Another theoretical route: if the initial vehicle entry wrongfully produced facts essential to a later detention, analyze *causation and lawful alternative/attenuation*. But criminal-case suppression "fruit of the poisonous tree" doctrine does **not automatically control** the factual eligibility or administrative-record rules in a CCP §1085 certification writ. Need separate authority and specific causal evidence. Do not demand suppression or constitutional damages from Whitman here.
