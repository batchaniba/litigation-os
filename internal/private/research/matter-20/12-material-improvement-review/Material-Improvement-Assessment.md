# Matter 20: Material-Improvement Assessment

The working packet is substantially more careful than its early research drafts, but its present weakness is still proof of a material Count One error, not lack of emphatic language. The review supports a tighter memorandum and a defined factual-resolution request; it does not establish a present right to either vacatur or a signature.

## Bottom line and decision chain

**Verdict: needs identified proof and procedural completion before filing as a merits-ready packet.** A usable draft can be prepared now, but the native recording, residual probable-cause facts, evidentiary foundations, operative pleading and live case setting are not all verified.

For the proposed incident-fact route, the chain is: competent incident evidence → findings establishing intentional restraint without lawful authority → qualifying victimhood → a material error in the certification determination → the legally available remedy, with helpfulness and other applicable predicates necessary before direct certification. The weakest current links are the no-lawful-authority finding after considering all remaining evidence and the connection between established findings and an unlawful no-qualification decision.

A proved legal-standard error is a separate possible remand route: identify the actual incorrect rule and its material application, without requiring the Court itself to decide every eligibility predicate before ordering lawful reconsideration. The current denial letter alone does not establish such an error merely by omitting an elements discussion or citing federal law; do not confuse that proof gap with a rule that legal-error remand always requires a completed victimhood finding. ([Common Cause, 49 Cal.3d 432, 442](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/49/432.html).)

This is a civil writ, not a criminal prosecution of the officers, so do not demand a criminal conviction or proof beyond a reasonable doubt; the ordinary civil burden applies absent a different controlling rule, and the petitioner must establish essential facts. ([Evidence Code §§ 115](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=115.), [500](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=500.); [Penal Code § 679.10(k)(1)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=679.10.).)

## The strongest opposition and the honest response

### CHP's strongest position

The report claims multiple indicators independent of walk-and-turn: HGN, Romberg timing/eyelids, eyes, speech, odor, earlier driving and no drinking after stopping. Correcting the test entry would not necessarily defeat objective DUI probable cause or section 40300.5(e) arrest authority; the later ethanol result is also not negative. The August order made no merits findings, helpfulness does not establish victimhood, and incomplete discovery does not demonstrate arbitrary certification review.

That is a substantial opposition, not a straw man. Section 40300.5(e)'s dissipation rule and Espinoza's totality holding make it legally plausible even if a walk-and-turn error is proved. ([Troppman](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2586143/troppman-v-valverde/); [Schofield](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2256187/people-v-schofield/); [Espinoza](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4378115/espinoza-v-shiomoto/).)

### Petitioner's strongest supported response

CHP's custody report expressly includes field-test performance in its rationale; the submitted transcript identifies a concrete, test-specific attribution conflict that can be checked objectively. The answer is not to label all observations fabricated, but to authenticate the recording, obtain findings on the disputed observations and show what remains after correction. If reliable findings establish qualifying unlawful restraint, the Court's existing Count One analysis supplies a pathway to examine whether the no-qualification refusal can stand. State order pp. 3–4; AGO 000031–000033; petition Ex. 5 pp. 9–12.

Remand is not a consolation prize available whenever discovery is unsatisfactory. It requires a proved material error; direct certification requires still more, including predicates, a single lawful outcome and proper pleading/notice. ([California Correctional Supervisors Organization (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/96/824.html).)

### Questions the judge is likely to ask

- **What survives the walk-and-turn correction?** Identify each other impairment fact and the evidentiary reason to credit or reject it; do not answer only with the car's stationary condition.
- **Where is the recording?** Identify exact file, provenance, clock, relevant segment and full context, plus an actual lodging/admission plan; the acceptance of a notice is not admission of the media.
- **Why is the certification denial materially wrong?** Connect resulting qualifying-crime findings to the denial's no-qualification conclusion without speculating about motive or relying on a dismissed explanation theory.
- **Why can the Court decide this by motion?** Identify whether the requested decision fits the statutory paper-motion conditions or explain the proposed essential factual proceeding.
- **Why direct relief under this petition?** Address original ¶ 8 and the Tier 3 prayer; preserve notice and procedural fairness rather than assuming a motion can silently expand relief.
- **What does the federal order add?** Only screening-stage plausibility, on an assumed-true record; use one short passage or omit it if later federal rulings undermine its usefulness.
- **What is actually before the Court?** Produce complete exhibits and identify the operative pleading, accepted proof of service, original receipt, signatures and case orders.

## Research disagreements resolved

- **Common Cause pinpoint**: Keep 49 Cal.3d 432, 442 for the mandamus/discretion proposition; a prior memo's suggestion that the actual discussion is missing from that pinpoint was incorrect, and page 445 concerns beneficial interest rather than a better substitute for that rule. ([Common Cause](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/49/432.html).)
- **Direct-certification priority**: A prior memo's preference for leading with a signature is a strategic proposal, not a primary-source finding or user decision; the existing remand-first hierarchy is preserved, with direct relief expressly conditional on proof, lawful power and notice. ([California Correctional Supervisors Organization](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/96/824.html).)
- **Reason-giving through Saleeby**: The case supports reviewability of discretion but cannot silently reverse the actual August 29 dismissal of the independent explanation count; use a material merits error, not conclusory dissatisfaction with the letter. ([Saleeby](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/39/547.html); state order p. 4.)
- **Criminal officer restraint as a fallback**: Section 146(a) concerns detention under color of authority without lawful authority, but it does not bypass proof of unlawful custody; do not add an underdeveloped new crime theory merely to avoid the same disputed element. ([Penal Code § 146](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=146.).)
- **Post-remand clocks and retained jurisdiction**: A proposed 30-day compliance/return period is a requested judicial term, not an automatic new-request statutory clock, and section 1097 does not prescribe a 36-month retention period. ([Penal Code § 679.10(j)(1)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=679.10.); [CCP § 1097](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1097.).)
