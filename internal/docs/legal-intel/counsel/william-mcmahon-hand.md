## 3. Practice patterns

- **Subject-matter concentration:** ADA/disability defense for state agencies dominates the sample — DMV customer-accommodation disputes (Hardy; the 2007 Novato matter), state-service ADA damages (ISC), and DOJ constitutional-record litigation (Pervez). Secondary: state-employer defense (Aguirre/EDD, FMLA/CFRA).
- **Dispositive-motion orientation:** every civil matter in the sample reached a dispositive motion — Rule 12 (Pervez), summary judgment (Hardy, ISC), or split SJ → trial (Aguirre). This is a lawyer whose cases get decided on papers or a built record, not on settlement leverage visible in the public record.
- **Full-lifecycle experience:** answer → discovery/protective order → dispositive motion → jury trial (closing) → Ninth Circuit affirmance (Aguirre arc). He is not a pleadings-only specialist.
- **Record-system fluency:** the *Arias* custodian testimony shows hands-on familiarity with CHP records infrastructure. It does not prove equivalent DMV hearing-system knowledge, but a multi-decade DMV defense portfolio makes procedural fluency with agency adjudications a safe assumption.
- **Cooperative where useful:** signed a stipulated protective order in Pervez (2019) — routine, professional discovery practice when it serves the defense.

## 4. Rule 12 and summary-judgment analysis

- **Rule 12(b)(1)/(6) — Pervez v. Becerra (E.D. Cal., 2019):** the state defendants' motion to dismiss the First Amended Complaint under both 12(b)(1) and 12(b)(6) was **granted** (June 28, 2019 order; hearing April 19, 2019). The case was a §1983 challenge alleging DOJ erroneously reported a Welfare & Institutions Code §5250 commitment to NICS, producing a lifetime firearms bar. *Caveat:* the order does not by itself prove McMahon authored or argued the motion — he is verified as state-defense counsel on the file (signed the protective-order stip), not as the motion's author. Describe as the defense team's win on his file, not his personal oral-argument win.
- **Summary judgment — Hardy (N.D. Cal., 2006):** defense SJ granted in a DMV disability-accommodation dispute (attribution likely, appearance unconfirmed — see table).
- **Summary judgment — ISC, Inc. v. California (9th Cir., 2010):** district-court SJ **affirmed**; counsel listing verifies McMahon on the brief. The decision turned on the ADA Title II damages standard — plaintiffs lacked evidence of **deliberate indifference**, and injunctive claims were moot. This is his cleanest published example of winning on a heightened plaintiff-side evidentiary burden.
- **Mixed SJ → trial — Aguirre (N.D. Cal., 2018–19):** defense SJ granted in part and denied in part; the surviving FMLA/CFRA claims went to a jury, which returned a **defense verdict**, affirmed on appeal. Shows willingness to try the claims that survive papers — and to win them.
- **Net read:** expect a jurisdictional/sovereign-immunity screen first (12(b)(1)), then a record-built SJ attack keyed to the plaintiff's burden (deliberate indifference, qualified immunity, evidentiary sufficiency). He does not appear to be a quick-settlement practitioner.

## 6. Style and behavioral observations (for a pro se opponent)

- **Forceful closer, once rebuked on the margins.** In Aguirre, the post-trial sanctions order identifies McMahon as the DAG who delivered closing argument and notes he referenced discovery disputes **outside the trial record**. The court denied sanctions and found **no bad faith**, but stated an experienced state lawyer should not need that warning. Expect a narrative-heavy, record-pressing style; at trial, be ready to object to extra-record references promptly and on the record.
- **Proceduralist first.** The Pervez 12(b)(1)/(6) win and the ISC deliberate-indifference affirmance point to a lawyer who looks for the exit ramp — jurisdiction, immunity, burden of proof — before litigating merits. Against a pro se plaintiff, that means early pleading attacks; the complaint must be airtight on standing, sovereign immunity, and the elements that trigger heightened proof standards.
- **Institutional memory.** Thirty-one years at the bar, overwhelmingly in AG civil defense, with repeat DMV/ADA exposure. Do not expect unfamiliarity with agency-procedure defenses (hearing-officer structure, administrative records, exhaustion-adjacent arguments).
- **No observed sharp practice beyond the single Aguirre rebuke.** Protective-order stipulation in Pervez was routine and cooperative. No discipline history found.
