## Memorandum of Points and Authorities

### A The record establishes an unperformed duty to rule

Rule 4.551(a)(5)(A) requires a superior court to rule initially on a habeas petition within 60 days. Subdivision (i) permits an extension for good cause stated in an order. The July 2 order set August 1, 2026 as Respondent's extended ruling date. The present petition accepts that order and seeks enforcement of the duty that remained after its deadline passed. (Exs. A-B.)

The rule recognizes an order to show cause, a denial, or an informal-response request as an initial ruling. (Rule 4.551(a)(8).) An order to show cause initiates further proceedings; it does not itself grant the requested habeas relief. (Rule 4.551(c)(3).) This petition therefore seeks the initial action the rule requires, with no demand that the superior court complete all merits proceedings within 15 days.

The HC-004 provides a second basis for requiring prompt action. The presiding judge's duty to assign and calendar a decision within 30 days is conditioned on a determination that the notice is complete and the petition remains unruled upon. (Rule 4.551(a)(6)(A)-(B).) The filed packet and current clerk record must establish what occurred. An absent completeness determination would call for prompt attention to that request; it would not erase the preexisting initial-ruling requirement or the court's own expired extension.

Petitioner does not contend that the rule permits only one extension or prohibits another notice. The relevant facts are that he requested a ruling through the prescribed procedure and that the current record establishes continued inaction after the extended date. The existence of further possible requests does not supply a decision in the case already pending.

In Maas v. Superior Court (2016) 1 Cal.5th 962, the Supreme Court explained that habeas decision timelines serve the objective of prompt relief for petitioners presenting meritorious claims. Although Maas concerned judicial disqualification, its discussion of the purpose of those timelines supports enforcing the applicable rule here. The current rule's deadlines, rather than the older timetable discussed in Maas, govern this request.

### B Mandate can secure a ruling while preserving judicial discretion

Code of Civil Procedure section 1085 authorizes a writ requiring an inferior tribunal to perform a legally required act. Section 1086 requires beneficial interest and the absence of a plain, speedy, and adequate ordinary remedy. Petitioner meets those requirements because his own habeas remains pending, the applicable ruling period has expired, and there is no decision available for review.

This Court need not evaluate the discharge report, determine Policy 19-03's validity, decide the scope of regulation 3574, or resolve the effect of the Sacramento proceeding to order Respondent to act. Those issues may affect the content of a ruling. They do not eliminate the obligation to issue one.

The requested order respects the distinction between requiring a judicial decision and prescribing its result. Respondent may request an informal response, issue an order to show cause when warranted, or deny the petition with the reasons required by rule 4.551(h)(1). If further proceedings follow, their governing deadlines and any properly entered extensions remain applicable.

The relief is also narrower than an original merits habeas in this Court. Petitioner has already submitted his claims to the superior court and seeks action in that file. He does not ask this Court to replace that proceeding with a new determination of his parole claims.

### C A short deadline and compliance report provide effective relief

The requested 15-day period is proposed relief based on the elapsed time, not a deadline supplied by rule 4.551 itself. Respondent has already reviewed the case sufficiently to enter its July 2 extension and has received the subsequent notice requesting a ruling. A short date certain accommodates the limited initial action sought. A copy of the ruling and a compliance report will establish that the writ's purpose has been fulfilled.

Petitioner requests an alternative writ or order to show cause in the ordinary course. If the Court finds the record sufficient for accelerated relief, rule 8.487 permits notice that a peremptory writ in the first instance is under consideration. The Court must afford the notice and opportunity to respond required by Palma v. U.S. Industrial Fasteners, Inc. (1984) 36 Cal.3d 171. Petitioner requests that procedure where appropriate; he does not contend this petition substitutes for the Court's notice.

Respondent should be directed to rule within the period fixed by this Court and report compliance. That order will resolve the delay issue while leaving disposition of the habeas claims to Respondent.
