## 1. GRANTABILITY ASSESSMENT — why this motion could be granted, partially granted, sent for factfinding, or denied

### 1A. The governing frame: the August 29, 2026 order already decided the shape of this fight

The Court overruled the demurrer to Count One on one conditional pathway, quoted verbatim from the order itself (Aug. 29, 2026 Order at 4):

> "If, at the trial on the merits, the Court were to credit Plaintiff's allegations that neither he nor his vehicle smelled of alcohol, that he showed no signs of impairment in talking with the officers or in performing the physical field sobriety tests he agreed to perform, and that the arresting officers lied about it in their police report, it could reasonably find probable cause for arrest was lacking, which could support a finding that the CHP abused its discretion in refusing to certify Petitioner's Form I-918."

Three consequences follow, and every grantability judgment below runs through them:

1. **The Court has already told the parties what would suffice.** A credited showing on odor, impairment signs, and report falsity could defeat probable cause and support an abuse-of-discretion finding. The motion's job is to convert that conditional into an adjudicated record — not to re-argue demurrer plausibility.
2. **The Court has also already recorded the adverse frame.** The same order recites the CHP-favorable facts (freeway-shoulder sleeping occupant, early morning hours, open alcohol container, PAS refusal) and cites People v. Thompson (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, 818 (facts known to the arresting officer, reasonable-caution test) with Vehicle Code §§ 23152(a), 23222(a), 23612(a) (Order at 3). Any motion that ignores that frame will be read as evasive.
3. **Counts Two and Three are gone.** Reason-giving (§ 679.10(i)(2)) and helpfulness-presumption (§ 679.10(h)) standalone claims were sustained without leave to amend, because the denial did not rest on noncooperation (Order at 4). Helpfulness survives only as a predicate element of Count One. Any brief that revives Counts Two or Three as claims — as opposed to using their subject matter as evidence within Count One — invites denial on law-of-the-case grounds.

### 1B. Outcome 1 — GRANT of direct certification (highest relief, weakest current support)

**What the Court would have to find, all of them:** (i) the facts known to the officers at the time of arrest (~01:11–01:12) did not supply reasonable cause to believe petitioner had driven under the influence, under Vehicle Code § 40300.5 and Penal Code § 836; (ii) the restraint was therefore an unlawful violation of personal liberty under Penal Code § 236 and § 679.10(c)(19) (and/or (c)(18), and/or (d)); (iii) petitioner was directly and proximately harmed (§ 679.10(o)(2)); (iv) petitioner has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful (§ 679.10(g)(1), with the (h) presumption applied); and (v) on those findings, certification is the only lawful outcome (CCSO, 96 Cal.App.4th at 827).

**Why it could be granted:** The walk-and-turn correction, if verified against the authenticated native recording, removes a stated probable-cause basis ("poor performance on the FST's," AGO 000032) that the Form 202 itself links to the arrest. Combined with the admitted absence of contemporaneous verbal disclosure of odor or slurred speech (Answer to Verified Petition ¶¶ 37, 44–45), the urine identification (¶ 36), and the admitted orientation facts (¶ 37), a court that credits the corrected record could find the remaining impairment evidence — disputed HGN/Romberg observations, an open container, admitted earlier driving — insufficient to persuade a person of reasonable caution at the time of arrest. The August 29 order's conditional pathway expressly contemplates that result.

**Why it is the weakest tier on the current paper record:** Four obstacles, each independently capable of defeating finding (i) or (v):
- **The impairment record is genuinely disputed, not one-sided.** CHP's sworn account records odor, slurred speech, red/watery eyes, HGN clues, and a Romberg estimate (AGO 000030–000032, 000048–000049). Petitioner preserves those observations; he does not erase them. A court need not resolve every observation in petitioner's favor to deny direct certification — it need only find reasonable cause on the totality.
- **Adverse totality authority is real.** Troppman v. Valverde (2007) 40 Cal.4th 1121 (reasonable-cause belief suffices; no proof of actual driving immediately prior to arrest required for §§ 23612/13353 purposes) and Espinoza v. Shiomoto (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 85 (DMV suspension affirmed; reasonable cause found on odor and emotional-state observations) show courts sustaining reasonable-cause findings on comparable observation sets. Neither decides this record, but both defeat any argument that the observations here are categorically insufficient as a matter of law. (See 03-authority-audit.md.)
- **The § 40300.5(e) dissipation exception is CHP's strongest circumstance.** People v. Schofield (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 968 holds a warrantless DUI arrest lawful under the destruction-of-evidence exception where reasonable cause exists. Dissipation does not supply reasonable cause, but if the court finds reasonable cause on the totality, (e) answers the warrantless-arrest objection outright. The motion must win reasonable cause first; (e) cannot be wished away.
- **The record has two open holes that block an "only one lawful outcome" finding.** The RFP No. 8 decision file is unproduced (what Serrato considered is unknown), and the transcript version is unresolved (April 9 vs. June 18, 2026 declaration). A court should not find "no reasonable alternative" on a record with those holes. CCSO itself affirmed denial of the writ — it states the rule, it does not compel this result.

**Assessment:** Direct certification is legally available as a prayer and is now the petitioner's directed primary objective (see § 3 below on prayer order), but on the current paper record it is the least likely tier to be granted outright. Its proper function in the replacement brief is to frame the full adjudication the court is asked to make, while the remand tier carries the motion if the court stops short of finding (i) or (v).

### 1C. Outcome 2 — PARTIAL GRANT: vacatur and controlled remand (strongest sustainable relief)

**What the Court would have to find:** (i) the April 20, 2026 denial, as written, identifies no element, no facts, and no lawful-authority analysis under § 679.10(c), (d), and (g); (ii) the agency account the decision rests on is materially inaccurate in the walk-and-turn respect (Form 202 AGO 000031 vs. authenticated recording at ~01:10:14–01:10:30); and (iii) a decision made without element analysis and on a materially inaccurate agency record is arbitrary and lacks evidentiary support, requiring vacatur — without the court deciding whether certification must ultimately issue.

**Why this tier is the strongest:** It requires no credibility finding against the officers on odor, HGN, or Romberg; no resolution of the § 40300.5(b) obstruction question; and no finding about what Serrato subjectively reviewed. It rests on two things the court can verify on the papers: the denial letter's face (Pet. Ex. 1, quoted in Aug. 29 Order at 2) and the Form 202's text against the authenticated recording. The remand directions then do the work the denial did not do — element-by-element determination, corrected-record review, circumstance-by-circumstance § 40300.5 findings, separate helpfulness determination, (k) bars, and a reviewable written decision — while expressly binding CHP to the adjudicated findings and statutory criteria without dictating an outcome the court has not adjudicated. That structure survives the opposition's best argument ("certification is discretionary") because it enforces the process by which discretion is exercised, rather than compelling its exercise in a particular direction.

**What could still defeat it:** If the court holds that a conclusory denial is sufficient where the underlying record supports the conclusion (i.e., that the letter's brevity is not itself an abuse of discretion when the Form 202 and investigation supply the reasoning), Tier 2 narrows to the walk-and-turn inaccuracy alone — and if the native recording does not confirm the transcript, even that leg falls. The remand tier therefore depends on authentication more than on rhetoric. See 02-serrato-decision-error-memorandum.md (the omission-vs-failure-to-consider distinction is the central vulnerability, stated honestly there).

### 1D. Outcome 3 — FACTFINDING / evidentiary proceeding (likely if the court credits neither side's paper record)

**Trigger:** The court finds genuinely material fact questions that the papers cannot resolve — paradigmatically: (a) whether the native recording confirms the walk-and-turn exchange; (b) the weight and foundation of the HGN/Romberg observations; (c) the roadside-geometry facts for § 40300.5(b); (d) the PAS-vs-chemical chronology. Under Code of Civil Procedure § 1090, the court may order a trial of disputed fact questions; § 1090 does not compel a jury and is not automatic authority for any particular bench procedure — the form is for the court to determine.

**Why this outcome is more likely than outright denial:** The August 29 order's pathway is expressly framed as a trial-conditional ("If, at the trial on the merits, the Court were to credit..."). A court that takes its own order seriously will hesitate to deny on papers a showing its order said could succeed at trial. The alternative procedural order (product 11) is drafted narrowly — five questions, three witness categories, authentication protocol — precisely so factfinding does not become a mini-trial of the whole DUI.

### 1E. Outcome 4 — DENIAL (what it would take)

Denial becomes likely if any of the following occurs: (a) the native recording does not confirm the walk-and-turn transcript (the material-error theory collapses); (b) the court finds reasonable cause on the totality even with the walk-and-turn inference removed (HGN/Romberg/container/admitted driving/0.073 carry the day); (c) the court holds the denial letter's brevity is not an abuse of discretion where the agency record supplies reasoning; or (d) the motion overclaims — e.g., leads with unproved "three facial errors," a perjury theory, a search-as-false-imprisonment theory, or a "Verified Answer" sworn-testimony characterization — and loses credibility on the claims it can prove. The replacement brief is engineered to avoid (d) entirely, because (d) is the only denial ground fully within petitioner's control.

### 1F. Bottom line

Grant (direct) is possible but not probable on the current paper record. Partial grant (remand) is the sustainable center of the motion. Factfinding is the likely fallback if the court credits neither paper record. Denial is most likely to come from overclaiming or authentication failure, not from the absence of a legal theory. The replacement package is therefore structured to make remand grantable even if direct certification is denied, and to make factfinding productive rather than dilatory.

## 2. RANKED REVISION PRIORITIES — the ten changes most likely to materially improve the probability of legally sustainable relief

1. **Authenticate the native recording or withdraw every transcript-dependent claim.** The walk-and-turn theory — the motion's strongest fact — is currently carried by a petitioner-prepared transcript whose declared version postdates the denial. Custodian-certified native MVARS with hash/provenance, confirmation that the roadside-audio file actually contains roadside audio (notwithstanding "no signal" notations), and a playback protocol are the single highest-value proof items. Without them, §§ VI–VII of any brief are conditional. (See 07-exhibit-and-authentication-plan.md.)
2. **Correct the Answer characterization everywhere: "Answer to Verified Petition," counsel-signed — not "Verified Answer" / sworn testimony.** The 12-review established that the October 12, 2026 pleading is an Answer to the Verified Petition, signed by counsel. Its admissions are bounded party admissions, not invented sworn testimony. Every "verified admission" in prior drafts must be conformed. This correction protects the motion's credibility on the admissions it actually has (stationary vehicle, handcuffs/transport, urine cup, orientation, admitted driving).
3. **Reframe the Serrato defect as reviewable on the decision's face + the agency record — never as "three facial errors" or a sham.** A letter's omission of element analysis does not prove failure to consider the elements; an INA citation does not prove USCIS prediction; the letter asserts no prosecution prerequisite. The sustainable defect is narrower and stronger: a decision that identifies no element, no facts, and no lawful-authority analysis, resting on an agency account that is materially inaccurate in the walk-and-turn respect, cannot be sustained on judicial review without the court supplying the reasoning the decision omitted. (See 02-serrato-decision-error-memorandum.md.)
4. **Lead the brief with direct certification only as the petitioner's directed primary prayer — with remand fully briefed as the grantable tier.** See § 3. The brief must not present direct certification as established by the current record; it must present it as the finding set the court is asked to make, with remand as the disposition if the court makes only the defect findings. A brief that demands only direct certification risks total denial when the court balks at the CCSO leap.
5. **Confront Troppman, Schofield, and Espinoza head-on in the probable-cause section.** The working packet's Mercer-centered analysis is obsolete as the lead authority: Troppman (reasonable-cause belief suffices; no actual-driving proof required) and Schofield (§ 40300.5(e) dissipation sustains a warrantless arrest where reasonable cause exists) are the authorities CHP will cite, and Espinoza (odor + demeanor → reasonable cause affirmed) is the totality comparator. The brief must distinguish each on its proper ground (DMV context; reasonable-cause presupposition; substantial-evidence review) rather than ignore them. (See 03-authority-audit.md.)
6. **Decide § 40300.5 exception-by-exception on pinned facts — and concede what is weak.** (a) No crash — no support. (b) Obstruction — not established on the current record; pin vehicle position, lane clearance, hazard lights from Form 202/CAD/beat sheet before filing. (c) Non-apprehension — weak (stationary, officers on scene). (d) Self-injury/property — weak (asleep, not driving). (e) Dissipation — CHP's strongest; it presupposes reasonable cause and cannot bootstrap it. Also address § 40300.6 (liberal interpretation), which CHP will invoke for (b) and (e).
7. **Complete the RFP No. 8 track on its own rails — meet and confer now, compel alongside, never hold the writ hostage.** The decision file is the minimum missing record for any claim about what Serrato considered. Calendar the § 2031.310(c) 45-day window from the October 20 verified responses immediately (verify computation). The writ motion notes the track as pending without asking for any inference from nonproduction, and without conceding any privilege.
8. **Resolve the transcript-version problem before filing, not in a footnote.** Side-by-side identification of the April 9, 2026 version vs. the June 18, 2026-declared version (dates, hashes, textual differences). Until complete, every quotation is conditional and no quotation is offered as proof of what the April 9 package contained. Bracketed stage directions are labeled as petitioner's descriptions, never as officer statements.
9. **Cut the theories that lose credibility: perjury (§ 118.1) as a standalone theory, search-as-false-imprisonment, "passed all tests," zero-BAC narrative, "video proves no odor," "short denial = sham," and any adversarial case catalog.** Each cut is explained in 04-redline-plan.md with its disposition. The door-opening sequence survives only as a one-paragraph chronology note, because a search is not a § 236 element — but the stage analysis in the replacement MPA shows that conclusion rather than merely asserting it.
10. **Fix the filing mechanics that currently make the packet unfilable as a merits-ready submission:** briefing schedule (none represented as ordered; stipulation owed before the November 4 CMC); hearing/CMC confirmation against entered orders (January 8 scheduled vs. reserved; November 4, 4:00 p.m.); operative pleading conforming (Answer citations to the filed copy); declaration execution (blank date/place/signature — to be completed only by the petitioner, truthfully, fact by fact); proposed orders lodged separately with findings the court must actually make (never pre-filled findings); and KeyCite/Shepard's verification (Westlaw blocked October 24; public-source verification only to date). (See 11-filing-preparation-audit.md.)

## 3. PRAYER ORDER — direct-certification-primary at the petitioner's direction, with the 12-review's caution preserved

The 12-material-improvement-review kept remand-first and warned: "Do not treat a research agent's reordered prayer as a user decision." That caution was correct when the reorder came from a research agent. **The present reconstruction reorders the prayer because the petitioner has now expressly directed it:** the assignment for this reconstruction states as its primary objective a judicial determination requiring CHP to execute and deliver Form I-918 Supplement B because the statutory predicates leave no lawful alternative, with vacatur as the alternative.

This reconstruction therefore leads the replacement brief with direct certification as the primary prayer. It does so with three safeguards that honor the substance of the 12-review's caution:

- **The brief does not present direct certification as established.** It presents it as the finding set the court is asked to make (MPA § I, § XII.A), with each finding bracketed as a finding the court must actually make. If the court cannot make any one of them, the brief's own structure directs it to the remand tier rather than to denial.
- **The remand tier is fully briefed, not a fallback paragraph.** The vacatur judgment and writ (product 10B) are independently usable, with their own findings, directions, and return procedure. A court that grants only remand grants the motion in part; it does not deny it.
- **The grantability assessment above (§ 1B) candidly rates direct certification as the least likely tier on the current paper record.** The petitioner is entitled to direct the prayer order; he is also entitled to know what it costs. If, after reading this assessment, he prefers remand-first, the reordering is mechanical (Notice tiers, MPA §§ I/XII, Paper 6 findings order) and is noted as such in 12-before-and-after-change-report.md.

No research agent's prayer order is presented here as the petitioner's decision. The petitioner's direction is recorded above, and the assessment's disagreement with its prospects is recorded alongside it.
