# Estate Plan Notes — Client (not part of the premarital agreement)

**Client's intent:** At death, named individuals receive scheduled inheritances. **99% of everything else** goes to charities
serving survivors of domestic violence, battered women, and victims of sexual assault and violence. The remaining
1% of the residue: **[decide who gets it]**.

## Vehicle

- **Revocable living trust**, not only a will. A trust avoids public probate, which matters at large values, and lets a trustee
  manage crypto without court supervision.
- At significant wealth, consider a **private foundation** or **donor-advised fund** as the charitable beneficiary. The trust pours the
  99% into it, and a board or advisors make grants over time.
- Gifts to qualified charities at death are fully deductible for federal estate tax, so the 99% share largely escapes estate tax.

## Charitable beneficiary clause (draft)

> The Residue Share shall be distributed to one or more organizations described in Internal Revenue Code sections 170(c)
> and 2055(a) whose primary purpose is serving survivors of domestic violence, intimate partner violence, sexual assault,
> or sexual violence, including shelter, legal advocacy, counseling, and prevention, as selected by the Trustee [or the
> Distribution Committee]. If no such organization can be identified, the Trustee shall distribute to organizations whose
> purposes most nearly match these purposes.

Use a **class of charities**, not only named ones. A named charity may close or merge, and the class description keeps
the gift from failing. Specific preferred charities can be listed as a non-binding wish.

## Client's mother — for her alone

**Intent:** Whatever goes to the client's mother goes to her and no one else. She may use it as she sees fit. Her husband
(the client's stepfather, who abused the client) must receive nothing, directly or indirectly.

**Default law already helps:** An inheritance is the recipient's separate property (Fam. Code § 770, and the same in most
states), so her husband has no automatic claim on it. **But it leaks in three ways:**
1. She gives it to him or spends it on him.
2. She mixes it into a joint account, where it can become shared property.
3. **She dies holding it.** If she has no will, or a will that favors him, it passes to her husband. This is the biggest risk.

**Two structures (choose one):**

- [ ] **Option 1 — Outright gift.** Simple, and fully "as she sees fit." Gaps 1–3 stay open. Add a non-binding wish letter.
- [ ] **Option 2 — Lifetime trust for Mother (recommended).** Fits both halves of your intent:
  - She is the **sole beneficiary** during her life. The trustee pays her anything she asks for her own benefit, including
    housing, medical, travel, and gifts to people other than her husband. That keeps it effectively "as she sees fit."
  - **Independent trustee.** Not her husband, and not anyone he controls.
  - **Spendthrift clause.** She cannot sign it over to him, and his creditors cannot reach it.
  - **Remainder at her death skips her estate** and goes to [charity class / named people]. Her husband can't inherit it.
  - Distributions can be paid directly to vendors (landlord, doctor) when that keeps money away from him.

**Exclusion clause (both options):**

> [STEPFATHER'S FULL NAME] shall not receive any distribution, benefit, or interest under this instrument, directly or
> indirectly, and shall not serve as trustee, agent, guardian, executor, or in any fiduciary capacity. For all purposes of
> this instrument he shall be treated as having predeceased me. No distribution to my mother shall be made for his
> benefit or into any account he owns or controls.

**Safety note:** If your mother is under his control or at risk from him, outright money can make her a target. The trust
version protects her as well as the gift.

## Crypto — the most important practical item

Crypto that the trustee cannot access is lost permanently. The plan needs:
- Title exchange accounts and wallets to the trust where possible, or name the trust as beneficiary.
- A **digital asset access plan**: where the private keys and seed phrases are, held in a way no single person
  can steal from (for example, a multi-signature wallet or a sealed letter held by the trustee's attorney).
- An express grant of authority to the trustee over digital assets (Prob. Code § 870 et seq., California's
  Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act).
- A trustee or co-trustee who understands digital asset custody.

## Coordination with the prenup

- Article 11 waives the spouse's death rights in separate property, so the trust can leave her nothing beyond the community share she already owns.
  Decide whether she gets **any** gift, such as a right to live in Cat Tail Drive for a period, or a scheduled sum. A
  zero gift is enforceable, but a modest gift makes the waiver look fair.
- Her half of the Joint Account and any community property is hers and cannot be given to charity.
