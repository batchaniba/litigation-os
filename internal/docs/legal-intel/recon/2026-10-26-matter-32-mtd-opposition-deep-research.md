## 1. What the opposition must do

The organizing proposition:

> Defendants collapse four temporally distinct physical acts into one presumed-justified encounter, then ask the Court at Rule 12 to treat contested police-report observations as established facts and to treat prior venue dismissals as a merits adjudication. The Court should decide each intrusion on the facts alleged to have been known at that moment, refuse to adopt disputed report contents against specific FAC contradictions, and — if any count is thin — grant targeted leave to correct identified defects rather than dismiss with prejudice.

The opposition wins if the assigned judge writes down, in the alternative:

1. **Pleading rule.** A police report or DS-367 identified or attached to the FAC is not a legally operative instrument whose contested factual recitations automatically control. *Khoja*; *Grier* n.1. The Court may look at the document; it may not convert a challenged odor/slur/confusion/beverage characterization into an undisputed brick.
2. **Door entry is independent.** Opening the passenger door at 00:56:43 — **three seconds after** “Open the window,” and **before any welfare question** — is a search. *Ngumezi*. A knock is not the door. Plaintiff’s October 23, 2026 correction (Matter 20): he **woke to the knock** and did not get a chance to lower the window. Stop writing that he was still asleep at the opening. FAC ¶ 16’s “still asleep” line is a filed historical allegation that must be reconciled by amendment, not repeated. Emergency aid is judged at the moment of entry, under *Brigham City*’s objective serious-injury standard — not later, after odor, FSTs, or a urine identification. See § 19.0 and § 19.2.
3. **Search and arrest are later questions.** Apparent “booze” through a window and a sleeping freeway driver may support inquiry. They do not automatically authorize interior seizure of the cup, a continuing search after Melano said “this is piss,” or a custodial arrest whose command comes **one second** after a voluntary PAS refusal (cuffs at eighteen).
4. **Warrant theory uses the actual affidavit.** Judicial deception is tested against Meza’s warrant affidavit to Judge Amy Sekany (WAR913372, ~02:34), not against a misread CHP 202 checkbox. *Chism* / *Franks* require deliberate or reckless falsity **and** materiality after correction. If materiality cannot be pleaded honestly, seek leave or narrow; do not invent a remainder that deletes unaffected facts.
5. **QI is conduct-specific and pleading-constrained.** *Keates*. A categorical “asleep on a freeway + some tests = immunity” paragraph does not decide the door-opening claim, the warrant claim, or any count whose predicate facts are specifically disputed.
6. **Futility fails.** Kim’s April 20, **2026** recommendation (the motion says 2025) is adverse reasoning on a different complaint. E.D. Cal. and Alameda ended by voluntary dismissal. Serrato is a different defendant and theory. Concrete amendments are identified. *Lopez v. Smith*.
7. **Rule 12(f) is the wrong tool for damages.** *Whittlestone*. Concede pro se § 1988 fees for Plaintiff’s own time (*Kay*). Keep costs. Preserve punitive only on deliberate/reckless facts, and admit the earlier without-prejudice withdrawal.

That is the whole brief. Everything else is support or noise.

**Tone for the filed paper.** Dense, record-tied, cite-clean. Lead with the Court’s job at Rule 12, then the timed sequence. One dry sentence on the Kim date error if it is still in the motion. No “fourth lawsuit” morality play. No video novel. No accusation that DAG Li fabricated law. No GitHub or document-store URL.

## 6. Threshold: how the Court must read the FAC

### 6.1 What we want the Court to say

The FAC specifically alleges that identified report statements are false and points to a contemporaneous recording for sequence, speech, orientation, and the cup identification. At Rule 12 the Court accepts those well-pleaded factual allegations. It does not try the video. It does not treat the officers’ report as a verdict.

### 6.2 Authorities (primary-text checked)

- ***Khoja v. Orexigen Therapeutics, Inc.***, 899 F.3d 988, 998–1003, 1014 (9th Cir. 2018). Incorporation-by-reference is a doctrine to prevent a plaintiff from omitting documents that defeat the complaint. It is **improper to assume the truth of an incorporated document if that truth is disputed and offered only to resolve a factual dispute against well-pled allegations.** Judicial notice does not cover disputed facts inside a noticed document. [Opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf).
- ***Grier v. Finjan Holdings, Inc.***, No. 21-16702, slip op. at 2 n.1 (9th Cir. Jan. 20, 2023) (mem.). If a complaint’s **specific facts** contradict specific facts in a **non-legally-operative** attached or incorporated document, the conflict is resolved for the plaintiff at pleading. A legally operative instrument (contract, note) still controls a mischaracterization of *its own terms*. [Opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/01/20/21-16702.pdf).
- A police report / DS-367 / CHP 202 is not a legally operative instrument. The warrant is closer to operative for what it **says**, not for whether its factual recitations are true.

### 6.3 Limits — do not overclaim

- *Grier* affirmed dismissal. Footnote 1 is the tool, not a rescue for conclusory “fabricated” labels.
- *Khoja* does not say “ignore all exhibits.” If ECF 5 actually attaches the CHP 202, the Court can read the **breath-odor** field and hold Plaintiff to that label. That is why we correct it.
- *Iqbal* still strips true legal conclusions. “No probable cause” is a conclusion. “They said ‘I think it’s booze,’ knocked, told me to open the window, and opened the door three seconds later before I could lower it, then asked if I was okay” is a fact. Do not use the old “while I was asleep” example.

### 6.4 Kim’s exhibit move, distinguished

Kim’s April 20, 2026 recommendation treated attached report observations as supporting PC and recommended dismissal without leave. Public text: [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10913465/brendan-ngehsi-newanforbi-v-state-of-california-et-al/). That recommendation:

- addressed a **different complaint** (State / CHP / official-capacity / *Monell* still in the case; three counts, not six);
- used the then-cited exhibit-over-allegation approach that *Khoja* / *Grier* now let us cabin;
- did not have the produced warrant/affidavit or the breath-odor field correction;
- was followed by objections (April 21, 2026) and then a **voluntary dismissal**, not a district-judge adoption we have verified.

**Do not say the recommendation was vacated, overruled, or “void for wrong venue.”** Say: it is adverse reasoning on a different pleading; the futility question is whether **this** FAC, or a proposed amendment, states a claim.

## 7. Count-by-count architecture

### IV. Warrantless entry — lead merits count

**Ask:** Deny dismissal as to Melano; keep Meza only on a pleaded integral-participant / failure-to-intervene theory if the FAC supports it (¶ 58 is thin — consider amending).

**Rule.** Opening a vehicle door and leaning into the interior is a Fourth Amendment search. Officers with reasonable suspicion for a stop, but without probable cause or another particularized justification (including a reasonable belief the driver is dangerous), may not open the door and lean inside. *United States v. Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d 1285, 1288–90 (9th Cir. 2020) — criminal suppression; bright-line search holding; **no emergency-aid holding**. [Opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf).

**Emergency aid, correctly stated.** Police may enter without a warrant when they have an **objectively reasonable basis to believe an occupant is seriously injured or imminently threatened with serious injury**. *Brigham City v. Stuart*, 547 U.S. 398, 403–04 (2006). Subjective motive does not control. *Michigan v. Fisher*, 558 U.S. 45 (2009) (per curiam), is the “objectively reasonable basis,” not certainty, refinement — **verify pin before filing**.

**What *Caniglia* is and is not.** *Caniglia v. Strom*, 593 U.S. 194 (2021), holds that *Cady* community caretaking does **not** authorize a warrantless home entry/seizure. It expressly recognizes ordinary aid to motorists as a caretaking *task*. Use it to stop a free-floating “community caretaking = any door we want” argument. Do **not** cite it as if it decided a freeway-shoulder vehicle case against the officers.

**Their *Mincey* cite.** *Mincey v. Arizona*, 437 U.S. 385 (1978), is a homicide-scene home warrant case. It is the ancestor of emergency aid, not the vehicle standard. Make the Court use *Brigham City*.

**Their VC 21718 move.** Even if stopping on a freeway shoulder can be a Vehicle Code problem, *Devenpeck* does not turn a suspected parking/stopping offense into interior-search authority. The FAC alleges the pre-entry words were rental / “wasn’t here earlier” / “I think it’s booze” — investigative, not “this person is dying.”

**Video gate / sleep correction.** Exhibit A: knock 00:56:07–00:56:40 → “Open the window” 00:56:40 → door 00:56:43 → “Hello” / “Uh-huh” 00:56:46 → “Are you okay?” 00:56:47. Plaintiff’s October 23, 2026 Matter 20 correction: he **awoke to the knock** and the door opened before he could lower the window. FAC ¶ 16’s “still asleep” is a filed historical line, not the fact we will swear to. Do not repeat it. Do not silently rewrite the FAC PDF. Put the wake / three-second / no-chance-to-comply sequence in the amendment proffer. ### I. Judicial deception / blood warrant

**Ask:** Deny if the FAC’s four contradictions plus omissions, applied to the **actual affidavit**, plausibly allege reckless falsity and materiality. In the alternative, grant leave to replead the affidavit table. Do not let them dismiss because the FAC said “DS-367 and arrest report” instead of “Sekany affidavit.”

**Rule (FAC already cites it).** An officer violates the Fourth Amendment by a warrant affidavit with deliberate or reckless false statements or material omissions if the affidavit, stripped of the falsehoods and supplemented with the omitted facts, would not support probable cause. *Franks v. Delaware*, 438 U.S. 154, 155–56 (1978); *Chism v. Washington State*, 661 F.3d 380, 386–89 (9th Cir. 2011). *Chism* is a **summary-judgment** “substantial showing” case. At Rule 12, plead particular falsity, a nonconclusory recklessness basis (personal knowledge + contemporaneous recording + urine ID known to the affiant), and a remainder theory. [Opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/08/25/10-35085.pdf).

*Galbraith v. County of Santa Clara*, 307 F.3d 1119, 1126 (9th Cir. 2002), and *Hervey v. Estes*, 65 F.3d 784, 789 (9th Cir. 1995): **existence trusted; re-read pins before filing.** They are the motion’s own cases. Use them.

**Drafting method — two columns, not a narrative:**

| Affidavit statement | Proposed treatment | Remainder risk |
|---|---|---|
| Asleep on shoulder; 21+ band; party; drove there; alone | Keep | High — these stay |
| “Parking lot” / confused | Video comparison; words vs. officer inference | Transcript itself has groggy answers |
| Vehicle odor / breath odor | Allege falsity with sequence + narration contrast; **not** “camera can’t smell therefore false” | Highest remainder risk if the Court keeps even one odor statement |
| Tests not as instructed | Identify each test vs. video coverage | FAC is thinner here; they noticed that |
| Chemical-test refusal | Keep as a fact of refusal; dispute its PC weight | Stays |
| Denial of drinking | Already in affidavit | Cannot be an omission |

| Medical / sleep-deprivation explanation | Omission only if disclosed **and** known then | Open fact |
| Later tox / no charge | **Do not add** | Unknown at 02:34 |

### VI. Retaliation — candid

**The problem.** The FAC’s protected-activity theory is the statutory right to refuse a PAS. That is a **statutory investigative-tool right**, not obviously First Amendment speech. *Camara* is a Fourth Amendment consent-search case. *Ballentine v. Tucker*, 28 F.4th 54, 62 (9th Cir. 2022), is real Ninth Circuit retaliation law — **verify it is not being asked to do PAS-refusal work it did not do.** *Ford v. City of Yakima*, 706 F.3d 1188 (9th Cir. 2013), is the motion’s framework and is pre-*Nieves*; do not pretend *Nieves* did not happen.

***Nieves v. Bartlett***, 587 U.S. 391, 398–407 (2019). Generally, PC defeats a retaliatory-arrest claim. ## 8. Qualified immunity — write it as four mini-analyses

Their QI section decides the case at a high level of generality (“asleep on a freeway, some tests, no alcohol found in the cup”). *Wesby* and *al-Kidd* require the opposite: particularized, pre-incident law.

**Rule 12 posture.** *Keates v. Koile*, 883 F.3d 1228, 1234–35 (9th Cir. 2018): take well-pleaded facts as true; ask whether those facts plausibly allege a violation of clearly established law in the specific context. If the complaint contains even one harmful act that would violate a clearly established right, the plaintiff goes forward. A Rule 12 QI denial says little about summary judgment. [Opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/03/06/16-16568.pdf).

| Count | Violation theory (accepted facts) | Clearly-established analogue to find or concede is missing | Rule 12 ask |

Do not claim it clearly establishes a three-second wait.
