                                            FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

10. On November 1, 2024, at approximately 00:54 AM, Plaintiff was lawfully parked on 4a dirt

shoulder off Interstate 580 East in Livermore, California, resting inside a rental Tesla Model Y.

Plaintiff had chosen to pull over out of an abundance of caution after feeling fatigued following a

long night of social activity, consistent with his professional training as a commercial truck driver

where safety and rest are paramount. At the time, Plaintiff was not engaged in any unlawful conduct

and posed no threat to public safety.

11, California Highway Patrol officers, including Defendant Santiago Meza-Gonzalez and

Defendant Officer Melano, approached Plaintiff's vehicle under the pretext of a welfare check. Upon

approaching the vehicle, one officer remarked, "It's a rental," and shortly thereafter, upon: observing

a upon observing a cup in the center console, stated, "I think it's booze," to which the other officer

replied, "Yeah?" These statements marked a shift from welfare concern to investigative suspicion of

DUI. The officers then turned their flashlights into the windows and began peering into the vehicle

 in an investigatory manner.


 12. Without Plaintiff's consent or a warrant, officers peered into the vehicle and speculated that a

 cup inside might contain "booze."


 13. Officers proceeded to open Plaintiff's passenger door without consent, probable cause, or

 exigent circumstances.

 14. Plaintiff was ordered out of his vehicle, despite having remained calm, compliant, and

 cooperative throughout the encounter. Plaintiff made no threats, no sudden movements, and posed

no apparent risk to officer safety. He was questioned extensively and subjected to field sdbriety

tests, despite exhibiting no signs of criminal activity or intoxication.

 15. Defendant Officer Melano conducted an intrusive search of Plaintiff's vehicle without consent

or probable cause. The search included reaching into the cupholders, examining the center console

area, and scanning around the footwells and floorboards of the vehicle. At no point did Defendant

Melano have any specific or articulable facts justifying the belief that Plaintiff's vehicle cpntained

contraband or evidence of a crime.

16. No evidence of intoxication or illegal activity was found.


17. Plaintiff was detained, placed in handcuffs, and transported for chemical testing under suspicion

of DUI, without sufficient probable cause.

18. Despite the arrest report reflecting that charges were sought, the California Highway Patrol later

issued correspondence characterizing the encounter as a mere "detention" because no charges were

filed by the District Attorney. Plaintiff asserts that this characterization is factually and legally

incorrect. He was subjected to handcuffing, placed in the back of a patrol vehicle, transported under

guard, and read chemical test advisements all indicia of a full custodial arrest within the meaning

of *Dunaway v. New York*, 442 U.S. 200 (1979).

19. As a result of Defendants' conduct, Plaintiff suffered emotional distress, humiliation, loss of

liberty, reputational harm, and economic injury. In particular, the administrative chemical refusal
