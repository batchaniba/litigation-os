# MODULE 2 — Maryland Address Confidentiality Program (ACP)

## 1. Authorizing statute — verified citation

**State Government Article, Title 7, Subtitle 3, §§ 7-301 through 7-313** ("Address Confidentiality Program"). **Administering agency: the Secretary of State** (Office of the Secretary of State), per § 7-303: "The Secretary of State **shall establish and administer** an Address Confidentiality Program for survivors of threatened, attempted, or actual domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, harassment, or human trafficking."

**Legislative history / recodification (verified from primary sources):**
- Original program: 2006 SB 25, Chapter 375 — "Requiring the Secretary of State to establish the Address Confidentiality Program for victims of domestic violence" (https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2006rs/billfile/sb0025.htm), codified as **Family Law §§ 4-519–4-531**.
- The Family Law provisions were **recodified into State Government Title 7, Subtitle 3**.
- **2026 consolidation: HB 265 / SB 91** (effective **June 1, 2026**) merged the former domestic-violence ACP and the human-trafficking ACP into a single program under § 7-303, expressly covering "survivors of threatened, attempted, or actual domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, harassment, or human trafficking" (SB 91 Third Reader: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/bills/sb/sb0091T.pdf).
- Note: older cross-references still exist in the code (e.g., Election Law § 1-101(b-1) still cites "Title 4, Subtitle 5, Part IV of the Family Law Article") — counsel should verify whether a given cross-reference has been updated. The program's public name is the **"Maryland Safe at Home Address Confidentiality Program,"** administered by the Office of the Secretary of State.

## 2. Eligibility (SG § 7-301 definitions; § 7-304)

**Who may apply** — § 7-304(a): (1) the individual on their own behalf; (2) a parent or guardian acting for a minor residing with them; (3) a guardian acting for a disabled person; (4) an individual residing in the same household as an eligible applicant or participant.

**Substantive standard** — the application (form required by the Secretary, § 7-304(b)) must contain:
- A statement that the applicant is a survivor of **threatened, attempted, or actual domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, harassment, or human trafficking** AND fears for their (or their child's) safety — § 7-304(b)(1);
- **Evidence of eligibility**, in any one of four forms — § 7-304(b)(2): (i) certified law enforcement, court, or other federal/State agency records; (ii) documentation from a DV/sexual assault/human-trafficking prevention or assistance program; (iii) documentation from a religious, medical, or other professional from whom the applicant sought assistance/treatment; **or (iv) the applicant's own statement of subjective fear** (i.e., a self-statement suffices — no police report required);
- A statement that disclosure of the actual address would endanger safety — (b)(3);
- A knowing/voluntary designation of the Secretary of State as **agent for service of process and receipt of first-class, certified, or registered mail** — (b)(4);
- Contact mailing address/phone for the Secretary — (b)(5); the actual address to be shielded — (b)(6);
- **Disclosure of any existing court order or pending court action** involving divorce, child support, custody, or visitation, and the issuing court — (b)(7);
- Sworn truth statement — (b)(8); signature and date — (b)(9);
- **Voluntary release and waiver of future claims against the State** arising from participation (except gross negligence) — (b)(10).

**Definitions** — § 7-301: "domestic violence" = "abuse" under FL § 4-501(b); "harassment" = Criminal Law § 3-803; "sexual assault" = enumerated Criminal Law sexual offenses; "stalking" = Criminal Law § 3-802; "survivor of human trafficking" per the federal force/fraud/coercion definition; "actual address" = residential, school, or work address.

**Term:** participation is for **4 years** from filing unless canceled/withdrawn (§ 7-304(c)(1)(ii)); withdrawal by signed request (§ 7-304(c)(2)); must **reapply** to continue.

## 3. Application process (official SOS sources)

- Applications go through **trained Application Assistants** — victim-services professionals at domestic-violence / human-trafficking program offices statewide (per the SOS "Safe at Home" brochure, https://sos.maryland.gov/Documents/SafeAtHomeBrochure.pdf). The Assistant helps determine whether ACP fits the safety plan, explains services and responsibilities, and assists with the application.
- To reach an Application Assistant: SOS ACP office — **410-260-3875**; brochure lists **1-800-633-9657 ext. 3875**; mailing address P.O. Box 2995, Annapolis, MD 21404-2995.
- The Secretary reviews the application and release; if properly completed and accurate, **designates the applicant as a participant** — § 7-304(c)(1)(i). The participant receives an ACP authorization card (name, substitute address, individual ACP number).

## 4. What the substitute address covers

- The participant **may request any person or State or local agency to use the substitute address**; once requested, the person/agency **shall** use it — § 7-308(a).
- **Acceptance is mandatory as presented:** "When a Program participant presents the address designated by the Secretary of State to any person, that address must be accepted as the address of the Program participant" — § 7-308(b)(1)(i). A person may not require an address that could physically locate the participant as a condition of a service/benefit, unless the service would be impossible without the physical location — § 7-308(b)(1)(ii). (Banks/financial institutions may require the request in writing on the Secretary's form — § 7-308(b)(2).)
- **Mail forwarding:** free confidential mail forwarding for **first-class mail, registered/certified mail, and legal papers** (SOS brochure; § 7-304(b)(4)). The participant may use the substitute address as a return address.
- **Service of process:** the Secretary of State is the participant's designated agent for service of process and receipt of mail; the ACP's receipt = the participant's receipt — the participant is legally responsible for obligations in documents the ACP receives (§ 7-304(b)(4)).
- **Real property:** a participant acquiring an ownership interest while in the program may request **shielding of real property records** under Real Property Title 3, Subtitle 1, Part II — § 7-308(c).
- **Voter registration:** the substitute address may **not** be used for voter registration; local boards must use the actual address for election purposes but may not disclose it publicly except to law enforcement or by court order — § 7-309, as amended by 2026 SB 91/HB 265 (effective June 1, 2026).
- **Schools:** public schools must accept the ACP authorization card/substitute address; the student need not disclose the actual address (SOS school guidance).
- **Confidentiality of the actual address:** a participant's actual address/telephone number held by the Secretary or a State/local agency **is not a public record** under the Public Information Act (GP § 4-101) — § 7-310(a). The Secretary may disclose only: to a law enforcement agency for law-enforcement purposes, as directed by court order, or to a State/local agency to verify participation — § 7-310(b). If the participant is subject to a court/administrative order, involved in a court/administrative action, or a party/witness in a civil/criminal proceeding, the Secretary **must notify the court** of participation and the substitute address — § 7-310(c).

## 5. Limits — stated honestly

1. **Does not erase existing public records.** The SOS brochure states plainly: "**The ACP cannot redact a public address**" — addresses already in public records (prior deeds, court filings, voter rolls, business filings) are not clawed back. It protects records **going forward only**.
2. **Not a witness-protection program; not protection from legal obligations.** Service of process still reaches the participant through the Secretary.
3. **Private companies are a practical gap.** The SOS brochure says private companies (stores, banks, phone/insurance companies) "do not have to accept the substitute address" — note the tension with § 7-308(b)(1)(i) ("any person … must be accepted"), which on its face applies to "any person." Counsel should not over-promise private-sector compliance.
4. **Mail limits:** does **not** forward **packages or magazines** — first-class mail and legal papers only; mail is delayed by the forwarding process.
5. **Effectiveness is conditional:** the program "is only effective if the victim has recently moved and the abuser does not know the victim's new address"; the participant must not file a USPS change-of-address; must notify ACP of moves/name/phone changes; the ACP is "only one part of a victim's overall safety plan" and "by itself … cannot keep a person safe."
6. **Government agencies can get a waiver:** a State/local agency with a bona fide statutory or administrative requirement for the actual address may apply to the Secretary for a **waiver** — § 7-308(d).
7. **Court-involvement carve-outs:** existing divorce/custody/support orders must be disclosed on the application (§ 7-304(b)(7)), and the Secretary must notify courts of participation in pending actions (§ 7-310(c)) — so in contested family-law proceedings the shield is porous by design.
8. **Term-limited:** 4 years, then reapplication required.
9. Enrollment entails a **waiver of future claims against the State** except for gross negligence (§ 7-304(b)(10)).

## Cross-module practice notes for Maryland counsel
- **Sequencing:** If the aunt pursues a protective order (separate module), the court/agency records generated can double as § 7-304(b)(2)(i) evidence for an ACP application — but ACP enrollment and any U-visa certification request are independent tracks with different decision-makers (Secretary of State vs. law-enforcement certifying official vs. USCIS).
- **Confidentiality asymmetry:** ACP shields the *address* from public records; VAWA confidentiality (8 U.S.C. § 1367) shields *immigration* information; Maryland's § 11-931 process itself has no confidentiality shield comparable to VAWA's, and § 7-310(c) *requires* court notification in pending actions. Counsel should map who learns what before any step is taken.
- **The § 7-304(b)(7) court-order disclosure** means ACP is not a tool for evading existing custody/support/divorce orders.
