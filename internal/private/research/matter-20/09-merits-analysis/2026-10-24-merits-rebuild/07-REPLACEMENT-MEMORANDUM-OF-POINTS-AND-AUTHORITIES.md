## I. INTRODUCTION: THE QUALIFYING-ACTIVITY DETERMINATION IS THE DECISION UNDER REVIEW

On April 9, 2026, Petitioner presented CHP an application for Form I-918 Supplement B alleging qualifying criminal restraint during a November 1, 2024 roadside encounter, with a contemporaneous-record-based submission. CHP acknowledged review on April 10; Captain Serrato denied on April 20, determining that the incident involved neither qualifying activity nor substantially similar qualifying activity under Penal Code section 679.10. (Verified Pet., Exs. 1–3; Respondents' Verified Answer (“Answer”), ¶49.) **That statutory determination—not a free-standing civil-rights damages claim—is the subject of the surviving writ cause.**

The Legislature expressly names false imprisonment and unlawful criminal restraint as qualifying activity; it does not require a prosecution or conviction. (Pen. Code, §679.10(c)(18)–(19), (g)(1), (k)(1).) The evidence establishes involuntary physical custody and an identifiable threshold question about the lawful basis for the warrantless arrest. It also identifies a specific, testable conflict between CHP's report attributing the nonperformance of a walk-and-turn exercise to Petitioner and an account that the officer declined to administer it because of unsafe terrain. Resolving that conflict, along with the statutory arrest-authority requirements, materially informs whether CHP's no-qualifying-activity determination may stand.

Judge Whitman held that if a merits factfinder credits the allegations that officers lacked grounds for impairment and misrepresented them in their report, lack of probable cause **could** support an abuse-of-discretion finding concerning certification. (Order Sustaining Demurrer in Part, Aug. 29, 2026 (“Demurrer Order”), pp.3–4.) The ruling left proof for a merits determination. Petitioner therefore requests certification if all predicates are found and discretion admits only that outcome; otherwise a judgment correcting an established material abuse through vacatur and defined reconsideration; if factual resolution is needed, a focused hearing or trial.

## II. CHP RECEIVED THE REQUEST AND ISSUED A SUBSTANTIVE QUALIFYING-ACTIVITY REJECTION

CHP admits it is a certifying entity and Captain Serrato was an authorized official, and that it received the April 9 materials, which included the form, supporting memorandum, transcript, arrest materials and detention certificate. (Answer ¶¶13–16, 49, 58–60; Verified Pet., Exs. 1–3.) Lieutenant Paul acknowledged review on April 10. Serrato's April 20 letter states that the incident does not meet California or federal qualifying-crime standards and that no substantially similar activity was identified. (Verified Pet., Ex.1; Demurrer Order, pp.1–2.)

Serrato **did not identify failure of helpfulness**, missing criminal charges, or delay as grounds. Petitioner contests the legality and material evidentiary basis of the no-qualifying-activity finding itself. The motion does not rest on an inference that a brief response proves no genuine review; the separate explanation-based claim was dismissed. (Demurrer Order, pp.4–5.) The inquiry remains whether CHP could lawfully reject the actual qualifying-crime allegation on facts the Court finds proved.

## III. THE STATUTE IMPOSES A CONDITIONAL DUTY, AND TRADITIONAL MANDATE ENFORCES ITS LIMITS

Section 679.10(c) specifically includes **unlawful criminal restraint** and **false imprisonment**. Section 679.10(d) includes substantially similar offenses by nature and elements. When a direct victim of qualifying criminal activity has been helpful, is being helpful or is likely to be helpful to its detection, investigation or prosecution, subdivision (g)(1) says a certifying official **shall certify**. (Id., (o)(2) [direct victim].) Subdivision (h) presumes helpfulness where the victim has not refused or failed to furnish reasonably requested information and assistance. Subdivision (k)(1) makes an open investigation, charges, apprehension, prosecution and conviction unnecessary prerequisites; (k)(3) forbids listed unrelated criteria. The conditional command does not make any submitted claim of a crime automatically conclusive.

Code of Civil Procedure sections 1085–1086 authorize traditional mandate to enforce a specially enjoined public duty and remedy material abuses of official discretion. *Saleeby v. State Bar* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547, 560–562, recognizes correction of an administrative abuse or action beyond lawful power. *California Correctional Supervisors Organization, Inc. v. Department of Corrections* (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827, acknowledges authority to compel the single reasonable exercise of discretion; it does not remove the requirement to establish the predicates. The court may require the statutory decision to be made **on legally correct facts and law**, but should not substitute an outcome while genuine permissible discretion remains.

## IV. INTENTIONAL RESTRAINT IS SUPPORTED; THE CONTROLLING QUESTION IS WHETHER THE CUSTODY LACKED LAWFUL AUTHORITY

Section 236 defines false imprisonment as unlawful interference with personal liberty. The elements include intentional restraint, confinement or detention causing a person to remain or go somewhere against that person's will; when lawful authority is contested, the factfinder must resolve unlawfulness. (Pen. Code, §236; Judicial Council CALCRIM No.1242; *People v. Agnew* (1940) 16 Cal.2d 655, 659–660.) Section 146(a) separately criminalizes, under color of legal authority, arresting or detaining a person against their will **without lawful authority**. Whether its elements and nature are substantially similar for §679.10(d) is an independent legal inquiry, not an automatic assumption.

Here CHP admits an encounter with Petitioner at his stationary Tesla on I-580, an ensuing investigation, physical custody, transport and Santa Rita Jail detention from November 1 to November 5, 2024. (Answer ¶¶34–42, 49; CHP Form 202, AGO 000029–000033.) The physical restraint and nonconsent do not alone prove a qualifying crime; the dispositive element is **absence of legal authority for the compulsory custody**.

The initial approach was identified as a welfare check; the asserted investigative authority must be measured at each material point of escalation, culminating in handcuffing at approximately 01:11–01:12 on the provided timestamped transcript and incident log. (Answer ¶34; Verified Pet., Ex.5, printed pp.2–11; incident log AGO 000022–000024.) The later blood warrant and laboratory test are distinct events, not retroactive facts available to the arresting officers at the time handcuffs were applied.

## V. WARRANTLESS DUI ARREST AUTHORITY REQUIRES THE FACTS OF A STATUTORY EXCEPTION, NOT MERELY AN OFFENSE LABEL

The officers did not observe Petitioner driving; CHP's Answer admits the car was stationary when they arrived, and the DS-367 marks earlier driving as admitted rather than observed. (Answer ¶34; DS-367 in CHP production, AGO 000029–000053.) The ordinary in-presence misdemeanor arrest question must therefore be evaluated under the actual authorization statute, **Vehicle Code section 40300.5**. It permits warrantless arrest on reasonable cause for prior DUI **when** one of the legislatively specified circumstances also exists: involvement in a crash; observation in or about a vehicle **obstructing a roadway**; inability to apprehend absent immediate arrest; immediate personal-injury/property-risk; or imminent destruction/concealment of evidence. (Veh. Code, §40300.5(a)–(e).)

A stationary vehicle on a freeway shoulder does not, by the phrase alone, prove that it obstructed the roadway within subdivision (b). Nor does the mere statement that the driver previously drove establish that the vehicle was obstructing, that immediate apprehension otherwise failed, or that the distinct immediate-risk or evidence-destruction conditions existed. These are **statutory predicates**, each requiring facts rather than a presumption from the DUI label. Petitioner asks the Court to decide the predicates actually supported by admissible evidence and to determine, separately, whether the contemporaneous facts provided reasonable cause for a §23152 offense. *Mercer v. Department of Motor Vehicles* (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753, 768–769, distinguishes physical control from actual volitional driving; its narrow license-suspension holding does not independently resolve section 40300.5.

This is a critical gateway to the asserted §236 restraint. Even if an initial welfare check or investigative detention was proper, it does not follow that later custodial arrest met every statute authorizing warrantless misdemeanor arrest. If the Court finds none of the necessary §40300.5 conditions, no other asserted lawful arrest authority, and lacks sufficient criminal cause after correcting material factual errors, it may determine whether the resulting compulsory custody constitutes qualifying criminal false imprisonment.

## VI. THE SOURCE RECORD PERMITS A CONCRETE CORRECTION TO CHP'S WALK-AND-TURN ACCOUNT

CHP Form 202 states: **“Walk and Turn: Newanforbi chose not to perform this test and related it was difficult.”** The same report discusses “poor performance on the FST's.” (AGO 000031–000032.) The version of the transcript appended to the original petition records Petitioner willing to attempt that separate exercise at approximately **01:10:23** and Officer Melano advising against attempting it on the uneven freeway shoulder; the officers did not administer it. (Verified Pet., Ex.5, printed p.9, approximately 01:10:14–01:10:30.) This exchange must not be confused with an earlier discussion of one-leg-stand.

Upon authentication and consideration of the original recording, the factfinder can determine whether the written report improperly attributes an officer's safety decision to the detained driver's unwillingness. **If so, the canceled exercise cannot count as a refused or failed walk-and-turn** in the lawful-custody calculus. This finding is modest but important: it removes a specific claimed factual contribution to custody and tests whether the remaining contemporaneous observations are sufficient. It does not require a criminal finding of intentional falsification from the discrepancy alone.

This distinction also matters to the statute's administration. The April application asserted criminal restraint with a timestamped transcript; if the Court finds that a materially inaccurate report narrative was used to reject that claim, the no-qualifying-activity decision cannot retain that premise on remand. If the Court finds, after properly resolving the facts, that custody lacked lawful authority and qualifying victimhood is established, the direct-certification claim follows from those broader findings—not merely from correcting one field-test entry.

## VII. THE VOLUNTARY PAS REFUSAL AND LATER BLOOD TEST DO NOT REPLACE THE PREARREST EVIDENCE

The petition's transcript records that officers told Petitioner the roadside preliminary alcohol screening (“PAS”) test was optional, that he declined at **01:11:15**, and that handcuffing followed immediately. (Verified Pet., Ex.5, printed pp.9–10; Veh. Code, §23612(i).) The post-arrest discussion of a chemical breath/blood test appears later, at approximately **01:15:54**. The distinct tests and timing should not be conflated.

The First District held that admitting voluntary PAS refusal at a DUI **trial** as consciousness-of-guilt evidence was error in *People v. Jackson* (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461, 1465–1470. That holding protects a legislatively recognized option; it does not purport to decide every prearrest reasonable-cause question. Here the test refusal's proximity to arrest makes it appropriate to scrutinize the independent contemporaneous lawful grounds, rather than treat exercise of the optional test choice as a substitute for criminal evidence.

The October CHP production reports a blood ethanol concentration of **0.073 ± 0.004 g% at approximately 02:38**—after arrest and after a magistrate issued a search warrant for blood evidence. (AGO 000033–000034, 000048–000049.) That later figure is not an evidence-based earlier BAC estimate on this record and is not a resolution of whether the officers had lawful arrest grounds beforehand. Nor does the absence of a later DUI charge conclusively disprove earlier reasonable cause. (Answer ¶42.)

The officers' identification of a particular cup as urine is admitted. So is the discovery of a **separate empty alcohol beverage can**. (Answer ¶¶36–37, 44–47.) CHP admits neither officer **said aloud** on the recording that alcohol odor existed or that Petitioner's speech was slurred; it does not admit no officer perceived an odor or that all other impairment signs were absent. (Ibid.) These precise admissions, properly considered with the original recording and testimony, narrow but do not eliminate the factual inquiry.

## VIII. THE APRIL 20 REJECTION MUST BE EVALUATED AGAINST THE QUALIFYING-OFFENSE FACTS, NOT A PRESUMED PROHIBITED MOTIVE

The decision announced a conclusion of no qualifying or similar crime. (Verified Pet., Ex.1.) The precise legal question is whether that **conclusion** remains tenable after the Court applies the actual offense elements, the lawful-arrest requirements, and facts it finds admissibly proved.

A verified Answer can narrow what occurred, and a produced 2024 recording may expose error in the incident account. Neither, merely by appearing in October 2026 discovery, establishes what Serrato privately reviewed in April 2026. CHP's response to Request for Production No.8 (RFP Response Set One) and its privilege log should be evaluated separately for the contemporaneous review file; **the existence of a log item or a May-dated memo is not proof of what Serrato considered before April 20**.

Petitioner seeks the strongest finding the evidence supports. **If** the Court determines that corrected material facts render the arrest unlawful and establish qualifying criminal restraint, an agency's opposite qualifying-activity finding may be set aside as outside lawful discretion. **Alternatively, if** the Court identifies a concrete statutory misunderstanding or reliance on a materially erroneous factual premise that affected the denial, but finds eligibility not conclusively proved, the lawful remedy is vacatur with directions against repeating that error. The briefing does not depend on the letter's brevity, speculate about hidden memoranda, or relitigate the dismissed separate reason-giving claim.

## IX. HELPFULNESS IS AN INDEPENDENT CONDITIONAL PREDICATE

Petitioner submitted a complaint concerning officers' conduct, supplied incident-specific information and his transcript with the certification request, reported the matter through CHP and POST channels, and remains willing to cooperate truthfully concerning the alleged officer crime. (Verified Pet., Exs.2–3 and appended POST documents; Answer ¶¶49, 54, 57–60; Petitioner's properly executed declaration.) Under Penal Code section 679.10(h), there is a rebuttable presumption of helpfulness unless the victim refused or failed to provide reasonably requested information or assistance. The Answer disputes the allegation that an investigating entity requested information from Petitioner; that response is not proof he declined a qualifying request.

The relevant assistance concerns the crime alleged **against Petitioner**, not the separate DUI investigation of Petitioner. Section 679.10(k)(3)(H) prohibits withholding certification based on cooperation or refusal to cooperate in a separate case. The April denial did not identify unhelpfulness; the proof is presented to establish a distinct statutory condition **if qualifying victimhood is found**, not to convert helping authorities into independent grounds for relief.

## X. THE COURT CAN GRANT RELIEF PROPORTIONATE TO THE FINDINGS IT ACTUALLY MAKES

**Direct certification:** Upon findings that there was criminally unlawful restraint, Petitioner directly suffered the qualifying activity, he satisfied §679.10(g)–(h), and **no reasonable lawful basis for refusal remains**, the conditional statutory command becomes the sole permissible course and CHP can be directed to sign and forward the form. (Pen. Code, §679.10(g)(1); *California Correctional Supervisors Organization*, supra, 96 Cal.App.4th at p.827.)

**Vacatur and controlled remand:** If the evidence establishes a narrower reviewable legal or material factual error infecting Serrato's qualifying-crime conclusion, but leaves a genuine authorized factual evaluation, the Court may set aside the April 20 refusal and require CHP to use the Court's actual adjudicated findings, correct identified error, apply §679.10(c),(d),(g),(h),(i)(2),(k) and issue a new certification determination. (*Saleeby*, supra, 39 Cal.3d at pp.560–562.) The order should impose only directions supported by the findings; a new denial is not barred solely because the original has been vacated.

**Material factual dispute:** CHP disputes central observations and source-record reliability. In traditional mandate, a return raising essential disputed facts permits countervailing proof and trial of necessary issues under CCP §§1090–1091. *Lassen v. City of Alameda* (1957) 150 Cal.App.2d 44, 47–48. Section 1094 permits a noticed-motion judgment absent triable material facts or upon a solely administrative record. If the Court cannot grant merits relief on submitted competent materials, Petitioner requests a narrowly specified evidentiary procedure concerning (i) the §40300.5 warrantless-arrest predicate; (ii) the actual material impairment evidence, particularly the walk-and-turn discrepancy; (iii) qualifying criminal restraint; and (iv) a specific defect, if any, material to Serrato's April decision.

## XI. CONCLUSION

Petitioner asks the Court to grant the First Cause of Action to the extent its factfindings establish a legally enforceable duty. He principally requests a writ requiring **direct certification** if the qualifying criminal restraint, victimhood and helpfulness predicates are found and no lawful discretion remains. Failing such comprehensive findings, he requests vacatur and **legally controlled reconsideration** only upon a proved material legal or factual defect in Serrato's denial. If essential facts cannot be decided on these papers, he requests limited merits factfinding under governing writ procedures. He seeks no damages, determination of immigration status, or relief on dismissed counts.
