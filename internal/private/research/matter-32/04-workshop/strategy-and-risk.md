## Central approach

Make this a precise pleading-stage dispute about identified conduct at identified times. The best opposition is not the longest one: it preserves viable Fourth Amendment theories, makes the pleading/exhibit distinction correctly, confronts the actual warrant, and asks for targeted amendment where the operative allegations are inadequate.

## Priority lanes

- **Pleading-stage treatment of disputed reports:** The defense cannot automatically treat every contested factual assertion in a police report as true just because the FAC identifies or attaches it. Khoja and Grier distinguish permissible consideration of a document from adopting disputed contents against specific allegations; use the actual current FAC, not a general declaration that every exhibit favors plaintiff. [Khoja, 899 F.3d 988](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf); [Grier, footnote 1](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/01/20/21-16702.pdf).
- **Separate door entry from later arrest:** Analyze what each officer knew when the passenger door was opened, not what an ensuing search or interview later revealed. Ngumezi supports treating physical entry into a vehicle as a search, but it does not resolve emergency aid involving a sleeping freeway driver. [Ngumezi](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf).
- **Narrow judicial-deception theory:** Identify Meza's own affidavit, its allegedly deliberate/reckless falsehoods and omissions, and why the corrected affidavit would not establish probable cause. Conclusory descriptions of a fabricated “packet” are not a substitute for materiality; Chism supplies the corrected-affidavit framework but arose at summary judgment. [Chism](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/08/25/10-35085.pdf).
- **Targeted leave to amend:** Explain what is new or more specific now, identify paragraph-level cures, and distinguish venue-related dismissals from a merits determination that amendment is futile. Do not assert that historical screening itself proves the current FAC sufficient.
- **Rule 12(f) vehicle:** Preserve the distinction between striking immaterial matter and deciding whether a damages demand is legally available. Whittlestone rejects Rule 12(f) as a vehicle to strike damages claims merely because they are legally unavailable; this does not immunize an unsupported damages demand from other proper challenges. [Whittlestone](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/08/17/09-16353.pdf).

## Critical concessions and repairs

- **Beverage versus odor:** FAC ¶¶ 12(c), 21, 23(a), 25, and 27 use or rely on a physical-beverage checkbox premise. Visual review of produced CHP 202 p. 2, AGO 000030, shows a checked breath-odor field, not a physical-container field; DS 367 p. 1, AGO 000040, checks odor too. Identify the precise ECF 5 exhibit and compare it before requesting amendment. Do not hide the correction.
- **Actual warrant facts:** Produced affidavit pp. 20–21 includes confusion/disorientation, a “parking lot” response, arrival from a party, admission of driving, odor from both vehicle and person, poor test performance, and chemical-test refusal. These are allegations in the officer's affidavit, not findings that they happened. But every uncorrected item must remain in the materiality test.
- **Recording limits:** Video can support speech, sequence, physical conduct, and timing if authenticated and complete. It cannot directly negate odor, and camera position may not show eyes or nystagmus well enough to resolve those disputes.
- **Transcript problems:** R183 calls Melano “Joselyn” in an action description, has inconsistent page labels, and presents some timestamps out of order. It also reproduces groggy/confused responses that the defense may use. Preserve it as plaintiff-prepared, not official or independently certified.
- **No charges / tox:** Later nonprosecution and toxicology may matter to damages, subsequent conduct, or credibility. They do not retroactively determine what facts supplied probable cause at 01:12 or 02:34.
- **Protected speech:** Do not equate each refusal to take a test or obey a command with constitutionally protected activity. Isolate verbal requests for a supervisor, criticism, and actual requests for counsel, then analyze the governing protection and causation.
- **Qualified immunity:** The motion's generalized argument is not a waiver of the defense. Supply a conduct-specific response using law existing on November 1, 2024, and identify unresolved predicate facts rather than declaring that immunity always requires discovery.

## Adverse totality

Wesby requires considering the whole picture rather than explaining away each suspicious fact in isolation, and recognizes that probable cause does not require ruling out innocent explanations. The workshop must answer the combination of sleep, freeway location, party travel, confused answers, alleged odor, tests, and refusal while keeping disputed facts disputed. [Wesby](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/15-1485_1qm2.pdf).

The defense may argue that sleep plus the “parking lot” response supports emergency aid and that non-odor circumstances independently support investigation or arrest. The response must use the actual sequence and legal standards for each intrusion, not a global “urine therefore no probable cause” syllogism.

## Recommended drafting allocation

- **Opening:** One-page theory of the case and requested disposition.
- **Pleading standard:** Explain contested exhibits with precise limits.
- **Fourth Amendment:** Entry, cup/vehicle search, seizure/arrest, and warrant/blood draw in temporal order.
- **Remaining claims:** Address fabrication and retaliation candidly, including limitations of the authorities.
- **Immunity:** Claim-by-claim analysis tied to pre-incident law.
- **Relief:** Narrow punitive/fee positions and specific alternative amendment.

The final brief must fit the applicable court-imposed limit. Current general Civil L.R. 7-3(a) and 7-4(b) provide a 25-page opposition limit, subject to applicable orders; do not use an oversized omnibus evidence narrative. [N.D. Cal. Civil Local Rules](https://cand.uscourts.gov/rules-forms-fees/local-rules/civil-local-rules).
