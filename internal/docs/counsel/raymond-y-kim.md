## 4. The §290.46 iteration chain — Lerma → Walker → Carrillo

This is the spine of the dossier. Kim has filed the Megan's Law employment theory **three times**, and each filing corrects the last one's fatal flaw:

- **Lerma (Sept. 2022 → Sept. 2023):** Pleaded §290.46 directly. **Killed at the MTD on sourcing** — Accurate's report showed Orange County criminal records, not the Megan's Law website (Dkt. 36, May 2023). The UCL count died on *Sonner* (failure to plead inadequate legal remedy). The Fair Chance Act individualized-assessment claim survived unchallenged. Settled Sept. 2023. **Lesson learned: source the report to the Megan's Law website on the face of the pleading.**
- **Walker (July 2024 → active):** Pleads a **Megan's Law class** outright. Sterling's report is quoted verbatim: "WALKER, GREGORY EUGENE IS CURRENTLY A REGISTERED SEX OFFENDER IN THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA" — sourced to the DOJ Sex Offender Search section, i.e., the MLW. Adds FCRA pre/post-adverse-action and ICRAA counts plus injunctive relief ordering individualized assessments. Defense counsel for the vendor (Sterling) is **Seyfarth Shaw — Jasmine Stanzick appeared Aug. 19, 2024**. This is **Kim vs. Stanzick, round one** — a full year before *Carrillo*.
- **Carrillo (Jan. 2025 → active):** The corrected third attempt. The complaint front-runs the sourcing issue; the class-cert reply briefs argue §290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits "**use of any** information" — no but-for causation required. Vons's own adverse-action notice ("influenced in whole or in part") is turned into an admission.

**The repeat-player map around him:** Morgan Lewis's **Andrew P. Frederick** defended Amazon against Kim in *Lerma* (2022) and now defends Amazon in the user's Matter 29 — Frederick has read Kim's playbook before. **Jasmine Stanzick** opposed Kim in *Walker* (for Sterling, since Aug. 2024) and wrote Asurint's opposition in *Carrillo* — she is the vendor-side specialist on this exact issue in both of the user's comparator cases. The Lerma→Walker→Carrillo chain is genuinely one long conversation with the same defense-side personnel.

## 5. His visible style in the Carrillo record (from the six PACER papers reviewed Sept. 18, 2026)

- **Pleading architecture:** direct §290.46 claim against the decision-maker (Vons) + Fair Chance Act §12952 + FCRA §1681b(b)(1) against the vendor (Asurint) on a *knowledge* theory + UCL §17200 borrowing — the same four-count shape as the user's Matter 27 (direct / Tameny / ICRAA / negligence per se / UCL).
- **Paper-trail litigator.** He builds cases on the defendant's own documents: the FCRA §1681k notice ("using such public record information to determine your eligibility for employment"), the pre-adverse notice ("We have identified the following convictions on your report to be job-related: Sex Offender"), the adverse-action notice ("influenced in whole or in part"). Each becomes an exhibit that proves "use."
- **The 30(b)(6) kill question.** Vons's corporate witness (Toni North) admitted under oath: "Q. But you relied partially on Megan's Law information; is that right? A. Yes." One clean admission = the "use" element. This is the deposition question to copy verbatim.
- **Willfulness from the vendor's own contract.** Asurint's 2011 Services Agreement promised it "shall not return Sex Offender Registry information in CA/NV"; the 2012 Addendum then sold SOR info at $400/background check. Promise-then-paid-breach = willfulness pleading.
- **Pattern evidence as substitute for a smoking gun.** 56 applicants flagged "Sex Offender," all failed, zero hired since Jan. 2021 — disposition data doing the work of a discriminatory email.
- **The "produce your assessments" RFP.** Demanded documents showing Vons's individualized assessments of ~900 denied applicants; Vons produced only a generic SOP — the absence became the proof that no assessments happened.
- **Damages ambition:** actuals, emotional distress, treble, statutory, the **$25,000 civil penalty**, exemplary, fees — the same penalty line the user pleads.

## 7. What this means for the user's matters (borrowable lessons)

1. **The "use of any" textual argument** (Carrillo reply briefs): §290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits use of *any* MLW information — no but-for causation. This directly answers the MVR-causation worry in Matter 27.
2. **The 30(b)(6) partial-reliance question, verbatim** — build the Decena/Altares deposition outline to land on it.
3. **The "produce your individualized assessments" RFP** — if they produce nothing but a policy, the absence is the evidence (Fair Chance Act counts in M27/M28).
4. **Disposition-data discovery** — how many applicants with registry hits were placed vs. rejected; a zero is a pattern.
5. **Subpoena the vendor's services agreement** — a promise-not-to-furnish followed by paid furnishing is the willfulness paragraph (InfoMart in M27, Accurate in M29).
6. **Plead the MLW sourcing on the face of the complaint** — the Lerma lesson; never let the report's source be ambiguous.
7. **Keep your own record clean** — Littler spent ten pages of the *Carrillo* opposition turning the plaintiff's probation violations into a credibility/adequacy sideshow. In an individual case there is no adequacy fight, but the defense will still investigate the plaintiff.
8. **Expect the (j)(1) "person at risk" reading and *Mendoza v. ADP*** — both are now visible in the *Carrillo* oppositions; brief against them in the M27 demurrer-opposition skeleton now, not later.
9. **Know who has read your playbook:** Frederick (from *Lerma*) and Stanzick (from *Walker*) have both faced this exact theory before. Nothing about the §290.46 claim will surprise them; the edge has to come from the user's own paper trail, not the theory's novelty.
