## Executive position
The strongest evidence is the **accepted conditional $23/hour full-time Palo Alto Manager Trainee offer (Oct. 22)**, the **HireRight report delivered to Hertz (Nov. 2; client review required)**, the **California registry hits bearing an express employment-use warning**, and the **Hertz pre-adverse notice dated Nov. 2**. The notice, however, specifies only `[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses-Does not meet`; it does **not** say that the offer has been rescinded or that the sex-offender entry caused the preliminary decision. Do not assert a completed denial or a lost start date.

### High-value new finding: trace the DOJ public-registry search
DOJ makes a material distinction between its publicly searchable National Sex Offender **Public Website** (NSOPW) and the nonpublic FBI National Sex Offender **Registry** (NSOR). NSOPW searches jurisdictional public websites via web services, whereas NSOR is restricted to authorized criminal-justice users. The HireRight researcher stated that the search was through the U.S. Department of Justice, cited California § 290.46, and marked two California hits. **This supplies a concrete, objectively based inference that public California website data may have been used**—NOT proof of which system or source the vendor used. Demand product name, URL/API, upstream source, query logs, result payload, date/time, researcher notes, and any separate court-file lookup. See [DOJ SMART fact sheet](https://smart.ojp.gov/nsopw-nsor-fact-sheet) and [2026 DOJ explanation](https://smart.ojp.gov/sorna/current-law/case-law/i-sorna-requirements).

### Core § 290.46 rule, remedy, and obstacle
Current law: **Penal Code §290.46(j)(1), (j)(2)(E), (j)(3), (j)(4)(A)** (not §290(j)). California DOJ explains prohibited employment use on its [Megan's Law penalties page](https://meganslaw.ca.gov/AboutMegansLaw/PenaltiesForMisuse). The statute permits actual damages and a court/jury amount up to 3x actual damage, minimum $250, plus other listed remedies including attorney fees, exemplary damages, or civil penalty up to $25,000. **Do not assume all measures stack, that each query is a separate count, or that a $25k civil penalty is automatic.** Pattern/practice preventive relief under (j)(4)(B) requires evidence of a pattern, not a single report. State-issued lawful court-file checks and authorized access saved by (j)(3) remain distinct from restricted web disclosures.

**Defense case #1, published:** [Mendoza v. ADP Screening & Selection Services, Inc. (2010)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2010/b214653/), B214653. The California Court of Appeal concluded that merely accessing, compiling, and redistributing website data by a third-party screener was not the employer-style *use* triggering this particular civil liability; the employment-decision maker was the relevant target. The case also sustained an **anti-SLAPP** ruling and fee exposure against the plaintiff. Historical 2010 opinion uses former subdivision **(l)**; current equivalent is **(j)**. **Do not plead HireRight as a §290.46 user based only on furnishing the report.**

**Defense case #2, closely analogous:** [Donaleski v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc., 2009 WL 1296257 (E.D. Cal. May 8, 2009)](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59146756add7b049342b0700/amp). There, even an initial tip from a website viewer did not yield liability where the ultimate employment decision was based on an *independent* legitimate investigation and conviction information. Hertz may argue its decision instead rested on (i) a separately obtained court conviction, or (ii) MVR test-refusal history (the only ground explicitly printed on Nov. 2). Need decision-maker review histories, program rule conditions, distinct adjudication criteria, flags and override logs to test causation.

**Pleaded-source roadmap:** if later facts substantiate, identify the *specific statutory website-origin item*, its chain (California website -> NSOPW -> vendor -> Hertz), which Hertz personnel received it, the words in the actual decision communications, and how it was used in hiring. "On information and belief" needs a factual foundation, not an invented admission. DOJ's description of how NSOPW works, vendor researcher notes, and HireRight product-name evidence supply reason to request further records but not necessarily enough to conclude source/use today.

### California Fair Chance: what the November 2 preliminary notice may have wrong
[Gov. Code §12952](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-2/division-3/part-2-8/chapter-6/article-1/section-12952/) requires, where a preliminary rejection **rests on conviction history**, written notice of *which convictions disqualify*, report copy, response rights, and a *deadline*; at least five business days to respond, with an additional five if the applicant promptly disputes accuracy and takes specific steps to get evidence. [2 CCR §11017.1](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-2/division-4-1/chapter-5/subchapter-2/article-2/section-11017-1/) deems emailed notices received **two business days after sending** (absent confirmed receipt under governing provisions). A bracketed, unfilled deadline and cryptic "Alcohols related offenses" *may* be deficient if this is the statutory conviction-history notice. Confirm actual notice PDF and all attachments, including whether the specific disqualifying conviction appears elsewhere. The record says a report and summary were supplied (facts AGAINST a standalone FCRA pre-adverse delivery theory). Administrative MVR chemical-test refusal is **not automatically a criminal conviction**; when based only on MVR screening, this particular §12952 notice procedure may not apply.

**New adverse controlling authority:** [McInerney v. Regents of University of California, B339467 (Cal. Ct. App., Sept. 18, 2026; pub. Oct. 13, 2026)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/crt-app-sec-dis-cal-div-two/483029.html): the Fair Chance law principally imposes **procedural requirements**; courts do not routinely second-guess the merits of the employer's individualized assessment, and compensatory damages for a missed five-day response period require proof of harm. The initial decision may be disputed and supplemented, but failure to provide a written assessment alone is **not** a violation—§12952(c)(1)(B) says writing is optional. An FEHA court action generally requires CRD administrative process and right-to-sue; confirm exhaustion before alleging it in federal court.

### ICRAA / federal reporting audit — potentially independent violations
* [Civil Code §1786.16](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-1-6a/article-2/section-1786-16/): standalone clear and conspicuous ICRAA disclosure (agency name/address/phone, investigation nature/scope, privacy site, etc.), written authorization, and checkbox election/copy within three business days of delivery. Here report copy was emailed at completion and checkbox was checked, tending to defeat a no-copy argument; scrutinize exact disclosure, not form title alone.
* [Civil Code §1786.28(a), (b)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-1-6a/article-2/section-1786-28/): agency must identify **source of public-record item and date initially reported or publicized** and keep strict currentness procedures for adverse records. Do not confuse researcher completion time with initial publication date.
* [Civil Code §1786.18(c)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-1-6a/article-2/section-1786-18/): 30-day accuracy verification for specified public records. The report's Oct. 30 verification notes may satisfy it; obtain source material.
* [15 USC §1681k(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t15/s1681k/): adverse public-record employment reporting, alternative contemporaneous notice OR strict procedures. [15 USC §1681b(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A15+section%3A1681b%29): disclosures/consent and pre-adverse report+rights.
* [Gilberg v. California Check Cashing Stores (9th Cir. 2019)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-16263/17-16263-2019-01-29.html), [Syed v. M-I (9th Cir. 2017)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/14-17186/14-17186-2017-03-20.html), and [Walker v. Fred Meyer (9th Cir. 2020)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/18-35592/18-35592-2020-03-20.html) govern the actual disclosures and FCRA pre-adverse notice. Walker rejects a theory that federal pre-adverse notice must invite discussion directly with the employer; California Fair Chance has additional duties when applicable.
* [Parsonage v. Wal-Mart Associates (2026)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2026/d083831.html) and [Yeh v. Barrington Pacific (2026)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2026/b337904.html) recognize a **state-law statutory remedy** without actual-damage proof for qualifying ICRAA violations. These state-law standing holdings do **not** eliminate federal Article III injury-in-fact hurdles if filing in federal court. Yeh also rejects UCL standing without loss of money/property.
* [Civil Code §1786.50](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-1-6a/article-4/section-1786-50/) supplies the $10k alternative statutory sum for an individual action and possible punitive damages for grossly negligent/willful conduct. [§1786.52](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1786-52/) limits parallel FCRA/ICRAA proceedings for the **same act or omission**. Assess overlap/alternative pleading rather than mechanically stacking federal and state recovery.
* [Kemp v. Superior Court (Cal. Ct. App. 2022)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2022/g061122.html): California seven-year bar for a conviction is measured using disposition, **release or START of parole**, not ending parole. Here report 11/2/26 and documented 12/2/20 release mean a categorical "2018 conviction too old" ICRAA claim presently **fails** on these facts; federal FCRA does not impose the same general seven-year limit on convictions. Do not plead the report's seven-year label as unlawful based solely on 2018 disposition date.

### Defendant identity is a real pleading issue
**The Hertz Corporation** issued the conditional offer and pre-adverse letter and procured the report. **Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.** is the parent named on an ADP work-opportunity-tax-credit screening request, which is NOT proof it employed the applicant or made the screening decision. Hertz's [2025 combined Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1657853/000165785326000008/htz-20251231.htm) states Hertz Global owns Hertz indirectly, management overlaps, but **Hertz Global does not conduct operating business itself**; operational contracts are generally with Hertz. Name and plead the operating corporation first; name the parent only upon **specific** joint-employer, agency, direct-policy participation, or alter-ego facts (shared officers alone not sufficient). **HireRight, LLC** belongs in claims based on its own reporting conduct, not automatically in the §290.46 claim.

### Forum/Rule 12(b)(6) architecture
A Penal Code §290.46 count standing alone is a **state-law** claim; §12952/ICRAA similarly are state law. Federal jurisdiction requires a genuinely viable FCRA/federal claim (28 USC §1331), or **complete diversity plus >$75,000 genuinely in controversy** under [28 USC §1332](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t28/s1332/). Do not append an unsupported FCRA count to manufacture federal jurisdiction. In California state court, expect **demurrer** and potentially anti-SLAPP, rather than a federal Rule 12(b)(6) motion. State forum may be preferable for an independent ICRAA statutory-damages case with no federal concrete injury.

A future federal complaint should satisfy Rule 8 and *Twombly/Iqbal*: date/offer/acceptance, specific employer, report procurement and recipients, exact legally deficient document language, protected statutory rights, actual violation, concrete injury if federally required, and plausible causal chain. Cite exhibits by paragraph only if the PDF text is verified and consistent; do not attach an entire unrelated private background packet unnecessarily.

### Damages rigor, not demand inflation
$23/hour x 40 hours = **$920 gross/week**, **$47,840 gross/52 weeks** before taxes, deductions, mitigation, tenure, or future contingencies. There is **no documented missed week as of Nov. 2**; these are only benchmark rates. Penal Code §290.46's remedies and ICRAA's individual statutory amount are **not** an automatic pot to add together without independent predicates. Reinstatement/job hiring, back pay, mitigation proof, actual expenses, and provable distress may affect later valuation where authorized. An at-will conditional offer undercuts any assumption of guaranteed multi-year wages.

## Opponent's best motion, pre-rebutted
**"Only a conditional offer."** Concede conditionality; not a breach-of-guaranteed-employment suit. Procedural/reporting causes may accrue before final rejection **if actual noncompliance occurred**.  
**"Notice only mentions alcohol."** Correct. Obtain which field and source drove the action; don't assume it was registry.  
**"Convictions checked independently."** *Donaleski* makes independent-origin decision decisive. Plead registry causation only when substantiated.  
**"HireRight merely transmitted information."** *Mendoza* disfavors a registry-`user` count against the vendor; separate ICRAA/FCRA acts.  
**"All disclosures were delivered and authorized."** True in general; exact content and timing, not generic titles, govern compliance.  
**"ICRAA 7-year stale conviction."** Not here; Dec. 2020 release is within seven years of Nov. 2026 report.  
**"Parent didn't hire."** Parent control not presumed; sue actual employer and substantiate any extension to holding company.  
**"No federal standing/jurisdiction."** Distinguish state statutory standing from federal Article III; choose valid forum on actual claims.
