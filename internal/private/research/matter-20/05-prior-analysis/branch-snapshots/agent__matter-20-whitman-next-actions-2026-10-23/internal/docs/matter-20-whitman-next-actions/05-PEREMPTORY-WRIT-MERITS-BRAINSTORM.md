## The precise question
On surviving Count One only, did CHP's April 20, 2026 refusal to issue U-visa certification violate the conditional duty in Penal Code section 679.10(g) or constitute an abuse of discretion under CCP section 1085? The contested statutory threshold is victimhood of qualifying criminal activity, principally alleged false imprisonment/unlawful criminal restraint. A suspicious DUI investigation, arguable Fourth Amendment infringement, or later non-prosecution does not, without more, establish a criminal false-imprisonment offense.

The August 29, 2026 Whitman demurrer order confirms Count One survives pleading review; it does not decide the disputed facts. Counts Two and Three are dismissed without leave, and Count Four was not timely amended. CHP and Serrato remain respondents. No federal section 1983 arguments, damages, or revival of dismissed claims.

## What goes in the NOTICE versus MEMORANDUM
The Notice and Motion should briefly identify (1) the court, docket, correct surviving parties, moving party; (2) actual hearing date, place and reservation confirmed after the October 28 CMC; (3) CCP sections 1085, 1088, 1088.5, and section 1094 only if appropriate; (4) the April 20 decision and single surviving count; (5) the material grounds for claimed legal error/abuse; (6) a precisely alternative form of relief; and (7) supporting memoranda, exhibits, declarations and service.

The detailed evidentiary argument belongs in the separate Memorandum of Points and Authorities. Under CRC 3.1113(d) ordinary opening memoranda have a 15-page limit absent permission; above ten pages requires TOC and table of authorities. Separate proposed orders/judgments are lodged and served but not attached to the memorandum (CRC 3.1113(m)). Exhibit citations should identify letter/number, page and paragraph/line, and video timecodes as needed (CRC 3.1113(k)). Do not treat the entire original video or counsel's file-transmittal as self-authenticating.

### C. Criminal false imprisonment: facts and lawful privilege must be addressed
California Penal Code section 236 is the proposed criminal false-imprisonment theory; 2026 CALCRIM 1242 sets out misdemeanor unlawful confinement. Felony false imprisonment has additional elements; do not invent them. False imprisonment is expressly among the activities in 679.10(c), but a warrantless arrest thought unjustified under civil law is not automatically a qualifying criminal offense.

Test the justification at: initial welfare check, roadside investigatory detention, the approximately 01:12 DUI arrest, later transport and confinement. CHP relies on Vehicle Code 40300.5, which allows certain out-of-presence DUI arrests where there is reasonable cause of previous impaired driving AND an enumerated circumstance (collision, obstructing roadway, immediate apprehension, imminent danger or risk of destroyed evidence). A vehicle parked on a freeway shoulder is not conclusively "obstructing a roadway" without scene proof; possible alternate statutory grounds also must be addressed. Prior admission of driving remains relevant. Neither a later blood result nor an absence of charges retroactively defeats probable cause assessed at the time.

## Conditional helpfulness and relief
Penal Code 679.10(h) creates a rebuttable presumption regarding cooperation where a qualifying victim has not declined reasonably requested assistance. Assistance in investigating the alleged qualifying officer misconduct is distinct from refusal of DUI PAS/chemical testing. Need exact requests and responses. Section 679.10(k) says prosecution/conviction are not prerequisites and bars certain grounds (such as criminal history or cooperation in a separate case), but NEVER accuse CHP of relying on a barred reason absent decision-file support.

The primary relief could command certification ONLY if the statutory elements and lack of remaining lawful discretion are proven. Alternative, if proven abuse or legal error but genuine discretion remains, seek vacatur and remand for lawful reconsideration under Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547, Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, and California Correctional Supervisors Organization v. Department of Corrections (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824. Brevity alone does not entitle Petitioner to remand.
