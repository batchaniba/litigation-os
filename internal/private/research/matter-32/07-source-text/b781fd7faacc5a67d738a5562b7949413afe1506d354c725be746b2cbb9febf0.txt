20                                            I. INTRODUCTION

21         This is a state tort action arising from the unlawful detention, false arrest, handcuffing,
22   forced blood draw, and incarceration of Plaintiff Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi by California
23   Highway Patrol officers on November 1, 2024, in Alameda County, California.
24
     The encounter began as a welfare check, escalated after Defendants' only articulated
25
     suspicion was conclusively dispelled, and culminated in Plaintiff's arrest immediately after he
26
     lawfully refused an optional preliminary alcohol screening test.
27
     Defendants lacked lawful justification to restrain Plaintiff. Nonetheless, they deprived him of

                                  A. Initial Encounter and Warrantless Entry
21
          On November 1, 2024, at approximately 12:54 a.m., Plaintiff was lawfully parked on the right
22
     shoulder of Interstate 580 East, just west of Isabel Avenue in Livermore, Alameda County,
23
     California. Plaintiff, a commercial truck driver, had pulled over because he felt fatigued and was
24
     and was resting in his rental Tesla Model Y. Defendants Meza-Gonzalez and Melano approached
25

26   the vehicle under the pretext of conducting a welfare check. Without Plaintiff's consent, without a

27   warrant, and without exigent circumstances, Defendant Melano opened the locked passenger door

     of Plaintiff's vehicle, constituting an unlawful warrantless entry and search. The CHP dashcam
 2
     video recording ("the Transcript") documents that Defendant Melano had to unlock the door to gain
 3
     entry, demonstrating Plaintiff had not consented to any search or entry of his vehicle.
 4

 5
                                            B. Dispelled Suspicion
 6
           Upon entering the vehicle, Defendant Melano observed a cup in the center console and
 7
     stated, "I think it's booze." This represented Defendants' sole articulated suspicion of criminal
 8
     activity. Defendant Melano then picked up the cup, smelled its contents, and conclusively
 9
10   determined: "This is piss. It smells like piss." (Transcript, 00:58:51-00:59:05). When Defendant

11   Melano offered the cup to Defendant Meza-Gonzalez for confirmation, Defendant Meza-Gonzalez

12   responded: "No I'm not gonna smell that." (Transcript, 00:59:05). This exchange, captured on the

13   dashcam audio-video recording, conclusively dispelled any reasonable suspicion that Plaintiff
14   possessed an open container of alcohol or was engaged in criminal activity. Plaintiff explained that
15
     he urinated in the cup because he did not want to exit the vehicle on the shoulder of a busy freeway
16
     at night. This explanation was reasonable, credible, and uncontradicted by any evidence.
17

18                            C. Lack of Objective Evidence of Impairment
19         Throughout the encounter, Plaintiff was alert, coherent, and cooperative. The dashcam video
20
     shows no objective signs of alcohol or drug impairment. Plaintiff correctly identified his location,
21
     explained his circumstances clearly, and answered all questions without confusion, slurred speech,
22
     or other indicia of intoxication. Plaintiff was wearing a distinctive orange "21+" wristband on his
23
     right wrist, consistent with having attended an adult venue earlier that evening. At no point during
24
     the encounter did either Defendant contemporaneously verbalize detecting any odor of alcohol
25

26   emanating from Plaintiff's breath or person. The dashcam audio recording contains no such

27   observation.
