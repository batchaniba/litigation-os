## 3. Motion-Practice Record: Rule 12(b)(6)

Three fully-read MTD rulings form the core of this section. The pattern is consistent:

**a. *Cardenas v. County of Napa*, No. 24-cv-04248-ASK — MTD GRANTED with prejudice (May 12, 2026).**
§1983 deliberate-indifference claim (jail suicide) against a private jail medical contractor. This was the **Fourth Amended Complaint**, found to repeat the Third Amended Complaint nearly verbatim with a single new paragraph; his predecessor (Judge Ryu) had already dismissed the prior pleading. He applied *Iqbal* facial plausibility rigorously, rejecting "undifferentiated group pleading" that lumped defendants together so no individual's role could be discerned. Leave denied as futile, citing *Thinket Ink Info. Res. v. Sun Microsystems*, 368 F.3d 1053, 1061 (9th Cir. 2004). Decided **without oral argument** under Civil L.R. 7-1(b). Tone note: the order pauses to acknowledge the plaintiff's grief in plain, humane language before ruling against her — empathetic in voice, unyielding on pleading standards.
Source: https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/candce/4:2024cv04248/432609/98/0.pdf (read in full).

**b. *Stillwater v. United States*, No. 25-cv-01995-ASK — MTD GRANTED without leave to amend (July 30, 2026).**
Pro se plaintiff's FTCA negligence claim dismissed **with prejudice for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction** under the postal exception, 28 U.S.C. § 2680(b), following an intervening 2026 Supreme Court decision; Rehabilitation Act damages barred by sovereign immunity and injunctive relief moot. The with-prejudice disposition tracks **jurisdictional bars, not pleading merits**. Notable: he expressly noted the government had *withdrawn* its discretionary-function argument in reply — a careful reader of the full briefing record, not just the moving papers.
Source: https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/4:2025cv01995/445382/49/0.pdf (read in full).

**c. *BLS v. NCL / Sychuk*, No. 4:25-cv-06776-ASK — MTD GRANTED IN PART AND DENIED IN PART (July 8, 2026).**
Denied the motion to strike outright; **denied** dismissal of the breach-of-contract and express-contractual-indemnity claims; granted dismissal **with prejudice only** as to equitable indemnity and contribution, on Carmack Amendment preemption — a pure legal question. Separately granted dismissal of an alter-ego claim but **invited the plaintiff to seek leave to amend** if new facts emerged. This is the clearest evidence he is **not a rubber stamp for movants**: well-pleaded claims survive, and with-prejudice is reserved for clear futility.
Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-4_25-cv-06776/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-4_25-cv-06776-0.pdf (operative pages reviewed via search-indexed text).

**Synthesized 12(b)(6) profile:**
- Applies *Twombly/Iqbal* facial plausibility with real rigor; conclusory labels and defendant-lumping will not survive (*Cardenas*).
- **With-prejudice dispositions correlate with clear futility** — fourth bite at the apple with no cure (*Cardenas*), pure questions of law (*BLS*/Carmack), or jurisdictional bars (*Stillwater*). First- or second-round MTDs on curable pleading defects should be expected to come **with leave to amend**.
- Willing to split the baby claim-by-claim (*BLS*); also willing to invite amendment where facts might cure.
- No observed MTD denials in full on substantive grounds yet — but the *BLS* partial denial shows he engages the merits rather than defaulting to dismissal. [UNVERIFIED whether he has ever denied an MTD outright; the indexed record is thin.]

## 4. Treatment of Pro Se Litigants

- **Procedurally generous, substantively exacting.** His Standing Order (§7) affirmatively directs pro se litigants to the Legal Help Center (Oakland federal courthouse, 4th Floor Room 470S; Mon/Wed 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.; (415) 782-8982) and the court's Pro Se Handbook, and permits manual filing (original + three copies) — institutional solicitude is built into his chambers practices.
- In a July 29, 2026 §1915A prisoner-screening order (No. 4:2026cv00692), he gave the plaintiff **28 days to show cause** with explicit step-by-step instructions and an express invitation to **amend to cure** deficiencies, rather than dismissing outright. Source: https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/4:2026cv00692/463129/6/0.pdf.
- In *Arrellano v. Sonoma County*, No. 23-cv-00187-ASK (May 12, 2026), he granted an unopposed summary-judgment motion under Rule 56(e) as to one plaintiff but gave a minor plaintiff proceeding through a guardian ad litem until May 26, 2026 to oppose or file a statement of non-opposition — care with vulnerable/unrepresented parties before entering judgment. Source: https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/4:2023cv00187/407187/115/0.pdf.
- **Bottom line for a pro se plaintiff:** expect fair procedure, clear instructions, and leave to amend where cure is possible — but **no dilution of pleading standards**. *Cardenas* and *Stillwater* show he will dismiss pro se (or sympathetic-plaintiff) cases when the law requires it, and he writes those orders carefully enough to survive appeal.

## 5. Magistrate-Judge Practice: §636, R&R, Discovery, Case Management

- **Consent dynamics (§2 of Standing Order):** in cases randomly assigned to him for all purposes, parties "will be asked to submit a form indicating their consent or declination to magistrate judge jurisdiction" and are asked to file it promptly. Portfolio note: in Matter 24 the AG's office **declined** magistrate jurisdiction (Sept. 14, 2026), triggering reassignment to Judge Breyer — declination is a live, consequence-bearing choice in his courtroom, not a formality.
- **Report & Recommendation work:** he handles referred matters for district judges — e.g., R&R on a motion for default judgment in *Cement Masons Health and Welfare Trust Fund v. Ronald R. Nelson Contractor, Inc.*, No. 4:25-cv-09424-JST (Judge Tigar) (signed Aug. 27, 2026; objections due Sept. 10, 2026). Comfortable in the R&R posture with de novo review looming.
- **Discovery management (Standing Order §5):** no formal noticed discovery motions without leave; disputes go through a **joint letter brief (5 pages max, 2.5 per side; 15 pages of exhibits)**. The **meet-and-confer must be live** (in person, videoconference, or telephone) — "a mere exchange of letters, emails, or messages does not satisfy the requirement." Noncompliance is **itself a basis for denial or sanction**: in *Stebbins* (No. 3:23-cv-00321-MMC, May 12, 2026) he denied relief where a party skipped the required conferral and warned that future noncompliance "will itself be a basis for denial of a motion or other appropriate sanction." Detailed, practitioner-grade expectations on privilege logs (no generalized claims), search-process transparency, courtesy copies of written discovery in editable format, and deposition conduct (speaking objections prohibited). Sanctions motions under Rule 37 must use the formal motion track, not the expedited letter procedure.
- **Case management (§6):** joint CMC statement due **7 days before** the initial CMC; conferences held by **Zoom webinar** by default.
- **Amended pleadings (§4):** any amended pleading must be filed **with a redline** against the last pleading — directly relevant if the M29 MTD is granted with leave.
- **MSJ practice (§3):** one summary-judgment motion per party without leave and a showing of good cause.
- **Sanctions temperament:** the Standing Order carries an explicit sanctions warning (monetary sanctions, pro hac vice revocation, dismissal, default). In practice he has shown willingness to escalate — *Capconvert* (No. 3:2026cv02149, July 28, 2026): ordered defendants to stipulate to a civil-contempt fee award or show cause why they should not be held in contempt, after rejecting their prior arguments. But escalation follows warning and a cure opportunity (cf. the *Stebbins* warning, the §1915A show-cause order, the withdrawal denial below). **Measured, not hair-trigger.**
- He denied a defense counsel's motion to withdraw **without prejudice** (June 17, 2026) where the record gave no assurance the court could communicate with the client going forward — protective of the court's ability to manage its docket and of unrepresented parties' interests. Source: https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/4:2023cv02363/412669/45/0.pdf.

## 9. Practical Takeaways for Matter 29 (Amazon MTD — opposition due Oct 13, 2026; hearing Nov 18, 2026) and Matter 32

1. **The opposition must plead facial plausibility with defendant-specific facts.** *Cardenas* is the controlling template for how he reads complaints: undifferentiated group pleading and conclusory labels (e.g., bare assertions of "willfulness" without factual content) will be rejected under *Iqbal*. Every FCRA/ICRAA element needs a factual anchor tied to the specific defendant.
2. **Expect rigorous, unsentimental application of pleading standards.** His ACLU/civil-rights background does not translate into leniency at the pleading stage — *Cardenas* dismissed a sympathetic civil-rights complaint with prejudice. Do not count on goodwill; count on well-pleaded facts.
3. **If the MTD is granted, the default should be leave to amend** — unless the defect is jurisdictional or purely legal. Have the amended pleading sketched before the hearing, and remember his redline requirement (§4 of the Standing Order) if leave is granted.
4. **Prepare the opposition to stand alone.** He decides MTDs on the papers under L.R. 7-1(b) (*Cardenas*). The November 18 hearing (a Wednesday — his regular 1:30 p.m. motion day) may be vacated in favor of a paper decision; check the docket the week before. If it goes forward, it is **in person by default** (Courtroom 4, Oakland); a Zoom request needs good cause **three business days** ahead.
5. **Do not skip or shortchange the meet-and-confer record.** Noncompliance with his Standing Order is itself grounds for denial or sanction (*Stebbins*). Any discovery skirmish in M29/M32 must show a live conferral first.
6. **His civil-rights fluency cuts both ways.** He will understand a §1983/FCRA theory faster than a generalist — and will spot a weak one faster too. Boilerplate on either side will not survive him; the side with the tighter element-by-element showing wins the pleading stage.
7. **Matter 32 (4:26-cv-08271-ASK):** same playbook. Note the consent/declination form will arrive early — the M24 history (AG declination → Breyer reassignment) shows the choice has real consequences; evaluate it deliberately rather than reflexively.
8. **Timing expectation for the Nov 18 hearing:** no verified ruling-interval data (see §6). Do not promise the user a decision date; the observable pattern is steady output with paper decisions, so a ruling within weeks of submission is plausible but unconfirmed.
