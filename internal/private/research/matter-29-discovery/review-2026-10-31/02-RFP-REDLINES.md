## RFP 8

Produce the policies, matching criteria, field-mapping specifications, name-normalization rules, and vendor specifications ~~in effect at any time from July 1, 2026 through October 31, 2026, limited to those applied to Plaintiff’s Screening Order 496623722 and the National Sex Offender Database Search performed for Plaintiff under Search ID 496623723~~ **that governed, were required to govern, or were actually used in processing Screening Order 496623722 or its component searches, including National Sex Offender Database Search 496623723 and the Santa Clara and San Joaquin County criminal searches. Produce each version operative from July 1, 2026 through completion of the October 2026 reinvestigation, including the version operative on July 7, 2026, whether or not Accurate contends its personnel or vendors actually followed it. Include criteria for selecting and transmitting name variants; interpreting name, birth-date and other identifier matches; excluding results; verifying disposition and supervision status; and distinguishing a true no-match result from an unavailable, incomplete, or failed search. This request concerns the systems and screening package used for Plaintiff and does not request source code or specifications exclusively concerning unrelated products.**

Why: closes the “we did not apply that rule” escape while keeping a connection to the actual platforms/package. Preserve the original broader category of policies; do not narrow to only a person-specific policy. Confidentiality is addressed through a protective order; trade-secret relevance and burden still require a case-specific showing. [FD pp. 25,28–31; RFP 8; FRCP 26(b)(1),26(c)(1)(G).]

## RFP 18

Produce the ordinary-course dispute-case file, intake logs, investigation activity logs, communications, findings, corrections, and status records concerning Plaintiff’s October 14, 2026 dispute, reference 496623722, and service request 20261014-15421955, through the date of production, ~~excluding communications between Accurate and its counsel protected by the attorney-client privilege and attorney work product~~ **including records of receipt and routing; communications to and responses from each source of disputed information; any search rerun or source verification; the factual basis for each decision; deletion and propagation of the alias across the report, search inputs, internal file, and client-facing systems; and generation and delivery of the revised report. Include records explaining the October 19, 2026 initiation entry, the revised report's continued display of an October 14, 2026 update date, and the assembly of the consumer-file disclosure bearing an October 27, 2026 cover date. This request seeks nonprivileged material. For withheld or redacted material, identify the claim and provide the information required by Rule 26(b)(5)(A), subject to any agreed or ordered logging protocol. An otherwise discoverable ordinary-course record or attachment is not excluded merely because counsel received it or litigation was pending.**

Why: eliminates an unnecessary drafting emphasis on counsel while retaining actual privilege. It does not claim every business-purpose communication is unprivileged; dual-purpose material requires the applicable legal test. Do not ask for legal strategy, counsel's mental impressions, or privileged advice. [CL p. 2; pp. 15–16, lines 543–580; FD p. 179, lines 2450–2497; FRCP 26(b)(3),(5).]

## RFP 19

Produce nonprivileged business communications between Accurate and Amazon about Plaintiff’s screening status, identity inputs, search results, exceptions or delivery, ~~excluding post-litigation attorney-client communications and attorney work product~~ **and about adjudication, eligibility, recommendations, review flags, the effect of a pending or delayed screening on onboarding or shift availability, the conviction-question response, and the dispute or correction of information. Include communications conveying or implementing business instructions through account representatives, shared portals, APIs, and counsel acting as business intermediaries, to the extent nonprivileged. The period is July 1, 2026 through the date of production. Claims of attorney-client privilege or work-product protection must be identified and described under Rule 26(b)(5)(A), subject to any agreed or ordered logging protocol; the presence of counsel or the commencement of litigation alone does not establish either protection.**

Why: captures the business consequences at the center of the constructive-denial theory and Defense 10. Communications between separately represented companies are not automatically attorney-client privileged, but a valid common-interest or other protection must be evaluated rather than presumed waived. Set Two additionally requests machine decision outputs, because “communications” may not capture configuration/state changes. [C ¶¶22–25; A p. 39:13–15; CL p. 2; RFP 19.]
