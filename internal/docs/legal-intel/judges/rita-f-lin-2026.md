## 3. Motion-Practice Record: Rule 12(b)(6)

A consistent profile emerges across ~15 reviewed rulings: rigorous *Iqbal* plausibility analysis, surgical claim-by-claim splitting, and leave to amend as the default unless futility is demonstrated (typically by repeat failures to cure).

**Outright denials (not a rubber stamp):**
- *Mansfield v. StockX*, No. 3:2025cv04250 (Oct. 3, 2025) — MTD **DENIED** in full. CLRA "drip pricing"/bait-and-switch claim under §1770(a)(9) survives; court credits the pleaded theory that advertised prices were never intended to be honored, citing S.B. 478's legislative findings.
- *Smith v. Rack Room Shoes*, No. 3:2024cv06709 (Aug. 4, 2025) — MTD **DENIED** as to CDAFA and Wiretap Act claims; granted without leave only as to UCL/CLRA; motion to strike denied.
- *Mobley v. Workday*, No. 23-cv-00770 (June 22, 2026) — MTD denied in part: FEHA claims survive (adequate California nexus — algorithmic tools designed/operated from CA headquarters); ADA proxy-discrimination allegations survive within scope of prior leave; race disparate-impact claim dismissed only because plaintiff never sought leave to add it.

**Split decisions (the dominant pattern):**
- *Grimbaldeston*, No. 3:2025cv05649 (Dec. 17, 2025) — venue transfer denied; MTD **granted as to the CLRA claim, otherwise denied**; leave to amend by Jan. 21 (order says 2025, evidently 2026). Establishes her standing rule, citing her own *Apple v. Starr Surplus Lines*, No. 24-cv-03738-RFL, 2024 WL 4834424 (Nov. 18, 2024): "punitive damages are not a claim" and are "not the proper subject of a motion to dismiss."
- *Allison v. PHH*, No. 3:2025cv05323 (May 14, 2026) — MTD granted as to CDAFA §§502(c)(2) and (8), **denied** as to §502(c)(1)(B) (unjust profit from personal data "can plausibly constitute 'damage or loss'"); dismissal with leave to amend ("cannot conclude on the current record that amendment would be futile"); cites her own *Tsering v. Meta*, No. 25-cv-01611-RFL (Jan. 12, 2026) and *Smith v. Rack Room Shoes*.
- *Balcal Construction v. Berkley*, No. 25-cv-02997-RFL (May 16, 2025) — decided **on the papers**, hearing vacated under L.R. 7-1(b); MTD granted as to unopposed claims against one defendant, **denied** as to punitive-damages request (same *Apple* rule).
- *3:23-cv-04152-RFL* (June 5, 2024) — MTD granted in part/denied in part with 21 days' leave to amend.

**Grants with leave (first-round pleading failures):**
- *Arbit v. Meta*, No. 3:2026cv01719 (July 31, 2026) — MTD **granted with leave to amend** (28 days; no new claims or parties; must include all claims/facts, no incorporation by reference, citing *Ferdik v. Bonzelet*). Classic *Iqbal* analysis: allegations "stop[] short of the line between possibility and plausibility"; no nonspeculative connection between 2017 whistleblowing and 2021–present incursions. Notably warns that the proposed SAC "do[es] not appear likely to be sufficient to cure."
- *Short*, No. 3:2024cv07908 (Apr. 8, 2025) — MTD **granted with leave**; PI and TRO denied (no likelihood of success); MTS denied as moot. Pro se plaintiff given "the benefit of the doubt" (see §4).
- *Carrillo v. Amy's Kitchen*, No. 23-cv-01359-RFL (Sept. 3, 2024) — MTD for lack of standing **granted with leave**; jury demand stricken (equitable claims, no Seventh Amendment right).

**Grants without leave (futility clearly shown):**
- *Doe v. Meta*, No. 3:2025cv01611 (May 21, 2026) — MTD granted, **dismissal with prejudice, no leave**: third amended complaint failed to cure deficiencies identified in the prior order.
- *Baqleh* (Apr. 10, 2026) — MTD granted, **no leave (futility)**: SAC's "failure to further evaluate/follow up/reassess" allegations conclusory; *Monell* claim fails twice over — no constitutional deprivation pleaded (*Gordon v. County of Orange*, 6 F.4th 961, 973 (9th Cir. 2021)) AND no formal/informal policy or longstanding practice ("isolated or sporadic incidents" insufficient, *Trevino v. Gates*, 99 F.3d 911, 918 (9th Cir. 1996)).
- *K.C. v. Town of Atherton*, No. 3:2024cv00507 (Sept. 24, 2025) — Fourth Amendment MTD granted, **no leave** after tentative ruling + hearing where counsel could not describe curative amendments. "The Court does not reach Defendants' qualified immunity arguments."

**Synthesized 12(b)(6) profile:**
- Applies *Twombly/Iqbal* with real rigor but no hostility to plaintiffs — outright and partial denials are common.
- **Leave to amend is the default**; with-prejudice dispositions correlate with repeat failures to cure (*Doe v. Meta*: third amendment), pure legal bars, or demonstrated futility after a tentative ruling (*K.C.*).
- Decides **claim by claim**; a weak count does not sink well-pleaded ones.
- Frequently reaches the merits **without reaching qualified immunity** (*K.C.*) — merits-first judge.
- Self-cites her own prior orders (*Apple*, *Tsering*, *Smith*) — read those three before briefing anything touching punitive-damages pleading, CDAFA, or wiretap claims.

## 4. Treatment of Pro Se Litigants

- **Benefit of the doubt on first-round pleading failures.** *Short* (Apr. 8, 2025): "Though it is not clear that Short will be able to cure the deficiencies identified above, this will be his first opportunity to amend after being informed of the legal requirements, and the Court is willing to extend him the benefit of the doubt, **particularly because he is proceeding without the benefit of an attorney**."
- **But no immunity from dismissal.** *Gonzalez v. Garland*, No. 25-cv-08871-RFL (Nov. 21, 2025): §1915A screening of a pro se prisoner's §1983 parole-denial complaint — **dismissed with prejudice** (parole officials immune from grant/deny/revoke challenges; no due-process claim in parole suitability hearing). *Scalant v. Santa Cruz County*, No. 25-cv-01204-RFL (Apr. 15, 2025): §1915A dismissal — public defender not a state actor; federal court cannot interfere with ongoing state criminal proceedings.
- **Institutional solicitude built into chambers practice:** her Civil Standing Order has a dedicated pro se section pointing to the Legal Help Center ((415) 782-8982); her October 13, 2026 resources order in Candelaria (Dkt. 25) is the standard form — routine, not a signal of displeasure.
- **Bottom line:** procedurally fair and patient with unrepresented litigants, substantively exacting. Immunity and jurisdictional bars are applied without hesitation even to pro se plaintiffs.

## 5. Unopposed Motions: Decided on the Merits, Not by Default

- **Candelaria, Dkt. 24 (Oct. 13, 2026)** — the portfolio's own data point: McMahon filed **no opposition** (due Sept. 23), yet the order contains a full Rule 15(a)(1)(B) analysis (FAC filed within 21 days became operative upon filing; Answer addressed only the superseded complaint) before granting. Nonopposition was noted, not treated as dispositive.
- *Balcal v. Berkley* (May 16, 2025) — "In light of Plaintiff's nonopposition," MTD **granted as to the unopposed claims** against one defendant — but the court still reached the merits of the opposed portion and **denied** dismissal of the punitive-damages request.
- **Takeaway:** she does not grant unopposed motions reflexively; the moving party's legal showing still has to be correct. Conversely, nonopposition to specific claims reads as concession of those claims.

## 6. Case Management: Scheduling, CMC, Discovery, Page Limits

From her Civil Standing Order (May 7, 2026 version read in full; current version effective Sept. 4, 2026):

- **MTDs on the papers by default.** "By default, motions to dismiss and remand will be submitted on the papers, and no hearing will be held. If a hearing is set by the parties, it will automatically be vacated. The Court will set a hearing if necessary." She "does not generally issue tentative rulings"; if no hearing is needed it is usually vacated by two court days before, often with a **notice of questions 2–3 court days prior**.
- **Page limits (real and policed):** SJ/class-cert/claim-construction/class-settlement-approval: 25 pages (reply 15). **All other motions: 15 pages; replies 10 pages.** Limits include summaries of argument; exclude title page/TOC/table of authorities/exhibits. Times New Roman 12, double-spaced. **No substantive arguments in footnotes** — "The Court may strike any filing that includes footnotes that do not comply." Motions to enlarge "will rarely be granted" (due 2 court days before the brief). Final brief at least 14 days before hearing. PowerPoint at hearings "generally not permitted" as a page-limit circumvention.
- **Amended pleadings:** redline/highlighted version vs. prior operative pleading required concurrently.
- **CMC practice:** if a dispositive MTD is filed, parties may stipulate to vacate the initial CMC (reset after the MTD); if a CMC is set while an MTD pends, the statement must propose a full schedule including last day to amend. **Trial "almost always" 12–16 months after the initial CMC**; criminal trials take priority over civil ones. Last day to amend typically 60 days after initial CMC.
- **ADR:** parties "should be prepared to present their preferred ADR process at the initial case management conference" and propose a deadline for a joint letter on scheduling the ADR proceeding.
- **Discovery:** a magistrate judge "in the vast majority of cases" handles discovery disputes; her cutoff is a **completion** date (all motion hearings held, orders issued, productions made by cutoff). Unreferred disputes go by joint letter, 5 pages max, after a **live** good-faith conferral; proposed orders from both sides. Model protective orders encouraged; sealing motions face "strict compliance" with L.R. 79-5 — she "strongly disfavors" them and will deny overbroad requests in their entirety.
- **AI:** standing-order § on generative AI mirrors Dkt. 25 — use not prohibited, but "counsel alone bears ethical responsibility," and the obligations "also apply to self-represented litigants."
- **Emergency applications:** must show "every reasonable effort" to notify the opposing party at the earliest possible time, or relief will not be granted.

## 9. Subject-Matter Notes: §1983/Monell, Employment, State Defendants

- **§1983/Monell rigor — directly relevant to Candelaria.** *Baqleh* is the template: Monell requires (1) a pleaded constitutional deprivation (*Gordon*) and (2) a formal/informal policy or longstanding practice — "isolated or sporadic incidents" do not suffice (*Trevino*). A FAC that pleads only individual misconduct without a policy/custom allegation will fail the Monell count even if individual-capacity claims survive.
- **Qualified immunity:** she tends not to reach it when the merits dispose (*K.C.*) — expect a merits-first analysis of any AG 12(b)(6).
- **§1983 limitations:** 2-year California personal-injury period, accrual on knowledge (*Cammas*, citing her own *Hart v. County of Mendocino*, No. 25-cv-04501-RFL, 2026 WL 986261 (Apr. 13, 2026)).
- **Employment:** *Mobley v. Workday* (June 22, 2026) — FEHA claims survive against an AI-screening vendor on California-nexus allegations; ADA proxy-discrimination theory allowed within scope of prior leave; new race theory excluded for lack of leave. She engages novel employment theories on their pleaded facts rather than dismissing categorically.
- **AG's-office defendants:** no AG-defended case located in the sampled record [UNVERIFIED] — but her AUSA background (2014–2018, same office family as the Civil Division's federal counterparts) means government-litigator conventions (meet-and-confer rigor, record citations) will read as baseline competence, not favor.

## 10. Practical Takeaways for Candelaria (Oct. 27 response deadline)

1. **Expect a paper decision if McMahon files a 12(b)(6).** Her standing order submits MTDs on the papers by default and auto-vacates party-noticed hearings. Build the opposition to stand alone within **15 pages** — no footnote smuggling (she polices that expressly), Times New Roman 12, double-spaced. Watch for a notice of questions 2–3 court days before any hearing she does set.
2. **The *Baqleh* Monell template is the FAC's exam.** If the AG moves against the Monell count, the FAC must show a pleaded constitutional deprivation *and* a policy/custom — not isolated incidents. Audit the FAC now against *Gordon*/*Trevino* before October 27; a targeted amendment beats a dismissal.
3. **Leave to amend is the likely safety net.** Her default is with-leave unless futility is clear (repeat failures: *Doe v. Meta*). Even a partial grant should come with a cure window — but note *Arbit*'s warning that she tells plaintiffs when their proposed cure looks insufficient, and *K.C.*'s use of a tentative ruling to test futility at hearing.
4. **Read today's orders as courtroom-management signals, not just rulings.** Dkt. 24 shows she decides unopposed motions on the merits (the AG's nonopposition didn't earn a default judgment — the Rule 15 analysis did the work). Dkt. 25's Rule 8 "short and plain" reminder and the AI-verification rule are standing instructions for everything filed from here on.
5. **The Nov. 12 hearing is moot; the Oct. 27 deadline is the next event.** If the AG answers instead of moving, the case proceeds to CMC scheduling under her 12–16-month trial track — have an ADR preference ready (her CMC checklist requires one).
6. **Do not test the page limits or the redline rule.** "Rarely granted" enlargements and mandatory redlines on amendments are the kind of procedural tripwires that cost credibility with a judge who is otherwise being fair to a pro se plaintiff. Her orders show she rewards clean procedure (Dkt. 24's docket cleanup is evidence) and punishes sloppiness symmetrically.
