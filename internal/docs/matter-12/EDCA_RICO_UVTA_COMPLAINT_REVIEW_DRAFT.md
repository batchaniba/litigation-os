# I. INTRODUCTION

1. This action arises from the sale of a defective commercial semi-truck and from evidence subsequently uncovered concerning the continuity and apparent migration of the seller's truck-dealership operation into a newly formed corporation.

2. On April 4, 2023, Plaintiff purchased a 2014 Volvo VNL diesel semi-truck, VIN 4V4NC9EH6EN142206, from Defendant DIAMOND TRUCK SALES, INC. (“Diamond”) for approximately $26,900 plus charges, paying more than $12,000 down and financing the remaining balance through a third-party lender.

3. Plaintiff purchased the truck for commercial hauling. Shortly after purchase, the truck suffered catastrophic emissions-system failure, entered a derate or “limp” mode, emitted excessive smoke, and became commercially unusable and unsafe for ordinary highway operation.

4. Plaintiff alleges that when Diamond induced the transaction it concealed material facts concerning the truck's DEF/SCR/emissions condition and represented, expressly or by material omission, that the truck was fit to be sold for the commercial use for which Diamond knew Plaintiff was acquiring it.

5. Plaintiff suffered business and property injury including purchase/down-payment loss, repair and towing expenses, financing-related loss, loss of use, and commercial-income loss.

6. Plaintiff filed a civil action concerning the transaction in San Joaquin Superior Court on February 13, 2026, No. STK-CV-UF-2026-0001094.

7. Public and third-party records subsequently revealed facts supporting investigation of a transfer or continuation of Diamond's operating truck-dealership business into Defendant ALPINE TRUCK SALES, INC. (“Alpine”), a California corporation formed September 2, 2025.

8. Those facts include the same specifically identified truck inventory appearing first under Diamond and later under Alpine with the same VIN, mileage, stock number, price, Turlock address, and telephone number; Diamond's old web property routing into Alpine-branded pages; common operating locations and telephone infrastructure; personnel continuity; and Diamond-identified physical equipment located at Alpine's current Bakersfield premises.

9. Plaintiff does not allege merely that two related companies have similar names or officers. Plaintiff alleges an objectively traceable continuity of inventory and operating infrastructure sufficient to support discovery into whether Diamond transferred assets, goodwill, inventory, accounts, websites, telephone infrastructure, customers, equipment, or other property to Alpine, and whether that transfer was undertaken without reasonably equivalent value or with intent to hinder, delay, or defraud creditors.

10. Plaintiff pleads a federal RICO theory only to the extent the filing gates below are completed with particularized facts. Plaintiff does not rely on unrelated internet complaints as if they were adjudicated predicate acts.
