## 1. Choosing the vehicle: PO vs. peace order vs. ERPO

| | Protective Order (FL Title 4, Subt. 5) | Peace Order (CJP Title 3, Subt. 15) | ERPO (PS Title 5, Subt. 6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who may petition** | A "person eligible for relief" — 8 categories incl. current/former spouse, cohabitant, blood/marriage/adoption relative, parent/child co-resident 90 days in past year, vulnerable adult, child in common, sexual relationship within 1 yr, rape/sexual-offense victim within 6 mo (FL §4-501(m)) | Anyone, **except** a person eligible for PO relief — the peace-order subtitle "does not apply to … a person eligible for relief, as defined in §4-501" (CJP §3-1502(b)(1); FL §4-510(b)) | Spouse; cohabitant; blood/marriage/adoption relative; child in common; current dating/intimate partner; current/former legal guardian; law enforcement officer; examining health professional (physician, psychologist, clinical social worker, LCPC, psych CNS, psych NP, LCMFT, health officer/designee who examined the individual) (PS §5-601(e)) |
| **What it takes** | "Abuse" — 7 acts: serious bodily harm, fear of imminent serious bodily harm, assault (any degree), rape/sexual offense (CL §§3-303/304/307/308), false imprisonment, stalking (CL §3-802), revenge porn (CL §3-809) (FL §4-501(b)) | 12 acts **within 30 days before filing**: serious bodily harm, fear of imminent serious bodily harm, assault, false imprisonment, harassment (CL §3-803), stalking, trespass, malicious destruction of property, misuse of telephone facilities/equipment, misuse of electronic communication, revenge porn, visual surveillance (CJP §3-1503(a)) | "Immediate and present danger of causing personal injury" to respondent, petitioner, or another **by possessing a firearm** — no abuse finding required (PS §§5-602, 5-603) |
| **Firearm relief** | Yes — surrender ordered at TPO stage only on qualifying facts; mandatory for duration of final PO | **No** firearm provision in the peace-order subtitle | Yes — the entire point of the order |
| **No-contact / stay-away** | Yes (broad catalog) | Yes, but "only the relief that is minimally necessary" (CJP §§3-1504(a)(3), 3-1505(d)(2)) | No — firearm dispossession only |
| **Final duration** | ≤1 yr (2-yr and permanent variants, §4-506(j)) | ≤6 months (§3-1505(f)) | ≤1 yr (+ 6-mo extension) |

**Triage rule for counsel:** if the petitioner is a current/former spouse or otherwise PO-eligible, the peace order is off the table — she must use the protective-order subtitle. An ERPO can run **alongside** a PO (the ERPO petition itself asks about PO/peace-order violations, PS §5-602(a)(1)(vi)(3)–(4)); it is a firearm-removal tool, not a substitute for no-contact relief.

## 2. Protective-order sequence (FL §§4-504 – 4-507)

**Filing.** District Court or circuit court; with a District Court commissioner when neither clerk's office is open (FL §4-503(a)(2)(iii)). Venue: abuse occurred in Maryland **or** the eligible person is a Maryland resident (FL §4-504(a)(3)). **No filing fees** for interim/temporary/final petitions or witness subpoenas (FL §4-504(f)). Petition is under oath and must state: nature/extent of abuse (with prior injury detail), each previous and pending action between the parties, respondent's whereabouts, financial resources (if financial relief sought), and whereabouts of any child or vulnerable adult (FL §4-504(b)). **The petitioner's address may be omitted** if the commissioner or judge finds disclosure would risk further abuse or reveal a shelter address; jurisdiction disclosure is made orally and in camera, never revealed to the respondent (FL §4-504(c)). Electronic filing is available from DV/sexual-assault/human-trafficking programs, child advocacy centers, vulnerable adult programs, hospitals, or other court-approved locations, with a video hearing the same or next business day (FL §4-505.1). Law enforcement must notify DPSCS of service within **1 hour** on the victim's request (FL §4-504(d)(2)). mdcourts.gov practice tip (tipsheet): "Be specific and detailed. Include information about the Respondent's access to firearms."

**Interim PO (commissioner, clerk closed).** Reasonable-grounds standard. Nine-item relief catalog (no-abuse, no-contact, stay out of residence, vacate + home possession with nonspouse limits, child custody, vulnerable-adult home possession, stay away from work/school/temporary residence, stay away from a family member's residence, pet possession); child-custody provisions enforceable by an officer using reasonable and necessary force. Effective until the TPO hearing **or the end of the 2nd business day** the clerk is open, whichever is first (rolls to the next open day if the court is closed); TPO hearing on the 1st or 2nd day a District judge sits. The order must carry violation/mandatory-arrest warnings (FL §4-504.1).

**Temporary PO (judge; ex parte permitted).** Reasonable-grounds standard after hearing. Firearm surrender + no-possession **only if** the abuse involved firearm use, a firearm threat, serious bodily harm, or a threat of serious bodily harm (FL §4-505(b)(2)(viii)) — counsel should plead qualifying facts specifically if TPO-stage surrender is wanted. Effective **≤7 days after service**, extendable to 6 months for service/good cause; court-closed rollover applies. The judge may skip to the final hearing if the respondent appears or was served (or the court otherwise has personal jurisdiction) **and** both parties expressly consent. Child or vulnerable-adult cases: the court forwards the case to the local DSS for investigation, report due by the final hearing (FL §4-505).

**Final PO.** Hearing **≤7 days after the TPO is served** unless continued for good cause (FL §4-506(b)). Standard: **preponderance of the evidence**, or respondent consent. Fourteen-item relief catalog: no-abuse, no-contact, no-entry, vacate + home possession (nonspouse limited to lease/deed-or-90-day), stay-away (work/school/family residence), child-care-provider stay-away, temporary custody, temporary visitation with safety-conditioned restrictions, **emergency family maintenance** (with earnings withholding), joint **vehicle** possession, **counseling/DV-program** attendance, filing fees/costs, **pet** possession, and a catch-all. **Firearm surrender is mandatory for the duration of every final PO** (FL §4-506(d)(12)). Mutual orders require detailed primary-aggressor findings (FL §4-506(c)). Before granting, denying, or modifying, the court may review all relevant open **and shielded** court records (FL §4-506(i)). Copies served in open court or by first-class mail constitute actual notice; service complete on mailing (FL §4-506(g)). Duration: **≤1 year**; 2 years if a new final PO issues for abuse within 1 year after a prior final PO expired and the prior order lasted ≥6 months; a **permanent** order (limited to no-abuse/no-contact) requires a prior order plus (a) a conviction with a ≥5-year sentence of which ≥12 months was served, (b) a subsequent-abuse conviction with the same, or (c) respondent consent — plus the victim's request (FL §4-506(j)).

**Modification / extension / appeal (FL §4-507).** Modifiable or rescindable during the term after notice + hearing. **6-month extension** for good cause. **2-year extension** on a preponderance showing of subsequent abuse (or respondent consent), weighed under a 4-factor test. Extension motions get a hearing **within 30 days**, and the order auto-extends if the hearing lands after expiry. **De novo appeal to the circuit court** by either party; the District judgment stays in effect until superseded.

**Violation.** **Misdemeanor**: 1st offense $1,000 and/or 90 days; 2nd or subsequent $2,500 and/or 1 year; no merger; sentences may run consecutive or concurrent; a prior peace-order conviction counts (FL §4-509). **Mandatory arrest with or without warrant** on probable cause (FL §4-509(d)). Orders must warn that violation may bring criminal prosecution/imprisonment/fine, and TPO/FPO must warn of contempt (FL §4-508). Enforcement form: CC-DC-DV-007 (Petition for Contempt).

**Firearm mechanics.** Surrendered firearms: officer gives retake-process info; transport/store in a protective case, damage-preventing manner; respondent may retake at TPO expiry (unless a §4-506 order requires surrender or the respondent is otherwise disqualified) and at final-PO expiry (unless extended under §4-507(a)(2) or disqualified); a respondent may transport an unloaded firearm directly to law enforcement after notifying the barracks/station, while carrying the order (FL §4-506.1). Separately, an officer at a DV scene may **remove a firearm on probable cause** of DV + an observed firearm; the agency stores it during the proceeding and the owner retakes it at the proceeding's conclusion unless surrender was ordered (FL §4-511).

**Shielding records (FL §4-512).** After denial/dismissal at any stage, or after a consent order expires, either party may file a written request to shield all court records. **3-year wait** after denial/dismissal/consent unless the requester files a **general waiver and release of all tort claims** related to the proceeding (form CC-DC-077). Hearing with notice to the other side; the court must make specific findings (no prior final PO/peace order between the parties, no guilty finding on abuse-related crime, nothing pending) and balance privacy/adverse-consequences against future-harm risk. Shielding removes records from public inspection and the Judiciary website entirely, but **not** from the Domestic Violence Central Repository; law enforcement, counsel of record, State's Attorneys, DSS employees, and authorized victim-services providers retain access.

**Law-enforcement duties.** A responding officer must protect the victim and escort her to retrieve clothing, personal effects, and medicine/medical devices for herself and children in her care, regardless of who paid for them (FL §4-502; officer immunity under CJP §5-610), and must give written notice of: a local DV program number, the right to seek a criminal charging document from a commissioner (or the State's Attorney if the commissioner declines), the right to file a PO petition in District/circuit court or with a commissioner when clerks are closed, and the right to an incident-report copy (FL §§4-503, 4-503.1). **Out-of-state/tribal orders** get full faith and credit, enforced only to the extent they afford relief permitted under §4-505 (ex parte) or §4-506(d) (FL §4-508.1(b)); an officer **shall arrest with or without a warrant** on probable cause of violation if the protected person has filed a copy with the court or presents a facially valid copy (FL §4-508.1(c)); registration form CC-DC-DV-016. Federal backstop: 18 U.S.C. §2265 — full faith and credit for qualifying protection orders; consistency requires issuing-court jurisdiction plus reasonable notice/opportunity to be heard (for ex parte orders, within the time state law requires and in any event within a reasonable time after issuance); cross/counter-petition orders need specific findings for each party. (§2265(d) notification/registration provisions not reviewed in this pass.)

## 3. ERPO sequence (PS §§5-602 – 5-610)

**Petition (PS §5-602).** Signed and sworn **under penalty of perjury**; must include: known information that the respondent poses an immediate and present danger of personal injury by possessing a firearm; specific supporting facts; the basis of the petitioner's knowledge (behavior, statements, or other information); number/types/location of known firearms; and supporting documents/information on (1) unlawful/reckless/negligent firearm use, display, storage, possession, or brandishing, (2) any act or threat of violence against self or another (firearm not required), (3) PO violations, (4) peace-order violations, (5) controlled-dangerous-substance or alcohol abuse. Filed in District Court; with a commissioner under §5-602(b)(2) when the clerk's office is closed (interim track). **No filing or service fees** (mdcourts.gov ERPO page). A good-faith petitioner is not civilly or criminally liable for filing (same page).

**Interim ERPO (commissioner).** Reasonable-grounds standard; the commissioner weighs all petitioner evidence and the time elapsed since the events. The order commands surrender of all firearms/ammunition to law enforcement and bars purchase/possession for its duration. On probable cause of Health-General Title 10 Subtitle 6 emergency-evaluation criteria, the commissioner refers the respondent to law enforcement. Effective until the TPO hearing **or the end of the 2nd business day** the clerk is open (court-closed rollover). The commissioner forwards the file to law enforcement for immediate service and transfers the case file to the clerk (PS §5-603).

**Temporary ERPO (judge; ex parte permitted).** Same reasonable-grounds standard. Surrender + no-purchase/no-possess. Effective **≤7 days after service**, extendable to 6 months for service/good cause; court-closed rollover to the 2nd open day with a final hearing by then. The judge may skip to the final hearing if the respondent appears, was served with the interim order, or the court otherwise has personal jurisdiction — **and** both parties expressly consent. Law enforcement serves immediately and must **electronically notify DPSCS within 2 hours** of service; service on the petitioner costs nothing (PS §5-604).

**Final ERPO.** Hearing **≤7 days after the TPO is served** unless continued for good cause; on the respondent's request it may be rescheduled to **≤30 days after the initially scheduled date** (PS §5-605(b)). Standard: **clear and convincing evidence** that the respondent poses a danger of personal injury by possessing a firearm. Duration **≤1 year**. Court may review relevant open and shielded records (Criminal Law, peace orders, POs, Health-General Title 10 Subt. 6, Public Safety). Service in open court or first-class mail = actual notice; service complete on mailing (PS §5-605).

**Extension / modification / appeal (PS §5-606).** Modifiable/rescindable during the term after notice + hearing. **6-month extension** for good cause after notice + hearing; extension motions heard **within 30 days**, with automatic extension if the hearing falls after expiry. **De novo appeal to the circuit court** by either party, heard **≤60 days** after filing; the District judgment remains in effect until superseded.

**Firearm logistics (PS §5-608).** Surrendering officer issues a **receipt (make, model, serial number)** to the respondent, keeps a copy, and explains the retake process. The agency transports/stores firearms in a protective case (if available), damage-preventing, and **may not mark** them. On expiry/termination the agency notifies the respondent of the right to request return; return only after verifying the respondent is not otherwise prohibited; deadlines — **14 days** after interim/TPO expiry or final-order termination, **48 hours** after final-order expiry. A respondent who doesn't want the firearms back (or is prohibited) may sell/transfer to a licensed dealer or an eligible non-cohabitant, or request destruction. A **third-party owner** gets the firearm back on proof of ownership + eligibility. Unclaimed after **6 months** post-notice: ownership rights extinguish, agency may destroy. Failure to surrender: on a State's Attorney's or officer's application with probable cause, the court may issue a **search warrant** for firearm removal at any identified location (PS §5-607).

**Violation.** **Misdemeanor**: 1st offense $1,000 and/or 90 days; 2nd or subsequent $2,500 and/or 1 year; **mandatory arrest with or without warrant** on probable cause (PS §5-610). Orders must warn of criminal prosecution/imprisonment/fine, and TPO/FPO must warn of contempt (PS §5-609).

## 5. Issue-spotting notes for Maryland counsel

1. **Eligibility triage first.** Map the relationship to §4-501(m) before choosing a vehicle. A PO-eligible petitioner cannot get a peace order (§3-1502(b)(1), §4-510(b)) — filing one anyway invites dismissal.
2. **ERPO is not a substitute for a PO.** It removes firearms; it grants no no-contact, stay-away, custody, or support relief. Where the concern is firearm access specifically, the two can run in parallel.
3. **TPO-stage firearm surrender in a PO is conditional.** Plead firearm use/threat or serious-bodily-harm facts with specificity if surrender before the final hearing is wanted (§4-505(b)(2)(viii)); mdcourts.gov's own guidance says to detail firearm access in the petition.
4. **Clocks are short and unforgiving.** Interim orders die on the 2nd business day; TPOs run ≤7 days after service (14 days after issuance from Oct 1, 2026); final hearings land ≤7 days after service (14 days after issuance from Oct 1, 2026); extension motions need a 30-day hearing; an ERPO respondent can push the final hearing up to 30 days out. Calendar every trigger from the **service** date (or **issuance** under the HB 497 transition), not the petition date.
5. **Safety logistics.** Address withholding (§4-504(c)), law-enforcement escort for belongings (§4-502), 1-hour DPSCS service notification on request (§4-504(d)(2)), and the commissioner's 24/7 availability when courts are closed (§4-503(a)(2)(iii)) are all statutory — build them into the plan.
6. **Consent orders have a shielding tail.** A consent final PO can be shielded after expiry, but within 3 years only with a general tort-claims waiver (CC-DC-077), and only if the respondent didn't violate it, no prior orders exist between the parties, and the petitioner consents (or, on refiling after a year, the court finds future abuse unlikely) (§4-512(e)). The DV Central Repository entry never disappears.
7. **Mutual orders are disfavored.** A court needs detailed primary-aggressor findings for a mutual PO (§4-506(c)); a federal court needs specific per-party findings for a mutual order to earn full faith and credit (18 U.S.C. §2265(c)).
8. **Out-of-state enforcement is capped.** Maryland enforces foreign protection orders only to the extent of relief Maryland itself could grant (§4-508.1(b)) — inventory the foreign order's terms against §§4-505/4-506(d) before relying on it.
9. **Legislative watch.** A 2026 first-reader bill (SB 20) would tighten firearm-surrender timing in PO cases; HB 1050 and SB 129 appeared in 2026 testimony on related DV/firearm provisions. **None verified as enacted** — counsel must check the current Code before advising.
