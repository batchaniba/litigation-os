# Term Sheet — Client Non-Negotiables

Tiers:
- **A = Enforceable**: a court will enforce it as written (property and money terms).
- **B = Partly enforceable**: can be restructured into a money/property term.
- **C = Not enforceable by a court**: personal-conduct terms. Can go in only as non-binding statements of expectations.

> Why tier C matters: California courts refuse to attach property consequences to marital conduct, because dissolution is no-fault
> (*Diosdado v. Diosdado* (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 470 — infidelity penalty void; *In re Marriage of Mehren & Dargan* (2004)
> 118 Cal.App.4th 1167 — drug-use forfeiture void; see Fam. Code § 2335). A conduct penalty inside the property
> sections invites an argument that the **whole agreement** is coercive. So conduct terms stay in a separate, severable,
> penalty-free schedule. *(Cites: run `citation-verify` before relying on them in anything filed.)*

---

## 1. Lawsuit recoveries — Penal Code § 290 employment suits → **Tier A**

**Position:** Every recovery from the §290.46(j) employment cluster (Matters 22, 25, 26, 27, 29, plus any later-filed suit on
the same theory) is the client's separate property.

**Default law without the agreement:**
- A cause of action that accrued **before** marriage is separate property (Fam. Code § 770).
- Back pay or lost wages for periods **during** the marriage, and any claim that accrues after the wedding, would be
  **community property** (§ 760). Personal-injury-type damages go through § 2603.
- So without an agreement, part of a settlement paid after the wedding can be community property.

**Drafting approach:** Use Fam. Code § 1612(a)(1). Classify as separate property all claims, judgments, settlements,
fee awards, costs, and sanctions, whenever they accrue or are paid, and all proceeds of them. Waive community
reimbursement claims for community time or money spent prosecuting the suits. Schedule each matter by name in Exhibit A.

## 2. Cryptocurrency bought with lawsuit money → **Tier A (if tracing holds up)**

**Position:** Crypto bought with lawsuit proceeds, plus all gains, is the client's separate property.

**Risks under default law:**
- **Tracing:** If lawsuit money is mixed with paychecks earned during the marriage, separate-property status can be lost.
- ***Pereira* / *Van Camp* apportionment:** If the client actively manages or trades the separate assets during the marriage,
  the community can claim part of the growth as a return on his labor.

**Drafting approach:**
- Classify the assets and their rents, issues, profits and appreciation as separate property, **expressly regardless of either spouse's
  effort, skill or labor**. That waives *Pereira* / *Van Camp* apportionment.
- Require a **dedicated separate-property account and wallets** (Exhibit B lists exchange accounts and wallet addresses).
- Optional fallback: deposits of community funds into those accounts are treated as gifts to separate property, or become
  a fixed-sum reimbursement only (no share of appreciation).

## 3. Public humiliation / public disrespect → **Tier B / C**

- **Not enforceable** as a stand-alone behavior rule. A court will not award money or property for "disrespect."
- **Partly reachable** through a **mutual** confidentiality and non-disparagement clause covering social media, press,
  and statements to the other spouse's employer or community. Enforceability in a California premarital agreement is
  **unsettled**, so it must be mutual, tied to no forfeiture of property, and severable. A clause that applies to only one spouse will be attacked as unconscionable.

## 4. Psychological games / triangulation → **Tier C**

Not enforceable by a court. The term is too vague to enforce and is conduct-based.
Put it in the **Statement of Mutual Expectations** (Schedule C): non-binding, no penalty, expressly excluded from any property consequence.
A practical alternative that does carry weight is a clause requiring both spouses to attend counseling before either files for dissolution.
Even that clause is only advisory in California.

## 5. International travel / Jamaica barred → **Tier C, with a Tier A workaround**

- **A ban on a spouse's own travel is not enforceable.** It restrains her personal liberty. A court will not enjoin her trip
  or penalize her for it. If the reason is unstated, it can also make the agreement look coercive.
- **Enforceable workaround (money):** Community or joint funds cannot be spent on international travel, or specifically on
  travel to Jamaica, without the client's prior written consent. Spending in breach is charged against her share on division
  (a reimbursement term under § 1612(a)(3)). Her **separate** funds remain hers to spend.
- If the concern is tied to the client's own constraints (for example, parole travel conditions), say so in a recital.
  A stated reason helps show good faith.

## 6. (Implied) Spousal support → **open**

Not raised yet. If the client wants to limit or waive support to protect the lawsuit and crypto money, § 1612(c) requires
her independent counsel, and the waiver can still be set aside if it is unconscionable when enforced. See `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.
