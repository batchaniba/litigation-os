 2

 3          Code of Civil Procedure section 1085 authorizes mandate to compel performance of an

 4   act specially enjoined by law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1085, subd. (a).) Mandamus generally does

 5   not control how an official exercises genuine discretion, but it may compel the official to

 6   exercise discretion under the correct legal standard and may correct an abuse of administrative

 7   discretion. (Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442-443.) Section

 8   1088 further permits issuance of a peremptory writ in the first instance when the application is

 9   made upon due notice; the notice must be at least ten days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1088.) If a

10   return raises a factual question essential to determination of the motion and affecting the

11   parties' substantial rights, Code of Civil Procedure section 1090 permits the Court, in its

12   discretion, to order that question tried; Petitioner requests a nonjury merits determination and

13   does not request a jury. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1090.) No plain, speedy, and adequate remedy

14   exists in the ordinary course of law: Penal Code section 679.10 provides no administrative

15   appeal from CHP's certification denial, and an award of damages would not supply the

16   certification, which must come from an authorized certifying official or entity. (Code Civ.

17   Proc., § 1086.)

18

19          Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1) provides that, upon the victim's request,

20   a certifying official "shall certify victim helpfulness on the Form I-918 Supplement B

21   certification" when the victim was a victim of qualifying criminal activity and has been, is

22   being, or is likely to be helpful to detection, investigation, or prosecution. Section 679.10

23   expressly includes false imprisonment and unlawful criminal restraint among qualifying

24   criminal activity and extends to state offenses whose nature and elements are substantially

25   similar to the enumerated activity. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subds. (c), (d); 8 U.S.C. §

26   1101(a)(15)(U)(iii).) Subdivision (h) supplies a rebuttable presumption of helpfulness where

27   the victim has not refused or failed to provide information and assistance reasonably requested

28   by law enforcement. Subdivision (k) provides that certification does not require an

                                                   Page 9
      PETITIONER'S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT OF MANDATE - 26CV191136
 1   investigation, charge, apprehension, prosecution, or conviction, and that separate-case

 2   cooperation cannot be a denial reason. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)(1), (k)(3)(H).) [Quote §

 3   679.10, subds. (b), (h), and (k) from the official text in the filed version.]

 4

 5           The review standard is abuse of discretion. (Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547,

 6   562.) A particular result may be compelled where the governmental entity has no discretion

 7   left to exercise because the duty has become ministerial under the governing law. (Common

 8   Cause, supra, 49 Cal.3d at pp. 442-443; California Correctional Supervisors, 96 Cal.App.4th

 9   at 827.) Direct certification is therefore available only if the proven record establishes the

10   statutory predicates and leaves no lawful reasonable alternative; otherwise the remedy is

11   vacatur and remand for lawful reconsideration.

14

15           A. The existing record permits a finding that probable cause for the DUI arrest

16   was lacking. Probable cause exists when the facts known to the arresting officer would

17   persuade a person of reasonable caution that the person arrested committed a crime. (People v.

18   Thompson (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, 818; Order at p. 3.) Here, the Court has already held that if

19   it credits Petitioner's factual showing concerning the absence of alcohol odor, the absence of

20   impairment in speech and field sobriety testing, and false statements in the report, it could

21   reasonably find probable cause lacking. (Order at p. 4.)

22

24   by an officer that Petitioner's breath or person smelled of alcohol; the liquid examined in the

25   vehicle is identified on the recording as urine; and Petitioner's speech is recorded throughout

26   the encounter. (Pet. Ex. 5; Decl. ¶¶ 7-10.) Petitioner performed the field sobriety tests the

27   officers administered. The preliminary alcohol screening test was described to him by the

28   officer as not required, and arrest followed refusal within about a minute. (Pet. Ex. 5 at

                                                    Page 10
      PETITIONER'S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT OF MANDATE - 26CV191136

 4   that one." (Pet. Ex. 6 at p. 3, line 18; Pet. Ex. 5 at 01:10:23–01:10:30.) On the record as

11   probable-cause question, and not as an adjudication of it.

13           B. If the detention lacked lawful authority, the alleged conduct falls within or is

14   substantially similar to qualifying criminal activity. False imprisonment is the unlawful

15   violation of the personal liberty of another. (Pen. Code, § 236.) Penal Code section 146,

16   subdivision (a) criminalizes a public officer's arrest or detention of a person against that

17   person's will under color of legal authority but without lawful authority — an offense

18   substantially similar in nature and elements to false imprisonment. Penal Code section 679.10

19   expressly identifies false imprisonment and unlawful criminal restraint as qualifying activity

20   and extends to offenses substantially similar in nature and elements. (Pen. Code, § 679.10,

21   subds. (c), (d).)

22

23           Petitioner does not ask this Court to convict any officer of a crime. He asks the Court

24   to determine whether the conduct shown by the record constitutes qualifying criminal activity

25   for the limited certification question. Section 679.10 does not require an investigation, charge,

26   apprehension, prosecution, or conviction as a condition of obtaining certification. (Pen. Code,

27   § 679.10, subd. (k) (1).) Petitioner deliberately does not plead perjury or any

28   officer-false-statement theory as an independent qualifying crime: the knowledge element of

 1   such a theory is not pinned in this record, and the report-vs-recording discrepancies are offered

 2   as evidence for the false-imprisonment showing, not as a standalone crime. No

 3   felonious-assault or obstruction theory is pleaded.

 6   request. Petitioner reported the incident through a citizen complaint and a POST complaint,

 7   gave CHP a transcript of its own recording keyed by timestamp to the asserted contradictions

14          That denial helps, not hurts, the helpfulness predicate. Subdivision (h) presumes

15   helpfulness where the victim has not refused or failed to provide information and assistance

16   reasonably requested; it does not expressly make an actual prior request a prerequisite, and a

17   reasonable assertion of unawareness prevents failure to cooperate from rebutting the

18   presumption. (See Attorney General Information Bulletin DLE-2024-05, Pet. Ex. 14, at pp. 2,

19   5.) Where no request was made at all — as the Answer states — there can have been no

20   refusal of one, and the presumption stands unrebutted. CHP's demurrer argument that the

21   presumption never triggers absent an actual request is a preserved legal argument, but the

22   statutory text and the Attorney General's bulletin can also be read to treat absence of refusal as

23   satisfying the condition. Petitioner's refusals of the voluntary PAS and post-arrest chemical

24   testing concern DUI testing during the incident, not a later request for assistance in detecting,

25   investigating, or prosecuting alleged officer criminal activity, and are not automatically

26   noncooperation. Separate-case cooperation cannot be a denial reason. (Pen. Code, § 679.10,

27   subd. (k)(3)(H).) Helpfulness is pleaded here only as an element of Count 1, not as a revived

28   standalone claim, and only because CHP has put cooperation at issue.

 8            F. Remedy. The primary rule is that mandate will not compel an official to exercise

 9   genuine discretion in a particular manner. But a particular result may be compelled where the

10   governmental entity has no discretion left to exercise because the duty has become ministerial

12   679.10, subdivision (g)(1) states that the certifying official "shall certify" when the statutory

13   predicates are met. If the Court finds those predicates established on the record and no lawful

14   reasonable alternative remaining, direct certification is the appropriate writ relief. (California

15   Correctional Supervisors, 96 Cal.App.4th at 827.)
