# Penal Code § 290.46 Employment-Use Restriction — Exhaustive Case Survey

**Compiled 2026-09-12.** Doctrinal research memorandum; not legal advice; requester is pro se.
**Scope:** every discoverable decision, docket, settlement, or news report applying California
Penal Code § 290.46's employment-use restriction to an employment decision.

> **Read the two framing corrections in § 0 before using anything below.** One is a statutory
> misnumbering that runs through the request and through this portfolio's own earlier memo; the
> other is a three-year date error on the lead comparator case.

## 0. Framing corrections

### 0.1 The employment-use restriction is § 290.46(j)(2)(E), not § 290.46(l)

The research request, and this portfolio's earlier memo
(`2026-09-12-case-law-and-counsel-analytics.md`, Part II § 1), both frame the provision as
**§ 290.46(l)**, with the earlier memo stating it was "formerly numbered (j)(2) in earlier
versions." **That is backwards.** The evidence:

| Source | Date | Subdivision used |
|---|---|---|
| *Donaleski v. Wal-Mart* order (E.D. Cal.) | May 2009 | "§ 290.4(d) and 290.46**(l)**" |
| *Lerma v. Amazon* order, Dkt. 36 (C.D. Cal.) | May 26, 2023 | "*See* Cal. Pen. Code § 290.46**(j)(2)**" |
| Accurate Background MTD memo, *Lerma* Dkt. 23-1 | Dec. 15, 2022 | "§ 290.46**(j)(2)**… (E) Employment" |
| UPS Rule 12(b)(6) motion, Matter 26 Dkt. 7 | Aug. 26, 2026 | "§ 290.46**(j)(2)(E)**" ×12; "(j)(3)" for exceptions |
| Newanforbi complaint + Sept. 9, 2026 opposition | 2026 | "(j)(1)", "(j)(2)(E)", "(j)(3)", "(j)(4)(A)" |

So **(l) is the pre-renumbering designation** that was operative when *Donaleski* was decided in
2009. The current structure, as used by every party and the court in the two modern cases, is:

- **(j)(1)** — authorization to use website information *to protect a person at risk*
- **(j)(2)(E)** — use "for purposes relating to … (E) Employment" is prohibited
- **(j)(3)** — carve-out list of entities otherwise authorized to access criminal history
- **(j)(4)(A)** — remedies: actual damages; an additional amount set by the trier of fact not
  exceeding three times actual damages and not less than $250; attorney's fees; and exemplary
  damages or a civil penalty not exceeding $25,000

**Practical consequence:** a brief filed today citing "§ 290.46(l)" cites a subdivision that no
longer carries the employment restriction. Every filed and draft document in this portfolio that
uses "(l)" should be conformed to "(j)(2)(E)" before filing.

**Flagged as unverified:** I could **not** confirm the renumbering vehicle against the statute
itself. `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` is blocked by this session's network egress policy (see
§ 6). Search results indicate SB 384 (2017) amended § 290.46 with one version effective Jan. 1,
2018 and a successor operative Jan. 1, 2022, which brackets the shift, but **the exact amendment
that moved (l) → (j) is not verified here.** Confirm against leginfo or Westlaw before relying on
the renumbering history in a filing. The *current* lettering is nonetheless solid — it rests on a
2023 federal order and 2026 filings by both sides.

### 0.2 *Lerma* settled in September **2023**, not September 2026

The request states *Lerma* ran "2022–2026" and "settled confidentially ~Sept. 2026." The docket
in this portfolio's own `documents` branch shows the case ended three years earlier:

The "3 of 5 claims dismissed May 26, 2023" half of the request **is correct** (§ 2.5). The

## 1. Bottom line

**There are four decisions that have ever ruled on a § 290.46 claim, and not one of them holds an
employer liable for an employment decision.** Two are unpublished federal district orders
(*Donaleski*, *McGroarty*), one is an unpublished federal order this portfolio holds in full
(*Lerma*), and one is a published California appellate opinion decided on anti-SLAPP grounds
(*Mendoza*). A fifth citation (*Pastrana*) could not be verified against any source other than
another court's citation to it.

Of those four, **only *Donaleski* and *Lerma* involved a defendant employer making an employment
decision.** *McGroarty* and *Mendoza* were both against consumer reporting agencies and both held
that a screener's republication is not "use."

The strongest corroboration of how empty this field is comes from the adversary: **UPS's Rule
12(b)(6) motion in the live Matter 26 case (Dkt. 7, Aug. 26, 2026) cites no § 290.46 case law at
all** — not *Donaleski*, not *Pastrana*, not *Mendoza*, not *McGroarty*. Management-side counsel
briefing this statute in 2026 had no case to cite (§ 2.6).

**One case new to this portfolio surfaced in this pass: *McGroarty* (§ 2.3).** It was found inside
the *Lerma* briefing, not by any web search.

## 2. The cases

### 2.1 *Donaleski v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.* — the only employer-side merits decision

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caption** | *Donaleski v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.* |
| **Docket** | No. 2:08-cv-00219-JAM-JFM (E.D. Cal.) |
| **Judge** | John A. Mendez (District Judge); Magistrate Judge John F. Moulds |
| **Date** | May 8, 2009 |
| **Posture** | Defendant's motion for summary judgment |
| **Disposition** | **GRANTED** — "Defendant's motion is GRANTED." |
| **Status** | **Unpublished.** Free full text on govinfo: `USCOURTS-caed-2_08-cv-00219-1` |
| **Subdivisions pleaded** | § 290.4(d) **and** § 290.46(l) |

**What the plaintiff alleged.** Donaleski, a Wal-Mart assistant manager, told Wal-Mart of his sex
offense conviction and incarceration around Nov. 27, 1995; managers permitted a leave of absence
for the incarceration and he returned to work. On or about Dec. 18, 2006, Yelena Lopushkova, an
hourly sales-floor associate at the Rancho Cordova store, told a coworker that her sister — a
non-employee — had recognized Donaleski from a photograph on a sex-offender registry website.
Lopushkova then did her own internet research and found him listed on the California Megan's Law
Website. The listing reached Store Manager George Rodriguez, who informed Market Human Resource
Manager Linda Fermin. A committee made the termination decision. Donaleski sought a civil penalty
plus statutory, general, economic and special damages exceeding $75,000; his **second cause of
action** was negligence premised on the termination.

**Why it failed.** The court found **Wal-Mart's committee did not terminate Donaleski because he
was listed on the Website** — the causation/"use" element. The negligence count fell with the
statutory claim.

> **⚠ Quotation caveat — read before quoting this case.** The prior portfolio memo attributes to
> *Donaleski* the sentence: "The statutory language of the CPC does not prevent an employer from
> engaging in an independent investigation and obtaining personal information from the ex-offender
> even if the employer is aware that the ex-offender's information is displayed on the Website."
> **I could not confirm that sentence appears in the opinion.** govinfo is egress-blocked in this
> session, so the opinion could only be reached through search-engine extraction, and targeted
> queries for that exact language did not return it. The *substance* of the ruling
> (decisionmaker-causation) **is** confirmed. **Do not quote the "independent investigation"
> sentence until someone opens the govinfo PDF and reads it.** It is the single most load-bearing

### 2.3 *McGroarty v. American Background Information Services, Inc.* — NEW to this portfolio

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caption** | *McGroarty v. American Background Information Services, Inc.* |
| **Docket** | No. 08-CV-1360 |
| **Citation** | 2009 WL 980417 (C.D. Cal. Apr. 8, 2009) |
| **Posture** | Motion to dismiss § 290.46 claim against a consumer reporting agency |
| **Disposition** | **Claim against the CRA dismissed** |
| **Status** | **Unpublished** (Westlaw-only) |

**Not in the request's known-case list and not in the portfolio's earlier memo.** Found inside the
*Lerma* record: Accurate Background's MTD memorandum (Dkt. 23-1 at 10–12) and its reply
(Dkt. 28 at 5) brief it at length.

**Facts.** Plaintiff authorized the CRA to run a background check for an employment application.
He alleged the CRA used the California Megan's Law website to determine whether he was a
registered sex offender, and that doing so violated Megan's Law. He urged the court to hold that
accessing and distributing website information *for a commercial purpose* is itself "use."

**Holding, as quoted in the *Lerma* briefing:**

> "[M]ere access to and distribution of the information, whether or not for a commercial or
> non-commercial purpose, are not bases for liability under the statute; the statute does not take
> into account the intent of the person gaining access to the information from the Megan's Law
> website, so long as that person does not 'use' the information for an improper purpose."

*McGroarty* also supplies the legislative-history point that in 2004 the Legislature **removed**
§ 290.4's penalty for redisclosure of registry information (2009 WL 980417, at *2).

**Verification status.** Verified as a **citation quoted in two filings by Morgan Lewis in a live
federal docket**, with consistent docket number, WL cite, court, and date across both filings, and
an entry in the table of authorities. **The opinion text itself was not retrieved** — Westlaw-only
decisions are not reachable from this session. Multiple web searches for the case returned
nothing, which is expected for an unpublished 2009 WL-only order.

**Why it matters, and its limit.** It is the earliest decision construing "use," and its
reasoning is *Mendoza*'s federal precursor. But **it is not an employment-decision case** — the
defendant was the screener, not the employer. Under the request's own guardrail it is not a merits
decision on employer liability.

### 2.4 *Mendoza v. ADP Screening & Selection Services, Inc.* — the only published decision

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caption** | *Mendoza v. ADP Screening & Selection Services, Inc.* |
| **Citation** | 182 Cal.App.4th 1644 (2010) |
| **Court / case no.** | Cal. Ct. App., 2d Dist., Div. 8 — No. B214653 |
| **Date** | March 23, 2010 |
| **Below** | L.A. Superior Court, Judge Emilie H. Elias |
| **Posture** | **Appeal from an order granting a Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16 (anti-SLAPP) motion to strike** |
| **Disposition** | **Affirmed** |
| **Status** | **Published** — binding on California state-law issues |
| **Subdivisions pleaded** | §§ **290.4 and 290.46**, plus ICRAA, plus declaratory relief |

> **⚠ Posture correction.** The portfolio's earlier memo describes *Mendoza* as "the only
> published California appellate decision construing § 290.46's 'use' prohibition," which is true
> but incomplete in a way that matters. ***Mendoza* is an anti-SLAPP case.** The Court of Appeal
> affirmed the striking of the complaint. The "not 'use'" reasoning is how the court resolved
> **prong two** — that Mendoza failed to show a probability of prevailing — after holding at prong
> one that providing employment-screening reports is constitutionally protected activity. Citing
> it as a straight statutory-construction holding overstates it; citing it as *only* an anti-SLAPP
> ruling understates it. Describe it accurately: a published holding on the meaning of "use,"
> reached at the second anti-SLAPP prong.

**The two questions presented**, per the opinion: (1) whether an employment-screening business has
a constitutional free-speech right to republish information disclosed on the Megan's Law Website
to its clients notwithstanding the statutory use prohibitions; and (2) whether the trial court
properly granted the business's anti-SLAPP motion to strike an MLW-based damages complaint.

**Holding, as quoted in the *Lerma* briefing (182 Cal.App.4th at 1657):**

> "[T]he [Megan's Law] statute is not intended to create liability for damages on the part of
> employment-screening businesses that access, compile and republish information disclosed on the
> [Megan's Law Website]. … [T]he statutory liability created by the [Megan's Law] statute should
> be limited to employers who 'use' information disclosed on the [Megan's Law Website] as a basis
> for an employment decision."

**Verification status.** Caption, volume/page, case number, decision date, panel, trial judge, and
anti-SLAPP posture all verified by multiple independent sources (CourtListener opinion page,
FindLaw summary for B214653 dated 03/23/2010, California Anti-SLAPP Project caselaw entry, Law.com
*The Recorder*, Proskauer's 2010 client alert, *Metropolitan News-Enterprise* Mar. 25, 2010). The
quoted passage is verified as quoted in a federal filing; **the page-1657 text was not read in the
opinion itself** (no reachable full-text source).

**Limit.** Like *McGroarty*, **not an employment-decision case** — the defendant was the screener.
Its holding is dictum as applied to employers, favorable dictum though it is.

### 2.5 *Lerma v. Amazon.com Services, LLC* — fullest record; a real § 290.46 merits ruling

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caption** | *Miguel Lerma, Jr. v. Amazon.com Services, LLC, et al.* (defendants: Amazon.com Services LLC, Amazon Logistics Inc., Accurate Background Inc.) |
| **Docket** | No. **8:22-cv-01727-FWS-JDE** (C.D. Cal.) — the request's "8:22-cv-01727-FWS" omits the magistrate initials |
| **Judge** | Hon. Fred W. Slaughter; Magistrate Judge John D. Early |
| **Filed** | Sept. 21, 2022 (Dkt. 1, class action complaint); FAC Nov. 15, 2022 (Dkt. 20) |
| **Plaintiff's counsel** | Raymond Y. Kim, Ray Kim Law APC |
| **Defense counsel** | Morgan, Lewis & Bockius (Andrew P. Frederick, Aleksandr Markelov, Kassia Stephenson) for Amazon; Troutman (Jessica Lohr) for Accurate |
| **Status** | **Unpublished** civil-minutes order; **full docket held in this portfolio** at `internal/public/documents/matter-29/lerma-v-amazon/` (70 filings, `documents` branch) |

**The five FAC claims**, per the order at 3:

1. California's Megan's Law, Cal. Pen. Code § 290.46 *et seq.*
2. Fair Chance Act, Cal. Gov. Code § 12952
3. ICRAA, Cal. Civ. Code § 1786.18 — pleaded as the **§ 1786.18(a)(7) seven-year rule**
4. FCRA, 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(1)
5. UCL, Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200

**Disposition (Dkt. 36, May 26, 2023), quoted verbatim:**

> "For the reasons set forth above, the court GRANTS the Motions and **DISMISSES WITHOUT
> PREJUDICE the first cause of action (violation of Megan's Law), fourth cause of action
> (violation of FCRA), and fifth cause of action (violation of the UCL) of the FAC.** Plaintiff
> must file a Second Amended Complaint, if any, within thirty (30) days of the date of notice of
> this Order."

So: **three of five claims dismissed at 12(b)(6), without prejudice. Claims 2 (Fair Chance Act)
and 3 (ICRAA seven-year rule) were not dismissed and survived.** The request's "3 of 5" is right.

**Why Claim 1 (§ 290.46) died — sourcing, quoted verbatim:**

> "Defendants argue Plaintiff's claim fails because Plaintiff does not plausibly allege that the
> conviction information in the background report was obtained from the Megan's Law website rather
> than publicly available court records. … **The court agrees with Defendants.** … As stated above,
> the background report indicates that information about Plaintiff's criminal history was obtained
> from Orange County criminal records."

On the pleaded alternative theory:

> "Although Plaintiff argues the FAC asserts a 'competing theory' as to how Defendants obtained
> the conviction information in the background report and Plaintiff is 'entitled to discovery on
> this issue,' … the court finds **the FAC's allegations are contradicted by documents that are
> properly subject to judicial notice.** … In short, the court need not accept as true Plaintiff's
> allegations that Defendants improperly obtained conviction information from the Megan's Law
> website when those allegations are not supported by the background report itself."

> "Accordingly, the court finds the FAC does not sufficiently allege a claim for misuse of
> information from California's Megan's Law website against either Accurate or Amazon and GRANTS
> both Motions as to Claim One (violation of California's Megan's Law)."

The dispositive record fact: Accurate's report searched **twelve databases** — four counties,
three federal districts, the National Sex Offender Database and others — and **only one,
"County Criminal – Orange County, CA," was marked "record found."** Everything else read "no
record found," "completed," or "pending."

**The alternative ground the court expressly did NOT reach (footnote 1), quoted verbatim:**

> "Accurate argues in the alternative that even if the claim survived, Plaintiff's claim fails
> because Megan's Law only provides for liability against the user of the information (rather than
> the agency that generates the information) and Plaintiff does not sufficiently allege Accurate
> acted as an agent of Amazon.) … **Because the court finds the FAC does not adequately state a
> claim for violation of Megan's Law, the court declines to address Accurate's alternative grounds
> for dismissal.**"

This matters: *Lerma* is **not** authority on the *Mendoza* user-vs-vendor question. The court
ducked it.

**Why Claim 5 (UCL) died — no inadequate-legal-remedy allegation:** the court applied *Sonner v.
Premier Nutrition Corp.*, 971 F.3d 834, 844 (9th Cir. 2020) and *Barranco v. 3D Systems Corp.*,
952 F.3d 1122, 1129 (9th Cir. 2020). Plaintiff had **conceded** the point and himself asked that
the UCL claim be dismissed without prejudice so he could refile in state court (Dkt. 26 at 17–18).

**Leave to amend granted**, the court finding no undue delay, bad faith, dilatory motive, repeated
failure to cure, undue prejudice, or futility.

**What happened next (docket-verified):** no SAC was filed → Order to Show Cause re dismissal for
lack of prosecution, Aug. 3, 2023 (Dkt. 43) → plaintiff's response Aug. 14, 2023 (Dkt. 44) →
Amazon answered the surviving claims Aug. 23, 2023 (Dkt. 45) → **Notice of Settlement Sept. 21,
2023 (Dkt. 46)** → order dismissing on notice of settlement Sept. 29, 2023 (Dkt. 47) →
**Stipulation to Dismiss Oct. 26, 2023 (Dkt. 48)**.

**Settlement terms — what is public, quoted from the filings:**

  The Parties anticipate that they will be able to file a stipulated dismissal of this action
  **with prejudice as to Plaintiff's individual claims and without prejudice as to the putative
  class claims** pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 41(a)(1)."
- Dkt. 48: "this entire case be and hereby is dismissed, **with prejudice as to the Plaintiff's

## 3. Cross-cutting angles from the request

### 3.1 The § 290.4 parallel provision — confirmed, but narrowed

**Both** of the identified employer/screener cases paired § 290.4 with § 290.46:

- ***Donaleski***: pleaded "California Penal Code **§ 290.4(d)** and 290.46(l)."
- ***Mendoza***: pleaded "violations of Penal Code **§§ 290.4 and 290.46**," plus ICRAA and
  declaratory relief.

But the parallel is weaker than it looks. Per *McGroarty*, 2009 WL 980417, at *2 (as briefed in
*Lerma* Dkt. 23-1), **in 2004 the Legislature removed the § 290.4 provision** that had imposed
liability on "[a]ny person who copies, distributes, discloses or …" — the redisclosure penalty.
Accurate Background used that legislative history affirmatively to argue that redisclosure is no
longer actionable. **Anyone pleading § 290.4 alongside § 290.46 needs to confirm the current text
of § 290.4 first** — I could not (leginfo blocked).

### 3.2 ICRAA § 1786.18(a)(7) paired with § 290.46 — confirmed, and the ICRAA claim is the survivor

*Lerma*'s FAC pleaded exactly this pairing, and the result is instructive: the § 290.46 claim was
dismissed and **the ICRAA seven-year claim was not.** The FAC's Third Cause of Action invoked
Civ. Code § 1786.18(a)(7) — no reporting of "records of conviction … that, from the date of
disposition, release, or parole, antedate the report by more than seven years" — alleging the
March 2022 report carried a conviction antedating it by more than seven years. Claim 3 is absent
from Dkt. 36's disposition and survived to Amazon's answer.

**Takeaway:** the ICRAA seven-year theory is structurally more durable than the § 290.46 theory,
because it turns on the *age* of the reported record rather than on *where the data came from* —
and sourcing is precisely what killed the § 290.46 claim in *Lerma*. Where a report contains
history older than seven years, plead both.

### 3.3 The pleading-stage lesson across all four cases

Every § 290.46 loss turned on the same element — **that the decisionmaker actually used
website-sourced information** — and never on the statute's scope:

| Case | Element that failed | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| *Donaleski* | Committee did not decide because of the Website listing | Summary judgment |
| *McGroarty* | CRA's access/distribution is not "use" | Motion to dismiss |
| *Mendoza* | Screener's republication is not "use" | Anti-SLAPP, prong two |
| *Lerma* | Report showed Orange County records, not the Website, as source | 12(b)(6) |

No court has construed "person at risk" in (j)(1), the (j)(3) carve-outs, or the (j)(4)(A) remedy
schedule. Those are open questions.

2024)." **I found nothing.** No docket, no caption, no news report, no practitioner mention.
Searches on the party names, the staffing agency, UCLA Health, § 290.46, and 2024 returned
UCLA-admissions and UCLA-Health-malpractice matters with no relation to the registry statute.

**Likely explanation:** conflation with ***Walker v. Fred Meyer, Inc.***, 953 F.3d 1082 (9th Cir.
2020) — the binding FCRA standalone-disclosure case that the portfolio's earlier memo discusses at
length in its ICRAA/FCRA section. That *Walker* has nothing to do with § 290.46.

**Do not carry this citation forward** without a docket number or a filed complaint. If the matter
is real, it is most likely a state-court Superior Court case that never generated an opinion, in
which case only PACER/state e-filing or the plaintiff's firm can confirm it.

### 5.2 A miscite inside the *Lerma* record, for the cite-check file

Accurate Background's MTD memorandum (*Lerma* Dkt. 23-1 at 10) quotes the statute correctly as
"§ 290.46(j)(2)" and then, in the very next sentence, attributes the quote to
"**Cal. Pen. Code § 290.46(d)(2)(E)**." (d) is the exclusions subdivision, not the use restriction.
A typo in a Morgan Lewis brief, harmless there — but worth noting, since it is the kind of slip
that propagates if quoted.
