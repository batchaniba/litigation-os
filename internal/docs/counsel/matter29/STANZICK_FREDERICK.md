### The Roman playbook

*Roman v. First Advantage/Sterling* produced a successful Rule 12(b)(6) attack **with leave to amend**. The defense attacked:

- the identity of the correct corporate defendant (established via SEC filing that plaintiff sued the wrong affiliate);
- FCRA preemption / unavailability of private injunctive relief;
- lack of qualifying UCL restitution;
- failure to plead inadequacy of legal remedies under *Sonner*;
- generalized pleading against multiple corporate entities.

The court accepted those attacks and dismissed with leave to amend. (Whether Stanzick personally authored the *Roman* motion is unverified — the signature block has not been checked — but it is precisely the argument set deployed by the screening-company defense ecosystem she works in.)

### Predicted September 22 moves — Accurate (Stanzick)

**1. The factual bridge — expect this first.** Your complaint relies substantially on timing and circumstantial indicators: extended screening, identity verification, loss of the shift, experiences in other matters. Your own portfolio identifies the *Lerma* problem: the report there affirmatively showed the sex-offender database returned no record, destroying the §290.46 sourcing theory. Expect some variant of: *you have not plausibly alleged what Accurate actually reported, where it obtained the information, or that Accurate accessed or communicated Megan's Law information at all.* This is your biggest battlefield.

**2. Corporate-entity precision.** *Roman* is the warning shot: First Advantage won dismissal in part by proving via SEC filing that the plaintiff sued the wrong affiliate. Your naming of Accurate Background LLC needs to be airtight — actual contracting/reporting entity, California entity identity, any DBA, and who generated screening ID Y94611404. **3. UCL attack — expect it nearly verbatim from *Roman*.** No restitutionary interest; damages are adequate; *Sonner*; injunctive relief unavailable or preempted to the extent predicated on FCRA. Pressure-test the UCL count before September 22 rather than waiting for their motion.

**4. ICRAA statutory-element attack.** Screening firms routinely separate criminal-record sourcing, consumer-reporting functions, investigative-consumer-report functions, employer decision-making, permissible reporting periods, and accuracy/reinvestigation duties. Expect them to force you to identify exactly which Accurate act violated which ICRAA section — no omnibus "ICRAA violation."

**5. Causation / employer-decision separation.** *"We supplied information. Amazon made the hiring decision."* Your strongest eventual evidence is not the consumer report alone — it is the chain: Accurate report + coding/status + transmission to Amazon + Amazon decision workflow. Matter 27's InfoMart report is powerful because it says Billing Code RK LOGISTICS, Client Package RK Logistics, NEEDS APPROVAL, PER CLIENT CRITERIA, and identifies the registry as the only adverse item. You need the Accurate equivalent.

### Two paths, not one

- **Path A — Answer + defenses.** Accurate preserves everything, denies registry information was used, and makes you prove what its report contained. Fits the *Boyer* pattern.
- **Path B — Surgical Rule 12(b)(6).** Not "your story is impossible" but targeted attacks on §290.46 causation, UCL remedies, ICRAA pleading, injunctive relief, and entity identity. Fits the *Roman* playbook.

Either would fit what we're seeing. Do not automatically forecast "September 22 → giant Rule 12 motion."

His likely September 22 posture: a Fair Chance Act compliance story (individualized assessment, business necessity, the decision workflow), separation from Accurate's conduct ("we received a report; we made an employment decision"), and — if a motion — narrower and more employer-flavored than Stanzick's.

## The seam: where the two defenses can conflict

This is the highest-value insight in the file. Accurate's and Amazon's optimal factual positions pull in different directions:

| | Accurate (Stanzick) | Amazon (Frederick) |
|---|---|---|
| Best fact | "Our report never contained registry info" | "Our decision never used registry info" |
| Best law | FCRA/ICRAA technical attacks; *Roman* playbook | Fair Chance Act compliance; employer-decision separation |
| Worst day | The report *did* flag the registry | The decision workflow *did* ingest the flag |

If Accurate denies the report contained registry information while Amazon's decision documents reference a registry-derived flag (or vice versa), one desk's position breaks the other's. **The transmission chain is the key evidence**: Accurate report + coding/status + transmission to Amazon + Amazon decision workflow, anchored to screening ID Y94611404.

Watch for:

- **Separate vs. joint motions.** If they file separately, compare factual premises line by line. Inconsistent positions between co-defendants are discoverable and usable.
- **The entity-identity attack cutting both ways.** If Stanzick argues you sued the wrong Accurate affiliate, that admission about *which* affiliate did the work hands you the correct target for discovery.
- **Settlement asymmetry.** The $250k demand went to Amazon only. If Amazon engages while Accurate fights, the desks diverge — and a settling employer's documents often illuminate the CRA's conduct.

## Pre-September 22 pressure tests

1. **UCL count.** Apply *Sonner* to your own pleading now: restitutionary interest, inadequacy of legal remedies, and whether any injunctive relief survives FCRA preemption. Fix it before they brief it.
2. **Entity precision.** Confirm the exact Accurate entity that contracted, reported, and generated screening ID Y94611404 — California entity, DBA, affiliate relationships. Don't give them a *Roman*-style dismissal.
3. **The factual bridge.** Inventory every allegation connecting Accurate to registry information: what was reported, to whom, when, with what coding. Mark what is document-backed vs. circumstantial. The circumstantial pile is where Stanzick will strike.
4. **ICRAA mapping.** For each ICRAA section pled, write the one-sentence version of which Accurate act violated it. If you can't, neither can the complaint — and she will notice.
5. **Watch the answer-vs-MTD tell.** An answer (Path A) signals they want discovery control and believe the report helps them. A surgical MTD (Path B) signals they think the pleading — not the documents — is the weak point.
