# Dossier: U.S. District Judge Dale A. Drozd — 2026 Docket Audit

**Who:** Dale Alan Drozd, U.S. District Judge, Eastern District of California, Sacramento.
Nominated by President Barack Obama (Nov. 12, 2014), confirmed 69–21 (Oct. 5, 2015). Previously
Chief U.S. Magistrate Judge (2011–2015) and Magistrate Judge (1997–2015) in the same district —
18 years on the magistrate bench before elevation. UCLA Law J.D. 1980 (Order of the Coif);
clerked for Judge Lawrence K. Karlton (E.D. Cal.). ABA rated "well qualified."
Sources: [caed.uscourts.gov](http://www.caed.uscourts.gov/caednew/index.cfm/judges/all-judges/5017/united-states-district-judge-dale-a-drozd-dad/),
[Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-161/issue-146/senate-section/article/S7157-1)

**Structural context:** E.D. Cal. is one of the most overburdened districts in the country — Drozd
himself declared a "judicial emergency" in a Feb. 2020 standing order, at one point carrying
~1,050 civil actions plus 625 criminal defendants. His emergency procedures: all motions decided
on the papers with **no oral argument**, no new civil jury-trial dates, and heavy referral of
motions to magistrate judges for findings and recommendations. The underlying caseload crisis has
not abated, and his 2026 output still shows the same operating pattern.

**Method honesty:** Sampled audit, not a census — built from ~20 signed 2026 orders found via
Justia/govinfo/CourtListener plus news and court records. Employment and general-civil
observations lean partly on late-2025 orders that set 2026 posture.

## Case-type breakdown (2026 observed activity)

**1. Immigration detention habeas (§2241) — the standout 2026 cluster.** At least six grants
observed, all ordering immediate release of ICE re-detainees and enjoining re-detention without a
pre-detention hearing before an immigration judge (government bearing clear-and-convincing
burden). Built on his own late-2025 precedents (*Perez v. Albarran*, *Ayala Cajina*,
*O.A.C.S. v. Wofford*); the government has repeatedly conceded new petitions are "not
substantively distinct." Observed: *Singh v. Warden* (4/2/26), *Li v. Warden* (4/9/26),
*Penaloza Vazquez* (5/15/26), *Veizaga Paulo v. Golden State Annex* (2/11/26), two pseudonym
asylum-seeker grants (2/11/26), California City Detention Facility case (6/12/26).

**2. Prisoner civil-rights §1983 — the E.D. Cal. staple.** Heavy volume; typically adopts the
magistrate judge's F&Rs in full after de novo review. *Kononov v. Sacramento County Sheriff
Dept.* (2/14/26): adopted F&R, granted MTD, dismissed pro se jail-inmate complaint **with
prejudice** after second amended complaint. *James v. County of Sacramento* (2/12/26): adopted
F&R denying pro se prisoner's permanent-injunction motion.

**3. State-prisoner habeas §2254.** *Jolivette v. Superior Court of Solano County* (7/27/26):
dismissed second amended petition without further leave, denied recusal motion and remaining
motions as moot. April 2026 orders dismissing unexhausted petitions without prejudice.

**4. Employment cases.** *Home Depot* matter (5/15/25, carrying into 2026 posture):
discrimination and retaliation claims dismissed **without further leave to amend**, hostile work
environment dismissed **with** leave; plaintiff directed to file third amended complaint within
30 days. CDCR Title VII case (9/29/25): summary judgment **for the employer** under *McDonnell
Douglas*. *Persinger* ADA/FEHA/§1983 (11/12/25): MTD granted; leave to amend only on the ADA
claim; Monell §1983 claims dismissed for alleging only a single instance of unconstitutional
conduct; motion to strike denied.

**5. Non-prisoner civil-rights §1983.** April 9, 2026 order (2:25-cv-02164, CHP/State of
California): §1983 Fourth/Fourteenth Amendment claims against the State dismissed **without**
leave to amend; IIED and negligence claims dismissed **with** leave; plaintiff given 21 days to
file SAC or notice of intent not to. Notable: plaintiff filed no opposition and the court
construed it as non-opposition per L.R. 230(c).

**6. General civil.** *California Mortgage Ass'n v. Bonta* (6/3/26): granted AG's MTD, denied
preliminary injunction, denied intervention as moot, closed case. IDEA stay-put case (5/11/26):
complaint dismissed without prejudice, 14 days' leave to amend.

**7. Criminal.** Substantial criminal docket (sentencings, Speedy Trial Act continuances) —
standard for E.D. Cal. district judges.

**8. The portfolio's cases.** 2:26-cv-02847-DAD-CKD (*Newanforbi v. UPS*) and
2:26-cv-02881-DAD-SCR (*Newanforbi v. FedEx*) — both his cases, with MJs Delaney and Riordan
respectively.

## Observable patterns — for a pro se employment civil-rights plaintiff

1. **The magistrate judge is the real audience at the Rule 12 stage.** Drozd refers civil
   motions to the MJ for F&Rs and adopts them in full after de novo review as a matter of
   routine. For UPS, the Oct. 7 hearing before Judge Delaney is effectively the dispositive
   event — Drozd's later review is highly deferential in practice. Win the F&R, win the motion.
2. **Expect no oral argument before Drozd himself.** His standing practice is submission on the
   papers (Local Rule 230(g)). The UPS MTD will be heard by Delaney, not Drozd — the briefing
   must carry the whole load.
3. **Rule 12 tendency: grant with leave, trim with prejudice only after repeated failure.**
   First MTDs typically get 21–30 days' leave to amend. Dismissal *without further leave*
   follows multiple failed amendments. Derivative/duplicative claims get cut.
4. **Always oppose — silence is construed as non-opposition.** L.R. 230(c): failure to file a
   timely opposition "may be construed as nonopposition." The April 2026 CHP order shows him
   granting dismissal partly on that basis.
5. **Pro se handling: liberal amendment, strict futility cutoff.** He recites the Ninth
   Circuit's "extreme liberality" on amendment and grants leave even unrequested — but once he
   finds amendment futile, the case closes with prejudice. Don't burn the amendment on a
   cosmetic fix.
6. **The 2026 immigration-habeas line shows he rules against the government when due process
   is cleanly presented** — six grants, government conceding. Not reflexively pro-government;
   pro-procedure.
7. **Settlement posture:** scheduling orders push parties toward magistrate-led settlement
   conferences. The demand offering a settlement conference before Delaney fits his
   institutional preference exactly.
8. **Timing realism:** F&R-to-order lags of weeks to months are normal on his docket. The Oct. 7
   UPS hearing will produce a recommendation; Drozd's ruling follows on his own timeline.
