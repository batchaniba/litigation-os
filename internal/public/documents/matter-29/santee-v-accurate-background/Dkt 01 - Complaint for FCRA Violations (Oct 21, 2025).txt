 2                                      FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS
 3   8.      In September 2025, Plaintiff applied for an employment opportunity with Canadian Pacific
 4
     Kansas City (“CPKC”).
 5
     9.      As part of the hiring process, CPKC used Defendant Accurate Background, Inc. to obtain
 6
     background reports on Plaintiff.
 7
 8   10.     Accurate furnished two inaccurate background reports to CPKC. Each report falsely

 9   indicated that Plaintiff’s Texas driver’s license had a restriction.

10   11.     Plaintiff’s driver’s license was, in fact, valid and unrestricted, as confirmed by the Texas
11
     Department of Public Safety and shown on Plaintiff’s temporary license issued September 17,
12
     2025.
13
     12.     After the first inaccurate report, Plaintiff promptly disputed the inaccuracy and provided
14
15   Accurate with a copy of his valid, unrestricted driver’s license.

16   13.     Despite receiving this documentation, Accurate issued a second inaccurate report again

17   falsely indicating a restriction on Plaintiff’s license.
18   14.     Plaintiff disputed the second inaccurate report as well.
19
     15.     Accurate eventually completed its reinvestigation and corrected the report to show
20
     Plaintiff’s license was valid and unrestricted.
21
     16.     However, because of Accurate’s repeated errors and delay in correcting the reports,
22
23   Plaintiff’s prospective employer delayed his training start date.

24   17.     Plaintiff’s original start date was October 13, 2025, but his potential employer informed

25   him that because it took so long to clear the license, the next available training class would not
26   begin until 2026.

                                    FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION
 6     Violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b) – Negligent Failure to Follow Reasonable Procedures
 7   20.      Plaintiff incorporates by reference all the above paragraphs of this Complaint as though
 8
     fully stated herein with the same force and effect as if the same were set forth at length herein.
 9
     21.      This is an action for negligent violation of the FCRA; 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et. seq.
10
     22.      Section 1681e(b) of the FCRA requires consumer reporting agencies to “follow
11
12   reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy” of the information concerning a

13   consumer.
14   23.      Accurate Background, Inc. routinely prepares employment background reports for large
15
     national employers such as CPKC. Accurate knew that these reports are relied upon to make
16
     immediate employment decisions and that any false indication of a driver’s license restriction
17
     would automatically disqualify a candidate from transportation-related positions.
18
19   24.      Despite this, Accurate twice generated and transmitted reports stating that Plaintiff’s

20   Texas driver’s license carried a restriction, even though Texas DPS records and the license itself

21   clearly showed it to be valid and unrestricted.
22   25.      Accurate failed to use reasonable procedures to verify the accuracy of driver’s-license
23
     information before releasing the reports to CPKC. A simple check of the state’s DPS database or
24
     review of the uploaded license image would have revealed the truth.
25
     26.      Accurate further failed to employ any quality-control or cross-check procedure to prevent
26
27   the same error from reappearing in a second report after Plaintiff had already disputed the first

 9   38.      This is an action for negligent violation of the FCRA 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et. seq.

10   39.      Section 1681i(a) requires a consumer reporting agency, upon receiving a dispute, to
11
     conduct a reasonable reinvestigation to determine whether the disputed information is inaccurate
12
     and to correct or delete any item found to be incorrect.
13
     40.      After Plaintiff disputed the first inaccurate report, Accurate had actual possession of the
14
     correct DPS license image showing no restrictions. Yet it failed to verify or correct its data and
15
16   instead regenerated the same false information in a second report.

17   41.      Accurate’s reinvestigation procedures were wholly unreasonable: it relied exclusively on
18   automated vendor data without human verification, ignored the documentary proof supplied by
19
     Plaintiff, and failed to contact the Texas Department of Public Safety to confirm license status.
20
     42.      Accurate’s failure timely to correct the report caused a weeks-long delay in clearing
21
     Plaintiff’s background, directly resulting in the lost October 2025 start date and substantial
22
23   financial harm.

24   43.      Defendant’s acts and omissions constitute negligent violations of § 1681i(a), entitling

25   Plaintiff to actual damages, attorneys’ fees, and costs under § 1681o.
