                      Critique Briefing & Prompt for External LLMs

Matter 24 — Newanforbi v. Serrato, N.D. Cal. 4:26-cv-05953-ASK

PURPOSE OF THIS PACK

This pack is for external LLM critique of Plaintiff's Opposition to Defendant Captain G. Serrato's August
26, 2026 Rule 12(b)(6) / 12(f) motion. Critique the Opposition's strategy, doctrine, citation accuracy,
concessions, and vulnerabilities — not Plaintiff's life story.

CASE IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Plaintiff sues Serrato (individual + official capacity) under § 1983 over the April 20, 2026 denial of a U-visa
Form I-918 Supplement B certification. Claims: substantive due process, procedural due process, First
Amendment retaliation, plus declaratory/injunctive relief. Magistrate Judge Ajay S. Krishnan screened the
original complaint under 28 U.S.C. § 1915 (Dkt. 5, June 29, 2026) and held it sufficient, including: Serrato
had discretion to certify but not to retaliate.

STRATEGIC POSTURE OF THE OPP (v4)

1. Lead with Krishnan's screening order — Defendant must talk past a judicial finding of plausibility.
2. Keep SDP/PDP alive via California Penal Code § 679.10 mandatory duties / helpfulness presumption /
(k), while accepting federal certification discretion (Perez Perez / Catholic Charities timing distinction).
3. Lean hard into First Amendment retaliation with but-for causation under Ford v. City of Yakima; restore
Perry / Umbehr against Defendant's "no entitlement" Notice ground.
4. Soften the remedy: do not demand a forced "yes" certification; seek vacatur / redo under correct
standards; concede fourth cause of action can be a prayer rather than a standalone claim.
5. Accept Defendant's related-case narrative as true for Rule 12 purposes (including Judge Fickes's denial of
judicial certification in Alameda 26CV168913) and distinguish: different certifying official, different legal
question, different conflict posture.
6. Kill Rule 12(f) strike of damages/fees/injunction under Whittlestone.
7. QI: official-capacity Ex parte Young survives; individual-capacity contested via clearly established
retaliation + non-arbitrary decisionmaking.
