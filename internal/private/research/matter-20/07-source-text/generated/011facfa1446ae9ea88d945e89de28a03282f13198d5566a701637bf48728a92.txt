 1                        MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

 2   I.    INTRODUCTION
 3         The Court should sustain the demurrers by Respondents Gerardo Serrato, Gary Paul, and

 4   Eric Dutra (collectively, the “Individual Respondents” or “CHP Officers”) to the petition for writ

 5   of mandate. For the reasons articulated in Respondent California Highway Patrol’s (“CHP”)

 6   demurrer to the petition for writ of mandate, and CHP’s reply in support of its demurrer, each of

 7   Petitioner’s four causes of action fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. See

 8   Respondent CHP’s Demurrer to Petition for Writ of Mandate; see also CHP’s Reply in support of

 9   Demurrer to Petition for Writ of Mandate.

10         Further, Petitioner has failed to state facts sufficient to allege that either Sgt. Dutra or Lt.

11   Paul is a “certifying official” within the meaning of Penal Code section 679.10(b). Therefore, the

12   CHP Officers respectfully request that the Court sustain the Individual Respondents’ demurrers

13   and dismiss this action against them without leave to amend.

14   II.   ARGUMENT
15         A.    Petitioner Has Waived Any Objection to the Joinders of Respondents
                 Gerardo Serrato, Gary Paul, or Eric Dutra.
16

17         As an initial matter, Petitioner made no objection to the CHP Officers joining in CHP’s

18   demurrer arguments. Thus, to the extent that Petitioner objects or intends to object to the CHP

19   Officers’ incorporation by reference of all points and authorities filed in support of CHP’s

20   Demurrer, Petitioner has waived such objections and should not be permitted to raise them.

21         B.    Petitioner Has Failed to State Facts Sufficient to Allege that Either Lt. Paul
                 or Sgt. Dutra is a “Certifying Official” Within the Meaning of Penal Code
22               section 679.10(b).
23         Petitioner is mistaken that the Court must accept as true his bare legal conclusions that Lt.

24   Paul and Sgt. Dutra are “certifying official[s]” within the meaning of Penal Code section

25   679.10(b). A court does not accept as true “contentions, deductions, or conclusions of fact or law

26   in that pleading.” (Yvanova v. New Century Mortg. Corp. (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919, 924.) Section

27   679.10(b) provides that a “certifying official” is: (1) the head of the certifying entity; (2) a person

28   in a supervisory role who has been specifically designated by the head of the certifying entity to
                                                         6
                          Individual Respondents’ Reply iso Demurrer to Petition for Writ of Mandate (26CV191136)
 1   issue Form I-918 Supplement B certifications on behalf of that agency; (3) a judge; and (4) any

 2   other certifying official defined under Section 214.14(a)(2) of the Title 8 of the Code of Federal

 3   Regulations. (See Penal Code § 679.10(b)(1), (b)(2), (b)(3), (b)(4).)

 4         At most, Petitioner alleges that Lt. Paul “is a CHP supervisory official with responsibility

 5   for processing the certification request” who “acknowledged receipt” of Petitioner’s certification

 6   request and was identified as the point of contact for “additional questions.” (Pet., ¶ 15.) But

 7   nowhere in the petition does it state that Lt. Paul was the head of the certifying entity (i.e., the

 8   head of CHP) or that he had been specifically designated by CHP to issue a Form B certification

 9   on behalf of CHP. And were Petitioner now to say so, the April 20, 2026 Form B certification

10   denial letter, which was signed by Capt. Serrato, would belie such assertions. (Pet., ¶ 14; id., Ex.

11   1.) Indeed, to infer that Lt. Paul was the head of CHP or that he had been specifically designated

12   by CHP to issue a Form B certification based on these allegations is not reasonable, and the Court

13   is not obligated to make such an inference. It does not reasonably follow that a CHP officer who

14   received and processed a certification request and serves as the point-of-contact for additional

15   questions is the “head” of CHP, or has the authority to approve and issue the certification.

16         Similarly, Petitioner alleges that Sgt. Dutra was “the assigned investigator and designated

17   recipient on the Supplement B,” to whom Petitioner submitted his certification request. (Pet., ¶

18   16.). But again, nowhere in the petitioner does it state that Sgt. Dutra was the head of CHP or

19   that he had been specifically designated by CHP to issue a Form B certification on behalf of CHP.

20   Petitioner is simply wrong that the demurrer standard requires inferring from allegations that Sgt.

21   Dutra had been assigned to investigate Petitioner’s complaint and received Petitioner’s Form B

22   certification request that Sgt. Dutra also had been specifically designated to issue said

23   certification on behalf of CHP. Again, it does not reasonably follow that a CHP officer who was

24   assigned to investigate Petitioner’s complaint and who received Petitioner’s certification request

25   is the “head” of CHP, or has the authority to approve and issue the certification. Further, if

26   Petitioner were to amend his allegations to so state, those allegations would be contradicted by

27   the April 20, 2026 Form B certification denial letter signed by Capt. Serrato.
