PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND AND ALLEGATIONS. The Petition challenges the CHP's
refusal to execute a Federal Immigration Form I-918 Supp. B U Nonimmigrant Status
Certification (the “I-918 Form” or “I-918”). Under federal immigration law, a noncitizen
immigrant may apply for an immigration status benefit if: (I) the alien has suffered physical or
mental abuse as result of being victim of criminal activity; (II) alien possesses information
concerning criminal activity; (III) alien is being helpful in investigating criminal activity; and
(IV) violated laws of the united states or occurred in the united states. (8 U.S.C. §
1101(a)(15)(U)(i).)

The list of enumerated crimes for which this law applies is as follows: "the criminal activity
referred to in this clause is that involving one or more of the following or any similar activity in
violation of Federal, State, or local criminal law: rape; torture; trafficking; incest; domestic
violence; sexual assault; abusive sexual contact; prostitution; sexual exploitation; stalking;
female genital mutilation; being held hostage; peonage; involuntary servitude; slave trade;
kidnapping; abduction; unlawful criminal restraint; false imprisonment; blackmail; extortion;
manslaughter; murder; felonious assault; witness tampering; obstruction of justice; perjury; fraud
in foreign labor contracting (as defined in section 1351 of title 18); or attempt, conspiracy, or
solicitation to commit any of the above mentioned crimes.” (8 U.S.C. § 1101, subd.
(a)(15)(U)(iii).)

Penal Code, section 679.10(g)(1) provides in relevant part:

"“Upon the request of the victim, ..., a certifying official from a certifying entity shall certify
victim helpfulness on the Form I-918 Supplement B certification, when the victim was a victim
of a qualifying criminal activity and has been helpful, is being helpful, or is likely to be helpful
to the detection or investigation or prosecution of that qualifying criminal activity. The certifying
entity shall forward completed Form I-918 Supplemental B certification to the victim, ..."

APPICLABLE LEGAL STANDARD. "A demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the factual
allegations in a [pleading]." (Redfearn v. Trader Joe's Co. (2018) 20 Cal.App.5th 989, 996.) The
Court must determine "whether the [pleading] alleges facts sufficient to state a cause of action
[or other claim for relief] or discloses a complete defense." (Id.) The court assumes "the truth of
the properly pleaded factual allegations, facts that reasonably can be inferred from those
expressly pleaded and matters of which judicial notice has been taken." (Id.) "As a general rule
in testing a pleading against a demurrer the facts alleged in the pleading are deemed to be true,
however improbable they may be," unless the "complaint contains allegations of fact inconsistent
with attached documents, or allegations contrary to facts which are judicially noticed." (Del E.
Webb Corp. v. Structural Materials Co. (1981) 123 Cal.App.3d 593, 604.) "The court does not,
however, assume the truth of contentions, deductions or conclusions of law." (Aubry v. Tri-City
Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) Courts "give the complaint a reasonable interpretation,
reading it as a whole and its parts in their context." (Goncharov v. Uber Techs., Inc. (2018) 19
Cal.App.5th 1157, 1165.)

Petitioner alleges that he is professionally employed as a truck driver. However, he was not
driving a truck but rather a rented Tesla Model Y at the time of his 11/1/2024 arrest. (Petition
Exh. 5 at p. 2 at 00:55:25 and Exh. 6.) Petitioner was apparently returning from Oakland to his
home in Stockton, when he pulled over onto the shoulder of Interstate-580 (I-580) and took a nap
in the Tesla at a little after midnight because he was tired. (Id. at Exh. 5 at p.3 at 00:57:30.)
Two CHP officers observed the Tesla and pulled over, noting that they had not seen it on an
earlier pass of the location. (Exh. 5 at p. 2 at 00:56:07.) The officers asked if he had been
drinking, which Petitioner denied. (Exh. 5 at p. 3 at 00:58:30.) The officers requested that
Petitioner step out of the vehicle and asked him for his driver’s license. One of the officers
found an empty can of Mike’s Hard Lemonade, an alcoholic beverage, in the Tesla. Petitioner
denied that he consumed the drink while he had been parked there or previously. (Id. at Exh. 5 at
pp. 4-6 and Petition ¶ 47.) The officers then had Petitioner perform field sobriety tests. It
appears that Petitioner resisted one of the tests that involved lifting one of his legs off the ground
and holding the position for several seconds. (Id. at pp. 8-9.) One of the officers then requested
that Petitioner take a preliminary alcohol screening test but advised that Petitioner was not
required to consent to the test. Plaintiff asked what the result would be if he refused to undergo
the test and the officer responded that it would be the officer’s discretion whether to arrest
Petitioner for DUI. (Id. at p. 10.) Plaintiff refused to take the test, and the officer arrested him.
(Ibid.) Petitioner alleges that he was handcuffed, taken to a CHP facility, forced to take a blood
test to determine if he had alcohol in his system, and was held in custody until 11/5/2024 at
which time he was released without charges. He was given a document stating that he had been
detained and not arrested. (Petition ¶¶ 38-42.)

However other allegations, including Exhibit 5, which Petitioner represents to be an accurate
transcript with the arresting CHP officers in the early morning hours of 11/1/2024, do tend to
support Respondents’ argument that CHP officers did have probable cause to arrest Petitioner.
Petitioner was parked on the shoulder of an interstate highway within the City of Livermore
sleeping in his vehicle at approximately 1:00 a.m. with an open alcohol container in this car, and
he refused to submit to a preliminary alcohol test when investigating CHP officers requested he
do so. (People v. Thompson (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, 818 [“Probable cause exists when the facts
known to the arresting officer would persuade someone of ‘reasonable caution’ that the person
arrested committed a crime]; see also Veh. Code, §§ 23152(a), 23222(a), 23612(a).)

Reason Giving Cause of Action. Respondents also challenge the sufficiency of Petitioner’s
pleading as to the second, “reason giving” cause of action. The governing section of the Penal
Code provides: “If a certifying entity does not certify a Form I- 918 Supplement B certification,
they shall provide a written explanation for the denial of the Form I-918 Supplement B
certification. The written denial shall include specific details of any reasonable requests for
cooperation and a detailed description of how the victim refused to cooperate.” (Pen. Code, §
679.10, subd. (i)(2).) Respondents are correct that the only specificity the statute requires
concerns a requestor’s failure to cooeprate with reasonable requests for cooperation. Here, the
denial was not based upon Petitioner’s noncooperation, which was apparently voluntarily
provided. It is was based upon the failure to “meet the standard of qualifying criminal activity.”
(CHP Denial Letter, Pet. Ex. 1.)

As outlined above, the denial letter did provide an explanation. The underlying incident did not
fall within any of the statutorily identified crimes which would qualify the application for
certification. There is no basis for requiring more detail here, because the statute only requires
specificity as to noncooperation. Under the canon of statutory interpretation expresio unius est
exclusion alterius – the expression of one thing is the exclusion of another; thus, we read the
statute to say that such detail is required only for noncooperation, but not to other cited reasons
for rejection.

Even if such detail were required, it is immaterial here, where noncooperation was not the basis
for denial. It would appear that there are no circumstances under which Petitioner could amend
to remedy this defect. Thus, the Demurrer to the Second Cause of Action for Failure to Comply
with Reason Giving Requirement is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

Rebuttable Presumption of Helpfulness. Accepting Petitioner’s allegations that he “cooperated
with every investigative channel available to him and has never refused or failed to provide any
information or assistance reasonably requested” (Pet. ¶ 4), the allegation appears to be
immaterial, where the Petition admits that refusal to cooperate was not a reason for denial.
Petitioner’s request was denied for a wholly independent reason (no proof of qualifying criminal
activity) such that CHP did not need to consider whether Petitioner had cooperated. Thus, there
was no opportunity to apply the presumption, and it is plainly not applicable. Thus, the Third
Cause of Action has no merit as a matter of law. Petitioner cannot plead additional facts to
remedy this defect, as the reason for rejection is memorialized in the CHP letter. (Petition Ex. 1.)
Thus, the demurrer is SUSTAINED WTIHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND as to the third cause of
action.

Declaratory Relief. While Petitioner’s claim for declaratory relief may ultimately become
duplicative of the surviving cause of action, the Court cannot make that determination at this
juncture. A Petitioner may plead cumulative and even inconsistent theories of recover in the
alternative. There is no basis for requiring him to elect remedies at the pleadings stage. Thus, the
demurrer to the fourth cause of action for declaratory relief is OVERRULED.

ORDER. The CHP’s Demurrer is SUSTAINED as to the second and third causes of action,
without leave to amend, and OVERRULED as to the first and fourth causes of action, as the
Petition will turn, at least in part, on contested issues of fact.
