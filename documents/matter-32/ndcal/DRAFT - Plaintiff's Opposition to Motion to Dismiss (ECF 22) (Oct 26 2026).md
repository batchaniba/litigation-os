## I. INTRODUCTION

Defendants collapse four timed physical acts into one presumed-justified encounter, then ask the Court at Rule 12 to treat contested police-report observations as established facts and to treat prior venue dismissals as a merits adjudication. The First Amended Complaint does not permit that.

The FAC alleges four distinct intrusions, each judged on the facts known at that moment:

1. **The door.** At 00:56:43, Officer Melano opened the front passenger door of Plaintiff’s parked Tesla. FAC ¶¶ 16, 56. No warrant. No consent. No emergency articulated on the recording. The words immediately before the opening — “It’s a rental,” “It wasn’t here earlier,” “I think it’s booze” — are investigative. FAC ¶¶ 14–16, 56. Opening that door and reaching the interior is a Fourth Amendment search. *United States v. Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d 1285, 1288–90 (9th Cir. 2020).

2. **The cup.** Between 00:58:49 and 00:59:07, Officer Melano seized the cup, smelled it, and said, on the recording, that it was urine. FAC ¶¶ 17–18. The cup-as-alcohol hypothesis dissipated on Defendants’ own words.

3. **The arrest.** Officer Meza-Gonzalez told Plaintiff the preliminary alcohol screening test was not required, then said that if Plaintiff refused it would be “up to my discretion” whether to arrest him for DUI. FAC ¶¶ 21–22. Plaintiff declined at 01:11:15. Eighteen seconds later, at 01:11:33, he was in handcuffs. FAC ¶¶ 23–24. No new observation is alleged in between.

4. **The warrant.** The blood was drawn on Meza-Gonzalez’s later affidavit to Judge Amy Sekany (WAR913372, about 02:34 a.m.), not on a misread checkbox. FAC ¶¶ 25–30. Judicial deception is tested against that affidavit. *Franks v. Delaware*, 438 U.S. 154 (1978); *Chism v. Washington State*, 661 F.3d 380 (9th Cir. 2011).

The motion treats the officers’ report as a verdict. It is not. A police report identified or attached to a complaint is not a legally operative instrument whose contested recitations control at the pleading stage. *Khoja v. Orexigen Therapeutics, Inc.*, 899 F.3d 988, 998–1003 (9th Cir. 2018). The Court may look at the document. It may not convert a challenged odor, slur, or confusion characterization into an undisputed brick.

One correction belongs in the open. FAC ¶¶ 26(d) and 28 describe a DS-367 field as “Beverage Present: Yes.” The produced CHP 202 (AGO 000030) marks “BREATH ODOR OF ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGE PRESENT.” That is an odor field, not a recovered-container field. Plaintiff does not defend the mislabel. He will conform it by amendment. The odor, speech, and orientation disputes remain. A recording can impeach narration habits. It cannot smell.

The motion should be denied. If the Court finds a pleading defect Defendants have identified, leave to amend should be granted. *Lopez v. Smith*, 203 F.3d 1122, 1130–31 (9th Cir. 2000) (en banc). The Rule 12(f) strike should be denied. *Whittlestone, Inc. v. Handi-Craft Co.*, 618 F.3d 970, 973–75 (9th Cir. 2010). Plaintiff concedes he cannot recover § 1988 attorney’s fees for his own time. *Kay v. Ehrler*, 499 U.S. 432, 435–38 (1991). Costs, and fees if counsel later appears, are preserved.

Defendants’ same-day magistrate-jurisdiction declination (ECF 23) is not a stay. The Civil L.R. 7-3(a) clock still runs from the October 26 filing.

## III. LEGAL STANDARD

A complaint survives Rule 12(b)(6) when it alleges facts that, accepted as true, state a claim for relief that is plausible on its face. *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009); *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544, 570 (2007). The Court takes well-pleaded factual allegations as true and construes them in the plaintiff’s favor. A pro se civil-rights complaint is read liberally. *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89, 94 (2007) (per curiam). Legal conclusions are not entitled to the presumption. “No probable cause” is a conclusion. “Melano opened the passenger door at 00:56:43 after saying ‘I think it’s booze,’ without a warrant, without consent, and without articulating an emergency” is a fact. *Iqbal*, 556 U.S. at 678.

Incorporation-by-reference exists to keep a plaintiff from omitting a document that defeats the complaint. It is improper to assume the truth of an incorporated document when that truth is disputed and offered only to resolve a factual dispute against well-pled allegations. *Khoja*, 899 F.3d at 998–1003, 1014. Judicial notice does not cover disputed facts inside a noticed document. *Id.* at 999–1000.

If a complaint’s specific facts contradict specific facts in a non-legally-operative attached or incorporated document, the conflict is resolved for the plaintiff at pleading. *Grier v. Finjan Holdings, Inc.*, No. 21-16702, slip op. at 2 n.1 (9th Cir. Jan. 20, 2023) (mem.). A police report, a DS-367, and a CHP 202 are not legally operative instruments. The warrant is closer to operative for what it *says*. It is not operative for whether its factual recitations are true.

Defendants ask the Court to do the opposite: read the report’s odor, speech, confusion, and test characterizations as if they were undisputed, then dismiss because those characterizations add up to probable cause. That is a summary-judgment argument dressed as a pleading argument. The FAC specifically alleges those characterizations are false and points to a contemporaneous recording for sequence, speech, orientation, and the cup identification. FAC ¶¶ 20, 26–28, 50. At Rule 12 the Court accepts those allegations. It does not try the video. It does not treat the officers’ report as a verdict.

The Court may read the produced CHP 202 for the *label* of the odor field. That is why Plaintiff corrects “Beverage Present.” The Court may not, on that field, find that an odor existed.

## IV. THE FOURTH AMENDMENT SEQUENCE

The motion’s emergency-aid, Vehicle Code, search, and arrest sections (ECF 22 pts. III–IV) treat the night as one event. The Constitution does not. Each intrusion has its own clock.

### A. Count IV — Opening the passenger door is a search

**Ask.** Deny dismissal of Count IV.

Opening a vehicle door and reaching the interior is a Fourth Amendment search. Officers with reason to inquire, but without probable cause or another particularized justification, may not open the door and enter. *Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d at 1288–90. *Ngumezi* is a criminal suppression case. Its search holding is the holding that matters here. It is not an emergency-aid case, and Plaintiff does not ask it to be one.

A warrantless search is presumptively unreasonable. The motion’s exception is emergency aid, cited through *Mincey v. Arizona*, 437 U.S. 385 (1978). *Mincey* is a homicide-scene home case. The standard the Court should apply is *Brigham City v. Stuart*, 547 U.S. 398, 403–04 (2006): officers may enter without a warrant when they have an **objectively reasonable basis to believe an occupant is seriously injured or imminently threatened with serious injury**. Subjective motive does not control. The question is what the facts known **at the moment of entry** would justify.

On the FAC, those facts are:

- A parked, intact Tesla on a freeway shoulder. FAC ¶¶ 11, 56.
- An occupant in a normal resting posture. FAC ¶ 56.
- No crash, fire, smoke, debris, or observed medical distress. FAC ¶ 56.
- Pre-entry words: rental; the car “wasn’t here earlier”; “I think it’s booze.” FAC ¶¶ 14–16, 56.
- Door opened at 00:56:43. No consent. No warrant. No emergency articulated. FAC ¶¶ 16, 56.

That is an investigative hypothesis about a cup, formed before any word to Plaintiff, followed by an unconsented opening of the cabin. It is not an objectively reasonable basis to believe he was seriously injured. The first welfare question the recording captures comes after the door is already open. The FAC’s Count IV does not depend on proving a negative about later FSTs, later odor claims, or a later urine identification. Those facts did not exist yet.

Defendants say a sleeping or unconscious driver on a freeway shoulder is itself the emergency. ECF 22 pt. III.A. The FAC alleges a parked, intact car and a person resting after fatigue — the conduct CHP tells drowsy motorists to do. FAC ¶¶ 11–12, 56. Presence on a shoulder, without more, is not serious injury or imminent serious injury. If it were, every legal rest on I-580 would authorize a cabin entry.

They also say suspected violation of Vehicle Code § 21718 (stopping on a freeway) or an apparent “booze” cup authorized the door. ECF 22 pt. III.B. A parking or stopping theory, if one existed, is not authority to open a passenger door and enter the interior. *Devenpeck v. Alford*, 543 U.S. 146 (2004), is about the offense-of-arrest *label*. It does not expand search exceptions. Sight of an ambiguous cup through glass is not probable cause to empty the cabin. Looking through a window from a lawful vantage is one thing. Crossing the threshold is another. *Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d at 1288–90.

Meza-Gonzalez is alleged as present and non-intervening. FAC ¶ 58. That is enough to keep him in Count IV at the pleading stage. If the Court wants a more particular integral-participant allegation, that is a leave issue, not a with-prejudice dismissal.

Plaintiff will, if leave is needed, conform paragraph 16 to the recording’s knock / “Open the window” / door order. See § IX. He does not ask the Court to decide Count IV on a line he is prepared to particularize. He asks the Court to decide it on the facts the FAC already pleads: an unconsented, warrantless opening of the cabin, after investigative words, with no articulated emergency.

### B. Count V — The cup and the continuing search

**Ask.** Deny dismissal of Count V as to the reach-and-seize of the cup.

After the door was open, Melano reached in, seized the cup, smelled it, and identified urine. FAC ¶¶ 17–18, 60. No consent. No warrant. If the door was an unjustified search, the cup seizure is its fruit. Even if some exception authorized *looking*, it does not automatically authorize handling the cup.

The officers’ own identification matters for what happened next. At 00:58:51–00:59:07 the cup-as-alcohol fact was gone. FAC ¶ 18. A continuing warrantless search of the cabin after that identification required some independent justification. FAC ¶ 61. The FAC alleges none was present.

Plaintiff does not argue that urine made every later investigative step impossible. He argues the cup-as-alcohol hypothesis dissipated, and that contested later facts — odor, slur, confusion, test administration — cannot be assumed true against specific FAC contradictions in order to save the search. *Khoja*, 899 F.3d at 998–1003.

### C. Count III — The arrest

**Ask.** Deny dismissal of Count III. Probable cause is not a Rule 12 finding on this FAC.

A warrantless arrest requires probable cause — facts and circumstances known to the officer sufficient for a prudent person to believe the suspect has committed a crime. *Devenpeck*, 543 U.S. at 152. The moment is 01:11:15–01:11:33. FAC ¶¶ 24, 50.

What the FAC says was known then:

- Plaintiff had been parked and asleep when contacted. FAC ¶ 50.
- The only suspected “beverage” had been identified as urine. FAC ¶¶ 18, 50.
- He had spoken coherently and identified his location. FAC ¶¶ 20, 50.
- No officer had verbalized an alcohol odor. FAC ¶¶ 20, 50.
- Melano had declined certain FSTs because of the ground. FAC ¶¶ 19, 50.
- Plaintiff had declined a voluntary PAS. FAC ¶¶ 21–24, 50.
- No new observation intervened between the refusal and the cuffs. FAC ¶¶ 24, 50.

The motion’s answer is a report-based totality: sleep, plus the cup, plus odor, slur, confusion, and tests as the officers later wrote them. ECF 22 pt. IV. Three problems.

First, at the arrest moment the cup is already urine. Defendants may not stack “booze cup” as a current fact. Officers may not disregard facts that undermine probable cause. *Sialoi v. City of San Diego*, 823 F.3d 1223, 1233 (9th Cir. 2016); *United States v. Lopez*, 482 F.3d 1067 (9th Cir. 2007). Urine identification dissipates the cup. It is not a magic wand for an odor they are alleged not to have had.

Second, odor, slurred speech, and disorientation are specifically contested. FAC ¶ 26(a)–(c). The FAC does not ask the Court to find “the camera smelled nothing.” It alleges the officers narrated the cup, the rental, and the urine, and never narrated an odor, and that the speech and orientation characterizations are contradicted by what the recording actually captures — a spelled surname, Livermore, highway signs, CDL protocol. FAC ¶¶ 20, 26. Those are specific-versus-specific conflicts. They are resolved for Plaintiff at pleading. *Khoja*, 899 F.3d at 1003; *Grier*, slip op. at 2 n.1. The Court cannot build probable cause out of the very recitations the FAC says are false.

Third, a voluntary PAS refusal is not probable cause. Vehicle Code § 23612(i). Meza-Gonzalez said so: “You’re not required to do this.” FAC ¶ 21. Refusal to consent to an investigative search does not create reasonable suspicion or probable cause. *Thomas v. Dillard*, 818 F.3d 864, 884–85 (9th Cir. 2016). The FAC already pleads that citation. The eighteen-second sequence, and the “discretion” condition at 01:11:03, are the opposite of a new observation. FAC ¶¶ 22–24.

A cup of urine is not an element of Vehicle Code § 23152(a). Sleeping on a shoulder after fatigue, as the FAC pleads for a CDL driver, is an innocent explanation. *District of Columbia v. Wesby*, 583 U.S. 48 (2018), does not require officers to rule out every innocent account. It also does not convert an innocent account into affirmative evidence of intoxication once the alcohol-cup theory is gone. The motion’s “asleep plus urine cup equals probable cause” syllogism (ECF 22 p. 10) leaves them on odor and FSTs. Those are the facts the FAC disputes.

The Penal Code § 851.6 certificate and the District Attorney’s declination are corroborative color. FAC ¶¶ 32–34, 51. They are not a legal finding of no probable cause, and Plaintiff does not offer them as one. Later innocence does not decide probable cause at the time. The FAC already says that. FAC ¶ 33.

## V. COUNT I — JUDICIAL DECEPTION

**Ask.** Deny dismissal of Count I. In the alternative, grant leave to plead the actual Sekany affidavit and a completed *Chism* table. Do not dismiss because the FAC said “DS-367 and arrest report” instead of “Sekany affidavit.”

An officer violates the Fourth Amendment by a warrant affidavit with deliberate or reckless false statements or material omissions if the affidavit, stripped of the falsehoods and supplemented with the omitted facts, would not support probable cause. *Franks*, 438 U.S. at 155–56; *Chism*, 661 F.3d at 386–89. *Chism* is a summary-judgment “substantial showing” case. At Rule 12, Plaintiff must plead particular falsity, a nonconclusory basis for recklessness, and a remainder theory. He has.

The blood draw is a Fourth Amendment bodily intrusion. *Missouri v. McNeely*, 569 U.S. 141, 148 (2013). The FAC alleges it issued on papers that contradicted the recording the affiant had just lived through. FAC ¶¶ 25–30.

The motion never reconstructs the actual warrant. It treats arrest-report disputes as warrant materiality. That is a defect in the motion, not in the claim. The produced affidavit is Meza-Gonzalez’s statement to Judge Amy Sekany, WAR913372, about 02:34 a.m. The FAC’s four contradictions and the urine omission are aimed at the papers that produced the blood. If the caption said DS-367, the cure is to name the affidavit. That is leave, not prejudice.

On the produced affidavit, a corrected-affidavit exercise keeps at least these remainder facts unless a specific, supported correction exists: a gray vehicle on the freeway shoulder; a driver who had been asleep; an orange 21-and-over wristband; a knock; a statement that he was okay; a “parking lot” answer; travel from a party; that he was alone; that he **denied** drinking (already in the affidavit — it cannot be an omission); claimed odor from the vehicle and the person; tests not performed as instructed; arrest about 01:12; chemical-test refusal. The affidavit does not claim the cup was alcohol. The urine identification is a candidate **omission** if known and material.

Plaintiff does not hide that remainder. He also does not concede it. At Rule 12, well-pleaded falsity of the odor, speech, and orientation statements, plus a known urine omission, is enough to deny the motion or to take leave. Materiality after a completed *Franks* correction is a finding for a developed record. *Chism* itself is summary judgment. *Galbraith v. County of Santa Clara*, 307 F.3d 1119, 1126 (9th Cir. 2002), and *Hervey v. Estes*, 65 F.3d 784, 789 (9th Cir. 1995) — the motion’s own cases — do not authorize dismissing a particularized deception claim because the officers later wrote a longer narrative.

Two corrections, stated once.

The “Beverage Present” characterization in FAC ¶¶ 26(d) and 28 is a misread of a **breath-odor** field (AGO 000030; DS-367 “Odor of alcoholic beverage,” AGO 000040). Plaintiff will amend the label. He does not abandon the odor dispute. He will not argue that silence on a recording proves the absence of an odor. He will argue the officers narrated what they chose to narrate, never narrated an odor, and that whether the odor statement is true is a fact question the Court cannot resolve against him on this motion. *Khoja*, 899 F.3d at 1003.

The warrant is not a Whitman custody review and not the CHP 202. Count I is a deception claim about Meza-Gonzalez’s Sekany affidavit. Keep it that.

## VI. COUNT II — FABRICATION

**Ask.** Do not dismiss Count II on the ground that “no charges were filed.” That is not what *Caldwell v. City of Albany*, 889 F.3d 1105 (9th Cir. 2018), holds. Preserve the count as an alternative statement of the warrant and detention injury.

*Caldwell* involved a charged, convicted, long-imprisoned plaintiff. It is not a holding that a fabrication claim dies if the prosecutor never files. Model Jury Instruction 9.38 is not a holding of this Court.

The blood draw, the seizure, and the warrant are Fourth Amendment injuries. FAC ¶¶ 29–31, 36. The FAC also alleges liberty and property from roadside and jail custody, a parole hold, and a CDL APS suspension. FAC ¶¶ 31, 36–37. Those can be pleaded as Fourth Amendment or due-process deprivations. They are not automatically a freestanding Fourteenth Amendment fabrication win, and Plaintiff does not spend this brief trying to make them one.

If the Court wants the fabrication count named more tightly to the blood-draw and detention injury, or folded into Counts I and III, that is leave. It is not a reason to dismiss with prejudice on a misread of *Caldwell*.

## VII. COUNT VI — RETALIATION

**Ask.** Deny dismissal of Count VI on the no-probable-cause path the FAC already pleads in the alternative. FAC ¶ 69. If the Court assumes probable cause, grant leave to particularize protected speech — or decide the count on the FAC as written. Do not dismiss with prejudice on a but-for argument that ignores the eighteen-second sequence.

A retaliatory-arrest claim requires protected activity, adverse action, and causation. *Nieves v. Bartlett*, 587 U.S. 391, 398–99 (2019). Generally, probable cause defeats the claim. *Id.* at 398–407. A narrow exception remains for objective evidence that similarly situated people who did not engage in the protected conduct were not arrested. *Id.* at 407. The exception does not require a virtually identical comparator. It still requires objective evidence, not timing alone. *Gonzalez v. Trevino*, 602 U.S. 653 (2024).

The FAC’s primary path does not need the exception. It alleges there was no probable cause. FAC ¶¶ 50, 69. On that path, *Nieves*’s bar is not the issue. The FAC then alleges: a statutory right to refuse a voluntary PAS, Vehicle Code § 23612(i); an advisement that the test was not required, 01:10:54; a statement that arrest would be “up to my discretion” if he refused, 01:11:03; a refusal at 01:11:15; and cuffs at 01:11:33 with no new observation. FAC ¶¶ 21–24, 66–68. That is *Mt. Healthy* pleading of but-for causation. The motion’s *Ford* / *Bostock* discussion does not erase those timestamps.

The motion says the advisement was lawful, so the arrest cannot be retaliatory. ECF 22 pt. V. The fight is not whether PAS is voluntary. The FAC already quotes the voluntary line. FAC ¶ 21. The fight is the eighteen-second arrest and the discretion condition. A correct advisement does not license an arrest *because* the driver took the officer up on it. *Thomas*, 818 F.3d at 884–85.

## VIII. QUALIFIED IMMUNITY

**Ask.** Deny or defer qualified immunity count by count. *Keates v. Koile*, 883 F.3d 1228, 1234–35 (9th Cir. 2018). Take the well-pleaded facts as true. Ask whether those facts plausibly allege a violation of clearly established law in the specific context. If the complaint contains even one harmful act that would violate a clearly established right, the plaintiff goes forward. A Rule 12 denial says little about summary judgment.

The cutoff date is November 1, 2024. The motion decides the night at a high level of generality: asleep on a freeway, some tests, no alcohol in the cup. ECF 22 pt. VI. *Wesby*, 583 U.S. at 63–64, requires the opposite: particularized, pre-incident law.

**Count IV.** Opening a passenger door and entering the interior was a search under *Ngumezi* as of 2020. 980 F.3d at 1288–90. *Brigham City*’s serious-injury standard was thirty years older. 547 U.S. at 403–04. On the FAC — investigative words, no articulated emergency, intact car, unconsented opening — no exception is well-pleaded. Defendants did not distinguish *Ngumezi*. They cited *Mincey*. The Court should not decide the door claim under an arrest-probable-cause standard, and it should not grant immunity at Rule 12 because the sequence is disputed. If they want a sleeping-motorist distinction, they can write it. Plaintiff does not claim *Ngumezi* ended the clearly-established inquiry by itself. He claims the FAC’s sequence, taken as true, states a search without a well-pleaded exception, and *Keates* takes that sequence as true.

**Count V.** If the door claim survives, the cup seizure is its fruit or an independent warrantless handling. Deny qualified immunity with the entry claim.

**Count III.** Arguable probable cause is their best qualified-immunity argument. It still depends on treating contested odor, speech, confusion, and test facts as true. At Rule 12 those facts are not true. They are the dispute. The Court cannot grant immunity by resolving that dispute for the officers.

**Count I.** If reckless or deliberate material falsehood is well-pleaded, *Franks* and *Chism* are the clearly established path. Keep the claim a deception claim. A “the warrant lacked probable cause on its face” theory is harder, and it is not the claim.

**Count II.** Secondary. Do not rest immunity here.

**Count VI.** Do not rest immunity here. The no-probable-cause alternative travels with Count III. A probable-cause-present speech claim is the harder qualified-immunity question, and it is not the one this brief needs to win tonight.

## X. THE RULE 12(f) STRIKE, FEES, AND PUNITIVE DAMAGES

**Ask.** Deny the Rule 12(f) motion. Rule 12(f) does not authorize striking a damages claim because the damages are precluded as a matter of law. That is a Rule 12(b)(6) or Rule 56 job. *Whittlestone*, 618 F.3d at 973–75. *Bureerong* and *Wilkerson*, the district cases the motion cites, are the move *Whittlestone* rejected.

**Fees.** A pro se litigant is not entitled to § 1988 attorney’s fees for his own time — including a pro se lawyer. *Kay*, 499 U.S. at 435–38. Plaintiff concedes that. The FAC prayer already says “to the extent recoverable.” FAC Prayer C. He reserves taxable costs. He reserves fees if counsel later appears. *Kay* is about self-representation. It is not about costs.

**Punitive damages.** Punitive damages against an individual § 1983 defendant require reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights, or evil motive. *Smith v. Wade*, 461 U.S. 30 (1983). The FAC alleges knowing or reckless falsity in the papers that produced a blood warrant, and an arrest eighteen seconds after a voluntary refusal the officer had conditioned on “discretion.” FAC ¶¶ 22–28, 38–42. In the Eastern District, Plaintiff withdrew a punitive request **without prejudice**. He acknowledges that. He maintains punitive damages here on the deception and knowing-falsity facts. If the Court wants that relationship pleaded in a separate paragraph, that is leave. It is not a 12(f) strike.
