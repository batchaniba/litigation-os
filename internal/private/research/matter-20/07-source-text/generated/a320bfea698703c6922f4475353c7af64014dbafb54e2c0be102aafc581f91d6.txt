 1           As to the “not a certifying official” argument raised by Respondents Paul and Dutra, it is

 2   independently meritless. As a threshold matter, the Petition does not merely support an inference

15                                      II. STANDARD OF REVIEW

16

17           A demurrer tests whether the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause of

18   action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) In ruling on a demurrer, the court must “assume the

19   truth of all facts properly pleaded by the plaintiff, as well as those that are judicially noticeable.” (

20   Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) The court “give[s] the complaint a reasonable

21   interpretation, reading it as a whole and its parts in their context.” (Ibid.) A complaint need not

22   plead facts in the precise language of the statute; it suffices if the facts alleged support a

23   reasonable inference that each element of the claim is met. (Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title

24   Guaranty Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26, 38-39 [“[w]e look to whether the factual allegations, liberally

25   construed, state a cause of action on any available legal theory”].)

26

27           “[A] demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally

28   construed, can reasonably be considered to state a cause of action on any theory, or if there is a

                                                    Page 3
            OPPOSITION TO INDIVIDUAL RESPONDENTS' DEMURRERS — NEWANFORBI v. CHP, ET AL.
 1   reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment.” (Quelimane Co., supra, 19

 2   Cal.4th at p. 38, citing Blank v. Kirwan, supra, 39 Cal.3d at p. 318.) The burden is on the

 3   demurring party to show that the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of

 4   action. (Ibid.)

 5

 6           On demurrer, the Court may also consider matters subject to judicial notice (Evid. Code, §

 7   452) and documents incorporated by reference in the pleading. (Barnett v. Fireman's Fund Ins.

 8   Co. (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 500, 505 [“a demurrer reaches exhibits attached to or incorporated by

 9   reference in the complaint”].) The Verified Petition incorporates fourteen exhibits, including the

10   April 20, 2026 denial letter (Exhibit 1), the dashcam transcript (Exhibit 5), the CHP arrest report

11   (Exhibit 6), and the Penal Code § 851.6 detention certificate (Exhibit 7). These are part of the

12   pleading and accepted as true on demurrer.

13

14                                           III. ARGUMENT

15

16           Penal Code § 679.10(b) defines “certifying official” as: (1) the head of the certifying

17   entity; (2) a person in a supervisory role who has been specifically designated by the head of the

18   certifying entity to issue Form I-918 Supplement B certifications on behalf of that agency; (3) a

19   judge; or (4) any other certifying official defined under § 214.14(a)(2) of Title 8 of the Code of

20   Federal Regulations. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (b)(1)-(4).)

21

22           The question on demurrer is not whether the Petition recites these categories verbatim. It

23   is whether the Petition alleges facts from which the Court can reasonably infer that each

24   individual Respondent falls within one or more of these categories. (Quelimane Co., supra, 19

25   Cal.4th at pp. 38-39.) Here, the Petition does more than support an inference: Pet. ¶¶ 14, 15, and

26   16 each expressly allege that the respondent “is a 'certifying official' within the meaning of Penal

27   Code § 679.10(b)” — using the statute's own operative phrase. As shown below, the Petition also

28   independently pleads the specific supporting facts for each Respondent, backed by exhibits

                                                   Page 4
            OPPOSITION TO INDIVIDUAL RESPONDENTS' DEMURRERS — NEWANFORBI v. CHP, ET AL.
 1   attached to and incorporated by reference in the Petition itself.

 3   A. Respondent Serrato Is a Certifying Official Under

 4   § 679.10(b)(1) and/or (b)(2)

 5

 6           Respondent Serrato's challenge to the Petition is the weakest of the three — so weak that

 7   Serrato's own MPA does not even raise it. Unlike Respondents Paul and Dutra, whose MPAs

 8   each contain a section demurring “for one additional reason” (that the Petition does not

 9   adequately allege certifying-official status), Serrato's MPA contains no such section. Instead, it

10   acknowledges that “Petitioner alleges that Capt. Serrato is a 'certifying official' within the

11   meaning of Penal Code section 679.10(b) and has attached to his petition the certification denial

12   letter signed by Capt. Serrato.” (Serrato MPA at 2:20-24.) The Court should overrule Serrato's

13   demurrer on this ground alone: the argument was not raised and is therefore waived.

14

15           Even if the argument were before the Court, it would fail on the merits. Pet. ¶ 14 alleges

16   that Serrato, as Captain and Commander of the CHP Dublin Area, “authored, signed, and issued

17   the April 20, 2026 denial letter on CHP Dublin Area letterhead under his name and title,”

18   “exercises supervisory and final decisional authority over certifications processed out of the

19   Dublin Area office,” and — in the statute's own words — “is a 'certifying official' within the

20   meaning of Penal Code section 679.10(b).” (Pet., ¶ 14.) The denial letter itself, attached to the

21   Petition as Exhibit 1, bears Serrato's signature and title.

22

23           These facts establish certifying-official status under multiple subsections of § 679.10(b).

24   As the area-level Commander exercising “final decisional authority” over certifications, Serrato is

25   the functional head of the certifying entity at the operational unit level, satisfying subdivision

26   (b)(1). At minimum, he is a person in a supervisory role who exercises certification authority on

27   behalf of CHP, satisfying subdivision (b)(2). And Serrato's actual conduct — personally signing a

28   certification denial under his name and title — is itself the strongest possible evidence that he

            OPPOSITION TO INDIVIDUAL RESPONDENTS' DEMURRERS — NEWANFORBI v. CHP, ET AL.
 1   holds the statutory role. A respondent who has exercised the very authority the Petition seeks to

 2   compel cannot credibly claim he lacks that authority.

 4           Respondents' own proposed order further confirms Serrato's status. The proposed order

 5   seeks to sustain the demurrers on the ground that certification is discretionary, not ministerial — a

 6   merits argument that presupposes Serrato is the official exercising the discretion in question.

 7   Respondents cannot simultaneously argue that Serrato exercised certification discretion and that

 8   he is not a certifying official.

10   B. Respondent Paul Is a Certifying Official Under

11   § 679.10(b)(2)

12

13           As a threshold matter, Pet. ¶ 15 does not stop at the facts Respondent Paul quotes in his

14   MPA — it goes on to expressly allege, in the statute's own words, that Paul “is a 'certifying

15   official' within the meaning of Penal Code section 679.10(b).” That allegation, standing alone,

16   must be accepted as true on demurrer. Respondent Paul's MPA does not address it, quoting only

17   the sentence that precedes it.

18

19           Respondent Paul argues that the Petition alleges only that he “is a CHP supervisory

20   official with responsibility for processing the certification request” (Pet., ¶ 15) and that this does

21   not satisfy any of the four categories in § 679.10(b). (Paul MPA at 2:24-3:5.) This argument

22   ignores the full scope of the allegations and misapplies the demurrer standard.

23

24           The Petition alleges far more than a bare label. Pet. ¶ 15 alleges that Paul “is the official

25   who acknowledged receipt of Petitioner's April 9, 2026 certification request by return e-mail on

26   April 10, 2026, and who is identified in the April 20, 2026 denial letter as the contact point for

27   'any additional questions.'” These are specific factual allegations — not conclusions —

28   establishing that Paul personally received, processed, and was the designated point of contact for

                                                    Page 6
            OPPOSITION TO INDIVIDUAL RESPONDENTS' DEMURRERS — NEWANFORBI v. CHP, ET AL.
 1   the certification request. Combined with the allegation that Paul is “a CHP supervisory official”

 2   (Lieutenant), these facts support the reasonable inference that Paul was specifically designated by

 3   CHP to process Form I-918, Supplement B certifications, satisfying § 679.10(b)(2).

 4

 5           The April 10, 2026 email from Lt. Paul — attached to the Petition as Exhibit 13 and

 6   incorporated by reference, and referenced at Pet. ¶ 15 — confirms this inference. In that email,

 7   Paul acknowledged receipt of the certification request and represented that he would ensure

 8   Petitioner was notified “by written correspondence.” This is not the conduct of an uninvolved

 9   bystander; it is the conduct of an official who has been designated to process certifications and

10   who is performing the function the statute assigns to a certifying official. On demurrer, the Court

11   must draw this reasonable inference in Petitioner's favor, and it need not draw any inference at all

12   as to the email's authenticity or contents: Exhibit 13 is part of the pleading itself. (Blank v.

13   Kirwan, supra, 39 Cal.3d at p. 318; Barnett v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Co., supra, 90 Cal.App.4th at

14   p. 505.)

15

16           Paul's argument also proves too much. If a CHP lieutenant who personally receives a

17   certification request, acknowledges it, processes it, and is identified in the denial letter as the

18   contact person is not a “certifying official,” then the statute's inclusion of designated supervisory

19   officials in subdivision (b)(2) is rendered a nullity. The Court should reject any construction that

20   makes statutory language surplusage. (People v. Pieters (1991) 52 Cal.3d 894, 919 [courts should

21   give effect to every word of a statute].)

23   C. Respondent Dutra Is a Certifying Official Under

24   § 679.10(b)(2) or, Alternatively, (b)(4)

25

26           As a threshold matter, Pet. ¶ 16 does not stop at the facts Respondent Dutra quotes in his

27   MPA — it goes on to expressly allege, in the statute's own words, that Dutra “is a 'certifying

28   official' within the meaning of Penal Code section 679.10(b).” That allegation, standing alone,

                                                    Page 7
            OPPOSITION TO INDIVIDUAL RESPONDENTS' DEMURRERS — NEWANFORBI v. CHP, ET AL.
 1   must be accepted as true on demurrer. Respondent Dutra's MPA does not address it, quoting only

 2   the sentence that precedes it.

 3

 4          Respondent Dutra argues that the Petition alleges only that he is “the assigned investigator

 5   and designated recipient on the Supplement B” (Pet., ¶ 16) and that this does not satisfy any of

 6   the four statutory categories. (Dutra MPA at 2:25-3:5.) This argument fails for several reasons.

 7

 8          First, the Petition alleges that Dutra is “the investigator assigned to CHP Citizen

 9   Complaint File No. 390.18437.17620 by the March 26, 2026 correspondence of Respondent

10   Serrato” and is “the official to whom Petitioner submitted the formal certification request on

11   April 9, 2026.” (Pet., ¶ 16.) These allegations establish that Dutra was formally designated by

12   Captain Serrato — a command-level officer — to handle the investigation and receive the

13   Supplement B submission. The March 26, 2026 Complaint Assignment Letter documenting this

14   formal assignment is attached to the Petition as Exhibit 11 and incorporated by reference — it is

15   part of the pleading itself, not extrinsic evidence. (Barnett v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Co., supra, 90

16   Cal.App.4th at p. 505.)

17

18          This formal designation by a command-level supervisor satisfies § 679.10(b)(2), which

19   covers “a person in a supervisory role who has been specifically designated by the head of the

20   certifying entity to issue Form I-918 Supplement B certifications on behalf of that agency.” Dutra

21   was specifically designated by the area commander to handle the complaint file and receive the

22   Supplement B; he was the official to whom Petitioner submitted the form. On demurrer, these

23   facts support the reasonable inference that Dutra's assignment encompassed the processing of the

24   certification request. (Blank v. Kirwan, supra, 39 Cal.3d at p. 318.)

25

26          Second, the Petition's characterization of Dutra as the “designated recipient on the

27   Supplement B” is itself significant. The word “designated” — drawn from the Petition's factual

28   narrative, not from a bare statutory label — mirrors the operative language of subdivision (b)(2)

                                                   Page 8
            OPPOSITION TO INDIVIDUAL RESPONDENTS' DEMURRERS — NEWANFORBI v. CHP, ET AL.
 1   (“specifically designated”). A respondent who is the “designated recipient” of the very form at

 2   issue in this litigation occupies precisely the role the statute contemplates.

 3

 4             Third, alternatively, Dutra may qualify under subdivision (b)(4) as a “certifying official

 5   defined under Section 214.14(a)(2) of Title 8 of the Code of Federal Regulations,” which broadly

 6   defines certifying officials to include law enforcement officers who have responsibility for the

 7   investigation of qualifying criminal activity. Dutra was assigned as the investigator on the

 8   underlying complaint file and was the point of submission for the Supplement B. These facts

 9   support a reasonable inference under (b)(4) as well.
