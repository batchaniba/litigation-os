## 1. Executive Summary

1. **The answer is procedurally defective in several ways that work in your favor.**
   - It invokes **CCP § 431.30(d)**, the subsection that permits a *general* denial when the complaint is **unverified**. Your complaint is verified, so § 431.30(e) controls, and denials must be made positively or on information and belief.
   - About **22 paragraphs** are answered with **"can neither admit nor deny,"** which is neither a denial nor a statement that Ross lacks information or belief.
   - **Complaint ¶¶ 1–21, 85–86 and 129 are not answered at all.** These include ¶ 10 (operation of the store and employment of DOE 1), ¶ 12 (DOE 1 was loss-prevention personnel) and ¶ 15 (course and scope of employment).
   - Under **CCP § 431.20(a)**, material allegations that are not controverted are taken as true.
2. **Ross made major admissions.** It admits:
   - you entered the Sunnyvale store on the night of Aug 25, 2026;
   - no receipt was requested; you were not searched; police were not called;
   - you were never accused of taking clothing or any other Ross merchandise;
   - no employee said anyone saw you take anything; no Ross merchandise was recovered; no EAS alarm sounded;
   - **the sole asserted basis for the stop was the beverage you carried into the store in open view five minutes after buying it next door** (¶ 57);
   - the cashier witnessed the encounter, and cameras covered the entrance and "captured the incident."

   Together, these admissions go most of the way toward defeating the probable-cause element of the merchant's privilege.
3. **All 16 affirmative defenses are boilerplate.** They plead no facts, as *FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima* (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367 requires. Several are negligence or premises defenses that do not fit intentional-tort or Unruh claims: accidental injury, assumption of risk, comparative negligence, lack of notice, and Prop 51 apportionment. Only the **First (shopkeeper's privilege)** is a real defense, and even it is pleaded without facts.
4. **Answer-related deadline:** the safe date to file a demurrer or motion to strike is **Monday, Oct 26, 2026**. A meet-and-confer is required first under § 430.41 (and § 435.5 for a motion to strike). If you cannot finish it in time, file a § 430.41(a)(2) declaration by **Oct 26**, which automatically extends the deadline 30 days to **Wed, Nov 25, 2026**.
5. **Discovery responses:** all three sets are due **Tuesday, Nov 17, 2026** (the conservative email-service computation). The latest arguable date, using the mail-service extension and Ross's own 35-day instruction, is Nov 19. Calendar Nov 17.
6. **Strategy recommendation:** do **not** rush a demurrer aimed at the unanswered paragraphs, because it would simply prompt Ross to cure them. Use them as admissions and lock them in with Requests for Admission. Instead, **(a)** send a meet-and-confer letter covering the defects and the defenses, **(b)** if needed, file a targeted demurrer or motion to strike against the inapplicable boilerplate defenses, and **(c)** serve your own discovery now. That discovery should include **Form Interrogatory 15.1** (facts behind each denial and defense), DOE 1's identity, the video, loss-prevention policies, and stop logs.

## 2. The Answer: Compliance with CCP §§ 431.20 and 431.30

### 2.1 Governing rules
- **§ 431.30(b):** an answer must contain a general or specific denial of the material allegations it controverts, plus a statement of any new matter constituting a defense.
- **§ 431.30(d):** a *general* denial is permitted **only if the complaint is not verified** (limited civil cases aside).
- **§ 431.30(e):** when the complaint is verified, denials must be made **positively or according to the information and belief** of the defendant. A defendant without enough information or belief may deny on that ground, but it has to *say so*.
- **§ 431.30(g):** defenses must be separately stated and must refer to the causes of action they are intended to answer.
- **§ 431.20(a):** every material allegation of the complaint that the answer does not controvert is **taken as true** for purposes of the action.
- **§ 446:** an answer to a verified complaint must itself be verified. When a corporation is a party, verification may be made by **any officer** of the corporation. An attorney may verify only in limited circumstances.

## 3. Affirmative Defenses (16)

**Standard.** The answer must plead affirmative defenses with the same factual specificity as a complaint, *FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima* (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367, 384. A plaintiff may **demur** to an answer within 10 days after service for failure to state facts sufficient to constitute a defense, or for uncertainty (CCP §§ 430.20(a)–(b), 430.40(b)). A plaintiff may also **move to strike** irrelevant, false or improper matter (§§ 435–436). Mere denials labeled as "defenses," and "reservations" of future defenses, are not affirmative defenses.

**Bottom line:** 15 of the 16 defenses are generic negligence or premises-liability boilerplate, apparently from a slip-and-fall form ("accident," "conditions," "notice," "DOCUMENTS provided," "claims for costs"). Only #1 is substantive.

**Whether to challenge:**
- **Benefits:**
  - It narrows the case.
  - It forces Ross to commit to facts behind the privilege defense.
  - It removes noise from the jury instructions.
- **Costs:**
  - It takes time, and the hearing is set by the court.
  - Leave to amend is usually granted.
  - Pressing the unanswered paragraphs prompts Ross to fix them.
- **Recommended route:** a **narrow demurrer** limited to the affirmative defenses (and, if you choose, a motion to strike Defenses 2, 12, 15 and 16 and the fee prayer). Pair it with **FROG 15.1** discovery.
- **Lower-cost alternative:** skip the motion. Serve FROG 15.1 and an RFA set, then use the boilerplate later in a summary-adjudication motion or motion in limine. Do not demur to the answer's unanswered paragraphs. Keep those admissions.
