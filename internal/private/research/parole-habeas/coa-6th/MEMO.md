#### Direct relief vs. remand — verified California law

The California Supreme Court has already answered this for **Board of Parole Hearings** denials, which is the closest published analog (DAPO earned-discharge is **not** BPH life-term suitability; flag that gap).

| Case | What the court did | Use / limit |
|---|---|---|
| ***In re Prather* (2010) 50 Cal.4th 238** (SCOCAL S172903) | Default remedy after a defective **Board denial**: “a new parole-suitability hearing in accordance with due process of law and consistent with the decision of the court.” The Court **reversed** a Court of Appeal that **ordered Molina released** without further Board (or Governor) proceedings. Directing the Board to consider only “new” evidence, or to grant parole outright on a first habeas win, “materially infringe[s] upon the Board’s discretion” and raises separation of powers. | **This is the controlling default.** A first-time grant of habeas on a DAPO review should, under *Prather*’s logic, be a **new review**, not a judicial walk-off. |
| ***In re Masoner* (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 1098** (pre-*Prather*, published) | Superior court found remand to the Board would be an “**idle act**” and **ordered release**. Court of Appeal **reversed**: the idle-act finding was incorrect; the Board must get a new hearing (and may consider later-arising evidence). | **On-point against** “remand is futile so discharge him” on a **first** defective denial. |
| ***In re Masoner* (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 1531** (*Masoner II*) | Governor **reversed a Board grant**. No evidence supported the reversal. Superior court **reinstated the Board’s grant** without remanding to the Governor. Court of Appeal **affirmed**. | Direct relief is available when the **executive already granted** and a later reversal is empty. **Petitioner has no CDCR grant of discharge.** Do not cite *Masoner II* as if it were a Board-denial case. |
| ***In re Lira* (2014) 58 Cal.4th 573** (SCOCAL S204582) | Even after a court vacates an unsupported gubernatorial reversal, “the remedy is **not** an order for the inmate’s immediate release”; the court reinstates the Board grant and the Board runs its usual release process. | Another Supreme Court statement that **immediate street release** is not the ordinary habeas order even after the inmate **wins**. |
| *Prather* on a **second** defective denial | Majority: after remand, the Board is bound by the court’s conclusion that the **old** record does not support unsuitability. If the Board denies **again** on the same empty record, a later order compelling a grant “may well be warranted.” (Discussed in the *Prather* opinions / later commentary; the **holding** of *Prather* itself reversed **first-instance** release.) | User’s “prior denials” (Geffon 2025 term-length habeas; Matter 9 mandate; Matter 10 SOMP-length) are **not** a second remand on **this** Dec. 2025 review. Do not treat them as *Prather*’s second-denial trigger. |

**Futility of *exhaustion*** is a different doctrine. It lets a petitioner **skip a 602 / administrative appeal** when the agency’s position is a clearly announced policy it has consistently defended. *In re Dexter* (1979) 25 Cal.3d 921, 925 (OpenJurist / Cal.3d text): exhaustion is the general rule for prisoner grievances; futility was argued where the action tracked “clearly announced board policy” the People did not dispute. *In re Muszalski* (1975) 52 Cal.App.3d 500: People must prove an available efficacious administrative remedy. *Ogo Associates v. City of Torrance* (1974) 37 Cal.App.3d 830, 834 — **UNVERIFIED slip this pass**; commonly cited for mandate exhaustion futility. **None of those cases hold that a court, having found a parole decision defective, may skip the new hearing and discharge.** They are for “I don’t have to file another 602.” Matter 19 is already in court. Using *Dexter* to jump from “Policy 19-03 is a blanket” to “therefore the court must discharge me” is a **category error**. Say so in anything he files, or the AG will.

*In re Sturm* (1974) 11 Cal.3d 258 (SCOCAL): due process requires a written statement of reasons sufficient for habeas review. Remedy in *Sturm* was **not** a judicial grant of parole (the petitioner was already on parole by the time of decision; OSC discharged). Use *Sturm* for **reasons**, not for discharge.

*Wilkinson v. Dotson* (2005) 544 U.S. 74, 82 (Cornell LII): success on a **procedural** parole challenge is “at most a new parole hearing,” which “would not necessarily spell speedier release.” Federal § 1983 / *Heck* case. Consistent with *Prather* on **shape of relief**. Do not refile as § 1983 (Matter 11 dismissed Policy 19-03 with prejudice).

*Tidewater Marine Western, Inc. v. Bradshaw* (1996) 14 Cal.4th 557, 571–76; Gov. Code, §§ 11342.600, 11340.5: unpublished standard of general application is void if used. That supports **striking Policy 19-03 as a ground** and forbidding its reuse on remand. It does **not**, by itself, compel discharge. **FLAG:** Policy 19-03 text is not in the repo — only the 1502-DR quote.

### 2.3 Why Matter 9’s 10-year holding does not foreclose this

Sacramento Super. Ct. **26WM000028** (Matter 9), Hon. James P. Arguelles: demurrer to a **CCP § 1085 mandate** sustained without leave, May 29, 2026 (tentative became the order). Two holdings, as recorded in this workspace from the 7-page tentative (`INDEX.md`; path `/documents/matter-09/CU_26WM000028_….pdf`):

1. **Vehicle.** Habeas, not mandate, because a parolee is in constructive CDCR custody. (*Ex Parte Bandmann* (1958) 51 Cal.2d 388, 396–97 — existence verified in HABEAS-OPTIONS; slip not re-pulled this pass.)
2. **No ministerial duty to discharge.** PC § 3000(b)(2)(B) (LegInfo, pulled this pass): for a crime committed on or after July 1, 2013, a person sentenced for an offense in Penal Code § 667.5(c)(3), (4), (5), (6), **(11)**, or (18) “shall be released on parole for a period of **10 years**,” unless a longer period is specified in § 3000.1. Sexual penetration by force is § 667.5(c)(11). Petitioner has not served 10 years. The court also noted it was **“not clear why CDCR undertook the early parole review process.”**

That is a holding about **mandate to compel immediate discharge** as a ministerial act. It is not a holding that:

- the November/December 2025 review complied with 15 CCR § 3574;
- Form 1502-DR was the lawful instrument;
- Policy 19-03 is a valid regulation;
- comments naming Eagan are an individualized statement of reasons;
- or that habeas cannot **vacate a defective review and order it redone**.

**Issue preclusion.** *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335, 341 (SCOCAL S011151): identity of issue, actually litigated, necessarily decided, final on the merits, same party / privity. The issue necessarily decided in Matter 9 was whether CDCR had a **ministerial duty to discharge him now** under PC §§ 3000 / 3008. The issue in a **process** habeas (wrong form, Policy 19-03, Eagan, no reasons) is not identical to “ministerial duty to discharge now.” Vacatur + **new review** is therefore not precluded. **Immediate discharge is much closer to the issue Matter 9 decided.** A court can still say habeas (abuse of discretion / due process) is not CCP § 1085, but Hawk can also say Arguelles already held the statute bars early discharge. **Plead discharge; do not pretend Matter 9 is irrelevant to prayer (1).** It is the main reason prayer (1) is weak and prayer (2) is the one that survives.

Matter 9’s dictum — CDCR *did* undertake an early review — still opens prayer (2): having elected to review, they had to review **this** file under the published procedure. The 10-year term answers “must a court walk him off today.” It does not license a rubber-stamp 1502-DR naming Eagan.

PC § 3008(d)(2) (LegInfo, pulled this pass) requires successful completion of a sex-offender management program as a *condition of parole*, with length determined by the certified professional in consultation with the parole officer and approved by the court — “up to the entire period of parole.” That is treatment duration. It is not a second holding that every discharge review may ignore § 3574. Matter **10** already told him SOMP *length* needs the clinician/agent determination first (denied w/o prejudice Apr. 27, 2026). Matter 19, framed as procedure of the **discharge review that occurred**, is not a redo of Matter 10.

**Do not** tell the Court of Appeal that Matter 9 was “wrong about the 10-year term.” Do tell it that Matter 9 does not exhaust or preclude a **process** claim, and that the pending Santa Clara petition still has work to do. That is why delay is prejudicial.

## 5. Rule 4.551 — verified from courts.ca.gov

Official text pulled October 28, 2026 from [Rule 4.551. Habeas corpus proceedings](https://courts.ca.gov/cms/rules/index/four/rule4_551). Rule last amended **July 1, 2026**.

Material lines (paraphrase only where noted; operative words are in the rule):

- **(a)(5)(A):** On filing, the clerk must immediately deliver the petition to the presiding judge or designee. **“The court must rule on a petition for writ of habeas corpus within 60 days after the petition is filed.”**
- **(a)(6):** If the court fails to rule within 60 days, the petitioner **may** file a notice and request for ruling.
- **(a)(6)(A):** The notice must include a declaration of the filing date(s), the date of the notice, and that the petitioner has not received a ruling, plus a copy of the original (and any amended) petition.
- **(a)(6)(B):** If the presiding judge or designee determines the notice is complete **and** the court has failed to rule, the PJ **must** assign the petition to a judge and **calendar the matter for a decision without appearances within 30 days** of the filing of the notice. If the assigned judge rules before that date, it may come off calendar.
- **(a)(8):** For (a)(5), the court “rules” by OSC, denial, or requesting an informal response.
- **(a)(9):** After an informal response, OSC or denial within 45 days of receipt of the response.
- **(i):** On motion or the court’s own motion, **for good cause stated in the order**, the court may shorten or extend time for any act under the rule. A copy must be mailed to each party.

**Applied:** The Jul. 2, 2026 order is a (i) extension, used once, to Aug. 1. The Sep. 1 HC-004 is the (a)(6) notice. Whether the PJ found the notice “complete” is **not in the portfolio** (UNVERIFIED — clerk file). If the PJ never assigned or calendared, that is a second breach of (a)(6)(B), not a defect in Petitioner’s notice unless the notice itself was incomplete.

## 11. Why not original Court of Appeal habeas (vehicle (b))

1. **The superior court already has the case.** *In re Hillery* (1962) 202 Cal.App.2d 293, 294 (published; text pulled from FindLaw / 20 Cal.Rptr. 759): the Court of Appeal has jurisdiction (Cal. Const., art. VI) but **discretion to refuse** original habeas when it was not first presented below, absent an extraordinary reason. Factual habeas belongs in the superior court.
2. **Higher courts may deny without prejudice** a habeas not filed first in a proper lower court. *In re Steele* (2004) 32 Cal.4th 682, 692 (SCOCAL S114551).
3. **Parole-challenge habeas is supposed to start in the conviction county superior court.** *In re Roberts* (2005) 36 Cal.4th 575; CRC 4.552(b)(2)(C). Petitioner **did that**. The defect is delay, not venue. The cure is to make that court rule, not to open a second original habeas.
4. **CRC 8.485(b)** keeps habeas out of the mandate chapter. Mixing “mandate or habeas” in one paper is how petitions get mis-docketed as civil ($775) or bounced for using the wrong form.
5. **Merits belong in Santa Clara first.** The pending habeas prays (1) immediate discharge on futility and (2) alternatively a new lawful review. Prayer (1) walks into Matter 9’s 10-year holding and into *Prather* / *Masoner I*. Prayer (2) is the stronger ask and still needs a superior-court ruling with reasons (rule 4.551(h)). Mandate preserves that court as the first-decision court.

Futility as an “extraordinary reason” under *Hillery* is a **backup sentence** in the mandate petition (the delay itself), not a reason to skip to vehicle (b) today. If the Sixth District denies mandate and Santa Clara still has not ruled, **then** revisit original habeas, using HC-001, attaching the mandate denial.
