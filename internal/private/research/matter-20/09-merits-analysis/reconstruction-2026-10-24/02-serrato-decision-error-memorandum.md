# 02 — SERRATO DECISION-ERROR MEMORANDUM

**Question:** Does the record affirmatively prove a defect in Captain G. Serrato's April 20, 2026 denial that requires vacatur — and if so, what exactly is it, on what evidence, and what is still missing?

**Short answer:** Yes — but the provable defect is narrower than prior drafts claimed, and stating it precisely is what makes it sustainable. The decision **as written** identifies no element, no facts, and no lawful-authority analysis under Penal Code § 679.10(c), (d), and (g), and it **rests on the agency's incident account**, which is materially inaccurate in the walk-and-turn respect (Form 202 AGO 000031 vs. the recording at ~01:10:14–01:10:30, subject to native-video authentication). A decision that supplies no reviewable reasoning and rests on a materially inaccurate agency record cannot be sustained on judicial review without the court supplying the reasoning the decision omitted. That is the vacatur theory. It does **not** prove that Serrato failed to consider the elements, applied a prosecution prerequisite, predicted a USCIS outcome, or conducted a sham review — and this memorandum does not claim any of those things, because the record does not prove them.

> "A thorough review of this incident was conducted. Five sentences. Everything in this memorandum is tested against those five sentences — not against what a reader wishes they said.

## 4. THE STRONGEST AFFIRMATIVELY PROVABLE DEFECT (the remand theory, stated exactly)

**The April 20, 2026 denial cannot lawfully stand in its present form because:**

(a) **On its face, it performs no statutory analysis.** It does not identify which qualifying crime was considered, which element failed, or which facts supplied lawful authority for the restraint, as § 679.10(c), (d), and (g) require the certifying official to determine. (Pet. Ex. 1.) A reviewing court cannot determine from the decision whether the correct standard was applied, which is the minimum Saleeby principle (reasons sufficient for judicial review) — invoked by analogy, not as a revived (i)(2) claim, which the August 29 order foreclosed.

(b) **It rests on the agency's incident account, and that account is materially inaccurate in a respect that goes to the determination.** The denial states "[a] thorough review of this incident was conducted." The agency's incident account is the Form 202. The Form 202 states petitioner "chose not to perform" the walk-and-turn because it was difficult (AGO 000031) and lists "poor performance on the FST's" as an arrest basis (AGO 000032). The contemporaneous recording, as transcribed (conditionally), shows petitioner expressing willingness and Officer Melano declining the test because of uneven ground (~01:10:14–01:10:30). A test the officers chose not to administer cannot have been poorly performed. The inaccuracy is material because the "poor performance" rationale is part of the stated probable-cause basis, and probable cause (lawful authority) is the contested element of the qualifying-crime determination.

(c) **The defect does not depend on Serrato's subjective knowledge.** An agency decision is not insulated from its own record's inaccuracy by the possibility that the decision-maker did not personally read the inaccurate document. The reconsideration on remand cures the defect by requiring a new decision on the corrected record by an official who does review it. No finding about what Serrato subjectively knew, read, or intended is requested or needed.

**What this defect does NOT establish (stated to prevent overreading):** It does not establish that Serrato failed to consider the elements (omission ≠ failure to consider); that any prosecution prerequisite was imposed; that USCIS eligibility was prejudged; that the review was a sham; or that certification must issue. Each of those would require evidence this record does not contain. The defect establishes vacatur + reconsideration. It does not, without more, establish direct certification — which additionally requires the D5 adjudication (see 01-matrix).

## 5. THE MINIMUM MISSING RECORD REQUIRED TO MAKE THE REMAND THEORY SUSTAINABLE

In priority order — the first item is load-bearing; the rest are required before filing, not before the theory is coherent:

1. **Authenticated native recording confirming the walk-and-turn exchange.** Without it, defect (b) is conditional and the remand theory rests on defect (a) alone (face-of-decision reviewability), which is thinner. With it, the theory has both legs.
2. **RFP No. 8 decision file (or a judicial determination that its absence does not block review).** The file would confirm or complicate defect (a): if it shows no element analysis, defect (a) strengthens from "decision as written" to "decision as made"; if it shows a reasoned analysis, the brief must answer that analysis on its merits rather than on the letter's brevity. Either way, the file converts speculation into adjudication. Calendar the compel window immediately (see 11-filing-audit).
3. **April 9 vs. June 18 transcript side-by-side.** Required before any "failure to consider submitted evidence" argument (candidate defect 5) can even be pleaded honestly — and before any transcript quotation is offered as proof of April 9 contents.
4. **Roadside-geometry pinning for § 40300.5(b).** Required for the D6 finding, not for the remand defect itself — but a remand order that directs circumstance-by-circumstance findings needs the record from which those findings will be made.

**If none of items 1–2 is obtained:** the sustainable fallback is defect (a) alone — vacatur on the ground that the decision as issued supplies no reviewable reasoning — plus a Tier 3 evidentiary proceeding to establish the walk-and-turn fact and the remaining D5 questions. That fallback is weaker than the two-legged theory but remains stronger than a direct-certification demand on an unauthenticated record, because it asks the court for process (a reasoned decision, a hearing) rather than for a compelled signature on disputed facts.
