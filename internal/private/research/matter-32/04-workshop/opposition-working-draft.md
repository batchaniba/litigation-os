## Proposed opening

Defendants' motion joins distinct events into a single presumed-justified encounter: opening a passenger door, investigating and searching a cup and vehicle, arresting plaintiff, and obtaining a blood-draw warrant. The Court should assess the factual allegations and legal justification for each intrusion separately, taking well-pleaded facts as true without converting contested police-report statements into adjudicated facts.

Plaintiff does not contend that a later decision not to prosecute proves an earlier arrest unconstitutional. Nor does plaintiff contend that a camera can record an odor. The question is whether the operative pleading identifies actionable conduct and, where the Court finds a defect, whether identified corrections and particularized allegations could cure it.

## Disputed report contents are not automatically established facts

Defendants cite alleged observations of intoxication as facts establishing probable cause. But the FAC challenges specific observations and attributes them to allegedly false reports. The Court may consider an incorporated document for appropriate purposes without accepting every disputed factual recitation as true against contrary, specific factual allegations; Khoja and Grier explain the limits of using incorporated materials to resolve disputed facts at pleading. [Khoja, 899 F.3d at 1002–03](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf); [Grier, footnote 1](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/01/20/21-16702.pdf).

This is not a request to disregard attachments wholesale. Plaintiff must distinguish what the officer wrote, whether plaintiff plausibly alleges that writing false, and whether the asserted inconsistency is actually present in the source. In particular, the produced CHP 202 contains a breath-odor field; plaintiff should not defend an inaccurate characterization of that field as evidence of a physical beverage.

## Physical entry requires its own justification

The FAC alleges that officers opened the passenger door before plaintiff's later arrest and blood-draw warrant. Ngumezi recognizes that opening a vehicle door and entering its interior is a search and that mere convenience in communicating is not sufficient justification. But that case did not decide whether emergency aid justified entry to assist a sleeping freeway driver; this opposition must address the actual alleged emergency circumstances rather than present Ngumezi as factually dispositive. [Ngumezi, slip op. at 6–11](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf).

[DRAFTING GATE: Verify exactly whether plaintiff woke or responded before opening, how long officers knocked, what objectively suggested medical distress, and whether the door opening included a physical intrusion. The preserved transcript puts “Are you okay?” after the opening; do not move that answer earlier.]

Facts learned later cannot be treated as facts already known at the moment of entry. The same temporal discipline applies in plaintiff's favor and against plaintiff: Melano's later identification of urine cannot retroactively eliminate the earlier perception of a suspicious cup, but the earlier suspicion does not by itself establish authority for every later search or arrest.

## Search and arrest require separate, time-specific analysis

Plaintiff should identify the alleged cup search, any additional interior search, removal from the vehicle, investigative detention, and arrest. The defense should identify the exception and factual basis for each challenged act, rather than treating a reasonable basis to ask questions as automatically sufficient for an interior search or custodial arrest.

The defense is entitled to rely on the totality of properly considered facts, not merely facts that are incriminating in isolation. Wesby rejects a divide-and-conquer analysis and does not require police to rule out every innocent explanation; plaintiff's medical and sleep explanations therefore need to be integrated with all supported facts, not used as an automatic veto over probable cause. [Wesby](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/15-1485_1qm2.pdf).

## Judicial deception must be tied to the actual affidavit

The produced warrant affidavit gives plaintiff a concrete source for identifying any alleged falsehood and omission. Chism analyzes deliberate or reckless deception by asking whether the affidavit, corrected and supplemented, would still supply a substantial basis for probable cause. That materiality requirement means plaintiff must retain all unaffected facts and explain why the remainder is insufficient, not simply identify one questionable statement. [Chism](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/08/25/10-35085.pdf).

The affidavit already reports plaintiff's denial of drinking. It also reports confusion, a parking-lot response, driving from a party, odor from vehicle and person, deficient test performance, and chemical-test refusal. Any proposed correction must address these actual statements with particularity and cannot infer all of them false because the cup contained urine.

## Fabrication and retaliation require their own elements

The motion's assertion that lack of criminal charges necessarily defeats every fabrication theory requires legal analysis, but a thinly cited motion does not relieve plaintiff of identifying the constitutional injury and causation. Do not assume a Fourteenth Amendment claim duplicates or escapes the requirements of a Fourth Amendment claim; research the exact precharge-deprivation authorities before making a categorical answer.

For retaliatory arrest, Nieves ordinarily requires absence of probable cause, subject to its objective-evidence exception. Gonzalez rejects an unduly narrow demand for virtually identical comparators; it does not remove the need for objective evidence supporting the exception or automatically excuse all plaintiffs from addressing probable cause. [Gonzalez v. Trevino](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-1025_1a72.pdf).

## Damages and fees should not distract from viable claims

Whittlestone rejects using Rule 12(f) simply to strike damages claims asserted to be unavailable as a matter of law. Plaintiff may preserve that procedural objection while still having to establish a legally and factually sufficient basis for punitive damages under the appropriate standard. [Whittlestone](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/08/17/09-16353.pdf).

The earlier E.D. Cal. opposition withdrew punitive damages without prejudice and withdrew pro se fees, with reservations. Plaintiff should acknowledge that history, state whether the current punitive request is maintained and why, and not seek attorney compensation for plaintiff's own pro se time.
