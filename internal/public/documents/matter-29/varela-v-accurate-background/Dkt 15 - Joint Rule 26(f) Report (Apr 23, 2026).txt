 8             1.     Statement of the case.
 9             Plaintiff: This action arises from Accurate’s reporting of inaccurate criminal record
10
         information on a consumer background check report prepared on Plaintiff for the purpose
11
12       of assessing his suitability for employment with non-party Jeronimo Logistics, LLC
13       (“Jeronimo”). In September 2025, Plaintiff applied for a position with Jeronimo. On or
14
         about September 4, 2025, Accurate issued a background check report on Plaintiff that
15
16       contained a felony conviction for which Plaintiff was released from prison in 2017—a
17       conviction older than seven years. Under California Civil Code Section 1786.18, criminal
18
         convictions older than seven years are prohibited from being reported for positions that
19
20       pay less than $125,000 per year, and Plaintiff’s position with Jeronimo did not meet this
21       salary threshold. Because of the inaccurate background check report, Plaintiff was not
22
         hired by Jeronimo. As a result, Plaintiff remained unemployed for approximately three
23
24       months before finding another position, causing him significant financial hardship,
25
         emotional distress, and reputational harm. Plaintiff asserts claims against Accurate for
26
         negligent and willful violations of the Fair Credit Reporting Act (“FCRA”), 15 U.S.C. §
27
28

     11681e(b), and the California Consumer Credit Reporting Agencies Act (“CCRAA”), Cal.
 2
      Civ. Code § 1785.14.
 3
 4          Accurate: Plaintiff claims that Accurate negligently and willfully failed to “follow

 5    reasonable procedures to ensure maximum possible accuracy of the information
 6
      concerning the individual about whom the report relates” in alleged violation of Section
 7
 8    1681e(b) of the FCRA and Section 1785.14 of the CCRAA. Accurate denies that it
 9    violated the FCRA or the CCRAA, and denies that it caused Plaintiff any damages.
10
      Accurate followed the requisite reasonable procedures at all times with respect to
11
12    Plaintiff. Accurate also denies that Plaintiff is entitled to statutory or punitive damages
13    because, at all times, Accurate engaged in good faith efforts to comply with the FCRA
14
      and the CCRAA, because Accurate’s reading of its obligations under the FCRA and the
15
16    CCRAA is and was objectively reasonable, and because any purported violations were
17    not willful under the law. Accurate further contends that, any damages available to
18
      Plaintiff must be reduced by his failure to mitigate any such damages.
19

22
      arises out of a question of federal law, in particular, the FCRA. See 15 U.S.C. § 1681p.

24    This Court has supplemental jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s CCRAA claims pursuant to 28

     1         3.    Legal issues.

               The parties identify the following key legal issues in this case:

 4             •     Whether Accurate’s reporting was inaccurate;
 5             •     Whether Accurate negligently and/or willfully violated the FCRA by not

15                   a direct and proximate cause of Plaintiff’s alleged damages;

16             •     Whether Plaintiff mitigated, or attempted to mitigate, his alleged damages;
17
               •     Whether Plaintiff is entitled to statutory, actual and/or punitive damages
