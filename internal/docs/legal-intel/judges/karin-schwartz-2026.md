## 4. Behavioral patterns — VERIFIED unless noted

1. **Merits over forfeiture.** The through-line of the audit: an unopposed
   §473(b) correction granted (Benjamin), a default set aside for attorney
   negligence (Hendricks), and — most telling — reconsideration granted under
   inherent authority to fix the *court's own* summary-adjudication error
   despite §1008 not being satisfied (Song). She does not let viable claims
   die on procedural missteps.
2. **Briefing discipline is enforced, not waived.** The Song ruling rebukes
   *both* sides: page-limit violations, unwieldy and diffuse factual
   presentations, bad exhibit citations, and making the court hunt for "the
   needle in the haystack." Precision and exact record citations are not
   optional in her courtroom.
3. **Tentatives are detailed — and provisional.** She issues full written
   tentative rulings. They are contestable: notify Dept. 20 by email at
   **Dept20@alameda.courts.ca.gov** and all parties, and click "Click to
   Contest this Ruling" in the eCourt portal's Tentative Rulings tab,
   **before 4:00 p.m. on the court day preceding the hearing** (per her own
   tentative-ruling procedure pages and the Alvarez/Landrum PDFs). Contested
   hearings proceed remotely via the court's own videoconference system.
   Landrum proves a tentative can be vacated/continued after contest-related
   developments — always be prepared to argue.
4. **Active, escalating case management.** Alvarez (discovery referee after
   noncompliance), In re City of Oakland (negotiated writ schedule, joint
   statements, remote appearances). She gives directions once, then escalates.
5. **Statutory textualist lean.** Chu denied discovery on the plain text of
   privacy statutes; New Classic Cooking rewrote a Prop 65 judgment rather
   than approving the parties' numbers. A decade-plus as a government lawyer
   shows: she reads the statute as written and checks the parties' work.
6. **Settlement posture.** Grants preliminary approval of class/PAGA
   settlements after real analysis (Huynh, $500,000); independently reviews
   Prop 65 consent judgments and modifies them (New Classic Cooking). No
   observed individual-settlement-conference practice in this sample.

## 5. Tactical read for Matter 27 — DIRECTIONAL (informed by the Gurney dossier, PR #776)

Opposing counsel Tod Gurney (shareholder, Garcia & Gurney; former Santa
Clara County Superior Court research attorney) is expected to demur — or to
take the §430.41 30-day automatic extension — on or about September 14,
2026, four days before the response deadline and the demand expiry. His
dossier predicts a bench-memo-style demurrer on four prongs. How each prong
likely lands with Judge Schwartz:

- **(a) "No employment relationship with RK."** At the pleading stage she
  tests what is *alleged*, not what is true — and Song shows she will not
  short-circuit a viable claim on a factual dispute dressed as a pleading
  defect. To the extent the complaint pleads RK's direct role (the July 2026
  background/MVR review chain: Randstad's request, RK's review, the decision
  not to move forward, transmission of the MVR), this prong is the weakest;
  expect overrule or sustain-with-leave, not a kill shot.
- **(b) "FCRA duties belong to the background-report company or the
  decision-maker, not RK."** Her textualist lean (Chu) makes this a
  statute-tracking exercise: to the extent the complaint pleads RK's own
  procurement or use of the report and an adverse action without the
  §1681b(b)(2)–(3) steps (clear disclosure/authorization; pre-adverse-action
  copy of report plus summary of rights; opportunity to dispute), the
  demurrer should fail on that prong. If the complaint blurs *who* did
  *what*, she may sustain with leave to re-plead roles distinctly.
- **(c) Uncertainty, §430.10(f).** General California law, not
  Schwartz-verified: uncertainty demurrers are disfavored and succeed only
  where the pleading is so unintelligible the defendant cannot reasonably
  respond (*Khoury v. Maly's of California* (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616).
  Nothing observed suggests she is more receptive than the norm; her Song
  impatience was with *briefing*, not complaints.
- **(d) §430.41 meet-and-confer.** Expect her to require the declaration; if
  Gurney's declaration is thin, note — without overplaying it — that
  §430.41(a)(4) bars deciding the demurrer on meet-and-confer insufficiency.
- **(e) Leave to amend vs. without leave.** DIRECTIONAL: everything observed
  about her says a first pleading defect gets leave unless the defect is
  incurable as a matter of law (e.g., time-barred on the face of the
  complaint). A WOLA ask on the FCRA causes, as Gurney's dossier predicts,
  runs against the entire grain of her observed practice.
- **(f) Opposition playbook.** Keep it short and element-by-element, with
  exact complaint-paragraph and exhibit citations — the Song lecture is the
  style guide. Lead with the merits-justice theme she herself uses. If the
  tentative is adverse, contest it before the 4:00 p.m. deadline and show up
  ready: Landrum shows the tentative can move. Do not concede the employment
  relationship as fact; frame it as a pleaded joint/putative-employer theory
  to be tested by evidence, not demurrer.
- **(g) Calendar leverage.** Even a sustained-with-leave demurrer leaves the
  December 17, 2026 CMC intact and the case alive — which is exactly the
  outcome her record predicts and exactly what keeps settlement pressure on
  past September 18.
