17   Fourth Amendment — Judicial Deception and Unlawful

22            38. Plaintiff re-alleges paragraphs 1 through 37.

24            39. An officer violates the Fourth Amendment by submitting a warrant application

25   containing statements that are deliberately or recklessly false, or that omit material facts, if the
26   application ~ stripped of the false statements and supplemented with the omitted facts ~ would
27   not support probable cause. Franks v. Delaware,438 U.S. 154, 155-56(1978); Chism v.
28   Washington State, 661 F.3d 380, 386(9th Cir. 2011).

2           40. On information and belief, Defendant Meza-Gonzaiez executed the DS-367 and arrest

 3   report under penalty of peijury and submitted them, or caused them to be submitted, to the

4    reviewing magistrate as the evidentiary basis for the blood-draw warrant application. The

5    challenged representations identified in paragraph 26 were material to the probable-cause

6    determination. When those representations are set aside and the omitted facts identified in

7    paragraph 27 are restored, the remaining application does not plausibly support probable cause to

8    believe Plaintiff was driving under the influence. Benavidez v. County ofSan Diego,993 F.3d

9    1134,1146-47(9th Cir. 2021)(applying Franks corrected-affidavit test),

11          41. Defendant Meza-Gonzalez acted with at least reckless disregard for the truth. An

12   officer who executes a sworn statement while a contemporaneous recording of the same

13   encounter ~ stored on his own patrol vehicle ~ contains information materially inconsistent with

14   that sworn statement acts with reckless disregard, at minimum. The right to be free from a

15   warrant obtained through materially false or misleading statements was clearly established before

16   November 1,2024. Franks,438 U.S. at 155-56; Chism,661 F,3d at 386; Galbraith v. County of

17   Santa Clara,307 F.3d 1119, 1126 (9th Cir. 2002)

18
