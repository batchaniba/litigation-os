ITEM: 1A
SOURCE READ: https://www.horvitzlevy.com/sites/default/files/2024-08/S274147.pdf (California Supreme Court slip / Horvitz reprint of *Meinhardt v. City of Sunnyvale*, S274147, filed July 29, 2024; also corroborated at https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/ and the official reporter cite 16 Cal.5th 643)
STATUS: VERIFIED (holding and quotes from the slip). Official *Cal.5th* page breaks were not confirmed against a bound reporter PDF.
HOLDING / TEXT: The Court **reversed** the Court of Appeal, which had dismissed the appeal as untimely. Holding: in administrative-mandate proceedings the time to appeal under rule 8.104(a)(1) begins with **entry of “judgment” or service of notice of entry of “judgment,”** not with the filing of an “order” or other ruling — **even where** an appellate court might later deem that ruling appealable in order to **save** an appeal. The Court **REJECTED** the Court of Appeal’s “order that is sufficiently final may be treated as the judgment that starts the clock” approach. The opinion discusses appealability, not preclusion. It does **not** say an order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend is itself a final judgment for res judicata.
EXACT QUOTE: "Relying on this practice of permitting an appeal by deeming a sufficiently final order or other ruling to be appealable, the Court of Appeal in this case dismissed the appeal, holding that in administrative mandate proceedings the filing of such an order is also the entry of judgment from which the time to appeal under rule 8.104(a)(1) begins. … For reasons that follow, we reject this approach." (slip pp. 10–11)
EXACT QUOTE: "This practice has been common in cases involving orders that reflect the trial court’s final decision but are not statutorily appealable, such as orders granting summary judgment … and orders sustaining demurrers without leave to amend (In re Social Services Payment Cases (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 1249, 1262, fn. 4 [treating an appeal ‘taken from the nonappealable order sustaining the demurrer … as a premature but valid notice of appeal from the subsequently entered judgment’])." (slip pp. 9–10)
EXACT QUOTE: "Instead, we conclude that the time to appeal in administrative mandate proceedings begins with the entry of ‘judgment’ or service of notice of entry of ‘judgment,’ not with the filing of an ‘order’ or other ruling, or service of notice of filing of such a ruling, even where an appellate court might deem such a ruling appealable in order to vindicate the right of appeal." (slip p. 13)
EXACT QUOTE (fn. 10 — describes ease of recognition, does **not** adopt deeming for the clock): "In some situations, such as in the case of an order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend, or the order denying the petition at issue in this case, it may be fairly easy for litigants to determine whether the ruling is sufficiently final to constitute the judgment in the case; in other situations, however, that determination may not be so easy." (slip p. 15 n.10)
PIN CONFIDENCE: PRIMARY for the slip text. SECONDARY for the bound pin 16 Cal.5th 643 (official reporter start page corroborated by multiple citators; page-by-page *Cal.5th* breaks not read in a reporter PDF).
PUBLISHED?: Published (California Supreme Court).
EXPRESSLY NOT DECIDED: Whether Meinhardt’s premature service of notice of entry triggered the 60-day period (slip n.4). The opinion does not decide res judicata, claim preclusion, or issue preclusion.
CONTRADICTS THE PROMPT?: No on the “adopt vs reject” dispute: the Court **REJECTED** treating a sufficiently-final order as the judgment that starts the appeal clock. Yes on any assumption that *Meinhardt* is a preclusion case — it is not. Bottom line: the decision makes it **EASIER** to argue that an order sustaining a demurrer, with **no judgment of dismissal entered**, is not the appealable judgment. It does **not** itself hold that such an order lacks preclusive effect. The preclusion argument still has to run through *Valvo* / *Berri* / *Goddard* / *Perez*, not through *Meinhardt*.

EXACT QUOTE: "The judgment was based upon formal matters of pleading, and concluded nothing save that the complaint, in the form in which it was then presented, did not entitle plaintiff to go to trial on the merits. HOLDING / TEXT: CCP § 430.10(a) is “no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action.” § 430.10(e) is “does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.” Exhaustion is described by the California Supreme Court as a **“jurisdictional prerequisite to resort to the courts”** (*Abelleira*; *Coachella Valley*). Parties and courts have raised it under **either or both** subdivisions. *Ramos* fn. 6 is the cleanest primary: the County’s general demurrer alleged **first** no jurisdiction and **second** failure to state a cause of action, **both** on the same exhaustion theory; the trial court sustained on the first ground and found it unnecessary to reach the others.
EXACT QUOTE (statute): "(a) The court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the pleading. … (e) The pleading does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action." (CCP § 430.10)
EXACT QUOTE: "we have described exhaustion of administrative remedies as ‘a jurisdictional prerequisite to resort to the courts’" (*Coachella Valley*, quoting *Abelleira*, 17 Cal.2d 280, 292)
EXACT QUOTE (*Ramos* fn. 6): "Defendants’ general demurrer to the complaint alleged first that the trial court did not have jurisdiction and second that the complaint did not plead facts sufficient to state a cause of action. In their memorandum of points and authorities defendants relied upon the same theory of law for both grounds of the general demurrer, namely, that plaintiffs had not exhausted their administrative remedies. The trial court sustained the demurrer on the first ground (lack of jurisdiction) and expressly found it unnecessary to consider other grounds specified in the demurrer." (4 Cal.3d 685 n.6)
PIN CONFIDENCE: PRIMARY.
PUBLISHED?: Statute / published opinions. N/A as a single “holding.”
EXPRESSLY NOT DECIDED: Which subdivision a particular trial court **must** use. Either is used.
CONTRADICTS THE PROMPT?: No. The prompt’s tension is real and is in the California sources: the doctrine is called jurisdictional (*Abelleira* / (a) vocabulary) while the same failure is also pleaded as failure to state a cause of action ((e)). *Ramos* shows both grounds on one demurrer.

## (2) What cuts against the plaintiff — stated plainly

1. ***Palomar* is adverse on the merits question.** If a California **judgment of dismissal after a general demurrer** exists, the Ninth Circuit has already said that judgment **is** on the merits and bars a second action on the same facts (989 F.2d at 364). The no-judgment / no-Smith-as-party / exhaustion-not-merits arguments have to stay in front of this sentence. Do not let *Palomar* be quoted as if it required a trial.

2. ***Pollock* is also adverse if a general-demurrer *judgment* exists.** 112 Cal.App.4th at 1428: the fact that the first case ended on a general demurrer “does not preclude” res judicata.

3. ***Meinhardt* is not a preclusion holding.** Using it as if the California Supreme Court decided res judicata is a mischaracterization. Footnote 10 even says a demurrer-WOLA order can be “fairly easy” to recognize as sufficiently final — that sentence, ripped from the rejected-clock discussion, is usable against a “not a judgment” argument if counsel quotes it without the holding.

4. ***Wicklund* fn. 1 is a Smith exhibit.** “Restraints on Wicklund’s disruptive behavior during his court-ordered therapy sessions **did not violate his First Amendment rights.**” Unpublished, but if they find it they will say the protected activity here was inside treatment. The panel **affirmed QI denial** only as to a **public** probation director for threats about litigation **outside** therapy. It is not a vendor-damages case and it is not clearly-established law (R. 36-3). **623 F. App’x 338 is a bad reporter cite.**

5. ***Caviness* is a defense state-action case.** Conduct-specific inquiry; a heavily regulated, publicly funded “public” charter operator was **not** a state actor for personnel conduct. Counsel will analogize CASOMB / CDCR regulation to Horizon’s charter statute.

6. ***Kirtley* is a defense nexus case.** Appointment, payment, and regulation were “significant links,” and “there the nexus end[ed].” Same vocabulary they will use for a CASOMB-certified contractor.

7. ***Richardson*’s caveat is double-edged.** The “systematically organized … for profit … limited direct supervision” frame can fit a standing SOMP vendor (helps deny QI) **or** they will say HOPE is the *Filarsky* “adjunct … under close official supervision” exception (parole agent on the CTM / Form 3043 dual signature). *Esche* **distinguishes** *Filarsky* and still does **not** hold QI unavailable — it dismisses for want of interlocutory jurisdiction.
