### The doctrinal picture

**The §1983 theory is the load-bearing wall, and state action is its gate.** *West v. Atkins* supplies the function-over-form premise and *Jensen v. Lane County* is the closest binding bridge — a private contract clinician inside a state statutory liberty-restraint process is a state actor under close-nexus/joint action, *and* denied qualified immunity in the same opinion. *Rawson* (975 F.3d 742) extends *Jensen* from the individual clinician to the private institutional provider and its staff. But *Merrill v. Mental Health Systems* is a nearly on-point 12(b)(6) dismissal of §1983 against private parolee-treatment providers on contract/certification/funding/coordination facts — it also embeds the unpublished *Gross v. Samudio* (10th Cir.), holding private sex-offender programs' decisions causing parole revocation were not state action. Survival turns entirely on **gravamen framing**: plead the gravamen as HOPE's participation in the parole-supervision machinery itself (mandatory referrals, CDCR-prescribed standards, Form 3043/no-show reports as parole-sanction inputs, willful joint activity with parole agents), not as "vendor with a contract." *Dennis v. Sparks* keeps the joint-action test alive where *Merrill*'s plaintiffs defaulted it — but only with pleaded facts of willful joint participation, not mere reporting to the state.

**Monell against HOPE is the strongest federal theory** once state action is established: *Tsao* confirms *Monell* applies to private §1983 entities, and *AE v. Tulare* sets a pleading bar the repo-record facts clear by a margin — verbatim manual language ("prohibited from discharging a client from treatment for any reason"), 16-location scope, Smith's policymaker role, identified Form 3043s and no-show records, and verified discovery admissions. Keep it an express-policy case; do not drift into failure-to-train (*Brown*/*Connick*).

**Retaliation is the strongest constitutional theory on the merits.** Five *Rhodes* elements map cleanly: false no-show logs + adverse Form 3043s to the parole agent, protected policy challenges, suspect timing, objective chilling, and falsity negating any legitimate goal. *Watison* is the one-to-one factual analogue (false disciplinary paperwork + false statements to a parole board stated a claim); *Brodheim* makes plaintiff's persistence legally irrelevant; *Wicklund* extends the framework to community supervision; *Hines* covers false institutional allegations; *Hartman*/*Nieves* have no application where the alleged retaliator is the adverse actor. The risks: state-actor status (separate track), pleading Smith's personal direction (no respondeat superior — *Iqbal*), *Bruce*'s burden of pleading-and-proving absence of legitimate goals, and QI particularity.

**The Bane Act is a viable force multiplier but pleading-sensitive.** *Cornell* + *Reese* (9th Cir., binding) reject any universal independence-of-coercion requirement, replacing it with a specific-intent test (purpose or reckless disregard toward a clearly delineated right). *Venegas* defeats any police-only/hate-crime-only framing; *Jones*/*Dixon*/*M.H.* confirm private/non-police defendants are proper. But *Shoyoye* is the case defense counsel will lead with — deliberate falsification, not clerical error, must be pleaded — and §52.1(k)'s speech-alone bar is a live dismissal risk unless the complaint pleads the reports *as a coercive mechanism* (six specificity elements: right + Smith's knowledge, exact entries/dates/recipient, Smith's knowledge of falsity and direction, concrete leverage, retaliatory purpose/reckless disregard, repeated course).

**RICO is weak and should stay lean.** Enterprise (Smith-as-person/HOPE-as-enterprise under *Cedric Kushner*) and pattern (decade of predicates under *H.J. Inc.*) are pleadable; proximate-cause standing is the dispositive defect. *Anza* is a structural twin — fraud on a government payor, plaintiff harmed downstream through distinct conduct — and *Bridge* does not cure it because *Bridge*'s plaintiffs were the direct, intended victims. Every verified billing-fraud RICO survivor was brought by the defrauded payor. Unless direct injury to plaintiff himself can be pleaded, expect dismissal (likely with leave to amend). Keep RICO particularized, alternative, and secondary — the §1983/Monell and Bane theories are the realistic vehicles.

**Fraud is curable; negligent misrepresentation is likely a loser.** *Small*'s actual-reliance rule is the fraud threat (plaintiff knew the reports were false; the parole agent relied), and *Randi W.* is the answer — a non-recipient intended victim can sue on misrepresentation made to an intermediary, and it survived demurrer, exactly the 12(b)(6) posture here. The economic-loss rule is a paper tiger: plaintiff has no contract with HOPE (*Robinson*/*Rattagan* in reserve). *Stansfield* specificity (who/when/to-whom/by-what-means per report) and *Jolly*/*Fox* limitations discipline are mandatory. Negligent misrepresentation dies on *Bily*/*Soderberg* duty (plaintiff was the report's subject, not its intended user) unless direct-to-plaintiff statements exist (*Ragland*); if none, drop the claim.

**The res judicata defense is weak on three independent elements.** Brief it from the unified memo, not this paragraph. The state court called exhaustion a *jurisdictional* requirement unmet before the FAC — *Perez v. City of San Bruno* + *Heath*, 708 F.2d at 1380 n.4. Smith was not a Matter 5 party. There is no judgment of dismissal; *Kay* (2007) finality is a snapshot they can cure. The kill that stays dead is the merits holding. Matter 6's first voluntary dismissal without prejudice is a preclusion nullity (*Semtek*). Do not voluntarily dismiss. The genuine dangers remain *Boeken* and *Migra* if a merits judgment is ever manufactured.

### Cross-track strategic reads

1. **Res judicata is ground one; state action is the insufficiency half.** Draft the preclusion opposition from the unified memo. *Merrill* is the analog for the state-action count, not the lead paper. Retaliation is the strongest theory once through the state-action gate.
2. **The matter record's Bane Act damages figure is legally wrong.** "Bane Act §52.1 statutory damages ($25,000 per violation)" must not be pleaded as a private claim: the §52.1(b) $25,000 civil penalty is public-enforcement-only (AG/DA/city attorney). Private remedies are compensatory/punitive damages, injunctive/equitable/declaratory relief, and attorney's fees (§52.1(i)). Correct before filing.
3. **Never voluntarily dismiss the N.D. Cal. action.** *Semtek*'s two-dismissal rule (FRCP 41(a)(1)(B)) is the only preclusion trap that could convert the harmless Matter 6 dismissal into an on-the-merits bar.
4. **Pleading discipline is the whole game.** Attribute specific reports to Smith personally (*Iqbal*/*Stansfield*); quote the manual language verbatim (*AE*); plead the six Bane specificity elements; plead *Fox* time-and-manner-of-discovery facts for each late report.
5. **The 9/21 responsive-pleading deadline is the first test.** Van Dine's ground #1 is res judicata framed as "resolved on the merits" — a characterization the tentative ruling's "jurisdictional requirement" language directly undermines.

# PART I — §1983 state action, private-corporation Monell, and Shannon Smith's individual liability

**Scope.** (1) Whether HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc. — a CASOMB-certified, CDCR-contracted community sex-offender treatment provider operating ~16 California locations — is a state actor for its alleged blanket policy of retaining parolees in treatment for their full parole terms; (2) whether *Monell*'s policy/custom/policymaker requirements apply to a private §1983 entity defendant and how to plead them; (3) Smith's individual liability for allegedly directing false no-show records and adverse CDCR Form 3043 reports in retaliation for protected conduct, including qualified-immunity exposure for private actors.

**Overall: state action MODERATE (pleadable, not safe); Monell MODERATE-STRONG (as an express-policy case); Smith individual liability MODERATE; private-actor QI contestable.**

---

## I.1 State-action cases

### *Jensen v. Lane County*, 222 F.3d 570 (9th Cir. 2000) — VERIFIED (primary opinion text). **BEST PLAINTIFF CASE.**
A private contract psychiatrist who signed and continued a statutory mental-health detention order was a "state actor" under the close-nexus/joint-action test: the county and the private group had "undertaken a complex and deeply intertwined process" of evaluating and detaining people, and the state was "so deeply insinuated" that the private conduct could "fairly be treated as that of the State itself." The court also **denied qualified immunity** to the private contractor under *Richardson* and *Halvorsen* (market forces undercut the timidity rationale; no firmly rooted immunity tradition).
*Matter 28 application:* The closest binding bridge from *West*'s prison-doctor rule to community-based compelled treatment. HOPE's alleged role — mandatory CDCR parolee referrals, CASOMB standards, official Form 3043 reports and no-show findings that feed parole-agent supervision/sanction decisions — maps onto *Jensen*'s "complex and deeply intertwined process" far better than mere treatment provision. Plead the interdependence specifically: who refers, what the vendor agreement requires, how parole agents use Form 3043, what consequences flow from adverse/no-show reports.
*Weakness/risk:* *Jensen* involved civil commitment — a classic physical-liberty deprivation — while Matter 28's deprivation is softer (continued compelled treatment; parole consequences via reports). Defense will argue the nexus is thinner where the challenged conduct is clinical decisions rather than detention orders. (Cite as 222 F.3d 570 (9th Cir. 2000); some databases mislabel the header as 1995.)

### *Rawson v. Recovery Innovations, Inc.*, 975 F.3d 742 (9th Cir. 2020) — VERIFIED (9th Cir. opinion PDF)
The Ninth Circuit reinstated §1983 claims against a private nonprofit behavioral-health firm and its staff, holding they "were acting under color of state law" for wrongful detention, forced medication, and misleading a court to extend involuntary commitment — reasoning from state imprimatur, affirmative statutory command, reliance on the State's police and *parens patriae* powers, and expressly analogizing to "private contract physicians rendering treatment services for prisoners at a state prison" (*West*).
*Matter 28 application:* Extends *Jensen* from the individual contract psychiatrist to a private *institutional* provider and its employees — answering "HOPE is a company, not a doctor." Supports treating Smith as a state actor alongside the entity.
*Weakness/risk:* Involuntary-commitment/forced-medication facts — core physical-liberty deprivations. Defense will argue compelled outpatient parole-condition treatment is a step removed, and that *Rawson*'s "normative judgment" approach was controversial (cert petition filed, No. 20-1287 — verify disposition before leaning on it at hearing).

### *West v. Atkins*, 487 U.S. 42 (1988) — VERIFIED (GovInfo U.S. Reports PDF)
A private physician under contract with the State to provide prisoner medical care acts under color of state law: "[i]t is the physician's function within the state system, not the precise terms of his employment, that determines whether his actions can fairly be attributed to the State."
*Matter 28 application:* Foundational premise — HOPE's corporate/private status does not end the inquiry. Frame CDCR as having an affirmative correctional/supervisory obligation toward parolees that it delegated to HOPE, with Form 3043 reporting as the delegation's enforcement arm.
*Weakness/risk:* *West* is a prison-medical-care case where the State bore an affirmative *constitutional* (Eighth Amendment) duty; no court has held CDCR bears an analogous duty to provide *community* treatment to parolees. Defense will cite *Merrill*'s refusal to find "complete delegation" on parolee-treatment facts.

### *Merrill v. Mental Health Systems, Inc.*, No. 3:16-cv-01090-GPC-JMA (S.D. Cal. Sept. 13, 2016) (Curiel, J.) — VERIFIED (full primary order). **MOST DANGEROUS CASE.**
On 12(b)(6), the court dismissed a §1983 claim against two private nonprofits providing state-funded substance-abuse treatment to parolees, holding the complaint failed state action under all four Ninth Circuit tests. State authorization, government funding, state referrals, coordination with the parole department, and even "control over Plaintiffs by virtue of the threat of parole revocation" did not convert the conduct into state action; leave to amend plus 60 days of limited jurisdictional discovery was granted.
*Matter 28 application:* The defense's lead case — confront head-on. Distinguishers: (a) **gravamen** — *Merrill* plaintiffs challenged facility conditions (bedbugs), and the court stressed "careful attention to the gravamen of the plaintiff's complaint" (*Blum*; *Caviness v. Horizon*, 590 F.3d 806, 812–13); Matter 28's gravamen is HOPE's role in the parole-supervision process itself (Form 3043 reports, no-show findings, blanket retention policy); (b) **joint action** — *Merrill* found joint action "not relevant because Plaintiffs do not allege that Defendants willfully participated in joint activity with the State"; a complaint that *does* allege willful joint participation with parole agents keeps the *Dennis* test alive.
*Weakness/risk (for plaintiff):* The public-function and governmental-nexus analyses are directly adverse ("not traditionally the exclusive prerogative of the State"; no *West* analogue on parolee-treatment facts). Worse, the opinion cites *Gross v. Samudio*, 630 F. App'x 772, 779 (10th Cir. 2015) — holding private **sex-offender** treatment programs' decisions causing parole revocation were not state action — nearly on-point against Matter 28's parole-consequences theory. (*Gross* is unpublished; retrieve and distinguish before the hearing — admission decision vs. ongoing supervisory reporting; no alleged joint action.)

### *Dennis v. Sparks*, 449 U.S. 24, 27–29 (1980) — VERIFIED
"Private persons, jointly engaged with state officials in the challenged action, are acting 'under color' of law." Ninth Circuit formulation: "willful participant in joint action with the State or its agents" (*Franklin v. Fox*, 312 F.3d 423, 445).
*Matter 28 application:* The doctrinal hook for the Form 3043 theory — false records generated *in coordinated concert with* parole agents to trigger parole consequences in retaliation for protected conduct. The complaint must allege facts showing agreement or substantial cooperation (who asked, what was supplied, what the agent did with it). *NCAA v. Tarkanian*, 488 U.S. 179 (1988), limits it: antagonists are not joint participants.
*Weakness/risk:* Merely reporting information to an official, or the official independently acting on it, is generally insufficient. If parole agents exercised independent judgment on the Form 3043s, the theory collapses into *Merrill*'s rejected version.

### *Tsao v. Desert Palace, Inc.*, 698 F.3d 1128 (9th Cir. 2012) — VERIFIED (primary opinion). (Correct cite is **698**, not 98 F.3d.)
*Monell*'s policy-or-custom requirement applies to **private** entities sued under §1983; but conclusory joint-action allegations are insufficient — a private party is a state actor only where a "willful participant in joint action with the state or its agents."
*Matter 28 application:* Controlling authority that HOPE can be reached by *Monell* — but only through policy/custom/policymaker, never respondeat superior. The verbatim manual language plus Smith's alleged direction fits the "express policy" and "order by a policymaking officer" prongs.
*Weakness/risk:* *Tsao* found no state action against a private casino that detained a patron and called police — cuts both ways; cite for *Monell*, distinguish on facts (no intertwined process, no delegated supervisory authority, no official reporting channel comparable to Form 3043).

### *Kirtley v. Rainey*, 326 F.3d 1088, 1092–95 (9th Cir. 2003) — formulation verified via *Merrill*'s quotation; full opinion not independently reviewed
The four state-action tests: (1) public function; (2) joint action; (3) state compulsion; (4) governmental nexus. Organizing framework for the opposition — plead all four in the alternative, leading with close-nexus/joint action. Cite-check pinpoints before filing.

## I.2 Monell pleading

### *AE ex rel. Hernandez v. County of Tulare*, 666 F.3d 631 (9th Cir. 2012) — VERIFIED (CourtListener PDF)
*Monell* claims are subject to *Twombly*/*Iqbal*: the complaint must plead "factual content that allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable" — threadbare element-recitals do not suffice.
*Matter 28 application:* The repo-record facts clear this bar by a margin: verbatim manual language, 16-location scope, Smith's policymaker role, identified Form 3043s and no-show records, verified discovery admissions. Quote the manual language; identify reports by date/recipient; tie the policy to each constitutional injury as the moving force.
*Weakness/risk:* Defense will argue the allegations are conclusory if the manual language is paraphrased rather than quoted or the policy-to-injury causation link is assumed, not pleaded. Keep the theory as express policy/policymaker decision — do not dilute into custom or failure-to-train.

### *Board of County Commissioners v. Brown*, 520 U.S. 397 (1997); *Connick v. Thompson*, 563 U.S. 51 (2011) — stated from settled doctrine; NOT independently verified this session
Failure-to-train *Monell* requires "deliberate indifference" — ordinarily a pattern of similar violations; rigorous causation to the policymaker's culpable decision. **Do not cite without verification; do not let the complaint drift into failure-to-train**, where the burden is heavy. The express-policy theory avoids it entirely.

## I.3 Smith's individual liability and qualified immunity

### *Starr v. Baca*, 652 F.3d 1202 (9th Cir. 2011) — VERIFIED
Supervisory liability may rest on "knowledge of and acquiescence in unconstitutional conduct by his or her subordinates"; detailed allegations of prior incidents plus acquiescence satisfy Rule 8(a), reversing a 12(b)(6) dismissal entered with prejudice.
*Matter 28 application:* Smith is alleged as more than a supervisor — personal direction of clinical/compliance policy and the false records — which is stronger than *Starr*'s acquiescence theory. Pair with the policymaker allegations to make her both the *Monell* policymaker and the individually liable actor.
*Weakness/risk:* *Starr*'s framework transfers imperfectly to a retaliation/false-reporting case; defense will argue retaliatory motive is governed by *Pratt*, and the *Starr* dissent's *Iqbal* critique gives a skeptical court ammunition.

### *Pratt v. Rowland*, 65 F.3d 802 (9th Cir. 1995) — VERIFIED via multiple secondary sources quoting the opinion; primary opinion not pulled
Retaliatory motive may be proved through circumstantial evidence (timing, irregular procedure), but the plaintiff must show the action was not serving a legitimate goal — and timing alone, where legitimate correctional objectives exist, is weak.
*Matter 28 application:* Sets the bar to plead *to*: allege Smith's awareness of the protected conduct, temporal proximity, irregularity/deviation from clinical protocols, and the absence or pretextuality of legitimate justifications. The alleged **falsity** of the records is the strongest *Pratt* fact — a false report serves no legitimate clinical goal. Anticipate the defense's verbatim *Pratt* argument: legitimate clinical judgment justified the reports; timing is coincidence.
*Weakness/risk:* A defense-favorable outcome on its facts; if the complaint rests on temporal proximity plus conclusory motive labels, expect a *Pratt*-based 12(b)(6) attack. Needs the "plus" factors: falsity, deviation from protocol, animus-evincing statements, or the blanket policy as the retaliatory instrument.

### *Richardson v. McKnight*, 521 U.S. 399 (1997) — VERIFIED (GovInfo U.S. Reports)
Private prison guards are **not** entitled to qualified immunity: no firmly rooted immunity tradition, and market pressures undercut the timidity rationale. (Entities never get QI in any event — *Owen v. City of Independence*, 445 U.S. 622.)
*Matter 28 application:* Lead Supreme Court authority that HOPE and Smith cannot assume QI travels with state-action status. HOPE's profile (multi-location institutional contractor competing for CDCR business, carrying its own liability insurance) fits the market-forces rationale.
*Weakness/risk:* *Richardson* left open private actors "closely related to governmental function" — the door *Filarsky* walked through.

### *Filarsky v. Delia*, 566 U.S. 377 (2012) — VERIFIED (GovInfo slip op.)
"A private individual temporarily retained by the government to carry out its work is entitled to **seek** qualified immunity from suit under §1983." The defense's best QI authority.
*Matter 28 application:* Distinguish, don't ignore: HOPE is not a briefly retained individual performing a discrete supervised task — it is a long-term, multi-location institutional contractor that allegedly *makes policy* (blanket retention rule) across 16 sites. *Jensen*'s application of *Richardson* is the counter-template (private contractor performing a "complex administrative task" including policymaking functions = no QI). And "entitled to seek" ≠ "entitled to receive": the clearly-established prong must still be litigated.
*Weakness/risk:* A court could read *Filarsky* functionally for Smith-as-individual-clinician. If so, the fight moves to the clearly-established prong — prepare it regardless.

### *Keates v. Koile*, 883 F.3d 1228 (9th Cir. 2018) — VERIFIED via secondary summaries; primary opinion not pulled
Affirmed dismissal **with prejudice** on QI: the caseworker's conduct did not violate clearly established law where there were no facts showing deliberately fabricated evidence; reliance on third-party clinical sources was not deliberate fabrication.
*Matter 28 application:* The cautionary case for the false-records claim: conclusory "false report" allegations, without pleaded facts showing *deliberate* fabrication (what the true facts were, how Smith knew them, what she reported instead), can be dismissed at the pleading stage. It also confirms QI can be resolved at 12(b)(6).
*Weakness/risk:* Defense-friendly. If Smith's reports are framed as clinical judgments, a court may treat falsity as non-actionable professional disagreement. The retaliation framing (motive + falsity) is stronger than a standalone "false report" due-process framing.

# PART II — First Amendment retaliation and Bane Act (§52.1)

**Core facts for this track:** After plaintiff exercised constitutional rights challenging HOPE's program policies, CEO Shannon Smith allegedly directed retaliatory reporting to plaintiff's parole agent — logging no-shows for sessions plaintiff attended (HOPE_000142/HOPE_000194; a no-show logged despite an agent-confirmed technical error) and generating adverse CDCR Form 3043 treatment-status reports.

**Overall: retaliation MODERATE-STRONG (leans strong); Bane Act MODERATE (moderate-to-strong if pleaded with specificity).**

---

## II.1 First Amendment retaliation (42 U.S.C. §1983)

### *Rhodes v. Robinson*, 408 F.3d 559 (9th Cir. 2005) — VERIFIED (primary opinion)
Five elements: (1) a state actor took adverse action against an inmate (2) because of (3) protected conduct, and the action (4) chilled First Amendment exercise, and (5) did not reasonably advance a legitimate correctional goal. Grievance-filing and civil-rights litigation are core protected conduct; the chilling inquiry is objective — speech "can be chilled even when not completely silenced."
*Matter 28 application:* Maps cleanly: false no-show logs + adverse Form 3043s to the parole agent; plaintiff's policy challenges (petition rights "of fundamental import"); suspect timing; objective ordinary-firmness test; falsity (agent-confirmed error logged as no-show) negates any legitimate goal.
*Weakness/risk:* Framed "within the prison context" — plaintiff is a community-supervision parolee, not an inmate. Defense will argue correctional-goal deference differs outside walls. (Answer: *Wicklund* imports the framework into community supervision.)

### *Watison v. Carter*, 668 F.3d 1108 (9th Cir. 2012) — VERIFIED via 9th Cir. restatements (see verification caveat below). **BEST PLAINTIFF CASE.**
Restates the five elements with three refinements: a "mere threat of harm can be an adverse action"; causation may be pleaded via "a chronology of events from which retaliation can be inferred"; a plaintiff who can't allege chilling may still plead "harm that is more than minimal." On the facts, false disciplinary complaint + false statements to the parole board, both in retaliation for grievances, stated a claim.
*Matter 28 application:* Closest factual analogue in the circuit: false no-show logs = *Watison*'s false disciplinary complaint; adverse Form 3043s to the parole agent = *Watison*'s false statements to the parole board. HOPE_000142/194 (falsity + chronology) give a stronger causation inference than bare timing, and liberty-threatening reports to a parole agent are "more than minimal" harm even if plaintiff kept complaining.
*Weakness/risk:* Full opinion text could not be pulled directly; holdings verified through *Moore v. Shinn*, No. 23-15302 (9th Cir. Feb. 26, 2026) (verbatim restatement) and a 2018 D. Or. order. Confirm pin cites (1114–16) against the reporter before filing.

### *Brodheim v. Cry*, 584 F.3d 1262 (9th Cir. 2009) — VERIFIED (ca9.uscourts.gov filed opinion)
The chilling inquiry is objective — plaintiff need not show speech "was actually inhibited or suppressed," only that the action "would chill or silence a person of ordinary firmness." The mere threat of harm can itself be an adverse action; protected conduct need only be a "substantial" or "motivating" factor.
*Matter 28 application:* Neutralizes the inevitable defense argument that plaintiff wasn't actually deterred (he persisted and sued) — persistence is legally irrelevant under the objective test, and *Rhodes* says it would be "unjust to allow a defendant to escape liability merely because an unusually determined plaintiff persists." A false adverse report to a parole agent plainly chills a person of ordinary firmness.
*Weakness/risk:* *Brodheim*'s facts were a verbal "be careful" threat, not paperwork falsification; defense will argue the standard was built for lower-severity conduct. The opinion carries a dissent signaling the chilling question can be fact-sensitive.

### *Bruce v. Ylst*, 351 F.3d 1283 (9th Cir. 2003) — VERIFIED (primary opinion). **MOST DANGEROUS CASE.**
Reversed summary judgment on retaliation: the *Hill* "some evidence" standard governs due-process attacks on disciplinary results, "not the correctional officer's retaliatory accusation" (*Hines v. Gomez*, 108 F.3d 265, 269). Suspect timing + reuse of previously-insufficient evidence + "make an example out of you" statements raised a triable motive issue. Plaintiff "bears the burden of pleading and proving the absence of legitimate correctional goals" (*Pratt*, 65 F.3d at 806); retaliation prohibition was clearly established law (no QI).
*Matter 28 application:* Two uses: (a) causation — Smith's alleged direction parallels the "higher-ups" ordering in *Bruce*; (b) element (5) — fabricated no-shows are a "cover or a ruse" that "cannot" serve a valid penological purpose. Also a QI anchor.
*Weakness/risk:* Puts the burden of pleading *and proving* absence of legitimate goals on plaintiff, and it's a summary-judgment case. If Smith offers any documented therapeutic/attendance-policy rationale, courts afford "appropriate deference" — mere disagreement with clinical judgment won't suffice, so the falsity evidence (HOPE_000142/194) is load-bearing.

### *Hartman v. Moore*, 547 U.S. 250 (2006) & *Nieves v. Bartlett*, 587 U.S. 391 (2019) — VERIFIED (*Hartman* via primary opinion; *Nieves* via secondary summaries — re-verify pins)
Retaliatory *prosecution* (*Hartman*) and retaliatory *arrest* (*Nieves*) require pleading/proving absence of probable cause. But *Hartman* expressly distinguishes "ordinary retaliation claims, where the government agent allegedly harboring the animus is also the individual allegedly taking the adverse action" (547 U.S. at 432–33) — those use the ordinary *Mt. Healthy*/*Rhodes* causation framework with no objective gate.
*Matter 28 application:* No probable-cause analogue applies: no arrest or prosecution is involved, and the alleged retaliator (Smith directing HOPE's reporting) is the same party taking the adverse action — exactly *Hartman*'s "ordinary retaliation claim."
*Weakness/risk:* Defense could argue the *parole agent* (recipient of the 3043s) is an independent decision-maker breaking the causal chain under *Hartman*'s logic. Counter: the adverse action is the false reporting itself, independently actionable regardless of downstream consequences (*Brodheim*: the threat itself suffices).

### False institutional reporting as adverse action; community-supervision application
- ***Hines v. Gomez*, 108 F.3d 265, 267–69 (9th Cir. 1997)** — VERIFIED (quoted in *Rhodes*): "the retaliatory imposition of a ten-day period of confinement and loss of television — justified by a correctional officer's false allegation that the plaintiff breached prison regulations — violated the First Amendment." Direct circuit authority that a *false* institutional allegation is cognizable retaliation.
- ***Wicklund v. Idaho Dep't of Corr.*, No. 12-35294 (9th Cir. Nov. 23, 2015) (unpublished)** — VERIFIED (Justia): a *probationer* in community supervision stated a retaliation claim where his supervisor threatened jail for protected litigation activity. Applied the *Rhodes* five-element test verbatim outside prison walls; held it clearly established since before 2009 that officials can't punish First Amendment-protected conduct (*Pratt*, 65 F.3d at 806). Unpublished (9th Cir. R. 36-3 — persuasive only). *Wicklund* noted restraints on disruptive behavior during court-ordered therapy don't themselves violate the First Amendment — a line Smith may try to exploit.

### *Pratt v. Rowland*, 65 F.3d 802 (9th Cir. 1995) — VERIFIED via verbatim quotations in *Hydrick v. Hunter* (9th Cir. 2006) and *Rhodes*
"The prohibition against retaliatory punishment is 'clearly established law' in the Ninth Circuit, for qualified immunity purposes" (65 F.3d at 806 & n.4); "timing can properly be considered as circumstantial evidence of retaliatory intent" (65 F.3d at 808); plaintiff bears the burden on element (5); courts afford "appropriate deference and flexibility" to officials' legitimate-goal proffers.
*Matter 28 application:* The QI anchor (since 1995) plus the timing-as-circumstantial-evidence rule for pleading causation from the sequence: protected challenge → false no-show logs → adverse 3043s.
*Weakness/risk:* The clearly-established holding is general; under modern QI doctrine a court may demand particularized precedent that *false treatment-status reporting by a CDCR vendor's CEO* was clearly unlawful — *Watison* (false reports to parole board) and *Wicklund* (community-supervision supervisor) are the best available but thin. *Pratt* also arms the defense (element-(5) burden, official deference).

**Retaliation 12(b)(6): MODERATE-STRONG.** Clean mapping onto all five *Rhodes* elements; *Hines*/*Watison* cover falsified paperwork as adverse action; *Brodheim* makes plaintiff's persistence irrelevant; *Wicklund* extends the framework to community supervision; *Hartman*/*Nieves* have no application. Pleading-stage risks: (1) HOPE/Smith's state-actor status (separate track); (2) pleading Smith's *personal direction* (no respondeat superior — *Iqbal*); (3) QI particularity. *Moore v. Shinn* (9th Cir. 2026) confirms the 12(b)(6) threshold for these claims is "low."

## II.2 Bane Act (Cal. Civ. Code §52.1)

### Critical corrections to premises (verified at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov)
1. **The $25,000-per-violation premise is wrong for a private plaintiff and must not be pleaded.** The §52.1(b) $25,000 civil penalty is **public-enforcement-only** (AG/DA/city attorney). A private Bane action under §52.1(c) gets damages (including §52), injunctive/equitable/declaratory relief, and attorney's fees (§52.1(i)) — **no civil penalty**. §52(a)'s $4,000 figure is the Unruh Act minimum, not a Bane penalty. The only $25k penalty a private plaintiff can get is §52(b)(2) per offense for **§51.7 Ralph Act** claims — not Bane. **Matter 28's damages section ("Bane Act §52.1 statutory damages ($25,000 per violation)") needs correction before filing.** No published case on stacking/counting the penalty was found.
2. **The "Venegas independence rule" shorthand is inaccurate — do not use it.** *Venegas* (32 Cal.4th 820) did not create a categorical rule that coercion must be transactionally independent of the violation. That language comes from *Shoyoye* (2012), on negligent-overdetention facts. *Cornell* (2017) and binding *Reese* (9th Cir. 2018) **rejected** a universal independence requirement, substituting a **specific-intent test** (purpose or reckless disregard toward a clearly delineated right).
3. **§52.1(k) — the speech-alone bar — is a live dismissal risk.** Defense will argue the reports are nonviolent speech. The answer is that the coercion lies in weaponizing a mandatory official channel to trigger parole consequences (per *Cornell*'s "essence" test and *Madden*), but the complaint must allege the coercive mechanism explicitly.

### Verified authorities
- ***Venegas v. County of Los Angeles*, 32 Cal.4th 820 (2004)** — VERIFIED via quotation in *Bender* and *Martinez v. Caraballo*. §52.1 is not limited to hate crimes and needs no discriminatory animus; violations need only be "accompanied by the requisite threats, intimidation, or coercion" (p. 843). Defeats "police-only/hate-crime-only" defenses; frame the false reports as deliberate misuse of a parole-control mechanism, not clerical error. *Risk:* defense argues the records *were* the retaliation, lacking any separate coercive feature.
- ***Jones v. Kmart Corp.*, 17 Cal.4th 329 (1998)** — VERIFIED (primary opinion). §52.1 reaches "anyone" with no state-action requirement per se, but a private actor can't violate a right binding only government; private liability works via coercive interference *through* government machinery (the court's example: enlisting police power). Even if HOPE's state-actor status is disputed, false reports funneled to the parole agent fit *Jones*'s "enlisting" route. *Risk:* if neither state action nor through-the-parole-agent interference is shown, *Jones* becomes defense authority.
- ***Chaudhry v. City of Los Angeles*, 751 F.3d 1096, 1105 (9th Cir. 2014)** — VERIFIED via quotation in official *Reese* opinion and *Madden*. §52.1 needs no discriminatory intent; Bane elements tracked the underlying constitutional claim. *Risk:* excessive-force facts; *Reese* later added the specific-intent requirement *Chaudhry* didn't discuss.
- ***Shoyoye v. County of Los Angeles*, 203 Cal.App.4th 947 (2012) — MOST DANGEROUS** — VERIFIED via extensive quotation in published *Bender*. Negligent jail overdetention ≠ Bane violation: coercion must be "independent from the coercion inherent in the wrongful detention itself" (p. 959); no liability for "mere negligence rather than a volitional act" (pp. 957–58). *Distinguish:* Matter 28 alleges deliberate, repeated falsification with retaliatory purpose — the volitional act *Shoyoye* found missing; must be pleaded as such. *Risk:* the lead case defense counsel will cite; anything reading as clerical error gives the court an off-ramp.
- ***Cornell v. City & County of San Francisco*, 17 Cal.App.5th 766 (2017) — BEST CASE** — VERIFIED (primary opinion). No textual independence requirement; coercion is an "aggravator"; the "essence" is preventing plaintiff from doing what law allows; specific intent = clearly delineated right + particular purpose (reckless disregard suffices). Sustained a Bane verdict on a baseless, career-ending retaliatory citation meant to undermine complaining. Closest analogue — official paperwork weaponized through a government channel after rights were exercised; also confirms private-actor reach. *Risk:* *Cornell* had arrest, guns, custody, a criminal citation — plead the concrete coercive leverage of Form 3043 reports (parole-violation exposure, custody, sanctions, chilling), not mere falsity.
- ***Reese v. County of Sacramento*, 888 F.3d 1030 (9th Cir. 2018)** — VERIFIED (official opinion text). No independence requirement, but specific intent required (*Screws*-based, via *Cornell*); reckless disregard suffices. **Correction to the assignment premise:** *Reese* did not lose outright — the jury returned a verdict *for* Reese on the Bane claim ($534,340); the district court set it aside post-verdict over a defective instruction; the Ninth Circuit held the independence reasoning was legal error but remanded for a new trial rather than reinstating. *Application:* controlling federal answer to a *Shoyoye*-based 12(b)(6) attack — plead Smith's knowledge of protected activity, knowledge of falsity, and purpose/reckless disregard as to parole consequences. *Risk:* defense will cite the outcome (no Bane judgment for plaintiff); police-shooting facts.
- ***Bender v. County of Los Angeles*, 217 Cal.App.4th 968 (2013)** — VERIFIED (full text via FindLaw; reporter cite per secondary reference). Affirmed Bane verdict where unlawful arrest + deliberate/spiteful beating of handcuffed plaintiff; rejected "coercion is inherent" defense; actionable coercion is "deliberate or spiteful" and beyond the inherent coercion. Pleading template — characterize entries as deliberate/spiteful retaliation, not mistake. *Risk:* beating case; proves the rule, not the paperwork analogy.
- ***M.H. v. County of Alameda*, No. C-11-02868 (N.D. Cal. Apr. 18, 2013)** — VERIFIED via quotation in published *Bender*. Bane claim survived against Corizon, a *private* contracted jail medical provider + nurse: deliberate indifference "necessarily exceeds negligence"; respondeat superior applied. Rebuts "police-only" framing; institutionally analogous private correctional contractor. *Risk:* unpublished district order; death/serious-medical-need facts.
- ***Dixon v. City of Oakland*, No. C-12-05207 DMR (N.D. Cal. Dec. 8, 2014)** — VERIFIED (primary GovInfo order). Bane claims against a *private security company + five employees* settled for $110,000; court awarded **$316,572.10 in fees against the private defendants under §52.1(h)** (pre-2022 numbering; current fees provision is §52.1(i)). Strongest "private actor feeding falsehoods into official machinery" analogue (guards' account → citizen's arrest/police citation ≈ HOPE's reports → parole action). *Risk:* fees-after-settlement, not a merits adjudication; underlying incident involved force/arrest.
- ***Madden v. Hicks*, No. 1:18-cv-00255-ADA-BAM (E.D. Cal. Feb. 7, 2023, adopted Mar. 15, 2023)** — VERIFIED (primary orders). Bane claim sufficiently pleaded where officer threatened inmate for "pushing claims" and relented only when inmate agreed not to file grievances — retaliation allegations supported the specific-intent inference under *Reese*. Closest retaliation-for-rights-exercise pleading analogue; same structure with the parole agent as the pressure point. *Risk:* unpublished pro se screening-stage order; involved an explicit verbal threat.
- ***Stamps v. Superior Court*, 136 Cal.App.4th 1441, 1449–1452 (2006)** — VERIFIED via quotation in published courts.ca.gov opinion. Bane Act is separate from, not part of, the Unruh Act; no "business establishment" limitation. Answers any Unruh-conflation argument.
- ***Pardella v. South Bay United Pentecostal Church*, No. 3:22-cv-05082-RFL, Dkt. 90 (N.D. Cal. Nov. 15, 2023) — DANGER** — VERIFIED. Dismissed vague Bane claim over false medical records/administrative abuse for failure to specify who said what, how it affected plaintiff, or its Bane nexus. *Warning:* the specificity trap — plead Smith as directing actor, exact entries (HOPE_000142/000194), Form 3043 reports, dates, parole-agent recipient, falsity, preceding protected activity, and concrete consequences. *Risk:* unpublished but persuasive on 12(b)(6) specificity.

**Bane 12(b)(6): MODERATE (moderate-to-strong if pleaded with specificity).** *Venegas* + *Cornell*/*Reese* + *Madden* + *Dixon*/*M.H.*/*Jones* supply every element at the pleading stage, where deliberate falsification and retaliatory motive are taken as true. **No published case holds that false reports to a parole agent, standing alone, satisfy "coercion,"** and §52.1(k) + *Shoyoye* give the defense real ammunition. The claim survives only if the complaint pleads the six specificity elements: (1) specific right/activity + Smith's knowledge; (2) exact false entries and 3043s with dates and parole-agent recipient; (3) Smith's knowledge of falsity and direction; (4) concrete leverage (violation exposure, custody, sanctions, chilling); (5) retaliatory purpose/reckless disregard; (6) a repeated course, not isolated error. A vague pleading (*Pardella* trap) likely gets dismissed with leave to amend.

### Verification caveats for Track B
*Watison* (pin cites via *Moore v. Shinn* restatement — confirm 1114–16 in reporter), *Nieves* (holding via secondary summaries), *Pratt* (via verbatim quotations in *Hydrick*/*Rhodes*), *Venegas*/*Chaudhry* (via quotations in published opinions), *Bender* (reporter cite per secondary reference; text verified), *Madden*/*Wicklund*/*Pardella*/*M.H.*/*Dixon* (unpublished — persuasive only). Do not file pin cites from this memo without reporter confirmation.

# PART III — Civil RICO (18 U.S.C. §1962(c)–(d))

**Overall assessment: WEAK as currently structured.** Enterprise and pattern are plausibly pleadable; proximate-cause standing is the major defect. Fraudulent billing directly injures CDCR, while plaintiff's lost wages/prolonged treatment are downstream through CDCR, parole agents, and other actors.

---

## III.1 Enterprise / distinctness

- ***Cedric Kushner Promotions, Ltd. v. King*, 533 U.S. 158 (2001)** — VERIFIED. A corporate employee/owner is distinct from the incorporated enterprise. Strongest configuration for Matter 28: **Smith = RICO "person"; HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc. = enterprise.**
- ***Sever v. Alaska Pulp Corp.*, 978 F.2d 1529 (9th Cir. 1992)** — VERIFIED. A corporation cannot be both person and enterprise under §1962(c), but officers/employees may be persons with the corporation as enterprise.
- ***Reves v. Ernst & Young*, 507 U.S. 170 (1993)** — VERIFIED. The operation/management test: Smith's alleged CEO direction of the billing scheme fits; secondary actors need some degree of direction.
- ***Living Designs, Inc. v. DuPont*, 431 F.3d 353 (9th Cir. 2005)** — VERIFIED. HOPE plus only its own staff as an association-in-fact is risky/non-distinct; an outside participant would be needed for that alternative.
- ***Boyle v. United States*, 556 U.S. 938 (2009)** — VERIFIED. Association-in-fact requires purpose, relationships, and sufficient longevity — but does not cure distinctness.

## III.2 Pattern / continuity

- ***H.J. Inc. v. Northwestern Bell Tel. Co.*, 492 U.S. 229 (1989)** — VERIFIED. Pattern requires continuity plus relationship; years-long regular business conduct can support both closed- and open-ended continuity. A ten-year billing practice is duration-strong if facts show it extended beyond plaintiff and was HOPE's regular business.
- ***Allwaste, Inc. v. Hecht*, 65 F.3d 1523 (9th Cir. 1995)** — VERIFIED. No rigid one-year rule; thirteen months could suffice.
- ***Religious Technology Center v. Wollersheim*, 971 F.2d 364 (9th Cir. 1992)** — VERIFIED. Short, finite activity without future threat fails — defense will invoke it.
- ***Howard v. AOL*, 208 F.3d 741 (9th Cir. 2000)** — VERIFIED. Conclusory "regular way of business" language is insufficient without concrete dates, invoices, recipients, and repetitions.
- ***Sever* (supra)** — also supports dismissal if the court characterizes the case as a finite retaliation scheme against one person rather than a business-model scheme.
- *Best framing:* a multi-parolee business-model billing scheme lasting years, with the false Form 3043/no-show reports as a concealment or continuation mechanism — not merely a one-person vendetta.

## III.3 Standing / proximate cause — the dispositive danger

- ***Anza v. Ideal Steel Supply Corp.*, 547 U.S. 451 (2006)** — VERIFIED. **MOST DANGEROUS CASE.** Fraud on a government entity; downstream competitor was not the direct victim — the plaintiff's harm flowed through conduct "entirely distinct from the alleged RICO violation." The Matter 28 structure is close: CDCR is the payor/direct victim; plaintiff claims downstream career and treatment harm. But-for damages would require the "intricate, uncertain inquiry" the directness requirement forbids.
- ***Holmes v. SIPC*, 503 U.S. 258 (1992)** — VERIFIED. RICO requires a direct relation between predicate conduct and injury.
- ***Hemi Group, LLC v. City of New York*, 559 U.S. 1 (2010)** — VERIFIED. Multiple intervening actors defeat directness. (**Do not miscitereport as 561 U.S. 247.**)
- ***Canyon County v. Syngenta Seeds, Inc.*, 519 F.3d 969 (9th Cir. 2008)** — VERIFIED. Constructing a speculative lawful counterfactual is the intricate inquiry proximate-cause doctrine avoids. Also rejects the "not merely derivative" distinction plaintiff might attempt.
- ***Bridge v. Phoenix Bond & Indem. Co.*, 553 U.S. 639 (2008)** — VERIFIED. First-party reliance is not required for mail-fraud predicates — but *Bridge* does not eliminate *Holmes*/*Anza* proximate cause, and its plaintiffs were the direct and intended victims, unlike plaintiff under the CDCR-billing theory. Closest Supreme Court analog to the open-ended scheme, but the directness gap remains.
- ***Painters & Allied Trades Dist. Council 82 Health Care Fund v. Takeda Pharm. Co.*, 943 F.3d 1243 (9th Cir. 2019)** — VERIFIED. **BEST PLAINTIFF CASE** — the only binding circuit authority reversing a 12(b)(6) dismissal of health-fraud RICO claims on proximate cause. *Limitation:* those plaintiffs were the payors directly parted from money — the structural position CDCR occupies here, not plaintiff.
- ***Mendoza v. Zirkle Fruit Co.*, 301 F.3d 1163 (9th Cir. 2002)** — VERIFIED. Wage-based RICO injury can be cognizable, but the labor-market fraud there was more direct.
- *Rescue path (narrow):* identify direct out-of-pocket treatment costs paid by plaintiff himself, if any — none were verified — or plead Form 3043 fraud as directly intended to cause prolonged treatment, though *Anza*/*Canyon County* remain serious barriers. **Do not invent direct payments; none were verified.**

## III.4 Rule 9(b) and conspiracy

- ***Edwards v. Marin Park, Inc.*, 356 F.3d 1058 (9th Cir. 2004)** — VERIFIED. Plead time, place, specific content, speaker, recipient, and why false for each predicate.
- ***Lancaster Community Hospital v. Antelope Valley Hospital District*, 940 F.2d 397 (9th Cir. 1991)** — VERIFIED. Particularize each defendant's role and plead at least two distinct predicates.
- ***Eclectic Properties East, LLC v. Marcus & Millichap Co.*, 751 F.3d 990 (9th Cir. 2014)** — VERIFIED. Complaint volume does not replace facts plausibly showing specific fraudulent intent (*Twombly*/*Iqbal*).
- ***Salinas v. United States*, 522 U.S. 52 (1997)** — VERIFIED. A conspiracy defendant need not personally commit two predicates, but facts must show agreement to further the enterprise.
- ***Howard v. AOL* (supra)** — §1962(d) conspiracy fails if no substantive RICO violation is adequately pleaded.
- Use the RFA/SROG admissions as particularity anchors — not substitutes for dates, invoice amounts, recipients, and roles.

## III.5 Remedies and limits

- 18 U.S.C. §1964(c): mandatory treble damages and suit costs after establishing injury and direct causation.
- ***Diaz v. Gates*, 420 F.3d 897 (9th Cir. 2005) (en banc)** — VERIFIED. Lost employment opportunities can constitute injury to business/property — but it does not solve proximate cause.
- ***Kay v. Ehrler*, 499 U.S. 432 (1991)** — a pro se litigant recovers no attorney's fees. Circuit-specific application to §1964(c) was not verified from a published Ninth Circuit case — mark as unverified before briefing.
- ***Beck v. Prupis*, 529 U.S. 494 (2000)** — VERIFIED. A §1962(d) claim needs injury by reason of a racketeering overt act.
- Double-recovery offset principle: **not supported by a verified case** in this pass — verify before citing.

**RICO bottom line:** Keep RICO lean, particularized, and strictly alternative. Do not build the case around it. §1983/Monell and potentially Bane Act are the realistic vehicles. Expect dismissal, likely with leave to amend, unless a direct injury to plaintiff himself can be pleaded.

### RICO do-not-cite / correction list
- Do not cite *Grimmett v. Brown* for pattern — it is an accrual/limitations case.
- *Hemi Group* is **559 U.S. 1 (2010)**, not 561 U.S. 247.
- Do not rely on *Neibel*'s §1962(d) gloss without addressing *Salinas* and *United States v. Fernandez*.
- *Sun Savings v. Dierdorff* was not verified from a primary source — do not use without verification.

# PART IV — Fraud / intentional misrepresentation and negligent misrepresentation

**Overall: intentional fraud WEAK-TO-MODERATE but curable by amendment; negligent misrepresentation WEAK — likely dismissed unless re-pleaded around direct-to-plaintiff statements.**

---

## IV.1 Pleading skeleton and specificity

- ***Lazar v. Superior Court*, 12 Cal.4th 631 (1996)** — VERIFIED. The five elements: (1) misrepresentation, (2) knowledge of falsity (scienter), (3) intent to defraud (induce reliance), (4) justifiable reliance, (5) resulting damage. Fraud must be pleaded with particularity — facts constituting each element. The false reports map onto elements 1–3; the fight is over elements 4 and 5.
- ***Stansfield v. Starkey*, 220 Cal.App.3d 59 (1990)** — VERIFIED. Fraud's particularity "necessitates pleading facts which show how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered" (at 73). In federal court this converges with Rule 9(b); N.D. Cal. requires corporate-fraud plaintiffs to allege the speakers' names, authority, recipients, content, and dates (*Edejer v. DHI Mortgage*). Plead each false Form 3043 and no-show log by date, author, recipient (named parole agent/CDCR office), and content. Group pleading against "HOPE and Smith" without attributing specific reports to Smith personally invites a 12(b)(6) attack on the individual defendant.

## IV.2 Reliance — the whole fight (intentional fraud)

- ***Small v. Fritz Companies, Inc.*, 30 Cal.4th 167 (2003)** — VERIFIED. **MOST DANGEROUS (fraud).** California recognizes "holder" fraud claims only on "a bona fide and specifically pleaded showing of the plaintiff's actual reliance." Every core Track D allegation involves HOPE lying **to** the parole agent and CDCR, with the parole agent relying to plaintiff's detriment. Plaintiff knew the no-show reports were false when made — he cannot have "relied" on them.
- ***Randi W. v. Muroc Joint Unified School Dist.*, 14 Cal.4th 1066 (1997)** — VERIFIED. **BEST CASE (fraud).** Where a defendant makes an affirmative misrepresentation to a third party presenting a foreseeable and substantial risk of harm, the ultimate injured plaintiff — who never received the statement — may sue. The demurrer was overruled. HOPE's false reports to the parole agent are structurally identical to the false recommendation letters: misrepresentation to an intermediary, foreseeable harm to the identifiable subject. Plaintiff is not a random foreseeable third party — he is the **specifically intended target** of the adverse action, stronger than *Randi W.*'s facts. Pair with *Bily*'s rule that intentional fraud reaches foreseeable third persons and CACI 1900's "intent to defraud the plaintiff or a particular class of persons to which plaintiff belonged."
- *Risk on Randi W.:* its duty analysis is anchored in foreseeable **physical** harm; Matter 28's harms are economic/reputational. Defense will distinguish on harm type. Counter: the case establishes that a non-recipient plaintiff can sue on a misrepresentation made to another — and it survived **demurrer**, exactly the 12(b)(6) posture here.

## IV.3 Negligent misrepresentation — boxed in by duty

- ***Bily v. Arthur Young & Co.*, 3 Cal.4th 370 (1992)** — VERIFIED. **MOST DANGEROUS (negligent misrepresentation).** For **negligent** misrepresentation, liability runs only to the person or limited class the report was intended to benefit or influence in a specific transaction — mere foreseeability is insufficient (Restatement Second of Torts §552). For **intentional** fraud, liability extends more broadly to reasonably foreseeable third persons. The Form 3043s and no-show logs were prepared **for** CDCR/parole officials — plaintiff is the report's *subject*, not its intended relying recipient. No duty runs to him for negligent misrepresentation. Conversely, *Bily*'s broader intentional-fraud rule is plaintiff's lifeline for the fraud claim.
- ***Soderberg v. McKinney*, 44 Cal.App.4th 1760 (1996)** — VERIFIED. Mere anticipation that information will reach unspecified third parties does not create a duty. Reinforces *Bily*: even if HOPE knew plaintiff would eventually see the reports in his parole file, that does not create a duty.
- ***Ragland v. U.S. Bank Nat'l Ass'n*, 209 Cal.App.4th 182 (2012)** — VERIFIED. **BEST CASE (negligent misrepresentation)** — the only escape: false statements made **directly to plaintiff** that induced **his own** conduct. Identify any false statement Smith or HOPE staff made to plaintiff himself (program requirements, graduation criteria, reporting representations) on which he justifiably acted. *If discovery has none, drop the claim* — it adds little that intentional fraud does not cover, and carries narrower damages.

## IV.4 Economic-loss rule — a paper tiger here

- ***Robinson Helicopter Co. v. Dana Corp.*, 34 Cal.4th 979 (2004)** — VERIFIED. The economic-loss rule does not bar fraud claims based on affirmative intentional misrepresentations (there, knowingly false certificates of conformance) independent of mere breach. The false Form 3043s and fabricated no-show logs are the *Robinson* analogue to the false certificates — distinct from merely deficient treatment. **Even better for plaintiff: he has no contract with HOPE (CDCR does), so the rule, which polices the tort/contract boundary between contracting parties, arguably does not apply to him at all.** *Weakness:* *Robinson*'s exception is narrow and still requires the plaintiff's reliance — it does not solve the *Small* problem.
- ***Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc.*, 17 Cal.5th 1 (2024)** — VERIFIED (op. filed 8/22/2024). A plaintiff may assert fraudulent **concealment** during a contractual relationship if (1) the elements are established independently of contractual rights/obligations and (2) the conduct exposes plaintiff to harm beyond the parties' reasonable contemplation when they contracted. Useful if a concealment theory is added (e.g., concealment of the no-early-graduation policy or of the reports' falsity); reserve given the no-privity point above.

## IV.5 Limitations

- ***Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 44 Cal.3d 1103 (1988)** — VERIFIED. Suspicion of wrongdoing triggers the limitations period; the plaintiff "cannot wait for the facts to find her." **The defense's best limitations authority:** plaintiff presumably knew in real time that attended sessions were logged as no-shows — that is contemporaneous suspicion of falsity, starting the clock then.
- ***Fox v. Ethicon Endo-Surgery, Inc.*, 35 Cal.4th 797 (2005)** — VERIFIED. Delayed-discovery pleading checklist: specifically plead the time and manner of discovery and facts showing inability to discover earlier despite diligence. Needed for the Form 3043/billing allegations — reports in government files plaintiff could not access may have been inherently undiscoverable until he saw his file. *Fox*'s specificity burden is demanding — conclusory "I only recently discovered" pleading fails.

## IV.6 Damages discipline

- ***Alliance Mortgage Co. v. Rothwell*, 10 Cal.4th 1226 (1995)** — VERIFIED. Per *Alliance Mortgage*, plead plaintiff's wage/supervision losses as **consequential damages proximately caused by the parole agent's reliance** (§3333), not benefit-of-the-bargain (no "bargain" — plaintiff paid nothing to HOPE; CDCR did; no fiduciary relationship pleaded). **The government's billing losses are the government's damages, not plaintiff's — keep them out of the fraud damages** (they belong to RICO/False Claims theories, if anywhere). Proximate cause will be attacked via the parole agent's discretionary decisions as an intervening actor; *Randi W.* helps (foreseeability of the intermediary's action). Negligent-misrepresentation damages are narrower — the wage-loss theory is materially stronger under intentional fraud.

## IV.7 Statutory notes (flagged, not fully re-verified today)

Code Civ. Proc. §338(d) (three-year fraud limitations); Civ. Code §§3300/3333/3343 (damages measures) — confirm pinpoint language before filing. Matter 28 is not a §3343 property transaction, so §3343's formula applies by analogy at most; the operative measure is §3333's proximate-cause detriment plus consequential loss per *Alliance Mortgage*.

**Fraud bottom line:** Falsity and scienter can be pleaded with *Stansfield* specificity from the Bates records. Survival depends on the *Randi W.* + *Bily* intended-victim theory — pleaded as fact, not conclusion — plus any direct-to-plaintiff misrepresentations (*Ragland* pattern). Expect dismissal with leave to amend if the complaint currently alleges only "defendants lied to CDCR and I was harmed."

## V.2 Preclusion authorities

### California claim-preclusion framework
- ***DKN Holdings LLC v. Faerber*, 61 Cal.4th 813, 824 (2015)** — VERIFIED. Claim preclusion needs (1) the same cause of action (2) between the same parties (3) after a final judgment on the merits. **No bar against Faerber, who was not a party** to the first action — reaffirming claim preclusion applies only between the same parties or those in privity (citing *Dillard v. McKnight*, 34 Cal.2d 209, 214 — business partners are not in privity). *Application:* Smith was not a Matter 5 party; CEO status ≠ privity. *Risk:* *DKN* also confirms issue preclusion *can* be invoked by a nonparty — but only for issues "actually litigated and necessarily decided."
- ***Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.*, 28 Cal.4th 888, 904 (2002)** — VERIFIED. California uses the **primary-rights theory** — one invasion of one primary right = one cause of action; *"Even where there are multiple legal theories upon which recovery might be predicated, one injury gives rise to only one claim for relief."* Establishes the test; the fight is over what counts as the same primary right.
- ***Boeken v. Philip Morris USA, Inc.*, 48 Cal.4th 788, 797–798 (2010)** — VERIFIED. **MOST DANGEROUS.** *"[T]he 'cause of action' is the right to obtain redress for a harm suffered, regardless of the specific remedy sought or the legal theory (common law or statutory) advanced... **the determinative factor is the harm suffered**."* **If the state judgment were ever treated as final and on the merits, the §1983/Monell, Bane Act, fraud, and RICO claims would likely be deemed the same cause of action** since they seek redress for the same injury from the same conduct.
- ***Goddard v. Security Title Ins. & Guar. Co.*, 14 Cal.2d 47, 52–53 (1939)** — VERIFIED. A judgment after a sustained general demurrer *may* be on the merits — *"where the demurrer sets up the failure of the facts alleged to establish a cause of action, and the same facts are pleaded in the second action"* — **but** a judgment on *special* demurrer for "technical or formal defects is clearly not on the merits," and "even a judgment on general demurrer may not be on the merits, for the defects set up may be technical or formal." Defense will quote the "absolute defense disclosed by the allegations" language; plaintiff answers that exhaustion is a procedural/pleading defect, not a substantive adjudication.
- ***Keidatz v. Albany*, 39 Cal.2d 826 (1952)** — VERIFIED. A demurrer judgment bars only to the extent it adjudicates that the pleaded facts state no claim — *"if... new or additional facts are alleged that cure the defects in the original pleading... the former judgment is not a bar"*; but if "the demurrer was sustained... on a ground equally applicable to the second [action], the former judgment will also be a bar." Caps preclusive scope at the pleaded facts; genuinely new federal-complaint allegations escape. *Risk:* the "ground equally applicable" sentence — if the federal complaint repleads the same operative facts, defense argues the exhaustion defect travels with them.

### The exhaustion exception (plaintiff's core merits-element defense)
- ***Perez v. City of San Bruno*, 27 Cal.3d 875, 882–883 (1980)** — VERIFIED. **BEST PLAINTIFF CASE (with *Heath*).** Caption is *City of San Bruno*, not a bare “Perez.” A dismissal on the ground of **prematurity** "was [not] one based on the merits," so a later action "is not barred" (quoting Restatement of Judgments §54).
- ***Heath v. Cleary*, 708 F.2d 1376, 1380 n.4 (9th Cir. 1983)** — VERIFIED. Pin is **1380 n.4**, not 1379 n.4. *"It appears that California courts would conclude that a denial of a writ of mandamus for failure to exhaust administrative remedies is **not a decision on the merits**"* (citing *Perez*, Restatement (Second) of Judgments §20(2)). **The closest on-point authority** — a Ninth Circuit prediction of California law that exhaustion dismissals are not on the merits. Combined with *Perez*, this defeats the "on the merits" element against **both** defendants in one stroke. *Risk:* a footnote prediction, not a California Supreme Court holding — but never contradicted.
- ***Johnson v. City of Loma Linda*, 24 Cal.4th 61, 70 (2000)** — verified as quoted in published federal orders (recommend final cite-check). "Exhaustion of administrative remedies is a **jurisdictional prerequisite** to resort to the courts" — mirrors the state court's own "jurisdictional requirement" framing.
- ***MIB, Inc. v. Superior Court*, 106 Cal.App.3d 228, 232–233 (1980)** — verified as quoted in published orders (cite-check). A finding as to jurisdictional facts may get preclusive effect *"although the dismissal is not res judicata on the merits of the underlying cause of action."* Concedes the main point; the caveat matters only for **issue** preclusion on jurisdictional facts.

### §1983-as-different-primary-right (assessed: WEAK — do not lead with it)
*Gonzales v. California Dep't of Corr.*, 739 F.3d 1226, 1233–1234 (9th Cir. 2014); *Furnace v. Giurbino*, 838 F.3d 1019, 1024 (9th Cir. 2016); *Franklin v. City of Kingsburg* (E.D. Cal. 2022): all reject the proposition that a §1983 claim categorically invades a different primary right than parallel state claims — courts look at the **harm**, per *Boeken*. No California case was found holding otherwise. **Do not brief the "federal right = different primary right" argument as settled law.**

### Final-judgment requirement
- ***Kay v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes*, 504 F.3d 803, 808 (9th Cir. 2007)** — year is **2007**, not 2008. Verified from the Ninth Circuit CDN. Applying Cal. law: *"the finality required to invoke the preclusive bar of res judicata is not achieved until an appeal from the trial court judgment has been exhausted or the time to appeal has expired"* (citing *Franklin & Franklin v. 7-Eleven Owners for Fair Franchising*, 85 Cal.App.4th 1168, 1174). Two live issues: (1) no judgment of dismissal appears in the Matter 5 register (CCP §581d requires entry of judgment); (2) CRC 8.104’s 60-day clock runs from a **judgment**, not from an order sustaining a demurrer. *Risk:* timing-based and curable — defendants can move for entry of judgment. The merits holding (*Perez* / *Heath*) is what survives that cure. See the unified memo.

### Nonparty preclusion (Smith's shield)
- ***Taylor v. Sturgell*, 553 U.S. 880, 892–895 (2008)** — VERIFIED (unanimous). "Virtual representation" (binding a nonparty for shared interests) is **disapproved**; nonparty preclusion allowed only in six categories: (1) agreement to be bound; (2) preexisting substantive legal relationship; (3) adequate representation in limited circumstances; (4) assumption of control of the litigation; (5) proxy/agent relitigation; (6) special statutory scheme. Smith fits none on current facts — CEO status is not a *Taylor* category. *Risk:* category (4) — if discovery shows Smith actually **controlled** the state-court defense (hired/paid counsel, directed strategy), *Montana v. United States*, 440 U.S. 154, binds her. Keep litigation-control facts in mind.

### Full faith and credit (§1983 is not exempt)
- **28 U.S.C. §1738**; ***Allen v. McCurry*, 449 U.S. 90, 96–99 (1980)** — VERIFIED. §1738 requires federal courts to give state judgments the preclusive effect the rendering state's courts would give; §1983 does not repeal §1738 — issue preclusion applies in §1983 suits.
- ***Migra v. Warren City Sch. Dist. Bd. of Educ.*, 465 U.S. 75, 80–85 (1984)** — VERIFIED. Extends to **claim** preclusion — even never-litigated §1983 claims are barred if state preclusion elements are met. **Runner-up most dangerous:** if defense ever satisfies the California elements, even the RICO/§1983 theories die with the state claims.
- Ninth Circuit three-element test: ***Robi v. Five Platters, Inc.*, 838 F.2d 318, 322 (9th Cir. 1988)**; ***Headwaters Inc. v. U.S. Forest Serv.*, 399 F.3d 1047, 1052 (9th Cir. 2005)**; ***Stewart v. U.S. Bancorp*, 297 F.3d 953, 956 (9th Cir. 2002)** — VERIFIED. (1) identity of claims, (2) final judgment on the merits, (3) identity/privity of parties. *Headwaters* (quoting *In re Schimmels*, 127 F.3d 875, 881): privity requires the later party be *"so identified in interest with a party to former litigation that he represents precisely the same right in respect to the subject matter involved"* — shared corporate affiliation alone does not suffice. *Stewart* notes a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal is "on the merits" (*Federated Dep't Stores v. Moitie*, 452 U.S. 394, 399 n.3) — which is why the *Perez*/*Heath* exhaustion exception, not the dismissal-form argument, is the load-bearing wall.

### Voluntary dismissal (Matter 6)
- ***Semtek Int'l Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.*, 531 U.S. 497, 505–509 (2001)** — VERIFIED. An "adjudication upon the merits" is the **opposite of a dismissal without prejudice**; a Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) first voluntary dismissal **without prejudice has no claim-preclusive effect**. The two-dismissal rule (Rule 41(a)(1)(B)) is the only trap: a *second* voluntary dismissal of the same claim operates as on the merits. **Practice point: never voluntarily dismiss the N.D. Cal. action.**

### Issue preclusion (the defense's fallback)
- ***Arizona v. California*, 530 U.S. 392, 414 (2000)** — VERIFIED. Issue preclusion attaches only to issues *"actually litigated and determined by a valid and final judgment"* where *"the determination is essential to the judgment."* The state court decided exactly one thing — failure to exhaust — and nothing about fraud elements, RICO predicates, Bane Act coercion, or Monell policy. No merits issue was "actually litigated."
- **Restatement (Second) of Judgments §20(2)** (via *Costello v. United States*, 365 U.S. 265, 285–88 (1961); *Bowden v. United States*, 106 F.3d 433, 441 (D.C. Cir. 1997)): *"A valid and final personal judgment for the defendant, which rests on the prematurity of the action or on the plaintiff's failure to satisfy a precondition to suit, does not bar another action by the plaintiff instituted after the claim has matured, or the precondition has been satisfied."*
- *Flagged defense point:* the exhaustion finding itself **was** actually litigated and essential — so Smith/HOPE could issue-preclude plaintiff from relitigating *whether he exhausted*. The answer: (1) exhaustion is now satisfied/cured or inapplicable; and (2) **§1983 has no exhaustion-of-state-administrative-remedies requirement as black-letter law** (*Patsy v. Board of Regents*, 457 U.S. 496 (1982) — cite-check before filing since outside the verified batch), nor do RICO or the Bane Act. The estopped issue is therefore immaterial to the federal claims.

**Preclusion bottom line:** The defense's best path is: (i) characterize exhaustion as a substantive "absolute defense" under *Goddard*, then (ii) invoke *Boeken* to collapse all federal theories into the same primary right, then (iii) manufacture Smith privity via alter-ego/agency. Each step is uphill, but step (ii) is genuinely dangerous law.
