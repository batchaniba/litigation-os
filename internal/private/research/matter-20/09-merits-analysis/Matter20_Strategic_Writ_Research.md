## 1. Recommended remedial theory and merits assessment

**Lead with the statutory right to a lawful victimhood determination, not with the length of the denial.** California expressly enumerates false imprisonment and unlawful criminal restraint as qualifying activities, creates a conditional statutory duty to certify, and presumes helpfulness when the victim has not failed to provide reasonably requested assistance. Penal Code §679.10(c)(18)-(19), (g)(1), (h), (k)(1). The April 20 letter rejected qualifying and substantially similar activity; it did not say petitioner was unhelpful. CHP admits receipt of the April 9 request packet and the denial. **M2, M4, M12-13**; **W4-6**. The superior court's surviving task is to determine whether that substantive rejection constituted reviewable abuse or legal error, then select relief justified by adjudicated facts.

**Tier 1—direct certification:** legally possible if the court determines the facts establishing qualifying criminal victimhood, helpfulness, and all other certification conditions, and finds only one lawful outcome. See *California Correctional Supervisors Organization, Inc. v. Department of Corrections* (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827. **On the supplied record, this result is not presently conclusively proved.** The arrest-related reports allege multiple impairment indicators, and the native dashcam is not included in either PDF.

**Tier 2—vacatur and controlled remand:** viable only upon an actual finding that CHP used an erroneous statutory test, relied on a demonstrably material factual error, disregarded competent relevant evidence in a legally abusive manner, or reached a determination outside permissible discretion. A short denial, no DUI charges, and a later detention certificate do not independently prove abuse. *Saleeby v. State Bar* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547, 560–563.

**Tier 3—focused merits factfinding:** a useful bridge, not a third substantive cause of action. The authenticated video, accurate reconstruction of the field-test chronology, objective prearrest observations, CHP's contrary records, and what formed the factual basis of certification can be determined under ordinary evidentiary rules. Code Civ. Proc. §§1090, 1091, 1094; *Western States Petroleum Assn. v. Superior Court* (1995) 9 Cal.4th 559, 575–576.

The motion packet already asks for remand first and direct certification conditionally (**W2, W13**); the requested research hierarchy would elevate **direct certification in the prayer as a conditional primary request**, make **remand the executable alternative**, and **retain a further-hearing fallback**. The narrative should not suggest direct relief is currently established by undisputed evidence.

## 2. Best route to direct certification

The shortest possible findings chain is: **(1)** CHP is a certifying entity and Serrato an authorized official (expressly admitted, **M3–4**); **(2)** petitioner was intentionally detained and transported against his will (CHP Answer ¶¶38–41; **M8–9**; petitioner declaration, **W15–18**); **(3)** no objectively lawful authority supported that custodial restraint after applying the contemporaneously applicable DUI and warrantless-arrest standards; **(4)** the unlawful personal restraint constituted Penal Code §236 or a qualifying substantially similar offense, and directly harmed petitioner; **(5)** petitioner possessed incident information and satisfies §679.10(g)–(h) helpfulness, with no proven refusal to furnish reasonably requested assistance about the alleged officer offense; **(6)** no unresolved statutory predicate or lawful basis for refusal remains.

One must not assert that a judicial finding of a prior criminal conviction or officer prosecution is required. Section 679.10(k)(1) expressly rejects those prerequisites, and U certification is distinct from USCIS's adjudication of status. Conversely, the words “shall certify” do not eliminate a genuine threshold evaluation of qualifying victimhood.

**What would change the evidentiary posture:** a custodian-authenticated original dashcam, a precise ruling rejecting report descriptions materially necessary to probable cause, findings on Vehicle Code §40300.5's alternatives, affirmative proof of the April decision's materially false predicate, and a finding that no remaining reasonable route sustains the no-qualifying-crime conclusion. Declarations and admissions may narrow issues; they do not automatically establish the disputed officer-sensory observations. Prove objective elements, not motive or presumed disciplinary findings.

## 3. Best route to vacatur and controlled reconsideration

Request that the court identify **the specific material error it actually finds** (for example, a false factual premise that petitioner refused the walk-and-turn because it was difficult **if that premise actually influenced a legally material custody determination**). It may then vacate the April denial, order decision on the April 9 application under §679.10(c), (d), (g), (h), (i)(2), and (k), prohibit reliance on the adjudicated error, and require reconsideration and a verified return by dates set in the judgment. Do not ask for a generalized “give it a more thorough review” order untethered to established legal error.

**Proof gap:** RFP No. 8 expressly seeks the original April 9–20 non-Pitchess decision file; the October 20 response promises nonprivileged documents that exist but does **not** affirmatively identify what Serrato read or explain the merits basis of the denial. **M30–31**. Separate discovery or appropriately limited evidentiary proof should resolve the April-decision nexus before representing it as fact.

On remand the court can bind CHP to **the law** and **its adjudicated findings**, while leaving unresolved credibility and threshold choices to the authorized official. That preserves the line recognized in *Saleeby* and *California Correctional Supervisors Organization*.

## 5. False-imprisonment and color-of-authority elements

Use **CALCRIM No. 1242 (2026)**, *People v. Agnew* (1940) 16 Cal.2d 655, 659–660, *Fermino v. Fedco, Inc.* (1994) 7 Cal.4th 701, 715–716, and Penal Code §236. Misdemeanor false imprisonment requires intentional restraint/detention or confinement causing a person to remain or go somewhere against their will; where lawful authority is contested, unlawfulness must be proved. It is a **general-intent** offense. Do **not** graft onto misdemeanor §236 a requirement to prove felony violence/menace, personal malice, fraud, criminal prosecution, or a prior conviction.

**Intentional restraint:** strong, essentially undisputed. CHP admitted the arrest, handcuffs, transport, and November 1–5 custody (**M8–11**, Answer ¶¶38–42). Against-will evidence is in **W16–18**. **No-lawful-authority element:** actively disputed. The Form 202 in **M42–46 (AGO 000029–000033)** reports HGN, Romberg, odor, slurring, red/watery eyes, earlier driving, can, PAS refusal; several are challenged, but not all are disproved by silence in audio. Absence of charges and §851.6 classification prove neither contemporaneous no probable cause nor criminal guilt. The evidentiary inquiry must decide what was known and legally sufficient **when custodial restraint occurred**.

**§146(a)** expressly covers a public officer arresting or detaining another against their will **without regular process or other lawful authority**. Its color-of-authority feature is supported by CHP uniforms, report and commands, but unlawful authority is unresolved. Treat it as an **alternative overlapping offense** only if a supported element-comparison under §679.10(d) identifies substantial similarity to listed false imprisonment/unlawful criminal restraint. A search without consent is not itself the listed offense; preserve the initial entry as timing/context if needed.

**Civil-immunity caution:** Penal Code §847(b) is limited by its own terms to **civil liability** for false arrest/imprisonment; do not misstate it as universal criminal immunity. It may inform the objective lawful-authority dispute, not erase §236 or §146. A U-certification decision is not a tort-damages judgment.

The fourth and fifth steps cannot be skipped. There is **no proved direct causal link yet** between Serrato's internal reasoning and the later October Answer's apparent test-description error.

## 7. Focused probable-cause and warrantless-arrest analysis

**DUI suspicion versus driving in presence.** CHP admits the car was stationary and contact was welfare-related (**M7–8, Answer ¶¶34–35**). *Mercer v. DMV* (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753, 768–769 explains the volitional-movement concept and the in-presence issue in its particular DMV implied-consent context. It **does not categorically bar proof of earlier driving by admission**, nor supersede Vehicle Code §40300.5. The officers had the driver's admitted prior travel. A lawful misdemeanor DUI arrest without witnessing driving still requires reasonable cause **and** an applicable statutory circumstance under §40300.5(a)–(e) (accident, vehicle obstructing roadway, flight/unavailability, threatened injury/property harm, or threatened destruction/concealment of evidence). Mere placement on a **shoulder** does not by itself establish §40300.5(b). Test every asserted alternative against grounded prearrest facts, not assumed scenarios.

**Impairment observations:** CHP reported an empty alcoholic can separately from the cup later identified as urine; the cup finding narrows—but does not eliminate—the alcohol-suspicion narrative. Its Answer admits no officer spoke odor or slurred-speech concerns aloud, not that no officer perceived odor or impairment (**M9–10, Answer ¶¶44–47**). HGN and Romberg descriptions require officer testimony/recording and, as appropriate, expert challenge. Fatigue or coherent responses are relevant but do not neutralize other evidence on their own.

**PAS:** *People v. Jackson* (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461, 1465–1470 forbids using refusal of voluntary preliminary alcohol screening as **consciousness-of-guilt trial evidence**. It does not decide every prearrest reasonable-cause issue. Note specifically the pre-PAS advice and separate post-arrest implied-consent exchange (**M82–83**); avoid conflation. Refusal to assist **in a separate DUI investigation** also cannot automatically rebut helpfulness regarding alleged officer criminal restraint under §679.10(k)(3)(H).

**Later evidence:** blood 0.073 ± 0.004 at **02:38** is later than custody and does not permit ungrounded retrograde calculations; it does not defeat §23152(a) impairment allegations. The **02:34** blood warrant does not retrospectively create the grounds existing at arrest. An inactive THC metabolite alone is not proof of contemporaneous active THC impairment. The §851.6 certificate (see statute) is a detention-record classification and lack-of-accusatory-pleading context, not a judicial ruling of police criminality.

**Earlier vehicle opening/parole:** transcript places front door opening ~00:56:43, rear door ~00:59:46, and dispatch parole statement ~01:11:42, after cuffs (**M74–77, M82**). Do not infer officer private knowledge conclusively from the first audible dispatch statement; seek CAD/CLETS timestamps and testimony. *People v. Sanders* (2003) 31 Cal.4th 318 limits post-hoc search-condition rationale. Keep this under one paragraph for context unless it establishes an actual criminal-restraint stage. An unlawful **search** alone is not §236 certification.

## 8. Traditional mandate, standard of review and factfinding

Code Civ. Proc. §1085 gives a court jurisdiction to compel acts specially enjoined by law; §1086 requires a beneficial interest and no adequate plain, speedy ordinary remedy. CHP's admission of official status and refusal supports those threshold points. *Saleeby* explains that traditional mandate corrects abuse/excess of authority, though not how an official should exercise a genuinely lawful choice. *California Correctional Supervisors Organization* supplies a direct “only one reasonable choice” formulation. *Common Cause* 49 Cal.3d 432 is frequently invoked, but its **p.442** discussion predominantly addresses **preliminary injunction standards**; cite more precisely for the actual principle being invoked, rather than attributing every proposition to p.442.

*Western States Petroleum Assn.* 9 Cal.4th at **575–576** generally limits extra-record evidence in **quasi-legislative** traditional-mandate cases but expressly preserves admission for **ministerial/informal** administrative actions if facts are disputed. This was an individual certification determination, with no formal evidentiary hearing identified. The better procedural characterization is **informal individual administrative action**, subject to ordinary admissibility and judicial control; not an automatic license to relitigate everything or to defeat statutory privileges.

Under **§1094**, a petition may be decided by **noticed motion** if it presents **no triable fact issue** or is **based solely on an administrative record**. **§1091** permits proof to countervail the return/Answer. **§1090** gives the court discretion to send an essential factual question to a jury with specifically stated questions; **it does not require a jury** and should not be cited as automatic authority for any preferred bench-trial procedure. Request the court to manage contested evidence appropriately and expressly preserve other available civil factfinding procedures. Section **1088.5** deals with petition service proof; avoid characterizing it as a blanket certified-record requirement. A valid hearing setting, service and exhibits remain independent procedural prerequisites.

Retained jurisdiction and a verified compliance return are conventional, requested **judicial terms** to implement a granted writ; 30-day reconsideration and 45-day return are proposed compliance dates, not a claim CHP violated the original 30-day statutory processing deadline.
