## The separation that should govern the motion
**Question 1 (Nov. 1, 2024):** At each stage of custody, what *did the officers know* and what lawful privilege to detain/arrest was available? A later laboratory result cannot retroactively negate reasonable cause assessed at arrest.
**Question 2 (Apr. 20, 2026):** When CHP rejected certification on the qualifying-crime threshold, what material *did it have/consider*, what legal test did it use, and did it rely on a prohibited consideration or materially incorrect premise? A contemporaneous mistake or abuse in the second decision can support review under CCP 1085 even if aspects of initial roadside conduct were legitimate. But a sparse denial alone proves neither statutory error nor failure to review.

This is a test plan, not a conclusion of officer criminal guilt or agency abuse.

## Potentially decisive fact: vehicle on shoulder versus obstructing roadway
The source-described CHP Form 202 says an occupied Tesla was stopped on the **right shoulder of eastbound I-580**, not that officers observed movement. The DS-367 reportedly marks **driver admitted driving**, not officer observed driving. These facts establish a fact-specific question; they do **not** independently establish a wrongful arrest.

**2026 governing statute:** Vehicle Code §40300.5, operative text verified, states officers may arrest without witnessing DUI driving when reasonable cause of preceding DUI exists and an enumerated condition applies: (a) crash, (b) person in/about vehicle **obstructing a roadway**, (c) immediate apprehension necessary, (d) immediate danger to person/property absent immediate arrest, or (e) destruction/concealment of crime evidence absent immediate arrest. CHP cites §40300.5 in arrest report. The modern statute is **broader than older two-exception versions** discussed in 1980s/90s cases.

**Roadway definition:** Vehicle Code §530: portion of highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel. A freeway **shoulder** need not automatically be a "roadway" within subsection (b). However, whether part of Tesla intruded on live lanes, posed an obstruction or other danger is genuinely unresolved. Need original forward native MP4, location frames and road markings, CHP's original scene notes, tow/CAD data, original photograph if any, and the officers' exact factual allegations of obstruction/safety/imminent loss of evidence.

**Important, potentially double-edged California cases:**
- *Mercer v. Department of Motor Vehicles* (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753 and *Music v. Department of Motor Vehicles* (1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 841: no observed vehicle movement and lack of statutory exception can invalidate warrantless DUI arrest under then-applicable law. *Music* found no roadway obstruction for a parked vehicle. These cases do not defeat the **additional modern** §40300.5(c)-(e) exceptions.
- *Villalobos v. Zolin* (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 556: lawfully arrested under §40300.5 where DUI-symptomatic person occupied vehicle obstructing **interstate highway**. This is direct **defense-side precedent** if CHP can show the Tesla obstructed traveled roadway. Distinguish on *verified actual position*, not rhetorical description.
- *Padilla v. Meese* (1986) 184 Cal.App.3d 1022: absence of §40300.5 exception does not necessarily end all potential lawful arrest bases; opinion considered citizen-arrest route. Review entire case and later treatment before citing.
- CALCRIM 2670 (2026) describes probable cause and lawful peace-officer performance; CALCRIM 1242 (2026) defines misdemeanor false imprisonment. **Civilly unlawful warrantless arrest does not, on its own, resolve the criminal false-imprisonment/U-visa victimhood inquiry.**

**Evidence gate:** build a five-column matrix for the time of arrest: (1) statutory §40300.5 basis CHP asserts, (2) fact officer actually knew, (3) original record/video proof, (4) contrary/adverse material, (5) would reasonable-cause and exception still exist after correcting any verifiable test misstatement? Never assert §40300.5 categorically inapplicable simply because vehicle stopped.

## The April 20 decision makes this mandamus, not a second DUI prosecution
**Statute in force**: PC §679.10(a) CHP a certifying entity; §679.10(b) authority/designation question for actual certifying official; §679.10(c)(19) expressly lists *false imprisonment* and (18) unlawful criminal restraint; §679.10(g)(1) certification conditioned on victimhood and helpfulness; (h) rebuttable helpfulness presumption (does not establish victimhood); §679.10(k)(1) ongoing investigation/charges/conviction unnecessary; (k)(3)(A) prohibits refusal because of *victim criminal history*, among other reasons. Agency denial "not a qualifying crime" is not itself a forbidden reason. **Confirm any suspected forbidden reason from actual decision records before making allegation.**

Potential exact evidentiary question for RFP 8: "When Serrato signed the five-sentence refusal April 20, which particular allegations were compared to the legal elements of PC §236, what video segments and CHP Form 202 were accessed, and what records were supplied by April 9?" RFP 6 asks when CHP received the Nov 2024 lab findings. If agency denied because simply no charges ever filed or because applicant's unrelated history, that would make an independently testable statutory argument **if actual evidence proves reliance**. But CHP's reported reason was absence of qualifying criminal activity; do not attribute an impermissible motive absent source support.

**March→May chronology:** a complainant-related CHP/POST inquiry appears in the March 2026 materials. Privilege log identifies May 9, 2026 video-review and officer-interview memoranda, which **postdate** Apr 20 denial. The existence of a later review does not establish that no video review occurred before denial, and unknown memo conclusions cannot be alleged. Exact discovery gap: earlier video access, intake/review emails, reviewer identity/delegation, instructions, nonprivileged checklist and what was known on April 20.

**DOJ's own published guidance:** California Attorney General Information Bulletin 2024-DLE-05 dated April 3, 2024 publicly explains the amended certification duties, helpfulness presumption, written-denial requirements, criminal-history prohibition and absence of prosecution prerequisite. This is authoritative **agency guidance** persuasive for how CHP should understand §679.10, not a binding court judgment or an admission about what Serrato did. The fact DOJ represents CHP does not establish the guidance was supplied or ignored.

**No automatic certification by judge:** Certifying agency does not grant U-visa status; federal USCIS determines immigration benefits. State court may direct statutory compliance and correct actual abuse, without dictating an unsupported result. Primary demand for actual certification should remain conditional on every predicate and no lawful discretionary alternative; fallback vacatur/remand requires proven error too.

- 0.073 ±0.004 BAC at 02:38 does not establish BAC at 01:12 or earlier driving; §23152(a) does not require a .08 result.
