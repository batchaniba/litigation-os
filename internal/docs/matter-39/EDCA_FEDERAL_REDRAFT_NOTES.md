## Claim architecture

### Count One — FCRA § 1681b(b)(3)(A)

Merits theory:
Best Buy procured and used a First Advantage employment consumer report, took adverse action based in whole or in part on it, and did not first furnish the report and Summary of Rights.

Injury theory:
Had the report been furnished before Best Buy ended the hire, Plaintiff could have identified the California-registry information and objected before losing the accepted job that § 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibited use of that source for employment.

### Count Two — ICRAA §§ 1786.40 / 1786.50

Use a **distinct adverse-action omission** from FCRA:
Best Buy did not advise Plaintiff, when the hire was denied/ended, that employment was being denied under circumstances in which an investigative report had been obtained.

The 2023 authorization naming First Advantage does not necessarily satisfy § 1786.40's later adverse-action advice requirement. But the fact Plaintiff already knew First Advantage's name makes this count less clean than Home Depot's and reinforces the need to plead the precise omission accurately.

### Count Three — Penal Code § 290.46(j)

This is the substantive centerpiece.

The complaint now alleges as ultimate facts on information and belief:
1. First Advantage reported Plaintiff's § 290.46 information to Best Buy;
2. Best Buy received/accessed/considered it;
3. Best Buy used it for an employment purpose; and
4. it was a substantial factor in the decision.

The exact source field and adjudication code are evidence reserved for discovery, not conditions precedent to pleading.

## Limitations — the main difference from Home Depot

The Best Buy event is October 2023.

### FCRA
15 U.S.C. § 1681p uses the earlier of:
- two years after discovery of the violation; or
- five years after occurrence.

Ninth Circuit cases treat constructive discovery as sufficient. *Ruiz v. Shamrock Foods Co.* applies *Drew* and holds the clock can begin when a reasonably diligent applicant should have discovered the facts constituting the violation.

Because Plaintiff knew in October 2023 that Best Buy required a background check and First Advantage participated in the screening process, Count One should **not be filed** unless there is a truthful later discovery event showing when Plaintiff actually or constructively learned that Best Buy took adverse action based in whole or in part on the consumer report without providing the pre-adverse package.

### ICRAA
Civil Code § 1786.52 allows two years from discovery. The same caution applies.

### Penal Code § 290.46
No directly on-point published limitations decision was located in the quick audit.

California's general rule is:
- CCP § 338(a): three years for liability created by statute other than penalty/forfeiture;
- CCP § 340(a): one year for a statutory penalty or forfeiture.

Section 290.46(j)(4)(A) mixes compensatory and penalty-like remedies: actual damages, an additional amount up to three times actual, exemplary damages, and a civil penalty up to $25,000.

The safest pleading strategy is:
- file before the October 2026 three-year anniversary;
- make actual damages and compensatory statutory liability the foundation;
- do not make the civil-penalty remedy the load-bearing theory;
- seek only remedies that remain timely after the court determines the governing limitations characterization.

## 12(b)(6) theory

Taking the well-pleaded allegations as true:

written offer → accepted offer → Best Buy expressly initiates background screening → Best Buy's own records identify First Advantage → same Plaintiff is on California registry → same CRA demonstrably reports that registry information in comparator employment screens → Best Buy receives/uses the report → Best Buy ends the hire → no independent reason communicated.

The complaint alleges the ultimate source-and-use facts affirmatively. Best Buy's contention that it relied on some other source is framed as a merits defense for discovery, not a concession in the pleading.
