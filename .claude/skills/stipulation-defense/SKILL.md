# Stipulation defense

Use this skill when the work is the deal paper: a stipulation, extension,
stay, continuance, or proposed order that would move a defense clock,
waive a right, expand their relief, or put his signature on counsel's
draft.

Do not use it on ordinary Plaintiff papers. Status reports, oppositions,
complaints, hearing notes, and discovery plans are not stipulations. Do
not recite "this is not a stipulation," "counsel may not affix," or
"does not extend any defense clock" on those papers. Write the paper as
the paper it is.

Use discretion. Extra defense time, a stay, a waiver, a floating reset,
or a conformed `/s/` on their Word file: be firm. Authenticity of their
own records, a name correction he already wants, advancing a hearing onto
his live calendar, or answering the Court's magistrate-as-settlement-judge
question: ordinary. If he has already agreed a number, write that number.
Do not paste the full counterpart machine onto every "can we stipulate."

When this skill applies: do not sign counsel's draft. Do not "just give
them the thirty days." Do not invent a friendlier paper. Do not expand
their ask.

Copy `.claude/skills/stipulation-defense/TEMPLATE.md`. Fill every
`[bracket]`. Delete unused clauses. Drop-in language is in `CLAUSES.md`.
Do not add a new style. Terms match the worked examples below — those
examples are history, not a new filing.

The user appears in propria persona. When the paper is a concession,
draft it as if the other side will later quote every word against him.

**Time runs against Plaintiff** on a defense-time ask. Evidence degrades,
custodians leave, retention windows close, and delay favors whoever holds
the documents. Do not agree to that kind of continuance as a courtesy.
Do not volunteer extra days. Do not let their extension paper drift
longer in his hands than it arrived.

## The lock

1. **Plaintiff-favorable paper.** Every term is the version most favorable
   to Plaintiff that still gets the deal on file. If a clause can be read
   two ways, write the reading that helps Plaintiff. Their draft is an
   opening offer, not a form.
2. **Defense clocks: shorten or hold.** Prefer the shorter date. Hold the
   existing date if shortening is not available. Lengthen only when
   Plaintiff has already agreed a number, and then only that number.
   Never write "or such later date as the parties may agree."
3. **Plaintiff clocks: do not compress.** "Shorter" is not automatically
   favorable. Do not cut his opposition, disclosure, amendment, or
   compliance time just to move the case. Preserve a demand date, a
   default right, and a hearing he is ready to argue.
4. **Continue the event, not the chain.** Moving a CMC or hearing does
   not silently move disclosures, ADR, Rule 26(f), expert dates, or a
   pending-motion briefing schedule. Enumerate what moves. Recite that
   everything else stays.
5. **Date certain.** Never "reset to run from the continued conference"
   or any floating anchor. A calendar date on every obligation. If they
   insist on an event trigger, add an outside date.
6. **Never broaden what they asked.** If they ask to move one date, move
   one date. Adding relief they did not request is a gift. Serrato Dkt.
   22 is why this sentence exists.
7. **Price every extension they want.** A continuance is currency. Charge
   for it: a date-certain disclosure, production of a named category,
   acceptance of service, authenticity of their own records, or "no
   further extensions absent order of the Court."
8. **Plaintiff drafts.** If they send a Word file, do not sign it. Return
   a Plaintiff counterpart (PDF he signed, plus Word if their e-filing
   workflow needs it). Leave his signature block blank on any version
   they prepare.
9. **He signs his own paper.** Counsel may not apply, reproduce, or file
   his signature, `/s/`, or a conformed name. Say that in the covering
   email. Do not put a 5-1(i) attestation on the counterpart that lets
   them file as if he signed their draft. One named file, in writing,
   only if he has read that exact final version and says so.
10. **Named party only. Named deadline only.** An Amazon extension does
    not move Accurate. A Randstad letter does not cover RK Logistics.
    Recite the other party's unchanged date.
11. **Single shot.** "This is a single extension. No further extension is
    implied." A second request is a new paper, or it is an opposition.
12. **Court-set dates stay** unless moving them helps Plaintiff
    (advancing a hearing so it is heard with a live calendar). Do not
    move a CMC, a demand date, or an answer deadline on Plaintiff's own
    clock as the price of their delay.
13. **Nothing is waived.** Preserve every defense, objection, and right,
    including Plaintiff's right to seek default if no timely response is
    filed. Without-prejudice on stays. Define the end of a stay (final
    judgment or dispositive order plus expiration of any appeal), toll
    Plaintiff's statutory clocks, and set a fallback response date when
    the stay lifts. Recite that it is not a stay of discovery unless
    that is the deal.
14. **If they refuse the counterpart,** do not sign theirs. Let the clock
    run, or oppose. Matter 26 is the opposition posture when they
    caption a "consent" motion he did not consent to.

## What "most favorable" means on a clock

| Their ask | Plaintiff's paper |
|---|---|
| 30 days to respond | 14, or the shortest local-rule window, or the date already agreed |
| "a reasonable time" / open-ended | A calendar date. Through and including that date. Stop. |
| Extend everyone | The asking defendant only. Recite that every other party is unchanged. |
| Continue the CMC "and all related deadlines" | Continue the CMC only. Hold disclosures, ADR, joint statement. Date certain if anything else must move. |
| Stay pending "resolution" of another case | Stay pending *final* resolution, defined; toll; fallback deadline; nothing waived. |
| Advance a hearing so it is heard with a live matter | Yes — that shortens Plaintiff's calendar. File it. |
| Compress his opposition / disclosure / demand date | No. His clocks stay. |
| Affix his `/s/` and file | No. Counterpart. He signs. Covering email says they may not. |

## When they send a draft

Do not mark up their file as the filing paper. Read it for the number of
days, the named party, and every date that would chain off the event.
Then write the counterpart from the template. If their draft already has
a conformed `/s/ Brendan…`, the covering letter or email says they may
not affix it — Matter 29 Accurate. Attach the counterpart. Never put a
GitHub or repo URL in anything that leaves the house.

Before he signs, say out loud:

- What is the stated reason, and is it still true today?
- What deadlines chain off the event? Read the scheduling order, not
  just their paper.
- Does any date approach Rule 4(m), a limitations period, an appeal
  window, or amendment as of right?
- Parallel proceeding: slowing this case while a related docket runs
  raises preclusion risk. Check both calendars.
- Read the **[Proposed] Order** separately. The order binds.
- Caption, case number, and party match this action.

Label their file in the document index as counsel's draft, not the
counterpart. "DO NOT SIGN" stays on that label.

## After it is on file

Pull the PACER or portal stamp. **Diff the filed version against what he
signed.** Retire the unfiled counterpart. Timeline row: who drafted, what
moved, what was expressly preserved, what was obtained in exchange.
Upcoming text names the defendant and "single extension; no further
implied" when that is the deal. A proposed or signed paper is not an
operative court deadline until the rule, the agreement, or an entered
order makes it one.

## Worked example (locked) — Matter 29 Amazon, August 31, 2026

Counsel (Morgan Lewis) emailed a draft and asked to apply Plaintiff's
e-signature. The deal on the email record: Amazon.com Services, LLC
only, through and including September 22, 2026. That is fourteen days
from the Rule 81(c) date of September 8, not thirty.

Plaintiff counterpart. He signed it. They did not get his `/s/`.

Terms that stay in every extension paper:

- Amazon only. Accurate's deadline is unchanged.
- Through and including September 22, 2026.
- Single extension. No further extension implied.
- Does not alter any date set by Court order.
- Does not waive any defense, objection, or right of any party,
  including Plaintiff's right to seek default if no timely response
  is filed.
- Civil L.R. 6-1(a); promptly filed. No Court order required.

Covering email: the extension is acceptable on those terms; do not
apply, reproduce, or file my signature; file the PDF or use the Word
if your e-filing workflow requires it; leave my block blank on any
version you prepare; the September 11 demand date is unchanged.
Attach the files. Never put a GitHub or repo URL in the email.

Accurate, September 1: same five paragraphs, Accurate only, same
September 22 date, same default reservation. Seyfarth's draft already
carried a conformed `/s/`. The letter said they may not affix it.

## Worked example (locked) — Matter 2 stay, April 13–14, 2026

DAG Li circulated a stay draft. Plaintiff did not sign it. Same-day
counter-draft with four additions, all of which Li accepted:

1. **"Final resolution"** means entry of final judgment or a
   dispositive order, plus expiration of any appeal period — not
   "when the federal court is done" in the abstract.
2. **Tolling** of applicable statutory deadlines during the stay.
3. **Fallback response deadline** if the parties cannot agree after
   the stay lifts.
4. **Without prejudice** — all claims, defenses, and rights preserved.

That is the stay posture. An open stay that dies when they say it dies
is their paper, not his.

## Worked example (locked) — advancing a hearing

Matter 20: joint stipulation to advance the individual respondents'
October 16 and October 23 demurrers to the August 28 CHP date. That
shortens Plaintiff's calendar and keeps the fight on one record. File
that kind of stipulation. Do not let a live hearing split onto a later
date because counsel's draft "preserves" the original setting.

## Worked example (locked) — do not expand their ask (Serrato)

Matter 24, Dkt. 22: DAG Li drafted a stipulation to continue the initial
CMC. The failure mode is adding a second item that vacates the
September 8 Rule 26(a)(1) disclosures, joint CMC statement, and ADR
certification, and resets them to run from the continued conference —
a floating anchor, more delay than the defendant asked, nothing in
exchange. That forfeits standing to object to his own expansion.

What the counterpart does: continue the conference only; hold the
September 8 dates or move them to a date certain; charge for it (a
date certain for Defendant's disclosures, or an express "this is not
a stay of discovery"). Do not bury live Serrato dates under optional
filings in other matters.

## Worked example (locked) — they caption consent he did not give

Matter 26: UPS filed a "Consent Motion" for more time on a reply after
Plaintiff did not consent. The lock here is not a stipulation. It is
the opposition. Do not cure their caption by signing after the fact.
The live clock stays unless the Court moves it.
