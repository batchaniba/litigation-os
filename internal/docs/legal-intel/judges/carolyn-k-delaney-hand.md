# Dossier: Chief U.S. Magistrate Judge Carolyn K. Delaney — Hand-Pulled MTD Playbook

**Who:** Carolyn K. Delaney, Chief United States Magistrate Judge, Eastern District of
California, Sacramento. Bio, structural context, and 2026 case-type breakdown are in the
companion sampled audit — read that first:
[Carolyn K. Delaney — 2026 docket audit](carolyn-k-delaney-2026.md). This hand pull does
not repeat it. It answers one question: **how does Delaney handle a Rule 12 motion in an
employment / background-screening case, from briefing through findings and
recommendations — and what does that mean for the UPS MTD?**

**Why now:** She is the referral magistrate judge on *Newanforbi v. UPS*,
No. 2:26-cv-02847-DAD-CKD (Matter 26). The MTD hearing is noticed for October 7, 2026,
before her; her findings and recommendations will decide what District Judge Drozd
reviews de novo after the 14-day objections window. UPS's reply (Dkt. 14, filed
September 14, 2026) asks for with-prejudice disposition and reframes its Exhibit A
("SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY") as national-registry sourcing.
This dossier is the playbook for the hearing that may not happen and the F&R that will.

**Method honesty:** This is not a corpus census. It is built from ten verified signed
orders (Justia/govinfo, 2011–2026) read in full or in substantial excerpt, plus
CourtListener's opinion search used as a finding aid. The sample deliberately overweights
her most on-point recent work: a 2026 FCRA/ICRAA employment MTD against a staffing
company and a trucking company (*Duncan*), a 2025 employment-discrimination case that ran
the full leave-then-41(b) arc (*Brown v. LucidWorks*), and her treatment of defendant
evidence at the pleading stage (*Engel*). Outcome counts below are an observed sample,
not a rate. Anything not verified against a primary source is labeled unverified. Where
this dossier says something about Matter 26 specifically, it rests on the portfolio's
own record of the UPS briefs (Dkts. 9, 10, 14), not on new document pulls.

## 1. The papers-not-podcast rule — the October 7 hearing may never happen

Delaney's most consistent habit, across every year of the sample, is deciding motions
**without oral argument under Local Rule 230(g)**. The pattern is not a quirk of one
case type; it spans employment cases, prisoner cases, and civil-rights cases:

| Date | Case | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1/30/26 | *Duncan v. Osterkamp Trucking*, No. 2:24-cv-02276-DC-CKD (FCRA/ICRAA employment MTD) | Motion submitted **without hearing** in October 2024 before the district judge; referred to Delaney August 2025; she issued the F&R on the papers. |
| 4/21/25 | *Engel v. Paragon Systems*, No. 2:24-cv-02479-DJC-CKD (pro se employment dispute) | Both MTDs **taken under submission without oral argument** under L.R. 230(g). |
| 5/23/25 | *Schmitz v. Asman*, No. 2:20-cv-00195-DJC-CKD | **Vacated the scheduled hearing**; decided under L.R. 230(g). |
| 6/3/21 | *Harris v. New Rez*, No. 2:21-cv-00636-KJM-CKD | Hearing noticed via Zoom; plaintiff appeared only by telephone and could not argue; Delaney heard no defense argument either, **expressly found oral argument would not materially assist**, and decided on the briefing under L.R. 230(g). |
| 12/23/25 | Nadeem Khan F&R (subject-matter jurisdiction) | Hearing **previously vacated to accommodate the briefing schedule**; then decided without oral argument as "suitable for decision." |
| 11/19/25 | *Brown v. LucidWorks*, No. 2:25-cv-0607-DC-CKD (PS) | Rule 41(b) motion "appropriate for decision **without oral argument** under Local Rule 230(g)." |

The *Harris* order is the tell: when a hearing effectively collapsed (plaintiff listening
by phone, unable to argue), she did not re-notice or wait — she found the briefing
sufficient and ruled. **The October 7 UPS MTD hearing should be treated as potentially
the whole record:** the opposition and reply must stand alone, because Delaney may
vacate it and decide on the papers, and if she holds it, the transcript will not be the
decisive record — the briefing will.

One asymmetry to note: papers-only treatment has not favored defendants in the sample.
*Schmitz* vacated a hearing and then recommended **denying** the defendants' Rule 41(b)
motion without prejudice. A vacated hearing is not a defense-favored hearing.

## 2. The leave-to-amend ladder — her "careful analysis" standard

Delaney's stated standard, from the April 2025 *Engel* F&R, is the engine of her
Rule 12 practice:

> Amendment is **freely granted if it appears possible to correct the pleading
> defect** — especially for pro se litigants — and leave is denied only when careful
> analysis shows the claim **cannot be cured**.

She applies that standard claim by claim, not complaint-wide. The sample shows the
ladder in both directions:

- **Leave granted because futility was unclear.** *Duncan* (1/30/26): she recommended
  **granting the Rule 12(b)(6) motion but with leave to amend** — 30 days from the
  district judge's order to file a Second Amended Complaint — because it was not clear
  the pleading defects were incurable. This is the closest comparator to Matter 26 in
  the whole pull: FCRA disclosure/authorization claims, ICRAA, wage claims, class
  allegations, and a **staffing-agency-plus-trucking-company** two-defendant
  structure. Her FCRA work in *Duncan* ran through the claims individually rather than
  as a block.
- **No further leave after an amendment failed.** *Eswaran v. Awoniyi*,
  No. 2:25-cv-00978-TLN-CKD (F&R 7/14/25): after one amendment opportunity, the FAC
  still failed and legal barriers remained, so she recommended **dismissal without
  further leave**. First chances are broad; second chances require the amendment to
  actually fix what the first F&R identified.
- **No leave when futility is legally clear.** *Harris* (6/3/21): recommended
  dismissal of the federal claims **without leave to amend** where the legal barrier
  was decisive.
- **The leave you don't use becomes the dismissal with prejudice.** *Brown v.
  LucidWorks* is the cautionary arc in full: the June 2025 F&R recommended dismissal
  **with leave to amend** the employment-discrimination claims; the district judge
  dismissed July 29, 2025 with 30 days to amend; the plaintiff never amended. The
  November 19, 2025 F&R then recommended **dismissal with prejudice under Rule
  41(b)** — working through the five-factor *In re Eisen* test — after the plaintiff
  also failed to oppose the 41(b) motion. Delaney had warned in October 2025 that
  failure to respond would be construed as non-opposition under L.R. 230(c); the
  deadline passed with no filing.

For Matter 26, the ladder matters in two directions. If she grants the MTD, *Duncan*
says the default is leave, not with-prejudice — **UPS's Dkt. 14 "with prejudice" ask
runs against her clearest recent comparator.** And if leave is granted, the *Brown*
arc says the amendment must actually cure the identified defects; an ignored deadline
turns leave into with-prejudice dismissal.

## 3. Claim-by-claim architecture — and the silence point

Every F&R in the sample follows the same skeleton: background, the complaint's
allegations, the governing legal standard, then **each claim in sequence**, then a
leave/futility determination, then an exact recommendation, then the 14-day
objections warning. She does not reason in generalities.

That architecture is why the Matter 26 silence point belongs in the UPS papers as a
**discrete claim-by-claim failure**, not as a rhetorical broadside. If UPS's
motion and reply genuinely do not address Count II or negligence per se, the
structure she reads for is: *"As to Count [X]: defendants offer no argument; the
claim stands unchallenged on this motion"* — a line item, not an accusation that
UPS "waived everything." She is a judge who distinguishes what a paper actually
establishes from an advocate's characterization of it (see §4 below); keep the
characterization out and let the absence do the work.

Two related *Duncan* data points on pleading-stage restraint:

- She **declined to strike class allegations at the pleading stage**, reasoning that
  class certification — not a Rule 12 motion — is the proper stage to test them. The
  class allegations in Matter 26's complaint should survive an MTD-era strike attempt
  for the same reason.
- She **rejected collective "defendants" allegations** that did not identify each
  entity's role and control, demanding defendant-specific facts. Matter 26 has a
  single defendant (UPS) — no collective-pleading exposure — but the principle cuts
  the other way too: the complaint should pin UPS as the **user of the report and the
  decision-maker**, with its own exhibit text, rather than letting UPS reframe itself
  as a passive downstream consumer of national-registry data.

## 4. Exhibits and evidentiary contradictions — the Ritchie rule

At the pleading stage, *Engel* shows her evidentiary gatekeeping is precise rather
than permissive. When defendants asked her to take judicial notice of a complaint from
a **different case**, she **denied** the request because they did not explain its
relevance to the dispute before her — she would not consider it. Her stated
Rule 12(b)(6) universe is the familiar *Ritchie* one: the court may consider
**documents attached to the complaint, documents incorporated by reference in the
complaint, or documents subject to judicial notice** — and she will not smuggle in
unexplained supporting material beyond that.

For the UPS Exhibit A contradiction, her practice creates a two-branch decision tree,
and **both branches are bad for UPS's reframing**:

- **Branch A — Exhibit A is excluded as extrinsic.** If she applies *Engel*-style
  gatekeeping and finds Exhibit A is neither attached to nor incorporated by the
  complaint nor properly noticed, UPS's national-registry reframing loses its
  evidentiary footing at the pleading stage. What remains is the complaint's own
  well-pleaded allegations, taken as true — the registry-sourcing allegation stands
  uncontradicted.
- **Branch B — Exhibit A is considered via incorporation by reference.** If the
  complaint's background-check allegations incorporate the report, then she reads
  the exhibit's own words, and the exhibit says: **"SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA
  SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY,"** "Record Found," "Decisional," and "INELIGIBLE." A
  judge who distinguishes what a document actually establishes from an advocate's
  characterization of it will weigh that text against UPS's brief, not with it.

Either way, the opposition's lead move is the one the dossier's earlier research
identified: open with UPS's own Exhibit A text, not rhetoric. Her practice suggests
she will do the same reading.

One caveat labeled honestly: **no negligence-per-se ruling was observed** in this
sample. The claim-by-claim point in §3 is a structural inference from her F&R
architecture, not an observed ruling on that cause of action.

## 5. The F&R-to-objections pipeline — where Matter 26 goes next

Her F&Rs are recommendations to the district judge under 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1), and
she says so in every one. The *Duncan* order is the live template for Matter 26:
"RECOMMENDING that [the] Motion to Dismiss be granted and Plaintiff be ordered to
file a Second Amended Complaint. Referred to Judge Dena M. Coggins. **Objections
due within 14 days** after being served with these findings and recommendations."

The boilerplate that follows is the one the companion Drozd dossier is built for:
any party may file written objections captioned "Objections to Magistrate Judge's
Findings and Recommendations" within fourteen days; a reply is due within seven days
after service of the objections; and **failure to object may waive the right to
appeal** the district court's order (*Turner v. Duncan*, 158 F.3d 449 (9th Cir.
1998); *Martinez v. Ylst*, 951 F.2d 1153 (9th Cir. 1991)). The objections deadline
is not advisory — it is the enforcement mechanism for de novo review.

The sequencing implication for Matter 26: Delaney writes the decision that matters
first, and the objections brief is the last paper Drozd reads before his own ruling
— which is exactly the relay the companion Drozd hand dossier playbooks.

## 6. Settlement before Delaney — authority, paper, and timing

Delaney is a regular settlement-conference judge. Her standard settlement order is a
study in what she values at the table:

- A **principal with full settlement authority must attend** (or be fully authorized
  to settle on any terms), with **"unfettered discretion and authority"** to change
  the settlement position during the conference. An authorization capped at a limited
  dollar amount or sum certain "can be found not to comply" with the requirement.
- Each party submits a **confidential settlement statement — no more than five
  pages** — to ckdorders@caed.uscourts.gov **at least seven days before** the
  conference. It is **not filed and not served** on the other side. (Her prisoner-case
  ADR orders follow the same seven-day confidential-statement pattern and stay the
  case 120 days pending the conference.)
- Under L.R. 270(b), parties elect whether the assigned magistrate judge conducts
  the conference and **waive later disqualification** based on the judge's settlement
  participation — expressly not consent to § 636(c) jurisdiction.

Two timing data points:

- *Eswaran* (7/14/25): she **denied a settlement-conference request as moot** where
  defendants had not been served and she was recommending dismissal without leave —
  settlement was "inappropriate at this time." She will not waste a conference on a
  case she is about to end.
- She **approved employment class settlements** in 2025. Those approval orders are
  fairness findings on negotiated deals, not evidence of her confidential negotiation
  style — labeled as such.

The tactical read for Matter 26, consistent with the recommendation already delivered
on the lapsed UPS demand: a Delaney settlement conference is procedurally natural
**only when both sides are participating and the case has merits posture** — i.e.,
the post-MTD window, not an immediate renewal of the lapsed demand. Her "unfettered
discretion" standard also means the demand that comes back should be one UPS's
principal can actually move off of at the table; a fixed-figure ceiling announced in
advance risks a compliance fight she has already told the parties she will win.

## 7. Tactical takeaways for the UPS MTD (ranked)

1. **Write the opposition as if the hearing is already vacated.** Six observed
   vacatur/submission-without-argument orders (2021–2026) across case types. The
   October 7 papers are the record. (Papers-only is not defense-favored —
   *Schmitz* vacated and then denied the defense motion.)
2. **Lead with Exhibit A, in UPS's own words.** "SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX
   OFFENDER REGISTRY" / "Record Found" / "Decisional" / "INELIGIBLE." Her
   *Engel* practice says she distinguishes a document's actual content from an
   advocate's characterization — and either her Ritchie gate excludes the exhibit
   (UPS's reframing loses its footing) or incorporation admits it (the exhibit's
   text contradicts the brief).
3. **Frame UPS's silence on Count II / negligence per se as discrete claim-by-claim
   failures.** Her F&Rs are built claim-by-claim; an unaddressed claim is a line
   item, not a rhetorical point. Let the absence do the work.
4. **The with-prejudice ask runs against her comparator.** *Duncan* (1/30/26) is the
   closest on-point F&R in the pull — same statutory family (FCRA/ICRAA),
   employment context, staffing-client structure — and she recommended **grant with
   leave to amend** (30 days), not with prejudice. Cite the ladder: leave where
   futility is unclear; with-prejudice only where careful analysis shows no cure.
5. **If leave is granted, amend within the deadline and actually cure.** The
   *Brown v. LucidWorks* arc is the warning: leave granted June 2025, no amendment
   filed, Rule 41(b) dismissal with prejudice recommended November 2025 — with a
   L.R. 230(c) non-opposition warning in between.
6. **Protect the class allegations from an MTD-era strike.** *Duncan* declined to
   strike class allegations at the pleading stage; certification is the testing
   stage.
7. **Pin UPS as the report's user and decision-maker, in defendant-specific terms.**
   She rejects collective "defendants" pleading and demands each entity's role and
   control — with a single defendant, the complaint should name UPS's role
   precisely and let the exhibit text close the loop.
8. **Calendar the objections relay now.** F&R → 14 days to object (waiver of appeal
   if not) → 7-day reply → Drozd de novo. The objections brief is the decisive
   paper in the district judge's hands; the companion Drozd playbook dossier governs
   its drafting.
9. **Hold settlement for the post-MTD window.** Her "unfettered discretion"
   authority standard, her mootness denial in *Eswaran*, and the lapsed demand all
   point the same way: the conference is strongest after the F&R gives the case
   merits posture.
