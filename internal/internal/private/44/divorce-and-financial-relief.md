# Divorce and financial-relief track

## Present Maryland framework

Maryland does not maintain a separate legal-separation action. A court may grant
an absolute divorce on statutory grounds that include a six-month separation,
irreconcilable differences, or mutual consent. The exact ground, jurisdiction,
venue, pleading, and corroboration must be selected from verified facts.

Potential relief may include:

- divorce and restoration/change of name if requested;
- use and possession or other interim relief where authorized;
- identification and valuation of marital property;
- transfer or adjustment of interests and a monetary award under Family Law
  § 8-205;
- alimony under Family Law § 11-106;
- suit money and counsel fees under Family Law § 11-110;
- custody, access, and child support only if applicable;
- enforcement or challenge of valid agreements;
- preservation orders, discovery, and lawful tracing of assets.

## Questions that control value and forum

1. Where did the parties marry, and where has each lived during the relevant
   period?
2. What is the exact separation date and living arrangement?
3. Are there minor or dependent children?
4. What property is titled jointly or separately, when was it acquired, and
   what debt attaches?
5. What income, retirement, businesses, benefits, insurance, and tax interests
   exist?
6. Were assets transferred, depleted, hidden, or encumbered? Record facts;
   do not access accounts without authority or accuse without proof.
7. Does Njiubu need interim support, housing, medical coverage, possession, or
   fee assistance?
8. Are there agreements, pending cases, foreign orders, or immigration issues?

## Planning rule

Do not use a divorce filing merely as leverage for a tort settlement. Coordinate
protective, financial, evidentiary, tax, benefits, estate, and immigration
consequences with qualified Maryland counsel. Preserve complete statements and
tax records; do not move, hide, drain, or destroy assets.
