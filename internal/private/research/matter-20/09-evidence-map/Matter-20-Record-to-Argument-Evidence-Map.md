# Matter 20 Record-to-Argument Evidence Map

This map connects the surviving Count 1 writ theory to specific record pages, the defense position, admissibility needs and the relief each proposition could support. It is a research and drafting aid dated October 24, 2026, not a filing, a finding that misconduct occurred, or a prediction of success.

## Controlling assessment

The strongest available structure is the one the court already identified: prove the disputed facts bearing on lawful custody, then connect those findings to an abuse of discretion in denying certification. The August 29 order says that if the court credits the specified allegations about absent alcohol odor, absent impairment and false reporting, it could find probable cause lacking, which could support an abuse-of-discretion finding; it does not decide those facts or direct certification. Record R01, PDF p. 4.

The immediate weakness is not proof that restraint occurred or that CHP received the request. It is the unresolved bridge from restraint to qualifying criminal activity, and from that activity to an unlawful certification decision; favorable admissions narrow that bridge but do not complete it. Records R02–R06 and argument rows A03–A09.

The best-supported drafting position is therefore conditional: seek merits relief upon established Count 1 findings, preserve a narrower reconsideration remedy if a material error is established but eligibility remains unresolved, and request an appropriate factual-resolution procedure if genuinely material disputes prevent decision on the papers. Do not portray any of those remedies as automatically available merely because discovery or the explanation is incomplete. See [CCP § 1085](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1085.), [CCP § 1094](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1094.) and [California Correctional Supervisors Organization v. Department of Corrections (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/96/824.html).

The June 29 federal screening order is added as R15 and arguments A19–A20 below. It provides limited persuasive support for legal plausibility, not a merits finding, an admission by CHP or proof that any disputed event occurred; it does not change the findings required by A05–A18.

## How to read the map

- **Record pinpoints**: PDF page numbers are one-based pages in the named source, not search-text line numbers. Bates numbers and Answer paragraph numbers are given where available; the merged packet's page numbering is not interchangeable with a standalone exhibit's numbering.
- **Status labels**: Established text means the document says it; admitted means the supplied Answer expressly admits the narrow fact; disputed means a material factual conflict remains; conditional means a legal inference requires further findings. None means the court has admitted a particular exhibit into evidence.
- **Time boundary**: The April 9 submissions and April 20 denial form the identified decision-time record. October pleadings/discovery can establish admissions or describe older evidence, but are not retroactively treated as submissions reviewed before the denial.
- **Quotation labels**: Text in quotation marks identified as verbatim is copied from the stated source. Everything else is synthesis or proposed advocacy; transcript quotations remain quotations of a petitioner-prepared transcript until checked against the native recording.

For the criminal-activity bridge, CALCRIM 1242 identifies intentional restraint/detention/confinement and making someone stay or go against that person's will, with unlawfulness included where lawful authority is in issue; do not invent a requirement of intent to break the law or omit lawful-authority analysis. See [Judicial Council, CALCRIM 1242, October 2025 revision, printed pp. 149–150](https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/calcrim_2025_supplement_0.pdf).

The warrantless-arrest issue cannot be resolved simply by saying no officer observed driving: statutory exceptions include roadway obstruction and specified immediate-arrest circumstances. See [Vehicle Code § 40300.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=40300.5.).

Alternative objective grounds also matter; Wolterman upheld an arrest on objectively supported public-intoxication facts despite the stated DUI basis, including obstruction, marked impairment and wandering into traffic. Distinguish those facts with evidence rather than ignoring the case the court itself cited. See [People v. Wolterman (1992) 11 Cal.App.4th Supp. 15, 18–21](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/11/supp15.html).

Espinoza must be addressed for totality-of-circumstances probable cause; coherent answers or individual favorable observations do not automatically negate the remaining indicators. See [Espinoza v. Shiomoto (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 85, 101–103](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2017/e064252a.html).

Jackson distinguishes statutorily optional PAS refusal from standard FST refusal and holds PAS-refusal evidence inadmissible as consciousness of guilt at trial; it does not itself decide that PAS refusal may never be considered in a probable-cause analysis. Do not use it for that broader unverified proposition. See [People v. Jackson, No. A128782 (Nov. 12, 2010)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1544901.html).

The statutory detention-certificate provisions concern release/disposition circumstances and do not themselves require a retrospective finding that the arrest lacked probable cause. See [Penal Code § 851.6(a)–(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=851.6.).

### Judicial-notice limits

A proposed request should seek notice of the existence, filing date, text and screening disposition of ECF No. 5, attaching all five pages. It should not seek judicial notice that officers fabricated evidence, that false imprisonment actually occurred, that Serrato retaliated, or that petitioner is entitled to certification; those are not established by the screening order.

California permits judicial notice of federal court records, but the existence and legal effect of a court record must be distinguished from the truth of disputed factual assertions or findings recited in it. Frame the request accordingly. See [Evidence Code § 452(d)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=452.) and [Sosinsky v. Grant (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1548, 1564–1569](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/6/1548.html).
