   1 Plaintiff JUSTIN RANGEL (“Plaintiff”), an individual files this Complaint and

   2 Demand for Jury Trial against Defendants ACCURATE BACKGROUND, LLC

   3 (“Accurate”); and DOES 1 to 50, inclusive (collectively “Defendants”), and based on

   4 information and belief, hereby alleges and complains as follows:

   5                                   INTRODUCTION
   6        1.     This action arises from the inaccurate and incomplete investigative
   7 consumer report (the “Report”) prepared by Defendant Accurate in connection with

   8 Plaintiff’s job application.

   9        2.     Despite Plaintiff’s submission of documentation, the Report continued
  10 to misstate his background history, failed to reflect accurate information, and

  11 ultimately caused the revocation of an employment opportunity.

  12        3.     Upon information and belief, Accurate failed to follow reasonable
  13 procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of the information it reported, and

  14 Defendants ignored their obligations under federal and California consumer

  15 protection statutes by relying on this inaccurate report, and in their actions once

  16 Plaintiff disputed the Report.

  17        4.     Specifically, Defendants violated the Fair Credit Reporting Act
  18 (“FCRA”), 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq., and the California Investigative Consumer
  19 Reporting Agencies Act (“ICRAA”), Cal. Civ. Code § 1786 et seq., by failing to

  20 maintain procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy, and by failing to conduct

  21 a reasonable reinvestigation upon receiving notice of dispute.

  22        5.     Defendants also violated the FCRA and ICRAA by procuring and using
  23 an inaccurate investigative consumer report in its employment decision-making

  24 process, and by failing to provide Plaintiff with a meaningful opportunity to respond

  25 before taking adverse action.

  26        6.     As a result of Defendants’ conduct, Plaintiff was denied employment,
  27 suffered financial loss, and experienced emotional distress and reputational harm.

  28        7.     Plaintiff now brings this action for violations of (1) the ICRAA, (2) the

  10                             FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS
  11        16.    Plaintiff is an experienced computed tomography (“CT”) technologist
  12 residing in San Diego, California.

  13        17.    In or around early May 2025, Plaintiff applied for a CT technologist
  14 position at Sharp Hospital (“Sharp”) in San Diego, California. As part of the pre-

  15 employment process, Plaintiff was required to undergo a background check.

  16        18.    Defendant Accurate Background, Inc. (“Accurate”) was retained to
  17 perform this background check.

  18        19.    Plaintiff’s employment was contingent upon successful completion of
  19 the background screening. His anticipated start date was May 21, 2025.

  20        20.    On or around May 14–15, 2025, Sharp’s human resources representative
  21 informed Plaintiff that his start date would be delayed because the background report

  22 had not yet been completed.

  23        21.    On or around May 20, 2025, Sharp accessed the finalized background
  24 report prepared by Accurate. The report erroneously associated Plaintiff with criminal

  25 convictions from Maricopa County, Arizona, involving a person named Justin Ray

  26 Rangel—who shares Plaintiff’s first and last name, but has a different middle name

  27 and date of birth.

  28        22.    Plaintiff had not been arrested or convicted of any crime, had no criminal
                                              4
                                   COMPLAINT FOR DAMAGES
Case 3:25-cv-01983-AJB-BJW      Document 1       Filed 08/01/25     PageID.5     Page 5 of 13




   1 record, and truthfully disclosed on pre-screening forms that he had no such history.

   2        23.    Despite the obvious discrepancy in middle names and lack of a match on
   3 full date of birth, Accurate included the Arizona criminal records in Plaintiff’s report

   4 without reasonable procedures to ensure maximum possible accuracy as required by

   5 law.

   6        24.    Plaintiff was not provided a copy of the background check prior to its
   7 delivery to Sharp. He only learned of the erroneous reporting after Sharp contacted

   8 him directly about it, approximately two weeks after the report was finalized.

   9        25.    Plaintiff immediately disputed the report with Accurate on May 30,
  10 2025. Accurate acknowledged the dispute and initiated a reinvestigation, but refused

  11 to provide Plaintiff a copy of the original report during the pendency of the dispute,

  12 denying him the ability to fully understand or respond to the allegations.

  13        26.    Due to Accurate’s reporting error, Plaintiff’s employment with Sharp
  14 was delayed by approximately four weeks. Plaintiff did not begin working until mid-

  15 to-late June 2025.

  16        27.    Plaintiff’s hourly wage as a CT technologist at Sharp is approximately
  17 $70 per hour, inclusive of shift differentials. As a result of the delay, Plaintiff lost

  18 approximately 160 hours of work, or about $11,200 in gross wages.
  19        28.    Plaintiff   also   suffered    emotional       distress,   including   anxiety,
  20 embarrassment, and financial strain, as a result of the delay in employment and the

  21 stigmatizing nature of the false criminal record.

  22        29.    Accurate ultimately removed the inaccurate criminal records from the
  23 report, but not until after Plaintiff’s start date was significantly postponed and after

  24 he took multiple steps to dispute and correct the record.

  25        30.    Accurate failed to employ and follow reasonable procedures to assure
  26 maximum possible accuracy in the preparation of Plaintiff’s investigative consumer

  27 report, including failing to match on full name and full date of birth, and

  28 inappropriately associating criminal records of another individual with Plaintiff.

  17 Code § 1786.2(d).

  19 Civ. Code § 1786.2(f), as it was used for employment purposes and included

  20 information on Plaintiff’s character, general reputation, and mode of living obtained

  21 through personal interviews or public records.

                     INSTRUCTIONS FOR ATTORNEYS COMPLETING CIVIL COVER SHEET FORM JS 44
                                                               Authority For Civil Cover Sheet

The JS 44 civil cover sheet and the information contained herein neither replaces nor supplements the filings and service of pleading or other papers as
required by law, except as provided by local rules of court. This form, approved by the Judicial Conference of the United States in September 1974, is
required for the use of the Clerk of Court for the purpose of initiating the civil docket sheet. Consequently, a civil cover sheet is submitted to the Clerk of
Court for each civil complaint filed. The attorney filing a case should complete the form as follows:

I.(a)    Plaintiffs-Defendants. Enter names (last, first, middle initial) of plaintiff and defendant. If the plaintiff or defendant is a government agency, use
         only the full name or standard abbreviations. If the plaintiff or defendant is an official within a government agency, identify first the agency and then
         the official, giving both name and title.
  (b)    County of Residence. For each civil case filed, except U.S. plaintiff cases, enter the name of the county where the first listed plaintiff resides at the
         time of filing. In U.S. plaintiff cases, enter the name of the county in which the first listed defendant resides at the time of filing. (NOTE: In land
         condemnation cases, the county of residence of the "defendant" is the location of the tract of land involved.)
  (c)    Attorneys. Enter the firm name, address, telephone number, and attorney of record. If there are several attorneys, list them on an attachment, noting
         in this section "(see attachment)".

II.      Jurisdiction. The basis of jurisdiction is set forth under Rule 8(a), F.R.Cv.P., which requires that jurisdictions be shown in pleadings. Place an "X"
         in one of the boxes. If there is more than one basis of jurisdiction, precedence is given in the order shown below.
         United States plaintiff. (1) Jurisdiction based on 28 U.S.C. 1345 and 1348. Suits by agencies and officers of the United States are included here.
         United States defendant. (2) When the plaintiff is suing the United States, its officers or agencies, place an "X" in this box.
         Federal question. (3) This refers to suits under 28 U.S.C. 1331, where jurisdiction arises under the Constitution of the United States, an amendment
         to the Constitution, an act of Congress or a treaty of the United States. In cases where the U.S. is a party, the U.S. plaintiff or defendant code takes
         precedence, and box 1 or 2 should be marked.
         Diversity of citizenship. (4) This refers to suits under 28 U.S.C. 1332, where parties are citizens of different states. When Box 4 is checked, the
         citizenship of the different parties must be checked. (See Section III below; NOTE: federal question actions take precedence over diversity
         cases.)

III.     Residence (citizenship) of Principal Parties. This section of the JS 44 is to be completed if diversity of citizenship was indicated above. Mark this
         section for each principal party.

IV.      Nature of Suit. Place an "X" in the appropriate box. If there are multiple nature of suit codes associated with the case, pick the nature of suit code
         that is most applicable. Click here for: Nature of Suit Code Descriptions.

V.       Origin. Place an "X" in one of the seven boxes.
         Original Proceedings. (1) Cases which originate in the United States district courts.
         Removed from State Court. (2) Proceedings initiated in state courts may be removed to the district courts under Title 28 U.S.C., Section 1441.
         Remanded from Appellate Court. (3) Check this box for cases remanded to the district court for further action. Use the date of remand as the filing
         date.
         Reinstated or Reopened. (4) Check this box for cases reinstated or reopened in the district court. Use the reopening date as the filing date.
         Transferred from Another District. (5) For cases transferred under Title 28 U.S.C. Section 1404(a). Do not use this for within district transfers or
         multidistrict litigation transfers.
         Multidistrict Litigation – Transfer. (6) Check this box when a multidistrict case is transferred into the district under authority of Title 28 U.S.C.
         Section 1407.
         Multidistrict Litigation – Direct File. (8) Check this box when a multidistrict case is filed in the same district as the Master MDL docket.
         PLEASE NOTE THAT THERE IS NOT AN ORIGIN CODE 7. Origin Code 7 was used for historical records and is no longer relevant due to
         changes in statute.

VI.      Cause of Action. Report the civil statute directly related to the cause of action and give a brief description of the cause. Do not cite jurisdictional
         statutes unless diversity. Example: U.S. Civil Statute: 47 USC 553 Brief Description: Unauthorized reception of cable service.

VII.     Requested in Complaint. Class Action. Place an "X" in this box if you are filing a class action under Rule 23, F.R.Cv.P.
         Demand. In this space enter the actual dollar amount being demanded or indicate other demand, such as a preliminary injunction.
         Jury Demand. Check the appropriate box to indicate whether or not a jury is being demanded.

VIII. Related Cases. This section of the JS 44 is used to reference related cases, if any. If there are related cases, insert the docket
      numbers and the corresponding judge names for such cases.

Date and Attorney Signature. Date and sign the civil cover sheet.
