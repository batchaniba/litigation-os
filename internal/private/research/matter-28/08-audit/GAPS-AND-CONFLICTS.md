## Do not carry these prior assertions into argument

- Fee payment and the August service order did not decide preclusion in plaintiff's favor.
- An exhaustion argument under Patsy does not erase state-judgment preclusion under section 1738.
- The signed state order is not invalid because a judge signed counsel's proposed order.
- Smith's absence from the caption does not defeat privity by itself, or prevent nonmutual issue preclusion.
- A low risk score does not itself compel completion; the regulation's grounds are nonexclusive.
- State-law procedural violations do not automatically establish federal due process liability.
- Policy text and compulsory attendance do not automatically prove scienter, mail/wire fraud, causation, or RICO injury.
- Insurance limits are not a settlement floor, and W-2 earnings are not themselves a lost-wage calculation.
- Private preparation is not automatically privileged work product merely because a historical memo called it that.
