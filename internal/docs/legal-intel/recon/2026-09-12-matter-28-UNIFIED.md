## 2. One-sentence answer on res judicata

Claim preclusion needs a **final judgment on the merits** between the **same parties** on the **same primary right**. Matter 5 has no judgment of dismissal. What the court decided was a **jurisdictional precondition**, not the claim. Shannon Smith was not a party.

The kill that survives a later CCP § 581d judgment is not “no judgment yet.” It is: **the state court decided a jurisdictional precondition, not the claim.** Ask Mag. Cisneros to write that down.

On a 12(b)(6), you may not need to reach that kill. The tentative, the minute order, and the defense-drafted formal order do not say the same thing. That is a disputed fact. Under *ASARCO*, a disputed affirmative defense is a denial.

## 5. How each element dies — and which ones stay dead

### 5.0 The Rule 12 gateway — cheapest win; write it first

***ASARCO, LLC v. Union Pac. R.R. Co.*, 765 F.3d 999, 1004 (9th Cir. 2014)** (CL FOUND; pin SECONDARY until the F.3d page is re-opened): dismissal on an affirmative defense is proper **only if** the defendant shows an **obvious bar** on the **face of the complaint**. Disputed facts → deny.

Three disputed facts on this record:

- Whether a judgment of dismissal was ever entered.
- Whether the actual basis was exhaustion / prematurity or § 430.10(e) insufficiency.
- Whether Smith is in privity with HOPE.

***Lee v. City of Los Angeles*, 250 F.3d 668 (9th Cir. 2001)** (year is 2001): the court may notice that a public record exists and what it says; it may not notice the **truth** of disputed facts inside it. She may notice that the formal order contains the § 430.10(e) sentence. She may not notice that the state court so held — because the tentative and minute order say otherwise.

***Khoja v. Orexigen Therapeutics, Inc.*, 899 F.3d 988 (9th Cir. 2018):** incorporation and Rule 201 are the only doors past the four corners; notice does not swallow disputed facts; the Ninth Circuit has already named the “concerning pattern” of using those doors to kill adequately pleaded claims. She cites *Khoja* in her 12(b)(6) boilerplate (#881 PRIMARY, *Gold Club* / *K.J.*).

### 5.1 No final judgment — wins today’s motion; does not stay dead

An order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend is **not a final judgment** and is **not appealable**. The appeal is from the ensuing judgment of dismissal. Until that judgment is entered, the ruling is not res judicata and the trial court may still change it.

- *Berri v. Superior Court*, 43 Cal.2d 856, 860 (1955). CourtListener: FOUND.
- *Valvo v. University of Southern California*, 67 Cal.App.3d 887, 891–92 & n.3 (1977) — “Until entry of judgment, a ruling on a demurrer is not res judicata.” Confirm PDF before filing.
- *Beazell v. Schrader*, 59 Cal.2d 577, 579–80 (1963).
- CCP § 581d — a **dismissal** ordered by the court, in a signed written order filed in the action, constitutes a judgment. An order that only **sustains a demurrer** is not a dismissal.

*Kay v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes*, 504 F.3d 803, 808 (9th Cir. **2007**) (not 2008), quoting *Franklin & Franklin*, 85 Cal.App.4th 1168, 1174 (2000): California finality does not attach “until an appeal from the trial court judgment has been exhausted or the time to appeal has expired.” CourtListener: FOUND.

***Meinhardt v. City of Sunnyvale*, 16 Cal.5th 643 (2024)** (Jenkins, J., unanimous) — **PRIMARY, opinion PDF read.** #881 said the Court “upheld deeming” a dispositive order a judgment. **That is the Court of Appeal rule the Supreme Court reversed.** The clock runs from entry of — or notice of entry of — a document titled **“judgment,”** not from an order, minute order, or other ruling, even one that “disposes of all issues and contemplates no further action.” Admin-mandate / appealability, not preclusion. It **reinforces** *Berri* / *Valvo* / § 581d. Do not brief #881’s paragraph.

***Sullivan v. Delta Air Lines*, 15 Cal.4th 288, 304 (1997)** exists. It is a CCP § 377.34 death-on-appeal case. Do **not** recruit it as a demurrer-as-judgment holding.

***Blackwell v. Carrington Mortg. Servs., LLC*, No. 24-cv-06494-BLF (N.D. Cal. Jan. 22, 2025)** (Freeman, J.) — PRIMARY. RJN Exhibit 3 was the demurrer order; **Exhibit 4 was an Order of Dismissal With Prejudice.** Freeman’s “final judgment on the merits” paragraph needs both. Matter 5 has **no Exhibit 4**. Not Cisneros. Not § 1983. Not exhaustion. If they cite it: (1) no dismissal-with-prejudice order; (2) Matter 5 ground was jurisdictional exhaustion; (3) Smith was not a party. Hold the distinguisher until they cite it.

Two consequences the earlier memos blurred:

1. **CRC 8.104’s 60-day clock runs from a judgment**, not from an order sustaining a demurrer. If there is no judgment, Monday, September 14 was not an appeal deadline. Do not file a premature appeal of a non-appealable order just to keep *Kay* open.
2. **Finality can ripen while Matter 28 is pending.** Filed July 20 — five days after the July 15 order. If they later obtain a judgment and appeal time expires, *Kay* can attach mid-case.

**Nunc pro tunc risk.** Van Dine can move in Dept. 10A for entry of a judgment of dismissal. Do **not** request that judgment. If they move, oppose any recitation that converts the “jurisdictional requirement” holding into a merits dismissal, and tell Mag. Cisneros that a later 581d judgment still would not be on the merits (§ 5.2).

### 5.2 Not on the merits — this is the once-and-for-all kill

**Best California case.** *Perez v. City of San Bruno*, 27 Cal.3d 875, 882–83 (1980). Caption is *City of San Bruno*, not a bare “Perez” and not *City of Bruno*. A dismissal for **prematurity** is not on the merits. After the missing fact exists, a later action is not barred. CourtListener: FOUND. Primary text read (SCOCAL / Justia).

Judge Abdallah’s words map onto *Perez* without translation: a **jurisdictional requirement**, unmet **prior to the filing of** the FAC.

**Best Ninth Circuit case.** *Heath v. Cleary*, 708 F.2d 1376, **1380 n.4** (9th Cir. 1983). Pin is 1380 n.4, not 1379 n.4. A California mandamus denial **for failure to exhaust** does not claim-preclude a later § 1983 action. Cites *Perez* and Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2). Also applies *Patsy* as “a flat rule without exception.” Primary F2 text. CourtListener: FOUND.

Caveat: *Heath* n.4 is a footnote prediction of California law, on mandamus facts. It has never been contradicted by the California Supreme Court. *Perez* is the California holding; *Heath* is the Ninth Circuit applying it to exhaustion.

**Supreme Court / Restatement.** *Costello v. United States*, 365 U.S. 265, 284–88 (1961) — failure to satisfy a precondition is not an adjudication on the merits. CourtListener: FOUND.

Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2): a judgment that “rests on the prematurity of the action or on the plaintiff's failure to satisfy a precondition to suit” does not bar a later action after the precondition is satisfied.

**General vs. special demurrer — do not lean on “formal defect” alone (#880 verification).** A judgment after a **general** demurrer often *is* on the merits (*Goddard*; *Palomar*, 989 F.2d 362, 364 (9th Cir. 1993); *Pollock v. USC*, 112 Cal.App.4th 1416 (2003)). Their demurrer was almost certainly general. The surviving argument is the exception UNIFIED already had: the **actual ground** was exhaustion as a jurisdictional precondition (CCP § 430.10(a) in the court’s words, (e) only in their proposed order); *Goddard* itself admits even a general-demurrer judgment “may not be on the merits” when the defect is technical; *Keidatz* — the ground is temporally capped and not “equally applicable” to a federal complaint that does not require state administrative exhaustion.

**Why *Goddard* does not save them.** *Goddard v. Security Title Ins. & Guar. Co.*, 14 Cal.2d 47, 52–53 (1939) says a judgment after a general demurrer *may* be on the merits. Two answers: *Goddard* itself admits even a general-demurrer judgment “may not be on the merits” when the defect is technical; and courts look to the **actual basis of decision**, not a defense-drafted § 430.10(e) recital.

**Why *Keidatz* does not travel.** *Keidatz v. Albany*, 39 Cal.2d 826 (1952): a demurrer judgment bars a second action only if the new complaint does not cure the defect, or if the ground is “equally applicable.” The Matter 5 ground is temporally capped (“prior to the filing of his FAC”). It is not equally applicable to a federal complaint that does not require exhaustion of state administrative remedies.

***Patsy v. Board of Regents*, 457 U.S. 496, 516 (1982):** exhaustion of state administrative remedies is not a prerequisite to § 1983. The only statutory carve-out is 42 U.S.C. § 1997e (PLRA). Reopen the U.S. Reports PDF before quoting a pin other than 516.

**PLRA — a parolee at liberty is not a “prisoner.”** § 1997e(h): “prisoner” means a person **incarcerated or detained in any facility**. The parole clause modifies *why* the person is detained; it does not make every parolee a prisoner.

The parole sentence in the opinion is discussion, not the holding. **#879 morning memo over-weighted it. #881 is right. Do not let *Page* carry the paragraph.** |

Halfway-house or parole-violation **custody** can re-enter the definition. GPS / curfew / mandatory treatment is not detention in a facility. He filed Matter 28 as a parolee at liberty. A later parole hold does not retroactively make the July 20 filing a prisoner suit.

## 8. State action, statutes, and the other counts

### 8.1 The sentence to build state action around

***Rawson v. Recovery Innovations, Inc.*, 975 F.3d 742, 753 (9th Cir. 2020)** (cert denied Oct. 4, 2021, No. 20-1287): “private parties may act under color of state law when the state **significantly involves itself** in the private parties’ actions and **decisionmaking at issue**.” Involuntary-commitment facts still distinguish; the published bridge to an institutional private provider survives.

***Kirtley v. Rainey*, 326 F.3d 1088, 1092 (9th Cir. 2003):** public function; joint action; compulsion; nexus. Lead **joint action and nexus**. Do not lead with public function — that is the *Merrill* / *Gross* test.

***Caviness v. Horizon Cmty. Learning Ctr., Inc.*, 590 F.3d 806 (9th Cir. 2010):** identify the **specific conduct**. An entity can be a state actor for some purposes and not others. They will cite it. Take it first: treating him as a private client is one thing; signing Form 3043 on a state-required retention determination that gates a compulsory parole condition is another.

**Pen. Code § 3008(d)(2)** (PRIMARY via CDCR adopted-regs quotation; pin on leginfo before filing). Persons paroled on or after July 1, 2012 “shall **successfully complete**” a SOMP; the period is “**not less than one year, up to the entire period of parole, as determined by the certified sex offender management professional** in consultation with the parole officer and as approved by the court.” A blanket no-completion-before-discharge rule removes the discretion the statute assigns to the certified professional. In force since 2012. Do not convert this into a federal right to graduate at one year.

**15 C.C.R. § 3574** (PRIMARY, Cornell LII / CDCR NCR 25-01). Emergency **operative October 14, 2024**; refiled March 24, 2025; Certificate of Compliance April 17, 2025. **Do not cite it for pre-October 14, 2024 conduct** — use the statute and *Dwyer*. Grace (Feb. 20, 2025) is during the emergency; ringleader (Aug. 19, 2025) and Form 1502 (Nov. 14, 2025) are after permanent.

### 8.2 QI — threshold first; *Esche* is not the holding

*Richardson v. McKnight*, 521 U.S. 399, 408–12 (1997): no QI for a “private firm systematically organized to manage the prison.” A sixteen-location CASOMB vendor with a founder-CEO who sets policy is that firm, not a *Filarsky* individual retained “on a temporary or occasional basis.” *Jensen*, 222 F.3d at 576–79; *Halvorsen*, 146 F.3d at 685; *Clement*, 518 F.3d at 1096; *Ace Beverage*, 144 F.3d at 1219–20. All SAFE.

***Estate of Esche v. Bunuel-Jordana*, Nos. 24-6244 et al. (9th Cir. Sept. 29, 2025)** (Berzon, J.) — **PRIMARY CDN published PDF.** Holding: **no interlocutory jurisdiction** over denial of a private hospital’s **good-faith defense** (liability defense, not immunity from suit). Safe-cite MISCHARACTERIZED stands. Reasoning on why QI usually does not transfer to private parties is usable. **Double-edged:** the panel recognizes the *Filarsky* contractor line. Never brief as “the Ninth Circuit denied QI to private providers.” Never make it the section’s anchor. If they raise good-faith / followed-the-statute at 12(b)(6), it is fact-bound — poor *ASARCO* vehicle.

*Chavez v. Robinson*, 12 F.4th 978 (9th Cir. 2021): state action **assumed**, QI **granted**. Do not cite as a denial.

### 8.3 Bane — underlying right, not a state-action on/off switch

Civ. Code § 52.1 applies to a person “**whether or not acting under color of law**.” *Jones v. Kmart Corp.*, 17 Cal.4th 329 (1998): Bane itself has no state-action element; the plaintiff still must identify an underlying legal right a private defendant can violate or interfere with. Do not say “Bane survives regardless of state action” or “Bane dies if § 1983 fails.” Analyze the right. Keep *Cornell* / *Reese* / *Sandoval* on specific intent (reckless disregard is enough). Do not plead the $25,000 § 52.1(b) civil penalty. `28/damages.json` still says it — fix before any opposition or CMC statement recycles the number.

### 8.4 Fraud — whose reliance

*Zetz*, 398 F. Supp. 3d 700, 713 (E.D. Cal. 2019): who / what / when / where / how. *Moore v. Kayport*, 885 F.2d 531 (9th Cir. 1989): particularity may relax for facts in the opponent’s knowledge if the Bates basis is stated — it does not excuse a thin count. *DZ Reserve*, 96 F.4th 1223 (9th Cir. 2024), is classwide-same-misrepresentation; poor fit.

***Gawara v. U.S. Brass Corp.*, 63 Cal. App. 4th 1341 (1998)** and ***Asmus v. United Airlines*, N.D. Cal. 5:26-cv-00962-BLF, Dkt. 21 (July 2, 2026)** (#882): *Randi W.* is foreseeable **physical** injury (Restatement §§ 310–311). Ordinary economic-loss fraud still needs **actual reliance**. A generic “lied to CDCR, CDCR acted, Plaintiff was harmed” chain is not enough. Build the Rule 15 grid around statements to **him**, what he understood, what he did or did not do, and concealment that would have changed his conduct. Treat the opposition as a leave-to-amend audition (*Orion Tire*; she said so in *Gold Club*).

### 8.5 RICO — *Harmoni* splits the theories

*Horn*, **604 U.S. 593 (2025)** (5–4, Barrett, J.): no categorical antecedent-personal-injury bar. The Court **did not** decide whether “business” includes employment. *Anza* and *Canyon County*, 519 F.3d 969 (9th Cir. 2008), survive. *Cedric Kushner*, 533 U.S. 158 (2001): Smith (person) / HOPE (enterprise) distinctness is pleadable. Does not solve *Anza*.

***Harmoni Int’l Spice, Inc. v. Hume*, 914 F.3d 648 (9th Cir. 2019)** (#882): billing-to-CDCR → incentive to retain → wage loss is the attenuated *Anza* scheme. Deliberately false official reporting **about Plaintiff** designed to trigger a compulsory parole consequence is closer to *Harmoni* scheme two (sham filing that legally required a government process). Billing is motive / pattern context, not the proximate-cause engine. Keep RICO last, lean, and plead leave to amend in the same breath.
