## 1. The finding that decides the res judicata ground

Defense counsel's previewed ground #1 is that Matter 5 "was resolved **on the merits and in
Defendants' favor**," so every claim here is precluded. **The Matter 5 record does not say that.**

### 1.1 What the court actually ruled — the tentative

`matter-05/Tentative Ruling on Demurrer (posted July 13, 2026, hearing July 15, 2026).pdf`,
quoted verbatim:

> "The court having read and considered Defendants' Demurrer to Plaintiff's First Amended Complaint
> (FAC), Plaintiff's Opposition and Defendants' Reply rules as follows. The court agrees with
> Defendants' analysis and the authorities cited in their Motion and Reply that **exhaustion of
> administrative remedies herein is a jurisdictional requirement** to the filing of Plaintiff's FAC.
> The court finds Plaintiff **failed to meet this jurisdictional requirement** prior to the filing of
> his FAC. Therefore, Defendants' Demurrer is GRANTED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.
>
> Hon. George J. Abdallah, Jr., Judge of the Superior Court"

The stated ground is **failure to exhaust administrative remedies, expressly characterized as
jurisdictional.** Not insufficiency of the facts. Not the merits of any cause of action.

### 1.2 What the signed order says — drafted by the defense

`matter-05/Order Sustaining Demurrer to FAC WOLA - Jul 15 2026.pdf` is captioned, on its own face,
**"[PROPOSED] ORDER ON DEFENDANTS KATIE PALACIOS AND HOPE PROGRAM'S DEMURRER"** — i.e. a
defense-prepared form, on Sims, Lawrence & Broghammer letterhead (Cynthia G. Lawrence, Bryan L.
Malone, **Blaze Van Dine**). Its operative line:

> "The demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is sustained without leave to amend **on the grounds
> that the First Amended Complaint fails to allege facts sufficient to state any cause of action.**"

**That is a different ground than the court gave.** "Fails to allege facts sufficient to state any
cause of action" is a merits formulation that would support claim preclusion. "Failed to meet a
jurisdictional exhaustion requirement" is not.

### 1.3 Why the difference is dispositive

A dismissal for failure to exhaust administrative remedies is **not a judgment on the merits** and
does not support claim preclusion — it is functionally a jurisdictional dismissal. The defense's own
previewed framing depends entirely on the recharacterized language in the order it drafted.

Note the continuity: **Van Dine appeared for Palacios and HOPE at the July 15 demurrer hearing**,
prepared the proposed order, and is now the lawyer invoking that order as a merits adjudication
against a different defendant in a different court.

### 1.4 Three independent defects in the preclusion defense

1. **Ground mismatch (§ 1.1–1.2).** For preclusion, what matters is the **actual basis** of the
   decision. The reasoned ruling says exhaustion/jurisdiction.
2. **No final judgment located.** California claim preclusion requires a **final judgment**. An
   order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend is **not itself appealable** — a party appeals
   from the **judgment of dismissal** entered on it. **No judgment of dismissal appears anywhere in
   the Matter 5 document set** (18 files reviewed). If none was entered, preclusion has not attached
   at all.
3. **Smith was not a Matter 5 party.** Matter 5 defendants were **Katie Palacios** (site/clinical
   director, LMFT 44555) and **HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc.** Matter 28 adds **Shannon Smith, LCSW**,
   CEO and Founder, in his individual capacity. As to HOPE, identity of parties is satisfied; as to
   Smith, the defense must establish **privity**, which is contestable for an individual-capacity
   claim premised on his own personal approval of policy.

**What is genuinely at risk even so.** Matter 5 pleaded **Bane Act § 52.1** and **fraud /
intentional misrepresentation** against **HOPE** — the same two claims now re-pleaded against HOPE
here. If a judgment was entered and the merits characterization sticks, those two counts against
HOPE are the most exposed. The § 1983 and RICO counts were not pleaded in Matter 5, but California's
primary-rights doctrine can reach claims that **could have been** brought, and state courts have
concurrent jurisdiction over both — so "I didn't plead it there" is not by itself an answer.

### 1.5 The single highest-value action item

**Obtain (a) the San Joaquin register of actions for STK-CV-UF-2026-0001032 to determine whether a
judgment of dismissal was ever entered, and (b) the reporter's transcript or minute order for the
July 15, 2026 hearing.** Plaintiff filed a Notice of Intent to Appear and Argue the tentative, and
the order recites that argument was heard — so the tiebreaker between the tentative's reasoning and
the proposed order's language is what the court said on the record. Those two documents decide
ground #1.

## 2. The pleaded theory, from the complaint

Source: `matter-28/Complaint_Newanforbi_v_Smith_NDCA (as filed, served on SLB Jul 22 2026).pdf`,
§ I ¶¶ 1–6.

- **HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc.** (d/b/a HOPE Program) is a **CASOMB-certified** LCSW professional
  corporation, CDCR's contracted sex-offender treatment provider, HQ Hayward, 16 statewide locations.
- **Shannon Smith, LCSW**, CEO and Founder, "personally approved, implemented, and enforced a
  **blanket retention policy** under which **no parolee could ever be determined to have completed
  sex offender treatment prior to discharge from parole**, regardless of individual clinical
  progress, compliance, risk reduction, or homework completion."
- **The internal-contradiction evidence.** HOPE's **Clinical Operational Manual (Sept. 2024)** says
  on one page that HOPE is "prohibited from discharging a client from treatment for any reason," and
  on the next describes a six-step graduation process ending in a Treatment Completion Certificate
  that clinicians are "not permitted to provide directly to clients."
- **The admissions.** In verified discovery in Matter 5, HOPE admitted that at **every** Containment
  Team Meeting in the relevant period it recommended continued treatment and **never** recommended a
  completion determination; the site Director admitted she never recommended completion at any CTM
  she attended. HOPE simultaneously claimed "multiple clients" at Stockton completed treatment
  pre-discharge while **refusing to identify a single one.**
- **Injury.** Trapped in mandatory treatment since **March 2021**; false reports to CDCR; denied
  parole discharge; continued GPS monitoring and curfew; **over $109,000 in lost wages** as a Class A
  CDL commercial truck driver.
- **Retaliation.** After he complained, HOPE's site Director labeled him a **"ringleader"** and
  **"abhorrent"** in front of his parole agents at a CTM on **Aug. 19, 2025**.
- **Counts:** (I–II) § 1983 individual capacity and Monell; (III) Bane Act; (IV) fraud and negligent
  misrepresentation; (V) RICO § 1962(c)–(d).

### 2.1 The statutory hook — verified

**Penal Code § 3008** is real and says what the theory needs. Persons paroled on or after July 1,
2012 must successfully complete a sex offender management program as a parole condition, for a period
"**not less than one year, up to the entire period of parole, as determined by the certified sex
offender management professional in consultation with the parole officer**"; the program and
providers must be **CASOMB-certified**.

**That clause is the case.** The Legislature vested the completion determination in the certified
professional's clinical judgment. A blanket policy that forecloses ever making that determination
does not merely disappoint a parolee — it **removes the discretion the statute confers**.

**Flagged:** the exact text of **subdivision (d)(2)** specifically was not retrieved (leginfo is
egress-blocked — § 6). The general § 3008 requirement is verified; pin (d)(2) before quoting it.

### 2.2 *In re Dwyer* — substance corroborated, citation NOT verified

The complaint's ¶ 2 rests on ***In re Dwyer*, No. SC-080355A (San Mateo County Super. Ct., Mar. 20,
2023)**, where the court allegedly found HOPE's policy "vitiates the Legislature's directive" under
§ 3008(d)(2), and where **Dr. Cristina Loree**, HOPE's Executive Director of Clinical Training,
allegedly testified that HOPE requires continuous treatment until parole discharge.

**Independently corroborated — the event happened.** ACSOL reported in **March 2023** that a San
Mateo County Superior Court judge ruled a parolee registrant was no longer required to participate in
an additional treatment program, after a hearing at which **a CDCR representative testified that "a
sex offender cannot successfully 'complete' a sex offender management treatment program"** while on
parole, and that the decision rested on **§ 3008(d)(2)**, which authorizes courts to disapprove
further participation as a parole condition.

### 3.1 The RICO standing point the defense did *not* make — and it now favors Plaintiff

The obvious attack on Count V is that lost wages flowing from parole restrictions are a **personal
injury**, not "injury to business or property" under § 1964(c). Van Dine argued only pleading
specificity. On the merits of standing, current law is **favorable**:

- ***Diaz v. Gates*, 420 F.3d 897 (9th Cir. 2005) (en banc)** — plaintiff pleaded a RICO injury by
  seeking damages for **lost employment and lost wages incurred while unjustly incarcerated**; the
  right to employment is a property interest **even though the loss derived from false imprisonment,
  a personal injury.**
- ***Medical Marijuana, Inc. v. Horn***, No. 23-365 (U.S. **Apr. 2, 2025**) (Barrett, J., 5–4) —
  under § 1964(c) a plaintiff **may recover treble damages for business or property loss even when
  the loss resulted from a personal injury.** The plaintiff there was **fired after a positive drug
  test** — i.e. lost employment was the RICO injury.

**Matter 28's $109,000+ in lost Class A CDL wages sits directly inside both holdings.** RICO
standing should not be the count's problem. Its problems are the **enterprise** element and Rule
9(b) particularity on the mail/wire-fraud predicates.

**One structural point in Plaintiff's favor on enterprise:** *Cedric Kushner Promotions, Ltd. v.
King*, 533 U.S. 158 (2001) holds a corporate employee/officer is a "person" distinct from the
corporation as "enterprise" — which is exactly the Smith/HOPE configuration pleaded. **Not verified
this pass; verify before briefing.**

## 4. The doctrinal question that makes this case interesting

Whether a **private, CASOMB-certified, for-profit treatment contractor** can be held liable under
*Monell* for a **blanket policy** that forecloses the individualized completion determination
Penal Code § 3008 assigns to it.

The building blocks are settled: private contractors performing delegated state functions act under
color of state law (*West*); the Ninth Circuit applies that to psychiatric and custodial contractors
(*Jensen*, *Halvorsen*); private entities do not get respondeat superior liability but **are** liable
for their own **policy or custom**; and a **blanket rule is the easiest possible *Monell* hook**,
because it is official policy by definition rather than a stray act.

What I could not find is any case applying that configuration to a sex-offender-treatment vendor
whose reports gate parole discharge. **That is a gap in my search, not a verified absence** — the
CourtListener sweep that would test it did not run (§ 6). Treat "novel" as a hypothesis.

**The structural link to Matter 26 (UPS).** Both are **categorical-rule** cases: a blanket rule
substituted for the individualized determination the law requires — § 290.46(j)(1)'s specific
justification in Matter 26, § 3008's clinical determination here. Both injuries are **career
displacement**. That symmetry is useful for framing, and dangerous for allocation: **both matters
claim employment loss, and Matter 28's damages are explicitly lost CDL wages.** Decide now how the
wage loss is allocated between matters, before an opponent frames it as double recovery.
