# INTRODUCTION

1. This action arises from an accepted Best Buy warehouse job in Tracy, California. On October 17, 2023, Best Buy congratulated Plaintiff on an offer for General Warehouse Worker / 2nd Shift at $22.60 per hour, requisition 928556BR, at Best Buy's warehouse at 6653 Hopkins Road, Tracy, California.

2. Best Buy required Plaintiff to complete Candidate Zone acceptance, background-check information, and background-check authorization. Plaintiff accepted the offer and completed the required onboarding steps, including the drug-screening process.

3. Best Buy's own October 17, 2023 onboarding records tie First Advantage directly to Plaintiff's transaction. At approximately 4:22 p.m., Best Buy sent Plaintiff a background-authorization confirmation identifying First Advantage Consumer Center. Minutes later, First Advantage generated a Best Buy drug-test order associated with Plaintiff, order Q09078711, under Best Buy account 11759586.

4. Plaintiff alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that First Advantage Background Services Corp. (“First Advantage”) was Best Buy's consumer reporting agency for Plaintiff's employment background screening; that First Advantage prepared and furnished a consumer report concerning Plaintiff to Best Buy; and that Best Buy received and used that report in deciding whether Plaintiff would begin or continue the accepted Tracy employment.

5. Plaintiff further alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that the First Advantage report furnished to Best Buy contained information disclosed pursuant to California Penal Code § 290.46, including Plaintiff's California sex-offender-registry information, and that Best Buy received, accessed, considered, and used that information for a purpose relating to employment.

6. After Plaintiff accepted the offer and completed the required screening process, Best Buy ceased the hiring process and did not place Plaintiff into the accepted warehouse job. Best Buy did not communicate an independent post-screen reason for ending the hire.

7. Before the adverse employment action became final, Best Buy did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the First Advantage consumer report and did not provide the written Summary of Rights required by 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A).

8. When Best Buy denied, withdrew, ended, or prevented the accepted employment under circumstances in which it had obtained an investigative consumer report, Best Buy did not advise Plaintiff that the report had been obtained in connection with the denial and did not provide the adverse-action advice required by California Civil Code § 1786.40.

9. The § 290.46 allegation is supported by specific facts rather than conjecture. Plaintiff was contemporaneously listed on California's Megan's Law website; Best Buy's own onboarding records identify First Advantage in Plaintiff's screening process; First Advantage employment reports concerning this same Plaintiff for other employers during the surrounding period retrieved and reported his California registry information; and Best Buy ended the hiring process after the screening without communicating another reason.

# FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

## A. The Written Best Buy Offer

20. On October 13, 2023, Best Buy confirmed receipt of Plaintiff's application for General Warehouse Worker / 2nd Shift, requisition 928556BR, at $22.60 per hour.

21. On October 17, 2023, Best Buy General Manager Jose Tapia emailed Plaintiff a congratulations offer for the warehouse position.

22. The offer required Plaintiff, within the specified onboarding window, to complete Candidate Zone acceptance, provide background-check information, and authorize the background check.

23. Plaintiff accepted the offer. Best Buy later sent correspondence expressly congratulating Plaintiff on accepting his offer.

24. The position concerned Best Buy's Tracy warehouse at 6653 Hopkins Road, Tracy, California 95377.

## B. Best Buy's Transaction-Specific First Advantage Records

25. On October 17, 2023, after the offer, Best Buy sent Plaintiff a background-authorization confirmation identifying First Advantage Consumer Center.

26. The background-authorization confirmation was part of Plaintiff's Best Buy onboarding process and followed Best Buy's instruction that Plaintiff complete the background-check authorization.

27. Minutes after that confirmation, First Advantage generated a Best Buy drug-screen order for Plaintiff, order Q09078711.

28. The First Advantage order identified Best Buy account 11759586 and reflected a Best Buy-directed screening workflow concerning Plaintiff.

29. Plaintiff completed the required drug screening. On information and belief, the drug screen did not independently disqualify Plaintiff.

30. These transaction-specific records establish that First Advantage was directly involved in Best Buy's screening of Plaintiff. On information and belief, First Advantage also prepared and furnished the employment consumer report used by Best Buy in the hiring process.

31. Best Buy and First Advantage possess the complete report, order profile, package identifier, component searches, source-of-record fields, adjudication result, client criteria, report versions, transmission history, access history, and records identifying the information Best Buy reviewed.

## C. Same-Consumer First Advantage Comparator Evidence

32. At the time of Best Buy's screening, Plaintiff was publicly listed on California's Megan's Law website established under Penal Code § 290.46.

33. Plaintiff's identity and underlying California registry entry were the same regardless of which employer requested a First Advantage employment screen. Employer-specific packages, adjudication rules, and presentation could vary, but the underlying consumer and registry information did not.

34. Plaintiff possesses a First Advantage employment background report concerning himself generated for United Parcel Service during the surrounding period. That report contained a sex-offender-registry component concerning Plaintiff and expressly identified the source of record as the California Sex Offender Registry. The registry component was adverse or decisional while ordinary criminal-search components were non-adverse.

35. In a separate First Advantage employment screening for Federal Express Corporation, FedEx later admitted in a responsive pleading that the First Advantage report identified Plaintiff as a California registrant.

36. Plaintiff does not allege that UPS's or FedEx's report is Best Buy's report. He alleges these comparator facts because they involve the same consumer, the same CRA, the same underlying California registry information, and the same type of employment screening, and therefore provide factual support for the allegation that First Advantage's Best Buy screening likewise contained Plaintiff's California registry information.

37. Best Buy's direct transaction-specific First Advantage records make the inference stronger here than in a case in which the identity of the screening vendor itself is unknown.

## D. Best Buy's Adverse Employment Action

38. After Plaintiff accepted the written offer and completed Best Buy's required screening process, Best Buy stopped the hiring process and did not place Plaintiff into the accepted warehouse job.

39. The cessation occurred after Best Buy initiated the First Advantage screening process.

40. Best Buy did not communicate to Plaintiff a no-show, failed drug test, cancellation of the requisition, lack of qualification, or other independent post-screen reason for ending the accepted employment.

41. Plaintiff alleges that Best Buy took adverse employment action based in whole or in part on the First Advantage consumer report.

42. Plaintiff alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that Plaintiff's California registry information reported through First Advantage was a substantial factor in Best Buy's decision.

## E. No FCRA Pre-Adverse Package

43. Before the adverse employment action became final, Best Buy did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the First Advantage consumer report.

44. Before the adverse employment action became final, Best Buy did not provide Plaintiff the written Summary of Rights prescribed by the FCRA.

45. Plaintiff therefore had no pre-decision opportunity to review the report Best Buy was using, identify the California-registry information in it, dispute or explain the report as appropriate, or advise Best Buy before the decision became final that Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibited use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for this employment purpose.

46. Best Buy's failure caused concrete injury. Plaintiff lost an accepted employment opportunity and resulting compensation. The omitted pre-adverse process was tied to that injury because Plaintiff alleges that the report contained the very registry information Best Buy was prohibited from using for the employment decision.

## F. No ICRAA Adverse-Action Advice

47. The First Advantage employment background report was an investigative consumer report within ICRAA because it was prepared for employment purposes and contained information bearing on Plaintiff's character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living, including criminal/public-record and registry information.

48. Best Buy's pre-screen authorization materials identifying First Advantage did not advise Plaintiff that employment had later been denied under circumstances in which an investigative consumer report had been obtained.

49. When Best Buy denied, withdrew, ended, or prevented the employment, Best Buy did not provide Plaintiff the adverse-action advice required by Civil Code § 1786.40 or advise him that an investigative report had been obtained in connection with the denial.

50. Count Two concerns that adverse-action omission. Count One concerns a different act or omission: Best Buy's failure before adverse action to furnish the actual report and federal Summary of Rights.

## H. California Registry Information and Best Buy's Use

57. Plaintiff is a person as to whom information is disclosed on California's Megan's Law website established pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46.

58. Plaintiff alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that the First Advantage report furnished to Best Buy contained Plaintiff's information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46, including his California registry information.

59. Plaintiff alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that Best Buy received, accessed, considered, and used that § 290.46 information for the purpose of deciding whether Plaintiff would begin or continue the accepted Tracy employment.

60. Plaintiff alleges that the § 290.46 information was a substantial factor in Best Buy's adverse employment decision.

61. The factual basis for those allegations includes: the written and accepted warehouse offer; Best Buy's immediate background-check requirement; Best Buy's transaction-specific identification of First Advantage; the First Advantage Best Buy order generated in Plaintiff's onboarding process; Plaintiff's contemporaneous California registry listing; First Advantage's demonstrated reporting of that same registry information in other employment screens of this same Plaintiff; Best Buy's cessation of the hiring process after screening; and the absence of a communicated independent reason.

62. The temporal sequence was: Best Buy offered Plaintiff the job; Plaintiff accepted; Best Buy initiated First Advantage screening; First Advantage processed Plaintiff in Best Buy's screening workflow; Best Buy received or accessed the screening result; and Best Buy then stopped the accepted employment.

63. Best Buy's use was for a purpose relating to employment within the meaning of § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

64. Plaintiff is unaware of any law authorizing Best Buy to use information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for the General Warehouse Worker / 2nd Shift position at issue.

65. The exact First Advantage source-field wording, adjudication code, access timestamps, internal reason codes, screening matrix, and decisionmaker identity are evidentiary details within Best Buy's and First Advantage's possession. Plaintiff's inability to quote those internal records before discovery does not alter his factual allegation that Best Buy used Plaintiff's § 290.46 information for the employment decision.

66. Any contention that Best Buy relied on a different source or reason is a merits defense to the allegations above. For purposes of this Complaint, Plaintiff alleges that the California registry information reported through First Advantage was used by Best Buy and was a substantial factor in the adverse employment action.

# FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION
## Fair Credit Reporting Act — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A)
### Against Best Buy Co., Inc.

67. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 66.

68. Plaintiff was a “consumer,” the First Advantage report was a “consumer report,” Best Buy procured or caused it to be procured for “employment purposes,” and Best Buy was a “person” using the report within the meaning of the FCRA.

69. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A), before taking adverse action based in whole or in part on an employment consumer report, the person intending to take adverse action must provide the consumer a copy of the report and a written description of FCRA rights.

70. Best Buy took adverse employment action based in whole or in part on the First Advantage report.

71. Best Buy did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the report before the adverse action.

72. Best Buy did not provide Plaintiff the written Summary of Rights before the adverse action.

73. Best Buy therefore violated § 1681b(b)(3)(A).

74. Best Buy's noncompliance was negligent within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681o and, on proof, willful within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681n. Best Buy operated a standardized background-screening workflow, affirmatively required the authorization, and used an established national CRA. Plaintiff pleads negligence and willfulness in the alternative.

75. As a direct result, Plaintiff suffered actual damages including loss of the accepted employment and compensation and loss of the pre-decision opportunity described above. If willfulness is proven, Plaintiff seeks statutory and punitive damages authorized by § 1681n.

# SECOND CAUSE OF ACTION
## California Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act
### Civil Code §§ 1786.40, 1786.50
### Against Best Buy Co., Inc.

76. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 66.

77. The First Advantage employment background report was an investigative consumer report within ICRAA.

78. Civil Code § 1786.40(a) provides that when employment involving a consumer is denied under circumstances in which a report regarding the consumer was obtained from an investigative consumer reporting agency, the user shall advise the consumer and supply the name and address of the investigative consumer reporting agency making the report.

79. Best Buy denied, withdrew, ended, or prevented Plaintiff's employment under circumstances in which it had obtained the First Advantage investigative consumer report.

80. Best Buy did not provide the adverse-action advice required by § 1786.40 when the employment was denied.

81. This § 1786.40 omission is pleaded as a different act or omission from Count One's pre-adverse failure to furnish the report and federal Summary of Rights.

82. Under Civil Code § 1786.50, Plaintiff seeks actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater, together with costs and any attorney's fees recoverable if counsel later appears. If gross negligence or willfulness is proven, Plaintiff seeks punitive damages as authorized by § 1786.50(b).

# THIRD CAUSE OF ACTION
## Unauthorized Employment Use of Registry Information
### Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A)
### Against Best Buy Co., Inc.

83. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 66.

84. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(1) authorizes use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 only to protect a person at risk, except as otherwise authorized by law.

85. Section 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for purposes relating to employment except as authorized by paragraph (1) or another provision of law.

86. Plaintiff alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that the First Advantage report furnished to Best Buy contained information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46, including Plaintiff's California registry information.

87. Plaintiff alleges, on information and belief and as an ultimate fact, that Best Buy received, accessed, considered, and used that § 290.46 information for a purpose relating to employment.

88. Plaintiff alleges that the § 290.46 information was a substantial factor in Best Buy's decision to deny, withdraw, end, or prevent Plaintiff's accepted Tracy employment.

89. The pleaded use was not authorized by § 290.46(j)(1), and Plaintiff is unaware of another law authorizing Best Buy to use information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for the warehouse position.

90. Best Buy thereby violated § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

91. Section 290.46(j)(4)(A) makes a prohibited user liable for actual damages and additional remedies specified in that subdivision. Plaintiff seeks timely, nonduplicative compensatory and other relief legally available under that provision.

92. Plaintiff does not presently seek pattern-or-practice preventive relief under § 290.46(j)(4)(B). If discovery establishes a pattern or practice and Article III requirements are independently satisfied, Plaintiff may seek leave to amend.

## Filing gates — remove before filing

1. **FCRA discovery date:** This is the most important federal-question gate. Ninth Circuit law applies actual/constructive discovery under § 1681p. 2. **ICRAA discovery date:** § 1786.52 provides two years from discovery. Do not file Count Two without a supportable date/event.
3. **Diversity:** Preserve §§ 11–13 even if Counts One or Two are removed. Confirm Plaintiff's California citizenship, Best Buy Co., Inc.'s Minnesota citizenship, and a good-faith amount in controversy exceeding $75,000.
4. **§ 290.46 timing:** File before the October 2026 third anniversary of the 2023 transaction to preserve the strongest argument that the compensatory statutory liability falls within CCP § 338(a).
