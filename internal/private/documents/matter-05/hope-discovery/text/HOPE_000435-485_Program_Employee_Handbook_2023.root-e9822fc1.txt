INTRODUCTION

Welcome

Welcome to HOPE Program, Licensed Clinical Social Worker Professional Corporation (“HOPE
Program”). One of the keys to our success is hiring great employees. We have hired you because
we believe you have the skills and the potential to help HOPE Program succeed.

We are glad to have you as a member of the HOPE Program team. We hope that your
employment proves mutually satisfying and that you will make an important contribution to our
future. Every employee has an important role in our operations and we value the abilities,
experience, and background that you bring with you. It is our employees who provide the services
that our clients rely upon and enable us to grow and create new opportunities in the years to
come.

Once again, welcome to HOPE Program; we are glad to have you with us.

Shannon Smith
CEO

Purpose of This Handbook
This Employee Handbook will familiarize you with the various aspects of working with us. We
encourage you to use it as a valuable resource for understanding HOPE Program.

This handbook is a guide to our employment practices and policies and is not intended as a
contract of continued employment. Nothing in the handbook or in any other personnel
document, including benefit plan descriptions, creates or is intended to create a promise or
representation of continued employment for any employee. We are an at-will employer. For
additional information, see the section on “At-Will” employment. Employees are required to
acknowledge, understand and adhere to these policies. If you have any questions or concerns
regarding your employment at HOPE Program, or any of our policies, please speak to your
manager or Human Resources (HR) at any time.

HOPE Program reserves the right to revise, modify, delete, or add to any and all policies,
procedures, work rules, or benefits stated in this handbook or in any other document, except for

the policy of at-will employment. Changes may be made at any time with, or without, prior
notice.

The handbook replaces all previous personnel handbooks, policies, practices, and guidelines. No
one other than Shannon Smith, CEO, has the authority to enter into any other agreement that
modifies Company policy. If a HOPE Program employee has a written employment agreement
that conflicts with the policies in this handbook, the written employment agreement will prevail.

Employee Relations

Standards of Conduct

Employees are expected to follow all HOPE Program policies and procedures. Specific conduct
which may result in immediate suspension or termination includes, but is not limited to: fighting;
stealing; insubordination; possession of weapons on HOPE Program premises; falsification of
business records including employment records, applications, client records, and expense and
time reports; possession, use, sale, or distribution of alcohol or controlled substances on HOPE
Program premises (see “Drug and Alcohol Policy” for further information), unauthorized
disclosure of confidential business or client records or information; violation of HOPE Program
policy, and violation of laws including intellectual property law.

Conflict Resolution

Misunderstandings and conflicts happen in every workplace. It is helpful to solve these before
serious problems develop. Many incidents resolve themselves naturally, but if a situation persists
that you believe is detrimental to you or to HOPE Program, please:

1. First, try to discuss the problem directly with your colleague as soon as possible. If you believe
a discussion with your colleague is not appropriate or has not worked, then proceed to the
next step.

2. Discuss the problem with your Clinical Director or Supervisor. If the issue is not resolved after
the discussion with your Clinical Director or Supervisor, or if you do not feel comfortable
discussing the issue with your Clinical Director or Supervisor, then request a meeting with
Human Resources. Human Resources will invite a member of leadership to attend the
meeting. To try to resolve the problem, the facts will be considered and, if necessary, an
investigation will be conducted. You may be asked to provide a written summary of your
complaint.

3. Any matter that leads to investigation, will be reviewed by Human Resources, the Executive
Clinical Director (ECD) and CEO. Upon conclusion, you will be advised of a final decision and
provided a coaching form to outline expectations.

HOPE Program does not tolerate any retaliation against employees who pursue this procedure.
This procedure does not prevent, limit, or hinder HOPE Program from taking disciplinary action
against any individual, up to and including, termination.

Progressive Disciplinary Process
At times, we may need to work with an employee to correct a behavior or improve performance.

HOPE Program will, when possible, follow the procedure described below. Nothing in this policy
gives an employee the right to receive any particular level of discipline in any given situation, nor
does anything in this policy limit our or the employee's right to terminate the employment
relationship at any time, with or without cause.

1. If an employee needs to improve their job performance or has violated HOPE Program’s
standards of conduct, the employee will be given a verbal warning describing the area in
need of improvement. This will be documented in the employee’s one-on-one folder for

2. If the employee fails to improve following a verbal warning, or if the employee has

suspension or termination appropriate, a manager will recommend suspension or
termination of the employee to Human Resources and the appropriate manager. Steps
one and two will be overridden in these circumstances and all suspensions and
terminations will be approved by the Executive Clinical Director or CEO.

Open Door Policy

HOPE Program encourages open communication among its employees and senior management.
Employees are encouraged to approach any member of the management team anytime with
questions or concerns.

Sexual and Other Forms of Prohibited Harassment and Discrimination

HOPE Program is committed to providing a work environment free of unlawful harassment and
discrimination. Organizational policy prohibits sexual harassment and harassment based on
pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions, genetic information, race, religious creed,
color, gender, gender identity, gender expression, national origin or ancestry, physical or mental
disability, medical condition, marital status, registered domestic partner status, age, sexual
orientation or any other basis protected by federal, state or local law or ordinance or regulation.
All such harassment and discrimination are unlawful.

Our anti-harassment and anti-discrimination policy applies to all persons involved in the
operation of HOPE Program and prohibits unlawful discrimination or harassment by any
employee, including managers, as well as third parties including vendors and clients, to the extent
that HOPE Program can control those third parties. It also prohibits unlawful harassment based
on the perception that anyone has any of those characteristics, or is associated with a person
who has or is perceived as having any of those characteristics.

Prohibited unlawful harassment includes, but is not limited to, the following behavior:
e Verbal conduct such as epithets, derogatory jokes or comments, slurs or unwanted sexual
advances, invitations or comments;
e Visual displays such as derogatory and/or sexually-oriented posters, photography,
cartoons, drawings or gestures;

e Physical conduct including assault, unwanted touching, intentionally blocking normal

e Threats and demands to submit to sexual requests as a condition of continued

e Retaliation for reporting or threatening to report harassment.

If you believe that you have been unlawfully harassed or discriminated against, you have the right
to make a complaint. Submit a written complaint to your own or any other Clinical Director or

provide details of the incident(s), names of individuals involved and names of any witnesses.
Clinical Director or Supervisor will refer all harassment complaints to the CEO. HOPE Program has

We will promptly undertake a thorough and objective investigation of the allegations. Every
attempt will be made to protect the privacy of the individuals involved, but we cannot and do
not guarantee that the complaint and the investigation will remain strictly confidential. If we

with the circumstances involved. Even if the conduct does not constitute a violation of any law
prohibiting harassment or discrimination, it may still be found to be a violation of this policy.

Any employee determined to be responsible for a violation of this policy will be subject to
appropriate disciplinary action up to, and including termination. Although we will try to advise
concerned parties of the outcome of the investigation, for reasons of privacy, the details of an
investigation’s outcome will not generally be shared. HOPE Program will not retaliate against any
employee for filing a complaint and will not tolerate or permit retaliation by management,
employees or co-workers.

HOPE Program encourages all employees to report any incidents of harassment and
discrimination forbidden by this policy immediately so that complaints can be quickly and fairly
resolved. The Federal Equal Employment Opportunity Commission and the California
Department of Fair Employment and Housing investigate and prosecute complaints of prohibited
harassment in employment. If you think you have been harassed or that you have been retaliated
against for resisting or complaining, you may file a complaint with the appropriate agency.

Consensual Romantic Relationships Between Employees

HOPE Program knows that employees sometimes engage in consensual romantic relationships
with each other. When these occur, the employees have a responsibility to report the
relationship immediately to their Clinical Director and Human Resources. In addition, employees
involved in these relationships must act professionally at all times and not engage in any conduct
during work that may be offensive or make others uncomfortable. Employees may not be in
romantic relationships with other employees. If such a relationship begins, it is the employees’
responsibility to promptly inform their manager and Human Resources so that, if possible, an
appropriate transfer may be arranged.

Abusive Conduct

HOPE Program also prohibits abusive conduct or bullying. Prohibited behavior, as defined under
California law includes “conduct of an employer or employee in the workplace, with malice, that
a reasonable person would find hostile, offensive, and unrelated to an employer's legitimate
business interests. Abusive conduct may include repeated infliction of verbal abuse or physical
conduct that a reasonable person would find threatening, intimidating, or humiliating, or the
gratuitous sabotage or undermining of a person’s work performance.”

If you believe that you have experienced, witnessed, or are aware of any incident of abusive
behavior at HOPE Program, immediately submit a written complaint to Human Resources.

Unlawful Retaliation

Retaliation for reporting or opposing possible unlawful or improper conduct, or for assisting in
the reporting or investigation of such conduct, constitutes employee misconduct and will not be
tolerated. HOPE Program is committed to providing a work environment that is free from all
forms of improper retaliation.

State and federal law prohibits retaliation against an employee or applicant on many grounds,
including, but not limited to, retaliation for reporting or opposing sexual or other unlawful
harassment, discrimination, health and safety violations, assisting in a workers’ compensation
proceeding, or for disclosing to a government or law enforcement agency violations of state or
federal statutes or noncompliance with laws or regulations.

If you believe that you have experienced, witnessed or are aware of an incident of possible
retaliation, immediately submit a written complaint to your own or any other Clinical Director,
and Human Resources. You will be asked to provide details of the incident or incidents, names of
individuals involved and of any witnesses. Clinical Directors will refer all complaints to the
appropriate manager. Depending on the circumstances of the complaint, HOPE Program may
retain an external investigator.

Employment

Employment at Will

We are an “at will" employer, which means that employment may be terminated by either the
employee or by HOPE Program with or without cause, and without prior notice. Nothing in this
handbook limits the right to terminate at-will employment. No employee has the authority to
enter into an agreement for employment for any specified period of time or to make an
agreement for employment on other than at-will terms. Only the CEO has the authority to make
any such agreement, which is binding only if it is in writing.

Introductory Period

Your first 6 months of continuous employment will no doubt be a learning experience. You will
learn your job duties and responsibilities, get acquainted with your Clinical Director, Supervisor(s)
and fellow employees, and familiarize yourself with HOPE Program in general. We refer to this
initial period of employment as your introductory period. At the end of 6 months, you and your
manager will review your performance. If your performance has been satisfactory, you are
enjoying your experience at HOPE Program, and you are going to continue your employment,
goals for the coming months will be established.

We understand that you will be learning a lot about your new job, you are still expected to
perform satisfactorily and your performance will be reviewed closely. Either you or HOPE
Program may end the employment relationship at any time during or after the introductory
period. Completion of the introductory period does not guarantee continued employment and
does not change the at-will nature of the employment relationship.

By the 60th day of employment, employees who work 32 or more hours per week are offered
the opportunity to enroll into Benefits Plan, which goes into effect on the 1° of the following
month.

Immigration Law Compliance

All offers of employment are contingent on verification of your legal right to work in the United
States. On your first day of work, you will be asked to provide original documents verifying your
right to work and, as required by federal law, to sign Federal Form I-9, Employment Eligibility
Verification Form. HOPE Program may be obligated to terminate your employment if at any time
you cannot verify your right to work in the United States.

reference checks are an important part of these processes.

Consistent with legal and/or contractual requirements, and with written permission, HOPE
Program may also review an applicant’s or an employee’s criminal conviction record, and related
information, and use such information when making employment decisions, but only to the
extent permissible under applicable law. All background checks will be conducted in strict

HOPE Program is an equal opportunity employer. In accordance with applicable law, we prohibit

Nondiscrimination Against and Accommodation of Individuals with Disabilities

We are committed to complying fully with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and other

Reasonable accommodation is available to all employees with disabilities where their disability

based on the merits of the situation, not the disability of the individual.

HOPE Program is also committed to not discriminating against any qualified employees or
applicants because they are related to or associated with a person with a disability.

This policy is neither exhaustive nor exclusive. HOPE Program is committed to taking all other
actions necessary to ensure equal employment opportunity for persons with disabilities in
accordance with the ADA and all other applicable federal, state and local laws. For more
information, go to: www.usdoj.gov/crt/ada/adahomi.htm.

HOPE does not allow animals in the workplace; however, an individual with a disability may

animal to work when medically necessary. HOPE reserves the right to evaluate that the

Employee Records

Employees are required to notify HOPE Program of changes in address, telephone number,
and/or family status (i.e., births, marriage, divorce, etc.) and update our HR electronic platform
accordingly. Employee records are the property of HOPE Program and access to the information
they contain is restricted. Employees may review their own file by submitting a written request
to Human Resources.

Employment Classifications
For purposes of salary administration and eligibility for overtime payments and employee
benefits, HOPE Program classifies employees as defined below. If you change positions during
your employment, you will be informed of any change in your status. Please direct any questions
you have to Human Resources.

Nonexempt Employees
Employees who are eligible to be paid overtime in accordance with applicable federal and
state wage and law hours. They may be either full-time or part-time employees.

Exempt Employees

Employees who are not eligible for overtime in accordance with applicable federal and
state wage and hour laws. Exempt employees are not paid based on the number of hours
that they work and are expected to complete their work.

Employment Status
HOPE Program has established the following categories for both nonexempt and exempt
employees:

Regular Full-time Employees

Regular full-time employees are at-will employees hired to work HOPE Program’s normal
40-hour work week on a regular basis. These employees may be "exempt" or
"nonexempt" as defined above. Full-time employees are expected to work a schedule of
no less than forty hours per week based on the requirements of their position

Regular Part-time Employees

Regular part-time employees are at-will and hired to work fewer than 40 hours per week
on a regular basis. Such employees may be "exempt" or "nonexempt" as defined above.
Part-time employees are expected to work a schedule that is agreed upon in advance,
both in terms of work days and hours in the office. Some benefits are pro-rated based on
the number of hours worked unless otherwise noted in this handbook. Certain coverage
and benefits may be subject to a minimum number of hours worked. Part-time employees
will not be eligible for some benefits, which are outlined in the handbook.

Temporary Employees
Temporary employees are at-will and engaged to work full-time or part-time with the
understanding that their employment may be terminated on or before completion of a

Attendance and Punctuality

Punctual and regular attendance is an essential responsibility of each employee at the HOPE
Program. Employees are expected to report to work as scheduled, on time and prepared to work.
Employees also are expected to remain at work for their entire work schedule. Late arrival, early
departure or other absences from scheduled hours are disruptive and should be avoided. Your
contribution is important to our work. If you are absent or late to work, you may place a burden
on fellow employees, and it becomes more difficult to do their jobs. Employees may not make
changes to their work schedules without prior written approval from their clinical director.

If you know in advance that you will need time away from work or will be late, you are expected

HEALTH, SAFETY, AND SECURITY

The safety and health of our employees is a priority at HOPE Program. We make every effort to
comply with all federal and state workplace requirements. Each employee is expected to obey
safety rules and exercise caution and common sense in all work activities.

Smoking

California law prohibits smoking in any public building or within 20 feet of a main entrance, exit,
or window of a public building. Most other states in which our employees work and reside also
prohibit smoking in workplaces and near public buildings.

HOPE Program does not permit smoking (including of e-Cigarettes or marijuana) in any Company
buildings, facilities, work sites, or vehicles. Employees wishing to smoke should do so during
break times, outside HOPE Program buildings in designated areas, and in accordance with local
ordinances. Employees who are working offsite must adhere to the relevant rules governing
smoking.

Drug and Alcohol

No employee may report to work or remain at work while under the influence of drugs (including
marijuana) or alcohol. Consideration will be made as appropriate for employees taking
prescription medication that may affect their ability to safely perform their job.

Employees are prohibited from possessing, furnishing, selling, offering, and purchasing illegal
drugs or other controlled substances, as defined under federal or applicable state law on HOPE
Program property, including marijuana. Employees who violate this policy will be subject to
disciplinary action up to and including dismissal. Any illegal substances found on Company
property will be turned over to local law enforcement and may result in criminal prosecution.

There may be occasions when alcoholic beverages are offered at HOPE Program-related social
events either on or off HOPE Program property. In that case, it is the responsibility of each
employee to behave in a professional manner and consume only a minimal quantity of alcohol,
if any, so as to not affect their ability to professionally represent HOPE Program or impact the
safety of the employee or any other party. HOPE Program assumes no responsibility or liability
for employee’s actions during or after such events.

Safety and Workplace Violence

HOPE Program intends to provide everyone with a safe and secure working environment. It is
everyone's responsibility to assist in maintaining a safe and secure environment. Confidential
materials should be kept out of sight and locked either in a file or office. Personal items of value
should not be kept in the office. Laptop computers and other portable devices of substantial
value should be kept in drawers. HOPE Program is not responsible for personal items lost,
damaged, or stolen in the workplace.

EMPLOYMENT SEPARATION

HOPE Program is an at-will employer. This means that neither you nor HOPE Program has entered
into a contract regarding the duration of your employment. You are free to terminate your
employment with HOPE Program at any time, with or without reason and we also have the right
to terminate your employment, or otherwise discipline, transfer, or demote you at any time, with
or without reason.

Voluntary Termination

We ask employees who wish to end their employment relationship to notify us at least four
weeks in advance of their intended departure. Please give notice in writing to your manager and
Human Resources. Proper notice generally allows us sufficient time to reassign your job
responsibilities, calculate all accrued overtime (if applicable) and to prepare your final paycheck and
other necessary paperwork.

Involuntary Termination

Although HOPE Program hopes that our relationships with employees are long-term and mutually
rewarding, the Company reserves the right to end the employment relationship at any time.
Involuntary termination from employment may be considered a dismissal (for inadequate
performance or misconduct), or a layoff. Layoff is defined as termination for non-disciplinary or
non-performance related reasons.

For a dismissal due to inadequate performance or misconduct or layoff, HOPE Program may
follow one of three options:

e Option 1: You may be terminated without notice at any time.

e Option 2: You may be given advance notice of the separation date and work up to and
including the separation date with full pay.

e Option 3: You may be given advance notice of the separation date, but be asked not to
report to work during the period between notification of termination and the
separation date. If this is the case, you will receive full pay up to, and including the
separation date.

Re-Employment

If you voluntarily leave HOPE Program and give at least four weeks’ notice, or if you are
terminated as a layoff, you may be eligible for rehire. If you are rehired by HOPE Program, your
hire date will be adjusted accordingly, and HOPE Program benefits will be based on your new hire
date.

Final Paychecks

HOPE Program will prepare your final paycheck and, with your written permission, deduct from
that any money you may owe the Company. You will usually receive your final paycheck on your
last day worked.
