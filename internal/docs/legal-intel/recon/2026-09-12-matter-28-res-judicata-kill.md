## 4. Element-by-element kill

### 4.1 No final judgment — wins today’s motion; does not stay dead

An order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend **is not a final judgment** and **is not appealable**. The appeal is from the ensuing judgment of dismissal. Until that judgment is entered, the ruling is not res judicata and the trial court may still change it.

- *Berri v. Superior Court*, 43 Cal.2d 856, 860 (1955) — order sustaining demurrer is not the final judgment; trial court may reconsider before judgment; “there is no appeal from a ruling on a demurrer but only from the ensuing judgment.” CourtListener: FOUND (`43 Cal. 2d 856`).
- *Valvo v. University of Southern California*, 67 Cal.App.3d 887, 891–92 & n.3 (1977) — “Until entry of judgment, a ruling on a demurrer is not res judicata and may be reconsidered and changed by the trial court.” (quoting *Berri*).
- *Beazell v. Schrader*, 59 Cal.2d 577, 579–80 (1963) — same appealability rule; CCP §§ 581d, 904.1.
- CCP § 581d — a **dismissal** ordered by the court, in a signed written order filed in the action, constitutes a judgment. An order that only **sustains a demurrer** is not a dismissal.

*Kay v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes*, 504 F.3d 803, 808 (9th Cir. **2007**) (not 2008), quoting *Franklin & Franklin v. 7-Eleven Owners for Fair Franchising*, 85 Cal.App.4th 1168, 1174 (2000): California finality does not attach “until an appeal from the trial court judgment has been exhausted or the time to appeal has expired.” CourtListener: FOUND (`504 F.3d 803`).

Two consequences the earlier memos blurred:

1. **CRC 8.104’s 60-day clock runs from a judgment**, not from an order sustaining a demurrer. If there is no judgment, Monday, September 14 is not an appeal deadline. It is a fallback date *only if* someone treats the formal order as a 581d judgment. Do not file a premature appeal of a non-appealable order just to keep *Kay* open. That would treat the order as if it were a judgment.
2. **Finality can ripen while Matter 28 is pending.** Matter 28 was filed July 20 — five days after the July 15 order — so even on their best story there was no finality at filing. If they later obtain a judgment and appeal time expires, *Kay* can attach mid-case. That is why this element is a snapshot, not a permanent bar.

**Nunc pro tunc risk (watch, do not invite).** Van Dine can move in Dept. 10A for entry of a judgment of dismissal. *Berri* itself says that after a demurrer is sustained WOLA, “the entry of a judgment of dismissal follows as a matter of course” if someone asks. Do **not** request that judgment. If they move, oppose any recitation that converts the court’s “jurisdictional requirement” holding into a merits dismissal, and tell Mag. Cisneros in the opposition that a later 581d judgment still would not be on the merits (§ 4.2).

### 4.2 Not on the merits — this is the once-and-for-all kill

California and the Ninth Circuit both treat a dismissal for **prematurity / failure to exhaust / failure of a precondition** as **not** a decision on the merits.

**Best California case.** *Perez v. City of San Bruno*, 27 Cal.3d 875, 882–83 (1980). Caption is *City of San Bruno*, not “Perez, 27 Cal.3d 875” and not *City of Bruno*. A 1974 damages action dismissed as premature (the threatened cessation of water service had not occurred) did not bar a later action after the event happened:

> “we cannot conclude that the judgment of dismissal in the 1974 action was one based on the merits. The instant action, therefore, brought following the occurrence of the event for which relief was sought, is not barred.”

And:

> “Where a judgment is rendered for the defendant on the ground of the non-existence of some fact essential to the plaintiff's cause of action, the plaintiff is not precluded from maintaining an action after such fact has subsequently come into existence.”

(quoting Restatement of Judgments § 54). CourtListener: FOUND (`27 Cal. 3d 875`, `616 P.2d 1287`). Primary opinion text read this pass (Stanford SCOCAL / Justia reprint).

Judge Abdallah’s own words map onto *Perez* without translation: a **jurisdictional requirement**, unmet **prior to the filing of** the FAC. That is prematurity. It is not a holding that the pleaded facts never state a claim.

**Best Ninth Circuit case.** *Heath v. Cleary*, 708 F.2d 1376, 1380 n.4 (9th Cir. 1983). Pin is **1380 n.4**, not 1379 n.4. After *Patsy* reversed the district court’s exhaustion dismissal of a § 1983 case, the panel added — having “note[d]” that neither the district court nor the defendants raised it — that a prior California mandamus denial **for failure to exhaust** also did not claim-preclude the federal action:

> “it appears that California courts would conclude that a denial of a writ of mandamus for failure to exhaust administrative remedies is not a decision on the merits. Cf. *Perez v. City of San Bruno*, 27 Cal.3d 875, 882–83 … (dismissal on ground of prematurity is not decision on the merits for res judicata purposes).”

The footnote then cites Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2) and the federal rule that “the dismissal of an action on the ground of failure to exhaust administrative remedies is not on the merits.” Primary opinion text read this pass (F2 reporter). CourtListener: FOUND (`708 F.2d 1376`).

Caveat, say it out loud: *Heath* n.4 is a prediction of California law in a footnote, on mandamus facts. It has never been contradicted by the California Supreme Court. *Perez* is the California holding; *Heath* is the Ninth Circuit applying it to exhaustion. Together they are the load-bearing wall against **both** defendants.

**Supreme Court / Restatement (same idea).** *Costello v. United States*, 365 U.S. 265, 284–88 (1961) — a dismissal for failure to satisfy a precondition (there, the affidavit of good cause) is a dismissal “for lack of jurisdiction” within the Rule 41(b) exception and is **not** an adjudication on the merits. “In order that a judgment may constitute a bar … the point of controversy must be … determined on its merits.” CourtListener: FOUND (`365 U.S. 265`).

Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2):

> A valid and final personal judgment for the defendant, which rests on the prematurity of the action or on the plaintiff's failure to satisfy a precondition to suit, does not bar another action by the plaintiff instituted after the claim has matured, or the precondition has been satisfied, unless a second action is precluded by operation of the substantive law.

That is the sentence to put in the proposed order.

**Why *Goddard* does not save them.** *Goddard v. Security Title Ins. & Guar. Co.*, 14 Cal.2d 47, 52–53 (1939) says a judgment after a general demurrer *may* be on the merits “where the demurrer sets up the failure of the facts alleged to establish a cause of action.” CourtListener: FOUND (`14 Cal. 2d 47`). Two answers:

1. *Goddard* itself: “even a judgment on general demurrer may not be on the merits, for the defects set up may be technical or formal.” Exhaustion as a jurisdictional precondition is that class of defect. *Perez* is the later, specific application.
2. Courts look to the **actual basis of decision**, not the caption of a defense-drafted form. The tentative the court affirmed, and the minute order, state the ground: jurisdictional requirement, unmet before the FAC. A § 430.10(e) recital is how California styles an exhaustion demurrer. It does not convert prematurity into a merits adjudication.

**Why *Keidatz* does not travel.** *Keidatz v. Albany*, 39 Cal.2d 826 (1952): a demurrer judgment bars a second action only if the new complaint does not cure the defect, or if the ground is “equally applicable” to the second action. The Matter 5 ground is “failed to exhaust **prior to the filing of his FAC**.” That finding is temporally capped. It is not a holding that these facts never state a claim. It is also **not equally applicable** to a federal complaint that does not require exhaustion of state administrative remedies. *Patsy v. Board of Regents*, 457 U.S. 496, 516 (1982) — “exhaustion of state administrative remedies should not be required as a prerequisite to bringing an action pursuant to § 1983.” *Heath* applied *Patsy* as “a flat rule without exception.” Cite-check *Patsy* pin before filing (existence is not in doubt; this pass did not reopen the U.S. Reports PDF).

### 4.3 Smith is not a party — permanent as to her, unless they prove control

*DKN Holdings*, 61 Cal.4th at 824–26: claim preclusion runs only against the same party or a privy. Joint-and-several liability, partnership, and cosigner status do **not** create privity. *Dillard v. McKnight*, 34 Cal.2d 209, 214 (1949) (partners). CourtListener: FOUND (`61 Cal. 4th 813`).

*Taylor v. Sturgell*, 553 U.S. 880, 892–95 (2008) disapproved “virtual representation.” Six categories only. CEO is not one of them. CourtListener: FOUND (`553 U.S. 880`).

**Honest caveat the five-track memo underplayed.** *DKN* also collected California cases that **do** treat derivative liability (corporation and its employees) as privity for some preclusion purposes: *Sartor v. Superior Court*, 136 Cal.App.3d 322, 328 (1982); *Lippert v. Bailey*, 241 Cal.App.2d 376, 382 (1966). Van Dine can cite those for Smith-as-CEO. Distinguish, do not ignore:

- Those cases presuppose a **merits** judgment against the corporation. Matter 5 has none.
- They are issue-preclusion / derivative-liability cases, not a rule that a CEO is bound by every order entered against the company.
- *Taylor* category (2) (preexisting substantive legal relationship) is the federal analog; it still requires a qualifying judgment.
- The only *Taylor* category that could still grow is **(4) assumption of control** (*Montana v. United States*, 440 U.S. 154). HOPE_000189 records that Palacios internally noticed Smith of service of the Matter 5 complaint. Notice is not control. Control means hired or paid counsel, directed strategy, or ran the defense. That is a fact issue. It is not pleadable from CEO title. It is the one Smith door that a 12(b)(6) denial does not lock forever.

For **HOPE**, this element is gone. That is why § 4.2 has to be in the federal order. A later judgment plus *Boeken* would otherwise still threaten the company.

### 4.4 Same claim (*Boeken*) — do not fight this as the lead

*Boeken v. Philip Morris USA, Inc.*, 48 Cal.4th 788, 797–98 (2010): the cause of action is “the right to obtain redress for a harm suffered, regardless of the specific remedy sought or the legal theory.” CourtListener: FOUND (`48 Cal. 4th 788`). *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.*, 28 Cal.4th 888, 904 (2002) is the same primary-rights rule.

*Gonzales v. California Dep't of Corr.*, 739 F.3d 1226, 1233–34 (9th Cir. 2014), and *Furnace v. Giurbino*, 838 F.3d 1019, 1024 (9th Cir. 2016), reject “§ 1983 = different primary right.” If the other elements were ever met, *Migra* would carry the never-litigated federal theories with the state ones.

**Do not brief the federal-right escape as settled law.** The Bane-on-Bane overlap is the most exposed. Win on judgment, merits, and (for Smith) parties. Mention *Boeken* only to show the court you have read their best case.

### 4.5 Issue preclusion is a different, smaller weapon

The only issue “actually litigated and necessarily decided” (*Arizona v. California*, 530 U.S. 392, 414 (2000)) is whether plaintiff exhausted **before filing the state FAC**. That issue is immaterial to § 1983 (*Patsy*; *Heath*), RICO, and the Bane Act. *MIB, Inc. v. Superior Court*, 106 Cal.App.3d 228, 232–33 (1980), even gives jurisdictional facts limited issue-preclusive effect “although the dismissal is not res judicata on the merits of the underlying cause of action.” Concede the narrow issue; it does not move a federal claim.

Matter 6 (E.D. Cal. 2:26-cv-00195, first Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) dismissal without prejudice, June 1, 2026) is a preclusion nullity. *Semtek Int'l Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.*, 531 U.S. 497, 505–09 (2001). It becomes a merits bar only if this action is voluntarily dismissed. **Never voluntarily dismiss the N.D. Cal. case.** Matter 6 is still undisclosed on this docket. Disclose it. Do not hide it and do not dismiss around it.

## 5. The holding that kills it once and for all

A 12(b)(6) denial that says only “no judgment has been entered” is a stay of execution. They can cure it in Stockton.

Ask for a written finding in the alternative, in this order:

1. **No final judgment.** The July 15 order sustains a demurrer. It is not a CCP § 581d judgment of dismissal. Under *Berri* and *Valvo*, it is not res judicata.
2. **Even if a judgment of dismissal is later entered in STK-CV-UF-2026-0001032**, the state court’s stated ground — failure to satisfy a jurisdictional precondition unmet “prior to the filing of” the FAC — is a dismissal for prematurity / failure of a precondition. Under *Perez*, *Heath* n.4, *Costello*, and Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2), that is **not** a decision on the merits and **does not bar** this action. The formal order’s generic § 430.10(e) recital does not recharacterize the court’s own words.
3. **Shannon Smith was not a Matter 5 party** and is not in privity by CEO title (*DKN*; *Taylor*). Claim preclusion does not run against her.
4. **Issue preclusion**, if reached, is limited to whether administrative remedies were exhausted before the state FAC, an issue that is not an element of the federal claims (*Patsy*).

Paragraph 2 is the sentence that survives nunc pro tunc. Paragraph 3 is the sentence that survives as to Smith even if paragraph 2 were ever lost. Paragraph 1 wins the motion filed in September. All three belong in the proposed order.
