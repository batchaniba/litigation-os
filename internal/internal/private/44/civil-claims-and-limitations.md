# Civil claims, damages, and limitations

## Potential theories to test

Maryland has abolished interspousal tort immunity. Marriage alone therefore does
not categorically bar one spouse's tort claim against the other. That does not
establish liability. Counsel should test incident-specific facts against, as
applicable, assault, battery, false imprisonment, intentional infliction of
emotional distress, trespass/property damage, conversion, fraud or concealment,
stalking-related statutory remedies, and other Maryland causes of action.

Each theory requires its own act, elements, defendant, accrual date, proof,
causation, injury, defenses, and remedy. “Severe torment since 2012” is an intake
description, not a complaint allegation ready for filing.

## Limitations map

- Courts and Judicial Proceedings § 5-101 supplies a general three-year civil
  limitations period unless another provision applies.
- Courts and Judicial Proceedings § 5-105 supplies a one-year period for civil
  assault, libel, and slander.
- Other torts, statutory claims, tolling rules, discovery rules, disability,
  fraudulent concealment, continuing-wrong arguments, and claim preclusion must
  be analyzed separately.
- Do not assume that ongoing marriage, separation, fear, delayed disclosure, or
  continuing emotional injury automatically tolls limitations.
- Record the date of each act, when injury was known, why action was delayed,
  any concealment, incapacity, threats, or control, and all periods of
  separation or renewed conduct.

Historic incidents may remain relevant to credibility, context, fear, damages,
protective relief, or a pattern even when a standalone damages claim is time
barred. Admissibility and permissible use remain court questions.

## Damages and recovery discipline

Track separately:

- medical and mental-health expense;
- lost earnings and earning capacity;
- relocation, security, repair, replacement, and property loss;
- noneconomic injury supported by admissible proof;
- restitution, compensation-program payments, insurance, support, or property
  awards that offset or duplicate a loss;
- punitive damages only if Maryland law, proof, and any insurance/public-policy
  limits permit them;
- fees and costs only where a contract or statute authorizes them.

Before a demand, assess collectability and actual insurance. Do not assume a
homeowner, umbrella, professional, or other policy covers intentional domestic
conduct; never invent policy language.

## Evidence-to-element matrix

For each proposed count, create a row with: act/date; element; source; witness;
admissibility issue; limitations rule; defense; compensatory loss; overlapping
remedy; and confidence. A claim does not move from “screening” to “candidate”
until every required element and time bar has an evidence-backed answer.
