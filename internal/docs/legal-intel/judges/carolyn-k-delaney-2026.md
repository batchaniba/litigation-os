# Dossier: Chief U.S. Magistrate Judge Carolyn K. Delaney — 2026 Docket Audit

**Who:** Carolyn K. Delaney, Chief United States Magistrate Judge, Eastern District of
California, Sacramento (501 I Street). Appointed magistrate judge August 1, 2011; became
**Chief Magistrate Judge in 2024**. Stanford J.D. 1988; former Assistant U.S. Attorney in the
Eastern District (1998–2008, 2009–2011), with a stint at DOJ's U.S. Embassy in Ankara. A 2024
*Daily Journal* judicial profile describes her as "known for her compassion and directness."
Source: [court bio](https://www.caed.uscourts.gov/caednew/index.cfm/judges/all-judges/5055/united-states-magistrate-judge-carolyn-k-delaney-ckd/)

**Method honesty:** Sampled audit, not a census — built from ~20 distinct matters with
observable 2026 activity (signed orders and F&Rs on Justia/CourtListener, the *Daily Journal*
profile, her court bio page). The UPS matter (2:26-cv-02847-DAD-CKD) is not publicly indexed
yet and is carried as repo-verified.

- *Ortiz v. High Desert State Prison*, 2:26-cv-01201-CKD (5/14/26): IFP granted, complaint

  referee.

- **ICE habeas grants (3/27/26, 4/17/26):** denied government's MTDs and ordered bond

## Observable patterns — for a pro se plaintiff opposing a Rule 12 motion in an employment civil-rights case

1. **She applies Twombly/Iqbal rigorously but almost always grants leave to amend.** The
   Duncan F&R is the template: a long, careful 12(b)(6) analysis ending in dismissal *with*
   leave to amend and a 30-day deadline. Denial of leave is reserved for incurable defects.
   For the UPS MTD, the realistic worst case is dismissal with leave to amend, not with
   prejudice.
2. **She decides Rule 12 motions on the papers more often than not.** The Duncan MTD was
   "submitted without hearing"; the 2/12/26 FTCA order vacated the hearing outright. An
   October 7 hearing date does not guarantee oral argument — she may vacate it and rule on
   the briefs. The opposition brief must be self-sufficient; do not save your best point for
   the hearing.
3. **She is strict about opposition procedure.** Her orders routinely warn that failure to
   file an opposition or statement of non-opposition is deemed non-opposition and an
   independent ground for Rule 41(b) dismissal. The White case shows she will recommend
   dispositive relief against a plaintiff who files a substantively empty opposition. File a
   real opposition, on time, addressing each ground.
4. **Exhaustion defenses get a hard look — and she knows the doctrine cold.** The 2026
   docket is full of exhaustion rulings (PLRA, Title VII/FEHA, immigration). In the
   employment-discrimination F&R she granted leave to amend specifically because exhaustion
   *might* be curable by pleading. If UPS's MTD leans on any exhaustion-adjacent argument,
   expect her to test whether the defect is truly incurable.
5. **She does not do the movant's work, but she does not do the plaintiff's either.** The
   immigration habeas pairs show a judge who follows the law past her sympathies in both
   directions. The *Daily Journal*'s "compassion and directness" line matches the paper:
   liberal construction for pro se pleadings, zero tolerance for procedural non-compliance.
6. **As a referral magistrate, she issues findings and recommendations — Judge Drozd
   decides.** Under E.D. Cal. Local Rule 302(c)(21), her MTD ruling in the UPS case will be an
   F&R with 14-day objections, and Drozd will review de novo. The objections deadline is as
   important as the hearing: it is the last word before the Article III judge.
7. **Settlement posture is active.** She is a regular settlement-conference judge, and the
   $250,000 demand offering an early settlement conference before her fits squarely within
   how this district uses its magistrate judges.

## Bottom line for the UPS matter

Delaney is a rigorous, procedure-strict, amendment-liberal magistrate judge. The closest 2026
analog to the UPS MTD (Duncan, an FCRA/ICRAA/Labor Code case) ended in dismissal *with* 30
days' leave to amend after a thorough Twombly/Iqbal analysis — that is the base case to plan
against. She often rules on the papers, so the written opposition must stand alone, and any
F&R she issues goes to Judge Drozd on 14-day objections.
