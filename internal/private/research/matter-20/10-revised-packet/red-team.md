## Proposed opposition (two pages)

Petitioner asks this Court to convert a denied Form I-918 Supplement B request into a judicial certification, or into a remand that writes the answer for Captain Serrato. The motion should be denied.

**1. Mandate does not pick the certification result.** Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1), uses “shall certify … when” two predicates exist. Those predicates are not established by filing a request. Traditional mandate “will not lie to control an exercise of discretion, i.e., to compel an official to exercise discretion in a particular manner.” (*Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442.) *California Correctional Supervisors Organization, Inc. v. Department of Corrections* (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827, allows compulsion of a particular choice only when “only one choice can be a reasonable exercise of discretion.” That court affirmed denial of the writ. Direct certification is not available on this record. Remand-with-a-script is the same demand in a softer caption.

**2. The November 1 encounter was a DUI investigation, not a qualifying crime.** False imprisonment requires an *unlawful* violation of personal liberty. (Pen. Code, § 236.) Officers found a car on the I-580 shoulder at about 00:55, an empty Mike’s Hard Lemonade can, a subject who said he had come from Oakland toward Stockton, reported HGN clues, a 43-second Romberg with eyelid flutters, and a refused one-leg stand. Form 202 also reports a distinct odor and slurred, confused speech. Blood drawn at 02:38 a.m. tested at 0.073 ± 0.004 g% ethanol. Vehicle Code section 40300.5 authorizes a warrantless DUI arrest on reasonable cause plus a listed circumstance. This Court has already observed that the petition’s own attachments — the empty can, roadside testing, and a PAS refusal — “tend to suggest probable cause,” citing *People v. Thompson* (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, *People v. Wolterman* (1992) 11 Cal.App.4th Supp. 15, and *Espinoza v. Shiomoto* (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 85. (Order, Aug. 29, 2026, p. 3.) A later detention certificate and the absence of a filed complaint do not adjudicate that arrest. (Pen. Code, §§ 849.5, 851.6.)

**3. One disputed FST line does not prove a crime or a certification error.** Petitioner’s own transcript is a litigant-prepared document with bracketed stage directions. Even if the native recording shows Officer Melano declining the walk-and-turn because the shoulder was uneven, the report still contains HGN, Romberg, the can, the freeway stop, the admitted prior driving, and the later blood result. A reporting conflict is a credibility item. It is not false imprisonment, and it is not a showing that Captain Serrato “acted arbitrarily, beyond the bounds of reason or in derogation of the applicable legal standards.” (*CCSO*, *supra*, 96 Cal.App.4th at p. 827.)

**4. PAS refusal is not the arrest theory Respondents admitted.** Answer paragraph 38 admits arrest shortly after refusal of a “chemical alcohol test,” and denies that detention rested solely on refusal of a voluntary test. *People v. Jackson* (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461 held it error to use an advised PAS refusal as consciousness-of-guilt evidence *at a DUI trial*. It did not hold that an officer may not consider the surrounding investigation when deciding whether reasonable cause exists, and the error there was harmless. Subdivision (k)(3)(H) is a guardrail against denying certification for noncooperation in a separate case. The April 20 letter did not invoke it. It is not a hidden ground of this denial.

**5. Helpfulness never arises.** The Court already held the helpfulness presumption immaterial while qualification was absent. (Order, Aug. 29, 2026, p. 4.) Petitioner’s POST complaint and this lawsuit are not assistance to a CHP investigation of its own officers. Answer paragraph 57 denies that any investigating entity requested information. There was nothing to refuse because there was no qualifying-crime investigation.

**6. Extra-record and later papers do not reopen the file.** The motion leans on an October 23 production, a privilege log of May 9 memoranda, and a September lodging confirmation that itself records a rejected proof of service. Informal-action review is not a license to try a DUI prosecution in a certification writ, or to infer bad faith from later-dated withheld documents. (*Western States Petroleum Assn. v. Superior Court* (1995) 9 Cal.4th 559, 576.) The native MP4s were not independently hashed in Petitioner’s research file. Until they are authenticated, Exhibit 5 is advocacy.

**7. The federal screening order is the opposite of a finding.** *Newanforbi v. Serrato*, No. 4:26-cv-05953-ASK (N.D. Cal. June 29, 2026) Dkt. 5, accepted the complaint’s allegations as true under 28 U.S.C. § 1915 and found plausible inferences. That is the screening standard. It is not preclusion, not a merits adjudication, and not a reason for this Court to certify.

**8. Exhaustion and unclean hands.** The denial issued before the 30-day mark (Answer ¶ 71). There is no missed-deadline claim to try. Petitioner’s remaining theory is that a lawful DUI stop, followed by a certification request and parallel federal civil rights case, requires CHP to certify him as a victim of its own officers. That is not a ministerial duty, and it is not a proper use of section 1085.

The Court should deny the motion, or at most, if it finds a purely legal misstatement in the April 20 letter, remand without prescribing the result and without directing execution of Form I-918 Supplement B.

## Patches made after this red-team

1. Memorandum § V — one paragraph stating that Petitioner does not ask the Court to treat HGN, Romberg, the refused one-leg stand, the empty can, or the 0.073 result as disappearing or as “passed.”
2. Memorandum § III — one paragraph stating that Form 202, the authenticated recording, and the verified admissions are incident-record facts on informal-action review, not a collateral DUI trial and not an invitation to infer Serrato’s thoughts from later-dated withheld papers.

No other rewrite was required. The opposition’s strongest point is lawful authority / remaining PC facts. That is why Alternative B (vacate and remand with instructions) remains the ordinary ask, and Alternative A stays behind an express single-lawful-result finding.
