## 6. Map: production vs. the Answer's admissions and denials

**Odor — paper-only, admittedly never voiced on camera.** CHP 202, DS 367, and the affidavit all claim a "strong" or "distinct" odor of alcohol from the vehicle and from the subject's breath and person. The Answer admits (¶¶44, 37, 45) that neither officer stated aloud on the recording that they detected alcohol odor, and neither told the subject his speech was slurred, hesitant, or impaired. The odor claim therefore exists only in writing, never on the contemporaneous recording.

**Slurred speech / confusion — admitted competence vs. denied coherence.** The report says "slurred" and "very confused and disoriented." The Answer admits the subject correctly identified Livermore, read highway signs aloud, spelled his last name, and said he drove from Oakland toward Stockton — but denies the dashcam "captures Petitioner speaking coherently throughout" (¶45) and denies he adequately performed the Modified Romberg (¶37). This is now a video-vs-report fight to be litigated from the recording, not from the paper.

**The cup and the can.** The Answer admits (¶47) the officers identified the open cup's liquid as urine; located an empty can of Mike's Hard Lemonade and stated aloud that it constituted alcohol; and the subject denied consuming it. The CHP 202 nevertheless checks "alcoholic beverage present: YES." The urine cup plus an empty can is a thinner "alcohol present" story than the checked box suggests. The CHP 202 narrative does not mention the cup or the can.

**Arrest timing — one minute after the PAS refusal.** PAS admonition 0111, refused; arrest 0112. The Answer admits (¶38) that shortly after the refusal, Meza-Gonzales placed the subject under arrest for DUI — but denies the detention was "based solely on the lawful refusal of a voluntary test." CHP's six stated arrest factors contain no test result.

**Driving element — admission-based, never observed.** DS 367 leaves "driving observed by this officer" unchecked and checks "driver admitted to driving." The citation declares the violation was "not committed in my presence, declared on information and belief" (§ 40600). The Answer admits the vehicle was not moving when observed (¶34). The § 23152(a) "drove while under the influence" theory therefore rests on the admission he drove from Oakland, the seat adjusted to his stature, and the officers' observations — the § 40300.5 reasonable-cause structure the brief should press.

**The warrant affidavit is thinner than the report.** Sworn 02:30:22, signed by Judge Sekany 02:34:50 (WAR913372). It omits the HGN details, the Romberg numbers (30 estimated as 43 actual, eyelid flutters), the OLS/WAT refusals, and the cup/can entirely, summarizing the FSTs as "did not perform as instructed or demonstrated." The Answer denies (¶40) the affidavit was "in any way false, misleading, or contradictory" — so the affidavit-vs-report comparison is itself contested brief material.

**FST notes destroyed.** The report states the field notes were taken on a 3x5 notepad, transferred to the report, and thrown out upon completion. The CHP 202 is the only record of how the tests went.

**Miranda flag (not a conclusion).** The CHP 202 checks "NOT ADVISED" for the admonition of rights; the interview was conducted prior to the FSTs. Whether any statement is at issue in the writ is a separate question — flagged, not assumed.

**The criminal case died.** No charges filed for DUI on November 1, 2024 (admitted, ¶¶42, 49); detention certificate issued November 5, 2024 quoting Pen. Code §§ 849, 849.5, 851.6; DMV administrative per se suspension of the Class A CDL on January 24, 2025; barred from commercial driving February 20, 2025–February 19, 2026 for refusing a chemical blood test (both admitted, ¶49). Under § 679.10(k), whether the absence of charges or a prosecution is a legitimate basis for denial is a live controversy traveling with Count 1.

**Certification chronology (admitted).** Request April 9, 2026 (Form I-918 Supplement B + memorandum + transcript + arrest-investigation report + detention certificate + exhibits); Lt. Paul acknowledged receipt April 10, 2026 ("under review"); Capt. Serrato denied April 20, 2026; denial issued before the 30-day statutory deadline (¶71). No procedural-delay claim is available; the fight is on the merits of the denial.

## 8. Substantive read (honest version)

**What helps.** At demurrer, the argument was that CHP's own documents conflicted; Whitman kept Count 1 alive because that is a factual dispute. This production turns the pleaded conflict into a documented one, mostly favorably: the arrest's two most subjective observations (odor, slurred speech) are paper-only and admittedly never voiced on camera; the only test result came back below per se with no drugs; no charges were filed; the stop began as a welfare check on a parked, sleeping man, not a violation stop; and the arrest followed the PAS refusal by one minute. For a § 679.10(g)(1) "shall certify" case whose theory is that an arrest without probable cause was itself the qualifying crime (false imprisonment — no charge or conviction required), that is a real evidentiary spine. It also kills CHP's ability to win on "the record is clear": the denial now has to be defended on a record full of holes, including holes CHP created by withholding its own internal review.

**What cuts the other way — stated plainly.**

- *The writ standard.* The petitioner need not prove false imprisonment beyond a reasonable doubt, but must persuade the court the certification conditions are met and the denial was an abuse of discretion. CHP has a non-frivolous probable-cause argument that survives everything above: red/watery eyes, HGN clues, a Romberg miss, the admission of driving from a party in Oakland wearing a 21+ wristband, and a 0.073 blood result. Thin-but-not-absent is where abuse-of-discretion review gets dangerous for the petitioner.
- *The audio is transcribed — work from it, and test CHP's denial against it.* A verbatim, timestamped transcript of the Camera 1 audio already exists in the repo (window 00:54:36–01:57:10; see the Transcripts section of the video-descriptions file in this PR). CHP admitted the officers never said they smelled alcohol, and the transcript's proof points run the petitioner's way (odor never verbalized, coherent speech, location and signs identified, urine cup) — but the Answer denies the video shows coherent speech "throughout," which reads as CHP believing parts of the audio help them. The transcript text, not the paper, settles that. Narrow gap to close: the transcript ends 01:57:10 while the forward clip runs to ~01:59:51.
- *Helpfulness is the quieter vulnerability, and this production does not touch it.* The Answer denies (¶57) that any investigating entity ever requested information from the petitioner. The production strengthens the qualifying-crime half of § 679.10; it says nothing about the cooperation half.
- *The holes are where the sensitive facts would be.* A 46-minute forward-video gap covering Dublin processing, a back-seat camera that recorded neither picture nor sound, and the two May 9, 2026 internal memos behind a privilege wall. A court deciding a factual dispute on an incomplete record tends to defer, and deference favors CHP.
- *Serrato's denial letter itself was not weighed in this pass.* The letter (Petition Exhibit 1) is the decision under review. If it denies on a ground this production does not reach — e.g., a legal characterization of petitioner as suspect rather than victim — the DUI evidence is only indirectly relevant. The letter should be in hand before drafting.

**Bottom line.** The production does not win the writ by itself. What it does is force the real factual fight the demurrer ruling contemplated, and it gives the petitioner the impeachment material to argue the denial was arbitrary rather than reasoned: the stated basis for the arrest is affirmatively impeached by CHP's own admissions and its own lab, the per se theory is dead, and the most relevant internal assessment (the May 9 video-review memo) is the one thing CHP will not show.
