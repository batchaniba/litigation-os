## Statute the file is being built toward

The subdivisions named for this suit are in Penal Code section **290.46**, the Megan's Law internet-website statute, not in section 290 standing alone. Current text (official legislative site, read November 2, 2026):

- **(j)(1)** A person is authorized to use information disclosed pursuant to this section only to protect a person at risk.
- **(j)(2)** Except as authorized under paragraph (1) or any other provision of law, use of information disclosed pursuant to this section is prohibited for purposes relating to health insurance, insurance, loans, credit, **(E) employment**, education, housing, or benefits, privileges, or services provided by any business establishment.
- **(j)(3)** This section shall not affect authorized access to, or use of, information under, among other statutes, Penal Code sections 11105 and 11105.3 and Labor Code section 432.7.
- **(j)(4)(A)** Use for a purpose other than paragraph (1), or in violation of paragraph (2), makes the user liable for actual damages, and any amount determined by a jury or a court sitting without a jury, not exceeding three times the actual damage and not less than $250, plus attorney's fees, exemplary damages, or a civil penalty not exceeding $25,000.

Those dollar figures are the statute's remedies. They are not a demand, and they are not being sent to anyone.

**(j)(4)(B)** is a pattern-or-practice injunction. One screening file does not establish a pattern. Do not plead a pattern from this record alone.

## What a 290.46(j)(2)(E) / (j)(4)(A) count has to prove

1. The information was disclosed pursuant to section 290.46 (the Megan's Law internet website), rather than only through a court file or another authorized channel saved by (j)(3).
2. A person **used** that information.
3. The use was for employment, and was not a use authorized by (j)(1) or by another law.
4. The damages or statutory remedy that follows from that use.

**GAP — who the "user" is.** *Mendoza v. ADP Screening & Selection Services, Inc.* (California Court of Appeal, 2010) held that a screening company which accessed the Megan's Law website, compiled the information, and furnished it to a prospective employer had not "used" the information within section 290.46. The prohibited use, on that opinion, is the employment decision, not the compilation. The case also went out on anti-SLAPP. Confirm the official reporter pin before citing it in a pleading. Do not name HireRight as the 290.46 user without dealing with *Mendoza*.
