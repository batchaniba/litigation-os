# Matter 37 — Federal Redraft v3 Notes

## Bottom line

The v2 architecture has been materially revised after a hostile limitations audit.

**Opening federal posture:** diversity jurisdiction + Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E)/(j)(4)(A).

**Reserved, not opening counts:** FCRA § 1681b(b)(3)(A) and ICRAA § 1786.40.

The reason is not that those statutes are unimportant. It is that the current record does not support pleading May 29, 2026 as a factual discovery date merely because that was when Plaintiff later pursued the employment-screening legal theory.

## Why the May 29 theory was retired

FCRA § 1681p uses the earlier of two years from discovery or five years from the violation. Ninth Circuit authority treats constructive discovery as sufficient. The question is discovery of facts constituting the violation, not later realization of the legal theory.

Home Depot's 2024 hire ended after a known background-screening process. On the present record, pleading May 29, 2026 as the date Plaintiff “first understood” the statutory omission is not strong enough to carry a federal complaint.

ICRAA § 1786.52 likewise contains a two-year discovery rule and should not be pleaded on a manufactured legal-awareness date.

If future records establish a later factual discovery event that truthfully satisfies those statutes, amendment can be evaluated.

## Why diversity-first is cleaner

Home Depot U.S.A., Inc. is a Delaware corporation with an Atlanta, Georgia principal place of business. Plaintiff is a California citizen.

The § 290.46 remedy permits actual damages, an additional amount tied to actual damages, fees, and exemplary relief. The v3 complaint pleads the amount in controversy in good faith from those non-penalty remedies and expressly does not rely on the civil-penalty option to establish jurisdiction.

## § 290.46 limitations posture

The employment use is alleged to have occurred around August 2024.

The complaint proceeds under CCP § 338(a) for the compensatory/remedial liability created by statute. Because § 290.46(j)(4)(A) also contains a civil-penalty option that could invite a § 340(a) characterization argument, the opening complaint does not seek or rely on that civil penalty.

This avoids pretending California appellate courts have already resolved the exact limitations characterization of every remedy in § 290.46(j)(4)(A).

## Source pleading

The claim remains information-and-belief but has a concrete basis:
- accepted Tracy hire;
- background screen intervened;
- Home Depot then ended onboarding;
- Home Depot public federal-court materials identify First Advantage as its California employment-screening CRA;
- UPS and FedEx same-consumer First Advantage records show that CRA retrieving/reporting Plaintiff's California registry information.

The UPS/FedEx reports remain comparators, not substitutes.

## Lerma / Donaleski

The complaint expressly acknowledges both defense concepts.

- Donaleski: independent lawful sourcing is different from use of § 290.46-disclosed information.
- Lerma: when the transaction-specific report is in the record and contradicts registry sourcing, the report controls.

The v3 draft does **not** say a defendant can never present its report on a Rule 12 motion. It says general public conviction records do not establish the source used in an unproduced Home Depot-specific report.
