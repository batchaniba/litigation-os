## Executive conclusion

The new public-record evidence materially strengthens a **Diamond → Alpine successor / voidable-transfer investigation**.

The strongest verified indicators are no longer family names or general similarity. They now include:

1. **Same-VIN inventory continuity.** A 2021 Peterbilt 579, VIN 1XPBAK8X8MD760791, was advertised under Diamond and later under Alpine with the same mileage (301,262), stock number (N15270-DELIVERY 2 U), price ($37,900), Turlock address, and telephone number.
2. **Dealer-account / website continuity.** Diamond's old website currently routes into Alpine-branded pages. Historical Diamond inventory used a different dealer/CRM account from current Alpine inventory, creating a concrete third-party records target for account migration and bulk inventory transfer.
3. **Physical-location continuity.** Alpine now publicly uses Turlock, Bakersfield, Fontana, Fort Worth, and Gaffney locations historically used by Diamond.
4. **Telephone continuity.** The Turlock number 209-375-7177 appears on both Diamond and Alpine listings for the same inventory.
5. **Personnel continuity.** A 2023 proof of service in Castillo Freight LLC v. Diamond Truck Sales Inc. identifies Sheran Osmani as a “Person Authorized to Accept Service of Process” for Diamond. Current portfolio SOS records identify Sheran Osmani as Alpine's registered agent/corporate officer.
6. **Related-company management bridge.** Public California filing-derived data for Diamond Premier Services Inc., formed August 2, 2023 at 1201 S. Soderquist Rd., identifies Saliman Osmani as CEO, Abdul W. Osmani as CFO, and Sheran Osmani as director/registered agent.
7. **Diamond equipment at Alpine's Bakersfield address.** California DIR's pressure-vessel database lists Diamond Truck Sales Inc. equipment at 7156 Golden State Highway, Bakersfield — Alpine's current Bakersfield principal/agent location.
8. **Creditor / litigation pressure before apparent operational migration.** Diamond had prior business-tort litigation; Merced Superior Court had a January 2026 order-of-examination matter against Diamond; Plaintiff filed the current action February 13, 2026; and a May 2026 employment action names Diamond, Alpine, and Saliman Osmani together.
9. **Similar customer allegations.** BBB and filed-case sources contain multiple allegations of commercial trucks represented as inspected / road-ready / operational followed by immediate serious failures, including a 2026 complaint expressly naming “Diamond Truck Sales / Alpine Truck Sales.” These are leads, not adjudicated facts.

### Present ranking

- **UVTA / transfer investigation:** strongest new theory.
- **Successor / mere-continuation theory:** materially strengthened; consideration and ownership records remain critical.
- **Civil RICO:** credible federalization theory, but **not filing-ready** until Rule 9(b) predicate acts are particularized and the enterprise/person architecture is completed.
- **Existing state filing:** preserve it. Do not voluntarily dismiss merely to obtain a federal caption.

# II. Legal framework

## A. UVTA — Civil Code § 3439.04

Section 3439.04(a)(1) makes a transfer voidable where made with actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud a creditor.

Potentially relevant statutory factors:
- insider transfer;
- retained possession/control;
- concealment;
- debtor already sued or threatened with suit;
- substantially all assets;
- equivalent consideration;
- insolvency;
- transfer near substantial debt.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3439.04.

Current evidence supports investigation of timing, continuity and creditor pressure.

Still missing:
- actual transfer date/instrument;
- consideration;
- shareholder identity;
- financial condition;
- substantially-all-assets issue;
- retained control.

## B. UVTA remedies — § 3439.07

Potential relief:
- avoidance to extent necessary to satisfy claim;
- attachment;
- injunction;
- receiver;
- other equitable relief.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3439.07.

## C. UVTA limitations — § 3439.09

Actual-intent claims:
- four years after transfer, or if later one year after transfer was or reasonably could have been discovered;
- seven-year absolute extinguishment.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3439.09.

A 2025–26 transfer is temporally fresh.

## D. Successor liability

Ray v. Alad Corp., 19 Cal.3d 22 (1977), states the general exceptions:
1. assumption;
2. merger/consolidation;
3. mere continuation;
4. fraudulent transfer to escape debts.

Traditional mere-continuation analysis focuses on:
- inadequate consideration; and/or
- common officers/directors/shareholders.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/19/22.html

Cleveland v. Johnson, 209 Cal.App.4th 1315 (2012), confirms successor liability can be established without a formal asset-purchase agreement where evidence supports a continuation/transfer.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2012/b233762.html

Do not rely only on same location/brand/family relationship. Get consideration, ownership, transfer, customer/inventory movement and business-continuity records.

## E. Civil RICO — proposed federal anchor

18 U.S.C. § 1962(c) prohibits a person associated with an interstate enterprise from conducting the enterprise's affairs through a pattern of racketeering activity.

18 U.S.C. § 1964(c) provides a private action for business/property injury and treble damages.

Sources:
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1962
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1964

### Person-enterprise distinctness

Do not plead Diamond as both the sole RICO person and the sole enterprise.

Working architecture:
- proposed RICO person: Diamond;
- enterprise: a distinct association-in-fact involving Diamond plus other proven participants in the continuing dealer operation.

Living Designs v. DuPont, 431 F.3d 353 (9th Cir. 2005):
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2005/12/05/0216947.pdf

### Rule 9(b)

Mail/wire fraud predicates require particularized circumstances.

Odom v. Microsoft, 486 F.3d 541 (9th Cir. 2007):
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2007/05/03/0435468.pdf

Swartz v. KPMG, 476 F.3d 756 (9th Cir. 2007):
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/05-35167/0535167-2011-02-25.html

Do not lump defendants together. Identify actor roles.

### Current RICO deficit

Before filing, identify for each predicate:
- date;
- actor;
- recipient;
- medium;
- specific representation/omission;
- why false/misleading;
- knowledge;
- payment/property induced;
- interstate mail/wire nexus;
- injury caused.

## F. RICO limitations

Civil RICO uses a four-year period.

The Ninth Circuit's August 12, 2026 Turrey v. Vervent decision reaffirms injury-discovery accrual.

Source:
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/12/25-2135.pdf

Because Plaintiff's truck catastrophically failed shortly after April 2023, Defendants will argue injury discovery occurred in spring 2023.

Treat spring 2027 as the conservative RICO danger zone unless a later injury-discovery date is supportable.
