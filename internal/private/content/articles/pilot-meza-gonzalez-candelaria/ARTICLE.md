# One Stop, Two Cases: Meza and Candelaria

At 12:55 on the morning of November 1, 2024, a California Highway Patrol cruiser pulled onto the dirt shoulder of eastbound Interstate 580 in Livermore and stopped behind a gray Tesla Model Y. Inside, a commercial truck driver had pulled off the freeway to sleep. "It's a rental. Huh," one of the two officers said, before either had stepped out. Within twenty minutes the driver, Brendan Newanforbi, was handcuffed in the back seat of that cruiser. Within four months, a one-year suspension of his Class A license had begun. He alleges it ended his work as a professional driver. The cruiser's camera recorded the hour in between.

That contact produced two civil stories. The first is a roadside case: what two officers did on a freeway shoulder. The second is a hearing-officer case: what happened months later when the Department of Motor Vehicles decided whether a commercial license would be taken away.

Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi, proceeding without a lawyer, has carried those questions through state court, the Eastern District of California, and the Northern District of California. Court captions use both *Newanforbi* and *Nforbi*. This article uses the party name as it appears on each caption.

The live federal dockets are *Nforbi v. Meza-Gonzalez, et al*, No. 4:26-cv-08271-CRB (N.D. Cal.), before Judge Charles R. Breyer, and *Nforbi v. Candelaria et al.*, No. 3:26-cv-08272-RFL (N.D. Cal., San Francisco), which remains with District Judge Rita F. Lin. Dkt. 29 (filed October 30, 2026) recaptioned the Meza-Gonzalez action `-CRB` and directly assigned it to Judge Breyer. Earlier captions in Alameda County, Sacramento County, and the Eastern District were dismissed without prejudice or were not pursued. Those closings are not rulings on the November 2024 encounter or the February 2025 administrative decision.

What follows is a record of the filed papers and the public orders. It is not a finding that anyone violated the Constitution, and it does not forecast either case.

## What the dashcam recorded

The account below follows a timestamped transcript of the CHP recording in Case No. RJ68845. Newanforbi prepared it himself and signed it under penalty of perjury, and it was filed as Exhibit A in his cases. It is his transcription, not a stipulated or official CHP transcript. The speaker labels and the bracketed descriptions of what the video shows are his. Times are the recording's clock. The recording window is 00:54:36 to 01:57:10 — about 62 minutes, not the two hours later pleaded in the First Amended Complaints.

**The knock.** At 00:55:37 the cruiser stopped behind the Tesla and its audio switched on. Officers Santiago Meza-Gonzalez and Jose Melano walked up the passenger side on the dirt shoulder, flashlights in hand, and shone them through the windows. "I think it's booze," Melano said. Then: "It wasn't here earlier when we passed by." Meza-Gonzalez knocked on the front passenger window and kept knocking. "Oh look at this," Melano said. At 00:56:40 he said, "Open the window." At 00:56:43, three seconds later, the transcript records that the passenger door was opened by Officer Melano.

"Hello." "Uh-huh." "Are you okay?" "Yeah, we good." Asked where he was coming from, Newanforbi said, "We're coming from uh, uh, a party." Meza-Gonzalez noted that the Tesla's dash clock read 12:57 a.m. and told him the shoulder "is not a parking lot." Newanforbi said he had been stopped there "like 5-7 minutes." Asked how much he had had to drink, he answered, "Zero."

**The cup.** "What's in that cup right there?" Meza-Gonzalez asked. "Don't drink it," Newanforbi said. "Yeah don't drink it." Melano took the cup. "Is it water? No this is piss. This is pee." Newanforbi: "I don't know what that is." Melano: "I'm telling you it's piss. It smells like piss." When Melano held the cup out, Meza-Gonzalez leaned back: "No I'm not gonna smell that." "OK. Leave it there. Put it back," Melano said. Five seconds later, Meza-Gonzalez asked Newanforbi to step out of the car.

**The can.** Newanforbi walked to the patrol car, gave his name, and spelled it: "NEW-WAN-FOR-BI." While Meza-Gonzalez walked him there, the transcript records, Melano opened a rear passenger door of the Tesla and searched. Melano then came over holding an empty can. "Did you drink that?" Meza-Gonzalez asked. "No." "You haven't drank anything since you've been parked?" "No."

**Where he was.** "You know where you're at right now?" "Right now, Livermore." Newanforbi read the exit sign about fifty yards ahead, "Isabella Avenue, Portola Avenue," and Meza-Gonzalez said, "Correct." Asked whether he had been driving, he said, "I was in the driver's seat, yes sir." He explained why he had stopped: "You know I'm a truck driver. So when I feel tired, when I feel fatigued, whenever I am unable to continue to drive that truck, I'm gonna pull over to the side of the road, try to get a nap. … How quickly can I move over to the side of the road, catch some shut-eye, wake up and continue on to finish the route?"

**The tests.** Meza-Gonzalez ran an eye test, then a test in which Newanforbi tilted his head back, closed his eyes, and estimated thirty seconds. When Meza-Gonzalez described a one-leg stand, Newanforbi pushed back: "It seems as though I'm passing them and then the level of difficulty just keeps increasing and increasing and increasing. I just wanna know if I've passed your test." "Yeah there's not pass or fail," Melano said. Meza-Gonzalez: "I go through all of them regardless if it's a pass or what you think is a pass or fail." Newanforbi said he did not know if he could balance on one leg. Meza-Gonzalez told him, "It's fine if you don't. You don't have to." Newanforbi agreed to try a walking test: "I mean I think we can do that." Melano cut in: "It's kind of sketch here bro. … it's on the side of the freeway and it's like uneven ground. I wouldn't do that one." "Alright then," said Meza-Gonzalez.

**"I don't consent to that."** At 01:10:33 Meza-Gonzalez asked him to take a preliminary alcohol screening test. "You may refuse to take this test," he said. "So you're saying that I am required or not required?" "You're not required to do this." "And if I choose not to consent to it?" "If you choose to not consent to it then it'll be up to my discretion whether I put you under arrest for DUI or not." The officers readied the device. "Yes or no?" At 01:11:15 Newanforbi said, "I don't consent to that." At 01:11:16 Meza-Gonzalez said, "OK. Brendan. Alright then. I'm gonna have you just turn around please. Place your hands behind your back." By 01:11:33 he was putting on the handcuffs.

**Breath or blood.** Meza-Gonzalez searched him, put him in the back seat of the cruiser, and attached the handcuffs to the vehicle frame. He then asked whether Newanforbi would take a breath test or a blood test at the jail. "I don't know. It seems like torture." "Right now you're not refusing," Meza-Gonzalez told him, "you're just saying that basically you don't want to choose." He read the chemical-test admonition and explained that a refusal would bring an administrative suspension of at least a year, "even if" Newanforbi was sober. "Even as a truck commercial driver, that's what you're willing to do, force the DMV's hand?" Newanforbi asked. "I'm not forcing the DMV to do it, that's their rule." At 01:22:15 Meza-Gonzalez asked, "So you're refusing to take a test?" The transcript records no answer before his "OK." The officers compared notes. Melano: "We just have to write what time we last passed him here because he wasn't here earlier." Meza-Gonzalez: "But he said he was here for seven minutes." Then Melano: "Yeah you want to start your warrant?"

At 01:36:47 Melano returned to read the implied-consent advisement. Newanforbi asked if it was Miranda. "No, no, no," Melano said. "Miranda rights we only give when we still wanna ask you questions about the investigation. … We already have everything we need right?" If he did not choose a test, Melano told him, "we're gonna get it anyways. It says it's going to be with a warrant and you're gonna automatically lose your license." "So which one are we doing sir?" … "Neither?" "Yep." "OK so we're getting a warrant."

**The last conversation.** Before the cruiser pulled away, the officers wondered aloud whether the Tesla had an alternator. Newanforbi explained: "I don't think electric vehicles have alternators. … Just like your phone gets charged." At 01:52:05 the cruiser left the shoulder. The recording ends at 01:57:10 with its arrival at the Dublin CHP transfer.

## What the complaints allege

The operative First Amended Complaint in the Meza-Gonzalez action (N.D. Cal. Dkt. 5, filed August 17, 2026) and the operative First Amended Complaint in the Candelaria action (N.D. Cal. Dkt. 6, filed the same day) describe the same starting point.

They allege that in the early morning of November 1, 2024, CHP Officers Santiago Meza-Gonzalez (badge 023295) and Jose Melano (badge 023054) treated a contact with a stationary vehicle, engine off, as a driving-under-the-influence investigation (CHP Case No. RJ68845 / CAD No. 241101GG0064).

The complaints allege a warrantless opening of a passenger door, a search of the interior, and an arrest under Vehicle Code § 23152(a). They allege that the officers themselves identified the only liquid in the car as urine, and that no officer said aloud that he smelled alcohol on the plaintiff's breath or person. The transcript also records an empty can, which Officer Melano brought from a rear-door search before the arrest. They further allege that a blood-draw warrant was later obtained and blood was drawn, and that a later administrative form designated a chemical-test refusal. On the recording, asked to choose breath or blood, he answered "Neither?" with "Yep."

The officer's account survives in the DMV decision that later suspended the license. It records that Meza-Gonzalez found Newanforbi "asleep" on the shoulder, knocked on the passenger window, and made contact "through the open right passenger side door". It records that he observed "Slurred Speech, Red and/or Watery Eyes, and Odor of Alcohol", that he "smelled the strong odor of an alcoholic beverage emitting from Respondent's breath and person while speaking to him in front of the patrol vehicle", and that Newanforbi performed "unsatisfactorily" on the eye test and the modified Romberg. It also records an arrest at 1:12 a.m. and Newanforbi's "admission to driving."

The Meza-Gonzalez complaint alleges that after the arrest, Meza-Gonzalez signed a DS-367 Officer's Statement and a CHP Arrest-Investigation Report under penalty of perjury. It alleges those papers were used to obtain the blood-draw warrant, to begin the license suspension, and to refer charges to the District Attorney. It alleges four entries that the recording does not support: a "strong distinct odor of an alcoholic beverage," which neither officer says aloud on the recording; speech described as "slurred"; a statement that he "seemed very confused and disoriented"; and a checked box reading "Beverage Present: Yes." The complaint concedes that odor, speech, and orientation are "experiential observations" an officer need not voice. It alleges that the recording, which captures coherent speech, correct orientation, and a cup identified as urine, contradicts them. A magistrate issued the warrant, and the complaint alleges that blood was drawn at the CHP Dublin Area office. The officers have not admitted these allegations, and their motion to dismiss (Dkt. 22) is pending.

Four days later, on November 5, 2024, the complaints allege, Dublin CHP issued a Detention Certificate under Penal Code § 849(b)(1) (Dublin CHP-01991; CHP File No. 202400898) reciting that the event was “a detention only, not an arrest,” for insufficient grounds to make a criminal complaint. The complaints allege that the Alameda County District Attorney declined to file charges.

Those are pleading allegations. The officers have not conceded them. A motion to dismiss in the Meza-Gonzalez action remains pending. The transcript is the plaintiff's own transcription of the CHP recording. The complaints are his allegations. Neither has been tested by a court.

## The legal issues, as the complaints frame them

The two live cases split one night into two legal problems.

### The roadside case: *Nforbi v. Meza-Gonzalez, et al*

The Northern District First Amended Complaint names Santiago Meza-Gonzalez and Jose Melano, each individually, plus Doe defendants. It does not name the State of California or the Highway Patrol. The six live counts, as recorded in the matter graph, are all under 42 U.S.C. § 1983: unlawful entry; unlawful search; unlawful arrest; *Franks* / judicial deception; fabrication of evidence; and First Amendment retaliation tied to a preliminary-alcohol-screening refusal.

The earlier Alameda caption, *Newanforbi v. Meza-Gonzalez et al.*, No. 26CV168913, had pleaded false imprisonment, battery, and a Bane Act claim under Civil Code § 52.1. That entire action was dismissed without prejudice on August 15, 2026. Those state-law theories, and the State/CHP defendant, are not in the live Northern District complaint.

A still-earlier federal caption, *Newanforbi v. CHP et al.*, No. 2:25-cv-01460-DC-CSK (E.D. Cal., filed May 27, 2025), had pleaded Fourth Amendment, due-process, and *Monell* theories against the State, the CHP, and the two officers. It was also dismissed without prejudice on August 15, 2026, after the Northern District complaint had already been docketed.

### The hearing-officer case: *Nforbi v. Candelaria et al.*

The Candelaria complaints describe a second proceeding. They allege that a DS-367 form and an arrest report triggered DMV Administrative Per Se Case No. 34739340; that Hearing Officer Diana Candelaria held a Microsoft Teams hearing on January 24, 2025; and that on February 11, 2025, she issued a Notification of Findings and Decision sustaining a one-year Class A commercial-license suspension from February 20, 2025, through February 19, 2026, plus a reissue fee and an SR-22 financial-responsibility requirement.

Before any hearing, the complaint alleges, DMV issued a temporary license valid "through the outcome of hearing" that barred him from "driving commercially". On December 9, 2024, he filed a five-page written response identifying the dashcam evidence and the Detention Certificate. He also offered the certificate and a transcript of the recording into the administrative record. The DMV's own notice described the hearing officer's job in one sentence: "The Hearing Officer will develop the facts and render a final decision on your matter."

The hearing was held over Microsoft Teams at 9:00 a.m. on January 24, 2025. Newanforbi testified under penalty of perjury. The complaint alleges that Candelaria marshaled and offered the Department's exhibits, ruled on his objections to them, and then decided the case. In the complaint's words, that crossed "from neutral fact development into advocacy." Her written decision summarizes his contention that the officer's claimed odor "from the passenger side of the vehicle" was "highly implausible." It then holds that, because the officer had certified the DS-367 under penalty of perjury, "It is presumed official duty has been regularly performed. No evidence was presented to the contrary." The complaint alleges that the decision does not mention the Detention Certificate or the dashcam contradictions. The suspension ran from February 20, 2025, through February 19, 2026. Defendants have answered the amended complaint (Dkt. 26). That answer was not in the portfolio when this draft was prepared.

The live First Amended Complaint names Diana Candelaria individually and Steve Gordon, Director of the California DMV, in his official capacity. The four live counts are: denial of an impartial adjudicator; denial of a fair hearing; declaratory relief under 28 U.S.C. §§ 2201–2202 seeking vacatur of APS Decision 34739340; and injunctive / *Ex parte Young* relief against Director Gordon.

The earlier Eastern District caption, *Newanforbi v. Candelaria*, No. 2:26-cv-00983-DC-CSK, pleaded a similar due-process theory, including a claim that Vehicle Code §§ 13353 and 13353.2 require a “lawful arrest” predicate and that the November 5 Detention Certificate removed that predicate before the January hearing. Magistrate Judge Chi Soo Kim issued findings and recommendations on June 15, 2026. The district judge never ruled on them. Plaintiff filed a notice of voluntary dismissal without prejudice on August 15, 2026.

A parallel state petition, *Newanforbi v. Candelaria, et al.*, Sacramento Superior Court No. 26WM000135, sought a writ of administrative mandate under Code of Civil Procedure § 1094.5 and Vehicle Code § 13559. It was filed May 18, 2026. A fee-waiver request was denied as incomplete, a later hearing did not go forward, and the filing-fee deadline lapsed around July 27, 2026. The portfolio records the petition as abandoned. The APS theory continued in the federal captions.

Published California decisions that the pleadings invoke — including *California DUI Lawyers Ass'n v. DMV*, 77 Cal. App. 5th 517 (2022), and *Knudsen v. DMV*, 101 Cal. App. 5th 186 (2024) — address the structure of DMV APS hearings. Whether they control this record is for the assigned judge.

## How a self-represented litigant moved the cases

The procedure is the part of these files that helps another person who may someday file alone. It can be described without arguing who should win.

### First filings, then a forum change

The first federal roadside case was filed in the Eastern District on May 27, 2025, as *Newanforbi v. CHP et al.* The first state roadside case was accepted in Alameda County Superior Court on February 4, 2026, after a January 30 attempt was rejected for pleading-paper format — an ordinary clerk problem that can stop a self-represented filing for days.

In Alameda, the plaintiff obtained a service extension, served the State/CHP and Officer Melano in March 2026, and met and conferred with a Deputy Attorney General about an anticipated demurrer. On April 14, 2026, the parties filed a stipulation staying the Alameda action pending the concurrent Eastern District case. The stay suspended discovery and deadlines. It did not decide any claim.

The first federal APS case, *Newanforbi v. Candelaria*, is recorded as filed April 27, 2026, in the Eastern District. The jurisdiction catalog notes that a later dismissal paper recites an earlier initiation date of March 19, 2026. A First Amended Complaint adding Director Gordon was filed the same month. The Sacramento mandate petition followed on May 18.

In August 2026 the plaintiff changed forums. He signed Northern District intake packets on August 7 under the *Nforbi* caption. The clerk docketed both new cases on August 10: Meza-Gonzalez as No. 3:26-cv-08271-PHK (Magistrate Judge Peter H. Kang) and Candelaria as No. 3:26-cv-08272-LJC (Magistrate Judge Lisa J. Cisneros). Five days later he dismissed the Eastern District and Alameda predecessors without prejudice — Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 41(a)(1)(A)(i) in the federal cases, Code of Civil Procedure § 581(b)(1) and (c) in Alameda.

A voluntary dismissal without prejudice is not a win and not a loss on the facts. It is a docketing choice. It also leaves a sequence later defendants may argue about: the Northern District complaints were docketed before the Eastern District notices of dismissal were filed. The new complaints disclosed the planned dismissals. Whether that sequence matters is for the courts.

### Amendment, service, and local rules

Both Northern District cases were amended almost immediately. In Meza-Gonzalez the clerk stamped a First Amended Complaint on August 14 (Dkt. 6) and another on August 17 (Dkt. 5). Dkt. 5 is operative. In Candelaria the same two dates produced Dkt. 5 and Dkt. 6; Dkt. 6 is operative. A later Candelaria notice (Dkt. 10) said so on the docket.

Service was not one event. Summonses issued August 10. Sheriff requests went out August 13. Candelaria and Gordon were returned as served August 14 at DMV Legal Affairs in Sacramento (Dkt. 7). Officer Melano's proof of service was filed August 17 (Dkt. 5-1). Officer Meza-Gonzalez required substitute service on August 27 and a later Rule 4(l) return (Dkt. 16 / 16-1, filed September 14). An administrative motion asking a Deputy Attorney General to accept service for Officer Meza-Gonzalez was filed September 3 (Dkt. 13). That motion is a public filing. It is not, by itself, an appearance by the officer.

Northern District practice also meant Civil Local Rule 3-15 certifications (Meza-Gonzalez Dkt. 15; Candelaria Dkt. 16), magistrate-jurisdiction forms, and a name-correction problem. The clerk's office would not merge two PACER name forms into one e-filing account while the captions differed. In Candelaria the parties stipulated (Dkt. 18, September 14); Magistrate Judge Cisneros granted the request (Dkt. 19, September 16). The Meza-Gonzalez caption remains *Nforbi*.

## What a reader can take from the public record

These points do not depend on who is right about November 1.

One incident can generate more than one civil case because the legal questions differ. A Fourth Amendment claim against arresting officers is not the same lawsuit as a due-process claim against a later hearing officer and an agency director. Readers tend to merge them into one story. Treating them as one caption is a choice, with two filing fees, two service campaigns, and two assignment tracks.

Forum and caption are not trivia. The same person appears as *Newanforbi* in the Eastern District and Alameda papers and as *Nforbi* in the Northern District refiles. The clerk's office treated those as different docket names. A later stipulation corrected one docket.

Amending quickly is ordinary under Rule 15 and still has consequences. A defendant who answers the original complaint may be answering the wrong paper. That is what the Candelaria strike order addressed. Identify the operative pleading on the docket, and say so if the numbering is confusing.

Service on individual officers is slower than service on an agency mail room. The Candelaria defendants were served at DMV Legal Affairs within four days. One of the two CHP officers required substitute service, a diligence declaration, and a later mailing. Service is complete only when the rules say it is, and the response clock runs from that date.

A magistrate judge's findings and recommendations are not a judgment. The Eastern District Candelaria F&R never became an order of the district judge, because a Rule 41 notice mooted it. Related-case motions can also split a cluster: the court related one Northern District action to another and refused to relate the third. That is Civil Local Rule 3-12 case management, not a comment on the facts.

None of those points says who should prevail. The recording, the DS-367, and a hearing officer's written decision now sit in front of two federal judges, and each judge will decide what they show.

## FAQ

**Are these two cases the same lawsuit?**

No. They share a November 1, 2024 CHP contact but name different defendants and theories. *Nforbi v. Meza-Gonzalez* is a § 1983 action against two officers. *Nforbi v. Candelaria* is a § 1983 action against a DMV hearing officer and the DMV director.

**Has a court decided who was right about the stop?**

No. Meza-Gonzalez is at a motion to dismiss. Candelaria has an answer to the amended complaint. Earlier cases were dismissed without prejudice or were not pursued.

**Why do some captions say Newanforbi and others say Nforbi?**

The filed complaints used different name forms. Eastern District, Alameda, and Sacramento papers use Newanforbi. The August 2026 Northern District complaints use Nforbi. This article follows each caption.

**What is an APS hearing?**

Administrative Per Se is the DMV license-suspension process after certain DUI-related arrests or alleged test refusals. It is not a criminal prosecution. The Candelaria complaints challenge APS Decision 34739340.

**Did the Northern District relate the two live cases to each other?**

The orders on file relate Meza-Gonzalez to *Newanforbi v. Serrato* and find Candelaria not related to Serrato. This draft does not treat Serrato as one of the two featured cases.

**Is this article a statement by the plaintiff to the court?**

No. It is an unpublished internal draft. The operative statements to the courts are the filed papers.
