                              23 may “judicially notice a fact that is not subject to reasonable dispute because it: (1) is
                              24 generally known within the trial court’s territorial jurisdiction; or (2) can be accurately and

                              26 R. Evid. 201(b). Further, a court “must take judicial notice if a party requests it and the
                              27 court is supplied with the necessary information.” Fed. R. Evid. 201(c). These conditions

                               1         Courts may take judicial notice of matters of public record. MGIC Indem. Corp. v.
                               2 Weisman, 803 F.2d 500, 504 (9th Cir. 1986). For example, courts may take judicial notice
                               3 of “‘[p]ublic records and government documents available from reliable sources on the
                               4 Internet,’ such as websites run by governmental agencies.” Hadley v. Kellogg Sales Co.,
                               5 243 F.Supp.3d 1074, 1088 (N.D. Cal. 2017).
