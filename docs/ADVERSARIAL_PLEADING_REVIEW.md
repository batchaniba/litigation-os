# Adversarial Pleading Review
## Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi — Portfolio-Wide Writing-Quality Pass

### Analysis Date: July 14, 2026

---

## Purpose and methodology

This is the highest-value, most uncomfortable check available for a pro se portfolio: read every initiating pleading — complaint, First Amended Complaint, or petition — the way the *actual* defense lawyer on the other side will read it on receipt, hunting for the single fastest way to knock out a claim on paper. Not a summary of what the filing argues; an audit of what it fails to prove.

Five parallel review passes each took a cluster of matters, read the operative pleading PDF(s) directly (not the `matters.js` case summaries), and scored every pleaded cause of action against:

- **Missing element** — does the pleading actually allege facts satisfying every element of the claim, or does it recite the legal standard and call it a day (the core Iqbal/Twombly problem)?
- **Unsupported assertion** — "on information and belief" allegations with no anchoring fact, or characterizations of a defendant's knowledge/intent asserted without a stated factual basis.
- **Internal inconsistency** — dates, figures, or characterizations that don't reconcile within the same document (or against the litigant's own attached exhibits).
- **Jurisdictional/standing/exhaustion defect** — venue, immunity, exhaustion, or adequate-remedy problems apparent on the face of the pleading.
- **Statute of limitations exposure** — gaps between injury and filing not addressed with a tolling or discovery-rule theory.
- **Damages pleading weakness** — damages pled only as "according to proof" with no factual predicate, or duplicated across causes of action.

## Cross-cutting patterns (read this section even if you skip the rest)

1. **The "information and belief" problem is systemic, not isolated to the two newest matters.** It sinks or seriously weakens the core theory in Matters 3 (state-actor status), 5 (fraud scienter as to Palacios individually), 7 (the "unwritten policy" premise), 16 and 21 (disparate-treatment comparators), and both Matters 25 and 26 (actual use of registry information — the one fact both cases are built entirely around). When the load-bearing fact of a cause of action is pled without any anchoring detail, that is exactly the sentence a motion to dismiss quotes back.

2. **Sworn filings contradict each other's own attached exhibits.** Matter 8's Petition swears a Detention Certificate was presented at a January 2025 hearing, but the certificate's own metadata shows it wasn't prepared until May 2025. Matter 8 also disclaims any evidence of driving while its own attached transcript and DS-367 contain a driving admission. Matter 6's FAC calls CDCR's review "wholesale adoption" while its own attached Form 1502-DR shows three independent layers of agency review. These aren't hypothetical opposing-counsel arguments — they're contradictions built into the litigant's own record, discoverable by anyone who reads the exhibits.

3. **Damages figures don't hold up to arithmetic scrutiny in several filings.** Matter 22's prayer asks for "not less than $500,000" for five to six weeks of employment at $2,673 in actual gross wages, with no mitigation analysis. Matter 14's wage-loss figure is pled as a range ("$1,534 to $2,301"), suggesting the underlying math was never actually run. Matters 25–26 have no wage figure at all — the applicant was never hired by either company.

4. **Cross-matter damages overlap is a real, recurring exposure**, independent of and consistent with the portfolio's own prior Consistency Audit: the CDL wage-loss window claimed in Matter 7 (Feb. 2025–Feb. 2026 DMV suspension) overlaps with wage-loss windows also claimed in Matters 5 and 6 for the same period under different causal theories. A superseding-cause argument is available to any of the three defendants.

5. **Statute of limitations is the single most dangerous, least-hedged category.** Matter 5's fraud claim pleads the litigant's own suspicion in November 2022, more than three years before filing. Matter 14's Government Claim was presented roughly 18 months after the six-month Government Code §911.2 deadline, with no late-claim relief sought. Matter 26 may face a one-year CCP §340(a) penalty-action characterization on its lead cause of action, filed with essentially no cushion. None of these are addressed with an explicit tolling theory in the pleading itself.

6. **Qualified immunity is under-briefed at the complaint stage across every federal individual-capacity claim reviewed** (Matters 1, 3, 7, 21, 24). Each cites general propositions ("retaliation is clearly established," "arbitrary denial of a mandatory duty is clearly established") rather than authority addressing the *specific* fact pattern — exactly what *Wesby*/*Kisela*/*White v. Pauly* require and exactly the gap a QI motion exploits.

7. **Some filings are genuinely strong and shouldn't be second-guessed.** Matter 3's direct-quote evidence and two sworn comparator declarations materially raise the bar on qualified immunity. Matter 7's dashcam chronology and structural-bias theory against Gordon are well-supported. Matter 22's Post-Adverse Action Notice is a real admission that makes its core statutory claim close to demurrer-proof. Matter 5's HOPE-corporate fraud allegations (as opposed to the Palacios-individual ones) are specific and dated. The point of this audit is to find the real weak points, not manufacture doubt everywhere.

### Group A — Matters 1, 3, 8

## Matter 1 — Newanforbi v. CHP et al. (2:25-cv-01460-DC-CSK)
**Filing reviewed:** Complaint for Violations of Civil Rights, filed May 27, 2025 (5 pages)
**Overall vulnerability:** **High**
**Lead attack:** The Complaint is so conclusory that Plaintiff's own Opposition to the Rule 12(b)(6) motion already conceded dismissal of the State, official-capacity claims, Count III (Monell), and punitive damages — leaving two individual-capacity claims that depend on facts (fabricated arrest report, cup not in plain view) appearing nowhere in the Complaint itself, only in the later Opposition, which cannot cure an Iqbal-deficient pleading without leave to amend.

**Findings:**
1. **Missing element (Monell)** — ¶25 is one conclusory sentence with no specific policy, prior incident, or training deficiency pled. (Already conceded.)
2. **Immunity defect on the face of the pleading** — State of California named despite Eleventh Amendment bar; officers sued in "official capacities" despite the same bar applying. (Already conceded.)
3. **Group pleading** — Count I never distinguishes Melano's search from Meza-Gonzalez's arrest order, despite the facts themselves differentiating their roles.
4. **Duplicative Due Process count** — Count II re-describes the same seizure already covered by Count I; under *Graham v. Connor*, arrest/detention reasonableness is analyzed exclusively under the Fourth Amendment.
5. **Qualified immunity gap** — The facts that would defeat QI (cup not in plain view, fabricated report contradicted by dashcam) are absent from the Complaint and surface only in the Opposition brief — too late to cure the pleading.
6. **Unpled causation** — The 5-day parole hold and jail conditions are recited in the narrative but never tied to a specific cause of action against these defendants; intervening custodial decisions by non-parties are a plausible superseding cause.
7. **Damages pleading weakness** — No dollar figures, no CDL suspension dates, no wage documentation — pure "according to proof" boilerplate.
8. **Cross-filing inconsistency** — ¶18 insists this was a full custodial arrest (Count I depends on it), while the companion Matter 8 Petition's central theory is the opposite — that CHP's own Detention Certificate proves this was "a detention only, not an arrest." Either sworn position is usable as an admission or judicial-estoppel argument against the other.

## Matter 3 — Newanforbi v. Derrick et al. (2:26-cv-00004-DJC-JDP)
**Filing reviewed:** First Amended Complaint (Doc 5), filed January 12, 2026 — operative pleading
**Overall vulnerability:** **Medium**
**Lead attack:** Defendant Palacios's pending MTD squarely attacks the FAC's state-actor allegations against her as legal conclusions rather than pled facts under any of the four Ninth Circuit state-action tests; if granted, it strips a private HOPE employee — and the "joint action" theory tying her to Derrick — out of the case at the pleading stage.

**Findings:**
1. **Missing element — state action (Palacios)** — ¶10's state-action allegation is a legal conclusion dressed as fact; this is the live defect SLB's MTD is actually built on.
2. **Missing element — supervisory liability (Noguchi)** — Limited to attending a meeting, signing conditions, and approving a report relying on others' input; under *Iqbal*, that risks being recharacterized as respondeat superior, which §1983 forecloses.
3. **Conceded duplicative claim** — Count III substantively re-pleads the First Amendment retaliation facts; Plaintiff has already all-but-conceded this in the Opposition to Palacios's MTD.
4. **Qualified immunity — pitched too generally** — Cited cases establish general retaliation principles, not authority addressing scheduling/accommodation denials in a mandatory reentry program specifically.
5. **Unsupported assertion** — ¶58 alleges the *existence* of favorable sworn declarations while expressly deferring their substance to discovery — an unusual admission that the complaint itself doesn't yet support the claim.
6. **Temporal mismatch** — Comparator A's favorable treatment is dated 8-9 months after most of the challenged adverse actions, weakening the contemporaneous-disparate-treatment inference.
7. **Damages pleading weakness** — Future lost wages pled indefinitely with no end date or mitigation analysis, over a year removed from the triggering event.
8. **Genuine strength** — The direct-quote evidence ("You do not have freedom of speech...") and two sworn § 1746 comparator declarations are unusually strong for a pro se pleading and materially raise the QI bar on Count I.

## Matter 8 — Newanforbi v. Candelaria, et al. (26WM000135)
**Filing reviewed:** Verified Petition for Writ of Administrative Mandate, filed May 18, 2026, with Exhibits 1-8
**Overall vulnerability:** **Medium**
**Lead attack:** The entire Petition rests on the wager that a Detention Certificate retroactively divests the DMV of jurisdiction it otherwise indisputably has — solely to escape the fact that this Petition was filed more than fourteen months after the ordinary 34-day Vehicle Code §13559(a) deadline expired. If a court treats the error as ordinary (non-jurisdictional) agency error, every cause of action is time-barred without reaching the merits.

**Findings:**
1. **Statute-of-limitations exposure, bet-the-case theory** — See lead attack. This jurisdictional-void-vs-voidable-error line is genuinely contested law, and DMV's counsel will attack it head-on as the cleanest, merits-free dismissal path.
2. **Internal inconsistency — the Detention Certificate's own metadata** — Exhibit 2 is dated "November 5, 2024" on its face but its own footer states it was "prepared by CHP22511... on 05/12/2025." ¶23 swears the certificate was presented at the January 24, 2025 hearing — nearly four months before the document's own system-generated preparation date. This discrepancy is never explained anywhere in the Petition.
3. **Unsupported assertion undercut by Plaintiff's own exhibit** — ¶35(d) claims "no evidence" of driving, but Plaintiff's own Exhibit 3 transcript and DS-367 both contain a driving admission.
4. **Exhaustion gap (Count IV)** — The structural-bias/dual-role theory doesn't appear in Plaintiff's original December 2024 Personal Statement to the agency — a forfeiture risk for want of exhaustion of that specific theory.
5. **Self-prepared "evidence"** — Exhibit 3 is a self-transcribed, argument-laden document with embedded advocacy callouts, not a neutral record — opposing counsel will argue it deserves little independent evidentiary weight.
6. **Venue vulnerability** — Vehicle Code §13559(a) plausibly functions as a specific venue statute directing review to Plaintiff's county of residence (San Joaquin); the Petition itself concedes San Joaquin is "also proper."
7. **Case-law overreach** — *Mercer v. DMV* is cited repeatedly for a jurisdiction-stripping proposition its actual holding doesn't clearly support.
8. **Genuine strength** — On the merits, the administrative record is unusually strong (three contemporaneous confirmations the liquid was urine, an officer's own admission there's "no pass or fail" on the FSTs, arrest ordered one second after a lawful PAS refusal). If the SOL threshold question goes Plaintiff's way, the merits are well-supported.

### Group B — Matters 5, 6, 7

## Matter 5 — Newanforbi v. Palacios, HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc.
**Filing reviewed:** First Amended Complaint, filed April 22, 2026 (STK-CV-UF-2026-0001032)
**Overall vulnerability:** **High**
**Lead attack:** The fraud claim — the theory every other cause of action pivots on — is self-defeating on limitations grounds: the FAC's own reliance narrative admits Plaintiff *considered filing suit as early as November 2022*, more than three years before the original complaint, which under *Norgart v. Upjohn* triggers inquiry notice regardless of when he later found the corroborating *Dwyer* order.

**Findings:**
1. **Statute of limitations (self-inflicted)** — ¶34(b) pleads Plaintiff's own suspicion of wrongdoing in November 2022; California's discovery rule runs from suspicion, not confirmation. The 3-year fraud SOL (CCP §338(d)) is likely already blown as pled.
2. **Fraud pleaded without particularity as to Palacios individually** — The January 2024 group representation is attributed only to "a HOPE clinician acting under [Palacios's] supervision" — no name, no fact showing her personal knowledge of that specific statement, as California's heightened fraud standard (*Lazar*) requires.
3. **IIED — doesn't clear the "exceeds all bounds of decency" bar** — Harsh administrative characterizations and insults routinely fail this threshold under *Hughes v. Pair* absent something more.
4. **Bane Act — coercion allegations restate the underlying violation** — Most of the pleaded "coercion" simply redescribes the predicate wrong rather than alleging a threat/intimidation *independent* of it, as *Shoyoye* and *Cornell* require.
5. **UCL restitution standing gap** — Seeks restitution of monies "wrongfully obtained... by HOPE," but HOPE's revenue comes from its CDCR contract, not payments from Plaintiff — under *Kwikset*, UCL restitution requires the plaintiff to have lost money *to* the defendant.
6. **Redundant declaratory relief claim** — Adds nothing factually beyond the damages counts already pled.
7. **Damages pleaded redundantly** — The same $109,408 CDL wage-loss figure is asserted under three separate causes of action without differentiation.
8. **What's strong** — The dated, named fraud incidents (Akins, Nov. 2022; the June 2024 homework-denial statement; the August 2025 recorded "routinely loses" admission) are genuinely well-pled with particularity as to HOPE corporately, and the negligence-per-se hook is real.

## Matter 6 — Newanforbi v. HOPE Psychotherapy, Inc.; Macomber
**Filing reviewed:** First Amended Complaint, Doc. 4, filed February 20, 2026 (voluntarily dismissed without prejudice; refiling preserved)
**Overall vulnerability:** **High**
**Lead attack:** The FAC's own attached Exhibit A (CDCR Form 1502-DR) directly contradicts its central Monell theory that HOPE's characterization was "incorporated wholesale... without independent review" — the exhibit shows three layers of independent CDCR review weighing six objectives beyond treatment status, with individualized commentary. A pleading that attaches an exhibit refuting its own causation theory is a textbook demurrer target.

**Findings:**
1. **Exhibit A undercuts the "wholesale adoption" theory** — Shows independent agent concurrence language and three separately-signed layers of review, not blind adoption.
2. **Heck v. Humphrey disclaimer contradicted by the actual damages theory** — The FAC disclaims challenging the duration of parole custody while its damages theory is entirely premised on the wrongfulness of continued supervision — internally inconsistent.
3. **Own pleading blames HOPE for a CDCR-authored rule** — The "fabricated eligibility criterion" is sourced (in the FAC's own text) to CDCR's Policy 19-03, yet damages are sought from HOPE, not the CDCR official who maintains that policy.
4. **Own exhibit contradicts the "manufactured non-completion" narrative** — Two of the four cited parole violations on the 1502-DR are literally "FAILURE TO ATTEND SEX OFFENDER TREATMENT" — not an arbitrary policy output.
5. **State-action theory is a stretch** — Treatment-progress reporting by a contracted vendor subject to independent multi-tier review is a weaker fit for *West v. Atkins*' public-function test than the FAC assumes.
6. **The two "judicial condemnations" are weaker than characterized** — *Dwyer* is an unpublished, non-precedential single-judge ruling on different facts; *ACSOL v. Macomber* is merely an order overruling a demurrer, not a merits adjudication.
7. **Damages theory overlaps Matters 5 and 7** — The same CDL-earning-capacity baseline is attributed to three different causal chains across three lawsuits; during the Feb. 2025-Feb. 2026 window, the DMV suspension pled in Matter 7 was a *complete* bar to commercial driving — a plausible superseding cause foreclosing HOPE's conditions from being a "substantial factor" during that same window.

## Matter 7 — Newanforbi v. Candelaria; Gordon
**Filing reviewed:** First Amended Complaint, filed April 27, 2026 (2:26-cv-00983-DC-CSK)
**Overall vulnerability:** **Medium**
**Lead attack:** Candelaria's conduct — presiding over a hearing, weighing evidence, applying an evidentiary presumption, issuing a written decision subject to judicial review — is quintessentially adjudicative. The FAC's conclusory recharacterization of this as "prosecutorial" doesn't grapple with *Cleavinger v. Saxner*'s actual multi-factor test, and even absent immunity, *Parratt/Hudson* poses a problem: CCP §1094.5 mandamus is a meaningful post-deprivation remedy Plaintiff concedes he never filed.

**Findings:**
1. **Absolute quasi-judicial immunity underargued** — Conflates "wrong" with "not judicial"; *Cleavinger* factors actually cut toward immunity here.
2. **Parratt/Hudson adequate-remedy problem** — Concedes mandamus "could theoretically" work but dismisses it only because it's slower/no damages — courts have repeatedly held that doesn't make it constitutionally inadequate.
3. **Core legal theory presented as settled but is actually novel** — No case cited actually holds a Detention Certificate retroactively destroys the APS "lawful arrest" predicate; this undermines both the merits and the QI "no reasonable officer could have believed" argument.
4. **Cited precedents establish institutional error, not personal damages liability** — All cited cases are administrative-mandamus reversals of DMV *decisions*, none impose personal damages on an individual hearing officer.
5. **Punitive damages target mismatch** — The line hearing officer faces punitive exposure while the supervising official who set the framework (Gordon) faces none, being sued only in official capacity.
6. **Damages overlap with Matters 5/6** — Same cross-matter superseding-cause problem identified above.
7. **What's strong** — This is the best-drafted of the three Group B filings: the dashcam chronology is detailed and internally consistent, the *Mathews* framework is correctly invoked, and the structural-bias theory against Gordon is comparatively well-supported by on-point authority.

## Matter 12 — Ngwa Nforbi v. Diamond Truck Sales, Inc. (STK-CV-UF-2026-0001094)
**Filing reviewed:** First Amended Complaint, signed May 11, 2026
**Overall vulnerability:** **Medium** (High as to the two fraud counts specifically)
**Lead attack:** The two fraud counts rest almost entirely on an "information and belief" allegation that Diamond had pre-sale knowledge of the defect — and the complaint itself admits this is unconfirmed and "will be amended as discovery permits." Under California's heightened fraud-particularity rule, this is demurrable on its face.

**Findings:**
1. **Missing element — fraud particularity** — No named Diamond agent identified for the actionable representations; the pre-sale-knowledge theory is an inference chain, not a stated fact.
2. **Unsupported assertion — scienter** — The entire punitive-damages theory depends on the same speculative information-and-belief knowledge allegation.
3. **Missing element — breach of contract term** — Count 4 paraphrases the implied-warranty claim (Count 5) rather than identifying a specifically breached term — largely duplicative.
4. **Damages pleading weakness — speculative lost income** — The $46,800 lost-income figure rests on an unexplained "$1,800/week" projection, and the complaint itself hedges on whether the underlying contracts were ever secured.
5. **Statute of limitations exposure** — Purchase was April 2023; complaint filed February 2026 (nearly 3 years later); the tolling allegation brackets discovery to a year-long window rather than pleading a specific date, exactly the imprecision *Fox v. Ethicon* scrutinizes.
6. **Inconsistent remedies** — Seeks both rescission (unwind the deal) and years of consequential/lost-income damages as if Plaintiff kept and used the truck — an election-of-remedies tension.
7. **Strong point** — The successor-liability/alter-ego pleading against Alpine is unusually well-developed, pleading five independent overlapping theories.
8. **Notice-of-breach timing** — Any required earlier notice of breach is being satisfied only by the complaint itself, filed ~3 years after purchase — a UCC §2607(3)(a) "reasonable time" problem.

## Matter 14 — Newanforbi v. Urrea et al.

**Overall vulnerability:** **High**
**Lead attack:** Every money-damages cause of action arises from a five-day detention that ended November 6, 2024, yet the Government Claim wasn't presented until May 13, 2026 — roughly 18 months later, far outside the six-month Government Code §911.2 deadline, with no late-claim relief ever sought. 6. **Bane Act specific-intent pleading** — Conclusory "punitive intent" characterization, indistinguishable from an ordinary supervision judgment call.

## Matter 16 — Newanforbi v. Rojo
**Filing reviewed:** Verified Complaint, filed July 7, 2026

**Lead attack:** The Bane Act claim depends on an "implicit threat" theory manufactured from the mere fact that a uniformed parole agent delivered a discretionary travel denial in person — with no actual threat cited. ## Matter 22 — Newanforbi v. Pilot Travel Centers LLC
**Filing reviewed:** Complaint for Damages, filed May 29, 2026 (settled July 11, 2026; reviewed as filed)
**Overall vulnerability:** **Low** on the core PC §290.46(j) and Tameny claims; **Medium** on the IIED/NIED counts
**Lead attack:** The Post-Adverse Action Notice is a genuine admission on the statutory element, so the real fight is a demurrer to the IIED/NIED counts on workers'-compensation-exclusivity grounds — a termination decision, even an unlawful one, is conduct within the "normal risks of the employment relationship," and NIED is almost never viable against an employer for a termination-related injury.

**Findings:**
1. **Internal inconsistency (timing)** — The complaint treats the Dec. 30, 2025 notice as the termination, but paychecks continue through January 23, 2026 — nearly four weeks later — muddying the causal story.
2. **Unsupported assertion (FCRA sub-claim)** — The FCRA pre-adverse-notice failure is pled "on information and belief" despite the complaint otherwise being meticulously documented — a fact peculiarly within Plaintiff's own knowledge.
3. **Missing/stretched element (FCRA theory)** — Conflates the *procurement* purpose (permissible) with the legality of a specific downstream decision — not what FCRA's "permissible purpose" doctrine addresses.
4. **Damages pleading weakness** — The $500,000 prayer floor for 5-6 weeks of $2,673 in actual wages is untethered to any mitigation or duration analysis.
5. **Cause-of-action weakness (IIED/NIED)** — Ordinary personnel decisions routinely fail the "extreme and outrageous" bar; NIED is close to paradigmatic workers'-comp-exclusivity territory.
6. **What's strong** — The §290.46(j) and Tameny counts are unusually well-pled: Defendant's own written notice explicitly and solely cites registry status, and no safety exception is even invoked.

## Matter 24 — Newanforbi v. Serrato (4:26-cv-05953-ASK)
**Filing reviewed:** Filed Complaint (Doc. 1), filed June 17, 2026
**Overall vulnerability:** **Medium-High**
**Lead attack:** Qualified immunity, combined with the absence of any controlling authority holding that PC §679.10 creates a federally protected liberty/property interest — no case is cited holding that a boilerplate U-visa certification denial violates the Fourteenth Amendment.

**Findings:**
1. **Missing element (property/liberty interest)** — Two of §679.10's three certification elements require judgment calls, the hallmark of discretionary (not ministerial) action — if discretionary, no due-process entitlement arises regardless of how arbitrary the denial reads.
2. **Qualified immunity gap** — Cited cases (a building-permit case, First Amendment retaliation cases) don't involve U-visa certification; likely a winning QI motion.
3. **Attenuated causation (retaliation count)** — The underlying protected activity targets different officers, not the named defendant; the complaint's own alternative explanation (an overworked office issuing a rushed denial) is at least as plausible under Twombly.
4. **Conclusory personal-involvement allegations** — No factual basis pled for how a Captain "personally possessed" evidence presumably in an evidence locker.
5. **Attenuated injury/standing** — Certification is not status; USCIS retains independent discretion, making the pled injury one step removed from the actual denial.
6. **Claim-splitting exposure** — Substantially re-pleads facts already at issue in two other pending actions.

## Matter 25 — Newanforbi v. Federal Express Corporation
**Filing reviewed:** Complaint drafted/verified July 15, 2026; not yet filed
**Overall vulnerability:** **High**
**Lead attack:** The central causation element — FedEx's *actual use* of registry information, as opposed to its mere presence in a file FedEx never had to explain — is pled entirely "on information and belief" with no anchoring fact, and never forecloses the far more mundane, equally plausible alternative explanations for a delivery-role denial (driving record, employment verification, credit, drug screen).

**Findings:**
1. **Missing element / conclusory pleading (core theory)** — Confirmed independently: every load-bearing fact (report contents, disclaimer, actual use) is pled on information and belief with zero corroborating detail.
2. **Unsupported assertion — self-defeating excuse** — Nothing explains why Plaintiff never exercised his separate FCRA right to request his own consumer file directly from First Advantage in the 16+ months since the denial.
3. **Factual inconsistency (application date)** — The complaint alleges application "in or around early 2025," but the actual Application Confirmation Email is dated July 17, 2024 — about seven months earlier. Opposing counsel comparing the complaint to the exhibits would catch this immediately and use it to impeach the reliability of the information-and-belief allegations generally.
4. **Missing element (Invasion of Privacy)** — Megan's Law registry information is, by statutory design, publicly posted; California's privacy tort requires a reasonable expectation of privacy, and courts are skeptical of privacy claims built on legally public information. This is the single most exposed count.
5. **Improper standalone claim (Negligence Per Se)** — Pled as a freestanding tort with no independent common-law duty alleged; California courts routinely sustain demurrers to this structure.
6. **Damages pleading weakness** — No wage rate, job title, or offer terms alleged at all — weaker than Matter 22's documented paystubs, since Plaintiff was never hired.
7. **Derivative claim collapse (UCL)** — Entirely parasitic on the §290.46(j) causation theory; falls if that does.
8. **Novel doctrinal extension (Tameny)** — Tameny is a wrongful-*termination* tort; extending it to a pre-employment failure-to-hire scenario is a less-settled application than Matter 22's straightforward termination.
