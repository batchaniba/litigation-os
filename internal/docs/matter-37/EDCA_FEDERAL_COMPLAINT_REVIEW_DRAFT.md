# INTRODUCTION

1. This action arises from an accepted Home Depot hire at its distribution and fulfillment facility at 5395 Hopkins Road, Tracy, California. Plaintiff accepted the position and began Home Depot’s onboarding process. Home Depot then required and ran an employment background check. On information and belief, Home Depot used First Advantage Background Services Corp. (“First Advantage”) as the consumer reporting agency for that screening.

2. The First Advantage relationship is not pleaded from guesswork alone. Public federal-court records contain a Home Depot-branded “BACKGROUND CHECK DISCLOSURE AND AUTHORIZATION” identifying First Advantage as the consumer reporting agency Home Depot would use to procure California investigative consumer reports. Other federal litigation describes First Advantage furnishing a consumer report to Home Depot for a prospective employee, and First Advantage itself later publicly featured a Home Depot representative in a program devoted to workforce screening practices. The precise order, package, transmission, and adjudication records for Plaintiff’s screening are in Home Depot’s and First Advantage’s possession.

3. On information and belief, First Advantage prepared and furnished Plaintiff’s employment background report to Home Depot in connection with the Tracy hire, and Home Depot received and used that report. After the background check, Home Depot ceased the onboarding process and ended, withdrew, or prevented Plaintiff from beginning or continuing the accepted Tracy employment on or about on or about August 2024.

4. Before taking that adverse employment action, Home Depot did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the First Advantage report and did not provide the written Summary of Rights required by the Fair Credit Reporting Act (“FCRA”), 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A). Home Depot thereby deprived Plaintiff of the statutory pre-decision opportunity to review the report, dispute its contents with the consumer reporting agency, and respond before the employment decision became final.

5. When Home Depot denied, withdrew, ended, or prevented the employment under circumstances in which it had obtained an investigative consumer report, it also did not advise Plaintiff that such a report had been obtained and did not provide First Advantage’s name and address as required by California Civil Code § 1786.40.

6. The third claim is narrower. California Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits use of information disclosed pursuant to California’s Megan’s Law website for purposes relating to employment except where another law authorizes the use. Plaintiff is informed and believes, and on that basis alleges, that the First Advantage report furnished to Home Depot contained information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46, including Plaintiff’s California registry listing or a derivative result sourced from that registry, and that Home Depot used that information, in whole or in part, in making the Tracy employment decision.

7. This action does not challenge Home Depot’s ability to consider criminal-history information lawfully obtained from an independent source when another law permits that consideration. The § 290.46 theory rises or falls on what Home Depot and First Advantage actually obtained, transmitted, adjudicated, accessed, and used. Those records are within Defendants’ or the nonparty CRA’s control and will identify the source of any registry information.

# FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION  
## Fair Credit Reporting Act — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A)  
### Against Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.

51. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 50.

52. Plaintiff was a “consumer,” the First Advantage report was a “consumer report,” Home Depot procured or caused it to be procured for “employment purposes,” and Home Depot was a “person” using the report within the meaning of the FCRA.

53. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A), before taking adverse action based in whole or in part on an employment consumer report, the person intending to take the adverse action must provide the consumer (i) a copy of the report and (ii) a written description of the consumer’s rights under the FCRA.

54. Home Depot took adverse employment action based in whole or in part on the First Advantage report.

55. Home Depot did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the report before the adverse action.

56. Home Depot did not provide Plaintiff the written Summary of Rights before the adverse action.

57. Home Depot therefore violated § 1681b(b)(3)(A).

58. Home Depot’s noncompliance was negligent within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681o and, on proof, willful within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681n. Willfulness is plausibly supported by Home Depot’s own publicly filed background-check form, which expressly recognized that before information from a consumer or investigative consumer report is used to make an adverse employment decision, Home Depot would provide the applicant the report and a summary of FCRA rights. That standardized language demonstrates corporate awareness of the pre-adverse obligation years before Plaintiff’s screening. Plaintiff pleads negligence and willfulness in the alternative.

59. As a direct result, Plaintiff suffered actual damages including lost employment compensation and the loss of the statutory opportunity to review and respond to the report before the employment decision. If willfulness is proven, Plaintiff also seeks statutory and punitive damages authorized by § 1681n.

# SECOND CAUSE OF ACTION  
## California Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act  
### Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1786.40, 1786.50  
### Against Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.

60. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 50.

61. The First Advantage employment background report was an investigative consumer report within ICRAA.

62. California Civil Code § 1786.40(a) provides that when employment involving a consumer is denied under circumstances in which a report regarding the consumer was obtained from an investigative consumer reporting agency, the user shall advise the consumer against whom the adverse action was taken and supply the name and address of the investigative consumer reporting agency making the report.

63. Home Depot denied, withdrew, ended, or prevented Plaintiff’s employment under circumstances in which it had obtained the First Advantage investigative consumer report.

64. Home Depot did not provide the advice required by § 1786.40 and did not supply First Advantage’s name and address at the time of the adverse employment action.

65. This § 1786.40 omission is pleaded as a separate act or omission from the pre-adverse FCRA violation in Count One.

66. Under Civil Code § 1786.50, Plaintiff seeks actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater, together with costs and any attorney’s fees recoverable if counsel later appears. If the violation is proven grossly negligent or willful, Plaintiff seeks punitive damages as authorized by § 1786.50(b). Home Depot’s California-specific background-check disclosure publicly identified First Advantage by name, address, and telephone number years before the events here, supporting an inference that Home Depot was familiar with the identity of its investigative CRA and the California regulatory framework governing those reports.

70. Plaintiff is informed and believes, and on that basis alleges, that the First Advantage report furnished to Home Depot contained information disclosed pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46, including Plaintiff’s California registry listing or registry-derived information.
