# FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION  
## Fair Credit Reporting Act — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A)  
### Against Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.

46. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 45.

47. Plaintiff was a “consumer,” the First Advantage report was a “consumer report,” Home Depot procured or caused it to be procured for “employment purposes,” and Home Depot was a “person” using the report within the meaning of the FCRA.

48. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A), before taking adverse action based in whole or in part on an employment consumer report, the person intending to take the adverse action must provide the consumer (i) a copy of the report and (ii) a written description of the consumer’s rights under the FCRA.

49. Home Depot took adverse employment action based in whole or in part on the First Advantage report.

50. Home Depot did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the report before the adverse action.

51. Home Depot did not provide Plaintiff the written Summary of Rights before the adverse action.

52. Home Depot therefore violated § 1681b(b)(3)(A).

53. Home Depot’s noncompliance was negligent within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681o and, on proof that Home Depot knowingly or recklessly disregarded the statutory requirement, willful within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681n. Plaintiff pleads negligence and willfulness in the alternative.

54. As a direct result, Plaintiff suffered actual damages including lost employment compensation and the loss of the statutory opportunity to review and respond to the report before the employment decision. If willfulness is proven, Plaintiff also seeks statutory and punitive damages authorized by § 1681n.

# SECOND CAUSE OF ACTION  
## California Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act  
### Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1786.40, 1786.50  
### Against Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.

55. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 45.

56. The First Advantage employment background report was an investigative consumer report within ICRAA.

57. California Civil Code § 1786.40(a) provides that when employment involving a consumer is denied under circumstances in which a report regarding the consumer was obtained from an investigative consumer reporting agency, the user shall advise the consumer against whom the adverse action was taken and supply the name and address of the investigative consumer reporting agency making the report.

58. Home Depot denied, withdrew, ended, or prevented Plaintiff’s employment under circumstances in which it had obtained the First Advantage investigative consumer report.

59. Home Depot did not provide the advice required by § 1786.40 and did not supply First Advantage’s name and address at the time of the adverse employment action.

60. This § 1786.40 omission is pleaded as a separate act or omission from the pre-adverse FCRA violation in Count One.

61. Under Civil Code § 1786.50, Plaintiff seeks actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater, together with costs and any attorney’s fees recoverable if counsel later appears. If the violation is proven grossly negligent or willful, Plaintiff seeks punitive damages as authorized by § 1786.50(b).

# THIRD CAUSE OF ACTION  
## Unauthorized Employment Use of Registry Information  
### Cal. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) and (j)(4)(A)  
### Against Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.

62. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 45.

63. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(1) authorizes use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 only to protect a person at risk, except as otherwise authorized by law.

64. Section 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for purposes relating to employment except as authorized by paragraph (1) or another provision of law.

65. The Home Depot-specific First Advantage report identifies information concerning Plaintiff whose stated source is **[EXACT CALIFORNIA REGISTRY SOURCE LANGUAGE]**.

66. Home Depot received and used that report for an employment purpose.

67. The registry information was a substantial factor in Home Depot’s decision to deny, withdraw, end, or prevent Plaintiff’s Tracy employment.

68. The pleaded use was not authorized by § 290.46(j)(1), and Plaintiff is unaware of another law authorizing Home Depot to use information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for the Tracy warehouse / fulfillment position.

69. Home Depot thereby violated § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

70. Section 290.46(j)(4)(A) makes a prohibited user liable for the civil remedies specified in that subdivision. Plaintiff seeks actual damages, any additional amount authorized by the statute, exemplary relief to the extent proven and legally available, attorney’s fees if counsel later appears and the statute authorizes recovery, and any other nonduplicative remedy available under § 290.46(j)(4)(A).
