# Standing Litigation Rule — No Free Work
## Adopted 2026-09-19. Pro se drafting discipline; not legal advice.

**We will not do opposing counsel's work for them, and we will not do the judge's
job for them.**

### 1. Opposing counsel's work
- We **research** their best arguments; we **never publish** them.
- Rebut what is actually filed — nothing more. No steelmanning their motion, no
  fixing their defects, no spotting issues they missed. If their paper misstates a
  standard, the misstatement is used, not corrected.
- Private neutralizers (e.g., answers to adverse authority) stay private until the
  other side cites the authority. No pre-buttal of cases they have not raised.

### 2. The judge's job
- We argue; the court decides.
- Never narrow our own claims, concede counts, volunteer weaknesses, or invite
  rulings against ourselves in filed papers. A concession offered to look reasonable
  becomes the dismissal order.

### 3. Carve-outs (non-negotiable)
- **Duty of candor:** controlling adverse authority is disclosed where the rules
  require it, whether or not the other side cited it.
- **Procedural obligations** the rules impose (meet-and-confer, disclosures, etc.).
- **Affirmative strategic disclosures ordered by Plaintiff** — e.g., the Matter 6
  disclosure. An ordered exception, not the default posture.

### Application note (Matter 28)
The opposition skeleton's neutralizers for *Gross v. Samudio* and *Blackwell v.
Carrington* remain in the private research memo. They enter the filed opposition
only if the defense cites those cases.
