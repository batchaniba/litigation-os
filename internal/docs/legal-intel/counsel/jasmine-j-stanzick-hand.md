## 4. FCRA motion-practice analysis (what the sample does and doesn't show)

- **Observed resolutions are settlements, not merits wins.** The two FCRA cases with visible outcomes — *Varela* (Accurate; joint stipulation of dismissal with prejudice) and *Boyer* (First Advantage; ~6-month life, terminated after joint CMC statement) — ended by agreement. *Wiesen* (2021, ~6 months) has the same shape. **No Rule 12(b)(6) win, no summary-judgment win, and no class-certification ruling attributable to her was found in the public sample.** Do not read this as "she can't win motions" — FCRA individual cases routinely settle, and Seyfarth's FCRA group (partners above her) owns the dispositive-motion record. Read it as: her matters resolve by negotiation, and the client (Accurate included) appears to fund early settlement authority.
- **§1681e(b) / §1681k relevance.** *Williams v. DISA* is the closest analog to an employment-purpose accuracy case in the sample (employment screener + employer co-defendants). *Mariscal v. AppFolio* sits against the backdrop of the FTC's $4.25M AppFolio settlement, which alleged §1681e(b) failures (vendor-fed mismatches, duplicate records, obsolete criminal/eviction data) — the same "reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy" theory family likely aimed at Accurate here. No §1681k(a)(1)/(a)(2) briefing by her was located; the disjunctive-notice-vs.-strict-procedures structure of §1681k is worth knowing independently (see Mondaq analysis of the employment-background-screening notice case law) because plaintiffs often misplead it.
- **Standing is on her radar.** She co-authored a Seyfarth/Mondaq analysis of *Nayab v. Experian* (9th Cir.) holding that a consumer need only allege an *unauthorized purpose* to survive dismissal while the *user* of the report bears the burden of proving an authorized purpose (http://www.mondaq.com/Article/867976). A defense lawyer who writes about Article III/permissible-purpose standing is a defense lawyer who will look for a standing argument. Expect any concrete-harm/standing challenge she can colorably make.

## 6. What this means for Matter 29

1. **Expect an answer on Sept. 22, not a Rule 12 motion.** She already stipulated to extend Accurate's response deadline to Sept. 22, 2026 (Dkt. 7) — the standard prelude to an answer, and it mirrors the McMahon pattern (answered rather than moved). Preserved defenses could still surface via Rule 12(c) or summary judgment later.
2. **Accurate is a repeat client of hers, and Accurate settles.** She defended Accurate in *Varela* (C.D. Cal., filed Jan. 2026), which ended in a joint stipulation of dismissal with prejudice — a negotiated resolution within months. Accurate was also Amazon's co-defendant in *Lerma v. Amazon* (C.D. Cal. 2022–2023), so the Amazon/Accurate reporting pipeline is familiar territory for this defense team.
3. **The fight will be factual, under §1681e(b).** Her playbook per the sample is not early dispositive motion practice but procedure-focused defense: the reasonableness of Accurate's matching/sources/reinvestigation procedures. Discovery into Accurate's data pipeline (vendors, matching logic, dispute handling) is where leverage lives — the FTC's AppFolio theories are the template for what "reasonable procedures" attacks look like.
4. **Watch for a standing/permissible-purpose argument** informed by her *Nayab* writing — particularly any claim premised on Amazon's *use* of the report rather than Accurate's *furnishing* of it.
5. **Settlement posture:** the sample suggests Accurate (through this counsel) resolves individual FCRA cases by stipulated dismissal rather than litigating to judgment. That cuts both ways — it signals willingness to pay nuisance-to-moderate value to make files go away, but also that they will not roll over on precedent-setting admissions.
