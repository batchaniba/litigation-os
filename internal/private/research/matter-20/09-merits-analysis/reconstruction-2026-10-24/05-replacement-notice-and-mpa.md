## MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

### I. INTRODUCTION — THE DECISION, THE DUTY, AND THE DEFECT

On April 20, 2026, Captain G. Serrato of the California Highway Patrol denied petitioner's request for Form I-918, Supplement B certification in five sentences. The denial identifies no qualifying crime considered, no element that failed, and no facts supplying lawful authority for the restraint at issue. (Pet. Ex. 1.) It states only that "[a] thorough review of this incident was conducted," that the incident "does not meet the standard of qualifying criminal activity identified in Section 679.10," that it does not meet the standards of the Immigration and Nationality Act, and that "no substantially similar qualifying criminal activity" was found.

Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1), provides that a certifying official "shall" certify when the official finds that the applicant was a victim of a qualifying crime and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful. The duty is conditionally mandatory: mandatory once the predicates are found. The question before this Court is therefore precise, and it has three parts, in order: (1) Do the established facts, on the corrected record, show that petitioner was the victim of a qualifying crime — specifically, false imprisonment under Penal Code section 236 — because the restraint imposed on November 1, 2024 lacked lawful authority? (2) Do they show that petitioner has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful? (3) If both predicates are established and no lawful alternative remains, must certification issue — or, if the record permits only the defect findings, must the denial be vacated and reconsidered under enforceable directions?

Petitioner asks the Court to make the findings that answer those questions, in the order stated, and to grant the corresponding relief: **direct certification** if the Court finds all predicates and that certification is the only lawful outcome; **vacatur and controlled reconsideration** if the Court finds the denial defective but stops short of the full predicate findings; and **focused factfinding** if the Court finds that a material question — paradigmatically, whether the authenticated recording confirms the walk-and-turn exchange — prevents judgment on the papers. Each tier has its own findings, stated in the proposed judgments, so that the Court can grant a lower tier without denying the motion.

This memorandum does not ask the Court to presume any finding. Every material fact is tied to an exhibit, an admission, or a conditional record whose status is stated. Where the record is incomplete, the memorandum says so and identifies the completion step.

### II. STATEMENT OF FACTS — THE RESTRAINT, STAGE BY STAGE

The restraint question is answered stage by stage, because different stages carry different legal consequences. A lawful welfare inquiry does not authorize later compulsory custody; a search, even if unlawful, is not itself false imprisonment; and an arrest's lawfulness turns on the facts known to the officers at the time of arrest, not on what was learned later.

**Stage 1 — Initial welfare inquiry (before ~00:56).** Officers encountered petitioner's stationary vehicle on the shoulder of Interstate 580 in Livermore in the early morning hours of November 1, 2024. A welfare inquiry into a stationary vehicle with a sleeping occupant is not, without more, a restraint. Petitioner does not contend otherwise. Nothing in Stage 1 supplies a qualifying crime, and nothing in this motion depends on it.

**Stage 2 — Passenger-door opening and physical intrusion (~00:56:43, ~00:59:46).** The passenger doors were opened during the encounter. Whether any opening was lawful is a search question. Petitioner preserves the chronology but does not argue that a search, even if unlawful, constitutes false imprisonment under section 236. The door sequence is therefore stated as context, not as a qualifying-crime theory. (See § V below.)

**Stage 3 — Investigative detention.** Petitioner was detained for investigation, including field sobriety investigation. Detention for investigation is lawful if supported by reasonable suspicion; its lawfulness is not the qualifying-crime question. Stage 3 matters because the observations made during it — odor, speech, eyes, HGN, Romberg, container, admitted earlier driving — are the facts the officers later relied on for arrest. Their weight is addressed in § VI.

**Stage 4 — Field sobriety investigation.** The Form 202 records that petitioner "chose not to perform" the walk-and-turn because it was difficult (AGO 000031) and lists "poor performance on the FST's" as an arrest basis (AGO 000032). The contemporaneous recording, as transcribed, records a different exchange at approximately 01:10:14–01:10:30: petitioner expressing willingness to perform the walk-and-turn ("I mean I think we can do that") and Officer Melano declining because the shoulder surface was uneven. **The transcript's status is conditional:** it is a petitioner-prepared transcript whose declaration is dated June 18, 2026, after the April 20 denial; the version delivered with the April 9, 2026 request has not yet been identified side-by-side. Bracketed stage directions in the transcript are petitioner's descriptions, not officer statements. No transcript quotation in this motion is offered as proof of what the April 9 package contained. The walk-and-turn theory stands or falls on authentication of the native recording, which petitioner is pursuing and which the Court can test at a focused proceeding if it cannot be resolved on the papers.

**Stage 5 — Arrest decision and handcuffing (~01:11–01:12).** Petitioner was handcuffed and arrested. The Answer to Verified Petition admits the handcuffs and transport (Answer ¶ 39) and admits petitioner was advised of a chemical test (breath or blood) or refusal with administrative consequences, with arrest following the chemical-test refusal (Answer ¶ 38). The record also shows a voluntary preliminary alcohol screening refusal at approximately 01:11:15, after advisement that it was not required, and a chemical-test discussion after arrest at approximately 01:15:54. The PAS and the post-arrest chemical test are different tests with different legal consequences, and this motion keeps them separate throughout. The Answer's ¶ 38 account and the transcript chronology present an apparent category/chronology mismatch, which petitioner raises only as a secondary accuracy point, never as the lead argument, and which petitioner will withdraw entirely if the authenticated recording contradicts the transcript chronology.

**Stage 6 — Transportation and custodial detention.** Petitioner was transported to the Dublin Area Office (4999 Gleason Drive) and held in custody from November 1 through November 5, 2024 (Answer ¶¶ 39, 41). The restraint — handcuffs, transport, multi-day custody — is admitted. The only contested question is whether it was lawful.

**The strongest stage.** Stages 5 and 6 supply the qualifying-crime theory: intentional restraint (admitted), against will (admitted), whose lawfulness turns entirely on whether the facts known at arrest supplied reasonable cause to believe petitioner had driven under the influence. Stages 1–4 supply the probable-cause calculus and, in the walk-and-turn respect, a material correction to the agency's account. No other stage supplies a stronger theory, and no theory in this motion depends on a stage that does not.

### IV. STANDARD OF REVIEW

Traditional mandate under Code of Civil Procedure section 1085 lies to compel performance of a mandatory duty and to correct an abuse of discretion. Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1), imposes a conditionally mandatory duty: on finding that the applicant was a victim of a qualifying crime and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful, the certifying official "shall" certify. Where the predicates are found and only one lawful outcome remains, the court may compel the action. (California Correctional Supervisors Organization, Inc. v. Department of Corrections (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827.) Where the agency's decision is arbitrary or lacks evidentiary support, the court may vacate it and direct reconsideration under specified standards, without dictating the substantive result where genuine discretion remains. (Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547, 560–562.)

Where facts are in dispute in a traditional mandate action challenging a ministerial or informal administrative action, extra-record evidence is admissible. (Western States Petroleum Assn. v. Superior Court (1995) 9 Cal.4th 559, 576 ["However, we will continue to allow admission of extra-record evidence in traditional mandamus actions challenging ministerial or informal administrative actions if the facts are in dispute."].) The certification decision here is an informal administrative action, not a quasi-legislative one; the headline holding of Western States barring extra-record evidence for quasi-legislative decisions does not apply. (Cf. Carrancho v. Public Employment Relations Bd. (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 1255 [quasi-legislative bar].) Petitioner therefore asks the Court to receive the authenticated recording and the production record for the limited purposes stated in the exhibit plan, and to order a focused proceeding under Code of Civil Procedure section 1090 if any material fact remains disputed after the papers.

### V. THE QUALIFYING CRIME — FALSE IMPRISONMENT UNDER PENAL CODE SECTION 236

Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (c)(19), lists false imprisonment as a qualifying crime. False imprisonment requires: (1) intentional restraint of another person; (2) against that person's will; and (3) without lawful authority. (Pen. Code, § 236; CALCRIM No. 1242.)

Elements (1) and (2) are admitted. Petitioner was handcuffed, transported, and held in custody for five days. (Answer ¶¶ 39, 41.) The contest is element (3): lawful authority. That element turns on § VI. If the Court finds that the facts known to the officers at arrest did not supply reasonable cause to believe petitioner had driven under the influence, and that no Vehicle Code section 40300.5 circumstance independently authorized the warrantless arrest, then the restraint lacked lawful authority, and false imprisonment is established as the qualifying crime. If the Court finds reasonable cause, element (3) fails, and the qualifying-crime theory fails with it — petitioner does not ask the Court to find a qualifying crime on any other basis.

**Alternative: unlawful criminal restraint, subdivision (c)(18).** The same facts, if found, establish unlawful criminal restraint as an enumerated alternative. Petitioner pleads it in the alternative; it rises and falls with the lawful-authority finding.

**Alternative: substantially similar activity, subdivision (d).** Petitioner pleads Penal Code section 146(a) — an officer acting under color of authority who arrests or detains against will without lawful authority — as substantially similar in nature and elements to false imprisonment. The shared core is strong: restraint, color of authority, absence of lawful authority. The similarity is thinner on gravamen (an officer-misconduct offense versus a crime against the person), and petitioner acknowledges that no published California case prosecuting an officer under section 146(a) has been located. The Court need not reach subdivision (d) if it finds subdivision (c)(19); petitioner asks the Court to address it only as an alternative, with the limits stated here.

**What petitioner does not argue.** Petitioner does not argue that an unlawful search, without more, is false imprisonment. He does not argue perjury under Penal Code section 118.1 as a qualifying crime; discrepancies between the Form 202 and the recording are offered only as accuracy evidence bearing on lawful authority. He does not argue that the absence of charges, or the issuance of a section 851.6 certificate, is itself an adjudication of unlawful arrest. The certificate (release November 5, 2024; document bearing a May 12, 2025 preparation footer) is offered only as part of the custody record, with its three dates kept distinct.

### VI. PROBABLE CAUSE AND VEHICLE CODE SECTION 40300.5 — THE CONTESTED ELEMENT

**A. The governing test.** A warrantless arrest for a misdemeanor requires probable cause to believe the offense was committed in the officer's presence (Pen. Code, § 836, subd. (a)(1)), unless a Vehicle Code section 40300.5 circumstance applies. Section 40300.5 permits a warrantless arrest where the officer has reasonable cause to believe the person had been driving while under the influence and any one of five circumstances exists: (a) traffic crash; (b) the person is observed in or about a vehicle obstructing a roadway; (c) the person will not be apprehended unless immediately arrested; (d) the person may cause injury to self or damage property unless immediately arrested; or (e) the person may destroy or conceal evidence unless immediately arrested. Section 40300.6 requires liberal interpretation of section 40300.5 to further safe roads and DUI control. The test is objective: the facts known to the arresting officer at the time of arrest, judged by the standard of a person of reasonable caution. (People v. Thompson (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, 818, cited in Aug. 29 Order at 3.)

**B. The admitted predicate — driving.** Petitioner admits he had driven earlier. The motion does not contest driving, and no argument in this motion depends on the absence of observed movement. (Cf. Mercer v. Department of Motor Vehicles (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753, 768–769, 770 [circumstantial evidence of movement may support a section 23152 conviction]; Troppman v. Valverde (2007) 40 Cal.4th 1121, 1137 [for Vehicle Code sections 23612 and 13353 purposes, reasonable-cause belief suffices; proof of actual driving immediately prior to arrest is not required].) Troppman is a DMV implied-consent case, not a section 40300.5 case, and petitioner does not cite it for more than the reasonable-cause yardstick: the question is what the officers reasonably believed, not what petitioner actually did. The fight is over impairment, not movement.

**C. The impairment evidence — stated fairly, then tested.** The agency's account records: odor of alcohol; slurred speech; red/watery eyes; horizontal-gaze-nystagmus clues; a Romberg estimate; an open alcohol container; admitted earlier driving; and a later blood result of 0.073 ± 0.004 g% from a 02:38 draw (AGO 000034). Petitioner disputes the weight of that account, not its existence, as follows:

1. **Walk-and-turn (material correction).** The Form 202's walk-and-turn entry and its "poor performance" arrest rationale (AGO 000031–000032) are contradicted by the recording as transcribed (~01:10:14–01:10:30), subject to the authentication condition stated in § II, Stage 4. A test the officers chose not to administer cannot have been poorly performed. If the authenticated recording confirms the transcript, one stated probable-cause basis falls.
2. **Odor and speech (weight, not erasure).** The Answer admits no verbal disclosure of odor or slurred speech is recorded (¶ 37). Petitioner does not argue that a recording's silence proves no perception occurred; he argues that an observation never disclosed contemporaneously, offered only in a later report, carries less weight than a contemporaneously disclosed observation, and that the Court should weigh it accordingly. The same limit applies to the HGN and Romberg observations: recorded, not founded, and their weight is for the Court.
3. **Urine cup (identification only).** The cup identified as urine (Answer ¶ 36) is distinct from the alcoholic beverage can. Petitioner argues identification only; he does not argue the cup proves or disproves impairment.
4. **Blood result (narrow use).** The 0.073 result is a later measurement, not a time-of-arrest fact. Petitioner does not argue a zero result, and does not offer retrograde extrapolation in either direction. (Cf. People v. Warlick (2008) 162 Cal.App.4th Supp. 1 [retrograde extrapolation admissible with foundation] — foundation is not laid here, by either side, and petitioner does not ask the Court to lay it.)
5. **PAS vs. chemical test (category separation).** The voluntary preliminary alcohol screening refusal (~01:11:15) is not a chemical-test refusal. (Veh. Code, § 23612, subds. (h)–(i); People v. Jackson (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461, 1465–1470 [PAS context; harmless-error affirmance, not a reversal case].) The post-arrest chemical-test discussion (~01:15:54) is a different event. Any argument that conflates them — by either side — is error.
6. **Warrant (separate question).** The later magistrate authorization to collect blood evidence authorizes collection; it does not supply the earlier arrest's lawful basis. Petitioner does not challenge the warrant and does not concede that it cures the arrest.
7. **Parole (timing).** Dispatch discussion of parole/search conditions at approximately 01:11:42 occurred after the passenger doors were opened and cannot supply facts known at arrest. The search question and the custody question are distinct; petitioner argues custody only.

**D. The section 40300.5 circumstances, one by one.** (a) No traffic crash is shown — no support. (b) Obstruction is not established on the current record: a shoulder location is neither obstruction nor non-obstruction, and vehicle position, lane clearance, and hazard lights are unpinned; petitioner will pin them from the Form 202, CAD log, beat sheet, and recording before the hearing, and the Court should not find (b) on the current papers. (c) Non-apprehension is weak: petitioner was stationary with officers on scene. (d) Self-injury/property is weak: petitioner was asleep, not driving. (e) Dissipation is respondents' strongest circumstance: under People v. Schofield (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 968, a warrantless DUI arrest is lawful under the destruction-of-evidence exception where reasonable cause exists. Dissipation presupposes reasonable cause; it cannot supply it. If the Court finds reasonable cause on the totality, (e) answers the warrantless-arrest objection. If it does not, (e) does not save the arrest.

**E. The adverse totality authorities, confronted.** Petitioner does not ask the Court to ignore the authorities respondents will cite. Troppman (above) confirms the reasonable-cause yardstick. Espinoza v. Shiomoto (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 85 affirmed a DMV suspension where reasonable cause was found on odor and emotional-state observations — a substantial-evidence affirmance, not a de novo probable-cause ruling and not a section 679.10 case. Espinoza shows that odor-plus-demeanor observations can sustain a reasonable-cause finding; it does not hold that these observations must. The distinction matters: this Court's task is to weigh this record, not to apply a categorical rule from either case. On the corrected record — walk-and-turn basis removed, odor/speech undisclosed contemporaneously, HGN/Romberg weight disputed, container and admitted driving remaining — petitioner submits the totality does not persuade a person of reasonable caution that petitioner had driven under the influence. If the Court disagrees on the totality, the qualifying-crime theory fails and the Court should proceed to the remand tier, not to denial of the motion as a whole.

**F. The consequence of each finding.** If the Court resolves the walk-and-turn issue in petitioner's favor, the "poor performance" basis falls and the remaining impairment evidence must carry reasonable cause alone — a finding that becomes available that was not available on the agency's account. If the Court resolves the PAS/chemical distinction in petitioner's favor, no chemical-test-refusal inference may be drawn from the PAS refusal. If the Court resolves § 40300.5(b) against petitioner on pinned geometry, (b) remains available to respondents — petitioner does not ask the Court to find (b) on the current papers. If the Court resolves the totality against petitioner, direct certification is unavailable and the motion's center becomes vacatur.

### VII. THE DENIAL DEFECT — WHY THE APRIL 20, 2026 DECISION CANNOT STAND AS WRITTEN

The defect is stated exactly, without overclaiming. (See 02-serrato-decision-error-memorandum.md for the full analysis.)

**First, the decision as written performs no statutory analysis.** It identifies no qualifying crime considered, no element that failed, and no facts supplying lawful authority, as section 679.10, subdivisions (c), (d), and (g) require the certifying official to determine. A reviewing court cannot determine from the decision whether the correct standard was applied. That reviewability defect alone warrants vacatur and reconsideration under directions that require the analysis the decision omitted.

**Second, the decision rests on the agency's incident account, and that account is materially inaccurate in the walk-and-turn respect.** The denial states "[a] thorough review of this incident was conducted." The agency's account is the Form 202. The Form 202's walk-and-turn entry and "poor performance" rationale (AGO 000031–000032) are contradicted by the recording as transcribed (~01:10:14–01:10:30), subject to authentication. The inaccuracy is material because the "poor performance" rationale is part of the stated arrest basis, and lawful authority is the contested element of the qualifying-crime determination. The defect does not depend on what Captain Serrato subjectively read: an agency decision is not insulated from its own record's inaccuracy, and reconsideration on the corrected record cures the defect.

**What petitioner does not claim.** Petitioner does not claim the letter proves Serrato failed to consider the elements (omission is not proof of non-consideration); that a prosecution prerequisite was imposed (the letter asserts none); that a USCIS outcome was predicted (the INA reference is argued as a standard, not as a proved prediction); or that the review was a sham (brevity, eleven days, and May memoranda prove nothing about April deliberation). Petitioner does not ask the Court to find any of those things. The vacatur theory needs none of them.

**What reconsideration must do.** On remand, respondents must: (1) determine, element by element, whether the restraint was false imprisonment under section 236, unlawful criminal restraint under subdivision (c)(18), or substantially similar activity under subdivision (d), on the corrected record; (2) make circumstance-by-circumstance findings under Vehicle Code section 40300.5; (3) determine helpfulness separately under subdivisions (g) and (h); (4) address subdivisions (k)(1) and (k)(3) expressly; (5) consider the identified admissible evidence (see exhibit plan); and (6) issue a new written decision stating the elements, facts, and lawful-authority analysis, and file a verified return. The remand binds respondents to the Court's adjudicated findings and to the statutory criteria; it does not dictate whether certification must ultimately issue, unless the Court has separately made the direct-certification findings.

### VIII. HELPFULNESS — AN INDEPENDENT FINDING

Helpfulness under section 679.10, subdivisions (g) and (h), is determinable independently of the qualifying-crime dispute. Petitioner filed complaints with CHP and with POST, supplied incident-specific information and documents, and remained willing to provide assistance. (Decl. ¶¶ 7–8.) The Answer admits that no investigating entity requested information from petitioner (¶ 57) — a fact that supports, rather than defeats, the subdivision (h) presumption, which applies where the victim has not refused reasonable assistance and was not aware of the investigation or request. Petitioner distinguishes cooperation with the investigation of alleged officer misconduct from compliance with the officers' separate DUI investigation: the statute asks whether petitioner was helpful in the investigation or prosecution of the qualifying criminal activity, and petitioner's complaints and submissions are the relevant cooperation. The Court can find helpfulness even if it reserves the qualifying-crime question for a focused proceeding, and petitioner asks it to do so.

### IX. RESPONSES TO RESPONDENTS' ANTICIPATED OPPOSITION

1. **"Certification is discretionary."** The duty is conditionally mandatory (subdivision (g)(1)); discretion operates over the predicate findings, not over whether to certify once they are made. The remand tier enforces the predicate process; the direct tier applies only where the predicates leave one lawful outcome. (CCSO, 96 Cal.App.4th at 827.)
2. **"Reasonable cause existed on the totality."** Answered in § VI: walk-and-turn basis removed (if authenticated), odor/speech undisclosed contemporaneously, HGN/Romberg weight disputed, container and admitted driving remaining. The Court weighs the totality; petitioner does not ask for a categorical rule.
3. **"Section 40300.5(e) authorized the arrest."** Answered in § VI.D: dissipation presupposes reasonable cause (Schofield). It answers the warrantless-arrest objection only if reasonable cause is found.
4. **"The denial's brevity is sufficient where the record supports the conclusion."** Answered in § VII: the decision as written supplies no reviewable reasoning, and the agency record it rests on is materially inaccurate in the walk-and-turn respect. If respondents produce the RFP No. 8 decision file showing a reasoned analysis, petitioner will answer that analysis on its merits; until then, the letter is the decision under review.
5. **"The transcript is unreliable."** Conceded in part: the transcript is conditional until the native recording is authenticated. That is why the motion's walk-and-turn theory is framed conditionally and why a focused proceeding is offered as the fallback — not as a concession that the transcript is wrong, but as the procedure by which its accuracy is tested.
6. **"Section 847(b) immunizes the arrest."** Section 847(b) addresses civil liability for an arrest with probable cause; it is not a certification bar and does not supply lawful authority where probable cause is absent. Petitioner does not seek damages in Count One.
7. **"The federal screening order is irrelevant."** Agreed — it is omitted from this motion.

### X. THE COURT NEED NOT DECIDE MORE THAN IT MUST

If the Court finds the direct-certification predicates, it should grant direct certification. If it finds only the denial defect, it should vacate and remand with directions. If it finds a material fact question it cannot resolve on the papers, it should order the focused proceeding described in the alternative procedural order. What the Court should not do is deny the motion outright on the ground that direct certification is not proved, when the defect findings independently support vacatur — the tiers are pleaded so that each can be granted on its own findings.
