                               Defendants’ Opposition to Plaintiff’s Motion for U-Visa Certification (26CV168913)
 1                        MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

 2                                            INTRODUCTION

 3         This case involves a search of Plaintiff Brendan Newanforbi’s (“Plaintiff”) vehicle and the

 4   subsequent arrest of Plaintiff for driving under the influence (“DUI”). Plaintiff alleges that the

 5   CHP officers began a DUI investigation and arrested Plaintiff for driving under the influence after

 6   searching Plaintiff’s vehicle without probable cause or a warrant while Plaintiff was resting in his

 7   car on the on the side of Interstate 580 East. This action is currently stayed pending the resolution

 8   of a substantially identical lawsuit Plaintiff filed in federal Court, Newanforbi vs Meza-Gonzalez,

 9   et al., Case No. 2:25-cv-01460-DC-CSK.

10         Plaintiff now seeks U-Visa certification by characterizing his civil action against

11   Defendants California Highway Patrol (“CHP”) and CHP officers Santiago Meza-Gonzalez and

12   Jose Melano (collectively “Defendants”) as “cooperation in the detection and investigation of the

13   qualifying criminal activity.” (Plaintiff’s Motion for Certification 8:17.) This argument is

14   without merit. Plaintiff cannot allege criminal activity in a civil suit and then deem himself

15   helpful to an investigation that does not exist to obtain a U-Visa. As this is a civil action only,

16   Plaintiff’s Motion for U-Visa Certification should be denied.

17                                               ARGUMENT

18   PLAINTIFF IS NOT QUALIFIED FOR U-VISA CERTIFICATION AS THE

19   ALLEGATIONS WITHIN HIS COMPLAINT DO NOT EQUATE TO “QUALIFYING

20   CRIMINAL ACTIVITY.”

21         The U-Visa is a visa intended for “victims of certain listed crimes who later help United

22   States law enforcement officials investigate or prosecute those crimes.” (Ordonez Orosco v.

23   Napolitano (5th Cir. 2010) 598 F.3d 222, 224.). Here, Plaintiff is seeking monetary

24   compensation through a civil suit against the CHP and two of its officers. Plaintiff’s request for

25   U-Visa certification based upon his civil case is without merit.

26         As Plaintiff acknowledges in his Motion for Certification, to qualify for U-Visa

27   certification, an applicant must establish, among other things, that (1) the applicant was a victim

28   of “certain qualifying criminal activity”; (2) the applicant “possesses information concerning the
                                                         2
                                Defendants’ Opposition to Plaintiff’s Motion for U-Visa Certification (26CV168913)
 1   criminal activity”; and (3) the applicant “has been helpful, is being helpful or is likely to be

 2   helpful” to government officials regarding the criminal activity. (8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U)(i).)

 3   Plaintiff cannot fulfill any of the above criteria as Plaintiff cannot use his civil suit to establish the

 4   existence of any criminal activity.

 5         This is a civil action brought by Plaintiff, not a criminal prosecution. No matter what

 6   Plaintiff alleges in the Complaint, civil actions do not carry criminal penalties. Even if,

 7   arguendo, Plaintiff fully prevails on each of his claims, Plaintiff would only be the victim of a

 8   civil tort, not a victim of “qualifying criminal activity” within the meaning of the U-Visa statute.

 9   Without the existence of any criminal activity, Plaintiff logically cannot establish that he

10   possesses information regarding criminal activity, or that he is helpful to government officials to

11   any criminal investigation as required under the U-Visa statute.

12         While Plaintiff cites to Title 8 of the Code of Federal Regulations section 214.14 subsection

13   (a) subdivision(14)(ii) to argue that the “absence of criminal charges, or even the refusal by law

14   enforcement to file charges, is not disqualifying,” (Motion for Certification 5:11-12), the cited

15   regulation states no such thing. Plaintiff’s quoted language does not appear anywhere in

16   regulation. Title 8 of the Code of Federal Regulations section 214.14 subsection (a)

17   subdivision(14)(ii) states in its entirety:

18         A petitioner may be considered a victim of witness tampering, obstruction of justice, or

19         perjury, including any attempt, solicitation, or conspiracy to commit one or more of those

20         offenses, if:

21                (A) The petitioner has been directly and proximately harmed by the perpetrator of the

22                witness tampering, obstruction of justice, or perjury; and

23                (B) There are reasonable grounds to conclude that the perpetrator committed the

24                witness tampering, obstruction of justice, or perjury offense, at least in principal part,

25                as a means:

26                         (1) To avoid or frustrate efforts to investigate, arrest, prosecute, or otherwise

27                         bring to justice the perpetrator for other criminal activity; or

28
                                                            3
                                   Defendants’ Opposition to Plaintiff’s Motion for U-Visa Certification (26CV168913)
 1                      (2) To further the perpetrator's abuse or exploitation of or undue control over

 2                      the petitioner through manipulation of the legal system.

 3   (8 C.F.R. § 214.14(a)(14)(ii).)

 4         Nothing in the text of Title 8 of the Code of Federal Regulations section 214.14 subsection

 5   (a) subdivision(14)(ii) changes the prerequisite for a U-Visa that Plaintiff establishes he is the

 6   victim of certain specified criminal offenses. Here, Plaintiff alleges in his civil case that

 7   Defendants committed various wrongdoings against him, but Plaintiff’s civil allegations are not

 8   the same as a criminal prosecution, nor do Plaintiff’s allegations of being wrongfully arrested for

 9   a DUI rise to the seriousness of a qualifying criminal offense for U-Visa certification, which

10   includes crimes such as murder, rape, and human trafficking. (See 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U).)

11   As a civil lawsuit does not establish the existence of criminal activity, Plaintiff is not qualified for

12   a U-Visa based upon the facts as alleged in his operative Complaint.

13                                              CONCLUSION

14         For the reasons stated above, Defendants respectfully request that the Court deny Plaintiff’s

15   Motion for U-Visa Certification in its entirety.
