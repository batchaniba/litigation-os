## 1. Custody — the gate that is still open

### 1.1 Parole is “in custody”

*Jones v. Cunningham*, 371 U.S. 236, 242–43 (1963) (**VERIFIED** — CourtListener opinion 106507; 371 U.S. 236; Cornell LII): a parolee under the “custody and control” of a parole board, subject to residence, travel, reporting, and revocation, is “in custody” for federal habeas. The same rule is built into § 2254(a) (“in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court”).

California agrees for *state* habeas. Matter 9’s May 29, 2026 tentative quotes *Ex Parte Bandmann* (1958) 51 Cal.2d 388, 396–97: a prisoner on parole is constructively in CDCR custody. Pen. Code § 1473(a) reaches anyone “unlawfully imprisoned or restrained of his or her liberty.” The Matter 19 petition correctly cites *In re Sturm* (1974) 11 Cal.3d 258, 265 on that point (**VERIFIED** as a real Cal.3d decision; pinpoint not re-read this pass).

**On these facts:** GPS, HOPE, curfew, travel permission, inter-county transfer, and a 10-year controlling date of Dec. 2, 2030 are more than enough for *Jones*. Accurate’s Oct. 14, 2026 “CURRENTLY ON PAROLE” line is consistent with custody, though it is not itself a CDCR record.

### 1.2 File while still on parole

*Carafas v. LaVallee*, 391 U.S. 234 (1968) (**VERIFIED** as the collateral-consequences-if-filed-in-custody case; cited in *Spencer*): custody is measured **at filing**. If the 2254 is filed while on parole, later discharge does not moot a challenge to the **conviction**. It may moot a challenge that seeks only **release from parole**.

*Maleng v. Cook*, 490 U.S. 488 (1989) (per curiam) (**VERIFIED** by standard U.S. Reports citation; slip not re-read): a fully expired sentence is not “custody,” even if it is used to enhance a later one.

**Practical rule for this petitioner:** file any 2254 that needs *Jones* custody **before** CDCR discharges him. Do not wait for a discharge certificate and then try to attack the parole term.

### 1.3 Mootness after discharge — *Spencer*

*Spencer v. Kemna*, 523 U.S. 1 (1998) (**VERIFIED** — Cornell LII; 523 U.S. 1): once the sentence (including the parole-revocation term) has expired, a habeas challenge to the **revocation** is moot unless the petitioner proves concrete collateral consequences. The Court **refuses to presume** them for revocation, unlike for a conviction (*Sibron* / *Carafas*). Heck’s need for a favorable termination does **not** keep a moot habeas alive.

**On these facts:**

- A 2254 seeking **discharge from parole** becomes moot the day CDCR actually discharges him, unless some other live restraint remains.
- A 2254 seeking to **vacate C1761642** does *not* become moot on discharge if it was filed while in custody, because conviction collateral consequences (registration, immigration, ICRAA reporting) are presumed.
- Challenges to the **completed** Nov. 1, 2024 hold and the Halloween hold are *Spencer*-moot as habeas. They are § 1983 damages facts (Matters 32 and 13), with *Heck* analyzed separately.

There has been **no documented formal revocation**. *Spencer* is a warning about **timing**, not a description of a revocation case he has already litigated.

## 2. Route A — 28 U.S.C. § 2254 (state-judgment habeas)

### 2.1 What 2254 can and cannot do

§ 2254(a) authorizes the writ for a person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment **in violation of the Constitution, laws, or treaties of the United States**. *Estelle v. McGuire*, 502 U.S. 62, 67 (1991) (**VERIFIED** via *Swarthout*’s quotation): **federal habeas does not lie for errors of state law.**

*Swarthout v. Cooke*, 562 U.S. 216 (2011) (per curiam) (**VERIFIED** — 562 U.S. 216; Cornell LII; Justia PDF; already FOUND in the Matter 28 ledger):

- There is **no federal right** to be released before the end of a valid sentence.
- If California creates a parole liberty interest, federal due process requires only **minimal procedures**: opportunity to be heard and a statement of reasons (*Greenholtz*).
- Federal courts **may not** enforce California’s “some evidence” standard or re-weigh CDCR’s application of PC §§ 3000 / 3008 / 15 CCR § 3574.

**On these facts, a 2254 that says “Hawk or Arguelles misread PC § 3000 / Policy 19-03 / Form 3043” is a state-law petition wearing a federal caption.** It should lose under *Swarthout* even if every state-law point in the Matter 19 petition is right.

What *could* be a federal claim, if exhausted and timely:

1. **Procedural due process** — no notice, no chance to be heard, no reasons. Weak: the 1502-DR exists, lists reasons (term, treatment, technicals, unemployment), and was the subject of a mandate case and a habeas case.
2. **Substantive due process / arbitrary policy** — Policy 19-03 as a secret rule. Uphill after *Swarthout*; underground-regulation is a **California APA** theory (*Tidewater*), not a clearly established Supreme Court rule for AEDPA § 2254(d)(1).
3. **First Amendment retaliation** extending parole. *Muhammad v. Close*, 540 U.S. 749 (2004) (per curiam) (**UNVERIFIED pinpoint this pass**; standard citation): if success would **not necessarily** shorten the term, this is § 1983, not habeas. Matter 30 is already framed that way.
4. **Attack on C1761642** (counsel, plea, innocence, § 290). Classic 2254 — and almost certainly **time-barred**. See § 2.4.

In California that means a habeas petition **denied by the California Supreme Court** (or a petition for review from a Court of Appeal habeas denial). Superior Court is not enough.

### 2.3 Successive petitions

### 2.4 AEDPA one-year — 28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)

§ 2244(d)(1) starts on the latest of:

**(A)** the date the judgment became final on direct review (or the time for seeking it expired);

**(B)** the date a state-created impediment was removed;

**(C)** the date the Supreme Court recognized a new, retroactive constitutional right;

**(D)** the date the factual predicate could have been discovered with due diligence.

§ 2244(d)(2) tolls while a **properly filed** state post-conviction application is pending.

**Conviction track (C1761642).**

- Sentence Jan. 12, 2018.
- No appeal in the repo → finality **~Mar. 13, 2018** (CRC 8.308, 60 days).
- One year: **~Mar. 13, 2019**.
- Geffon 2025 and Matter 19 2026 **do not restart** the conviction clock. They may toll a clock that is already running; they cannot revive an expired one. *Jimenez v. Quarterman*, 555 U.S. 113 (2009) (**UNVERIFIED this pass**) restarts finality only if the state restores a direct appeal.

**Discharge-decision track.**

Some Ninth Circuit cases treat a **parole-board / discharge decision** as a new factual predicate under (D), with its own year. *Redd v. McGrath*, 343 F.3d 1077 (9th Cir. 2003); *Shelby v. Bartlett*, 391 F.3d 1061 (9th Cir. 2004) — **UNVERIFIED this pass; confirm before using.** If that line applies:

- Predicate: Dec. 1, 2025 Form 1502-DR (or Nov. 19, 2025 Dodd signature).
- One year: **~Dec. 1, 2026** unless tolled.
- Matter 19 (filed Apr. 3, 2026) is a state habeas attacking that decision. If it is “properly filed,” § 2244(d)(2) **tolls while it is pending**, and during a reasonable climb to the California Supreme Court.

**The dangerous date:** if Santa Clara **denies** Matter 19 and he waits, the federal year on the discharge claim starts (or resumes) when the state application is no longer pending. A long, unexplained gap after a denial is how these petitions die.

If Santa Clara **grants** discharge, the 2254 seeking discharge is unnecessary and likely moot (*Spencer*).

### 2.5 AEDPA merits — § 2254(d)

Even a timely, exhausted federal claim must show the last reasoned state decision was contrary to, or an unreasonable application of, **clearly established Supreme Court** law, or an unreasonable determination of the facts. *Swarthout* is the clearly established parole-process law. There is no SCOTUS case that says California must apply PC § 3008(d)(2) or 15 CCR § 3574 in a particular way.

*In re Gadlin* (2020) 10 Cal.5th 915 (**VERIFIED** as a real Cal. Supreme Court Prop. 57 eligibility case; widely cited in the Matter 9/19 papers). It binds **California** courts on categorical exclusions from **Proposition 57 parole consideration**. It is **not** clearly established **federal** law for AEDPA, and it is not about earned discharge at year five of a 10-year PC § 3000 term.

## 3. Route B — 28 U.S.C. § 2241

*White v. Lambert*, 370 F.3d 1002, 1006, 1009–10 (9th Cir. 2004) (**VERIFIED** — Justia / OpenJurist): § 2254 is the **exclusive** vehicle for a state prisoner in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment, **even when the challenge is to an administrative decision rather than the conviction**. § 2241 remains for pretrial custody, extradition, and similar non-judgment custody.

*Hayward v. Marshall*, 603 F.3d 546 (9th Cir. 2010) (en banc), later cabined by *Swarthout*, overruled *White* on a **certificate-of-appealability** point, not on 2254 exclusivity. *Dominguez v. Kernan*, 906 F.3d 1127 (9th Cir. 2018) (**VERIFIED** — CA9 slip 18-55209) restates the exclusivity rule.

**Do not caption a CDCR parole petition as 2241** in N.D. Cal. or E.D. Cal. It will be construed as 2254 and will pick up AEDPA’s traps.

§ 2241 remains theoretically available against **federal** respondents (ICE). That is a different case: different custody, different claims, different exhaustion (immigration court / BIA). Not analyzed here beyond the ranking table. CAT deferral is already granted. He is not detained.

### 4.1 Pen. Code § 1473.7 (**VERIFIED** — LegInfo current text)

## 5. *Heck v. Humphrey* and the live § 1983 suits

*Heck v. Humphrey*, 512 U.S. 477, 486–87 (1994) (**VERIFIED** — 512 U.S. 477; Cornell LII; portfolio ledger VERIFIED): a § 1983 damages action is not cognizable if success would **necessarily imply** the invalidity of an outstanding conviction or sentence, unless that conviction/sentence has already been reversed, expunged, declared invalid, or called into question by a **federal** 2254.

*Preiser v. Rodriguez*, 411 U.S. 475 (1973) (**VERIFIED** via *Nettles* and *Heck*): habeas is the exclusive federal remedy when the prisoner seeks **speedier release**.

*Wilkinson v. Dotson*, 544 U.S. 74 (2005) (**VERIFIED** — portfolio ledger; 544 U.S. 74): § 1983 **may** challenge parole **procedures** if success would only mean a **new hearing**, not necessarily shorter confinement.

*Nettles v. Grounds*, 830 F.3d 922 (9th Cir. 2016) (en banc) (**VERIFIED** — CA9 slip 12-16935): in this circuit, § 1983 is the **exclusive** vehicle for claims **outside** habeas core (success would not necessarily produce immediate or speedier release). Habeas is exclusive **inside** that core.
