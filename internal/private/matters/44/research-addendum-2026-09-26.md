## Corrections and sharpening

### 1. Criminal Injuries Compensation — use the statute, not the old pamphlet

The deep memo correctly points at Crim. Proc. §§ 11-808 to 11-811. Two clocks are now in tension:

- **Statute (current):** § 11-809(a)(1), as amended effective July 1, 2025, generally requires filing within **4 years** after the later of discovery of the crime/death or specified post-conviction discovery events. Child-abuse claims may be filed at any time.
- **COMAR 12.01.01.04 and older CICB pamphlets** still recite a **3-year** incident clock and a 48-hour report expectation unless good cause is shown.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Treat § 11-809 as controlling and have counsel reconcile COMAR before any claim is drafted. Do not tell Njiubu a 3-year door is closed if the act was discovered inside 4 years. Property loss is still not a CICB category. CICB is last-resort and offsets other recovery. Caps in § 11-811 include $45,000 medical and $45,000 mental-health counseling, with a separate disability schedule.

CICB contact (public program, not an instruction to call about this file): 410-585-3010 / 1-888-679-9347.

### 2. Criminal exploitation of an older or vulnerable adult is a separate lane from the SAFE Act

Est. & Trusts Title 13, Subtitle 6 is civil. Crim. Law § 8-801 is the criminal analogue: obtaining property of a vulnerable adult or a person **at least 68** by deception, intimidation, or undue influence. Restitution of the property is mandatory on conviction. Do not collapse the two. The State controls § 8-801.

### 3. Housing exit needs a qualifying paper

Real Prop. §§ 8-5A-02 and 8-5A-03 let a victim tenant end future lease liability with written notice plus a qualifying document (final protective order, qualifying peace order, or a recent qualified-third-party report). The tenant then has up to 30 days to vacate and owes rent only for that window. The respondent-spouse's lease liability is not automatically killed. Lock-change relief is adjacent (§ 8-5A-05). This lane does not start on a USER REPORT of mistreatment alone.

### 4. Work leave and unemployment are document-gated

- Earned sick and safe leave: Lab. & Empl. § 3-1305(a)(5) covers absences for DV / sexual assault / stalking against the employee or a family member, used for medical care, victim services, legal process, or temporary relocation. Employers with 15+ employees pay it; smaller employers still accrue unpaid leave (§ 3-1304).
- Unemployment: leaving work because continued employment would jeopardize safety due to domestic violence can be good cause, but the agency will want an active or recent protective order, other non-temporary court order, or a police record. Do not assume a quit is covered.

### 5. Protective-order firearm terms are not automatic on a temporary order

Final orders under Family Law § 4-506 carry mandatory firearm surrender when the statutory criteria are met. Temporary-order firearm relief is narrower. An Extreme Risk Protective Order is a different petition and needs present, specific firearm-danger facts. Do not treat "there might be a gun" as an ERPO file.

### 6. 2012-start history is context, not a live damages clock

Anything before September 26, 2023 is outside the general three-year civil window unless a discovery, concealment, or disability rule is actually proved for that act. Civil assault older than one year is in even worse shape under § 5-105. Historic acts can still support fear, pattern, equitable factors in a divorce monetary award (§ 8-205), and protective-order fear-of-harm analysis. They do not mint a 14-year battery complaint.
