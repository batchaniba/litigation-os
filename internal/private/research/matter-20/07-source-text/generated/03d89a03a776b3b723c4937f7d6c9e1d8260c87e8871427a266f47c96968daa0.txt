19                                           I. INTRODUCTION

20

21          This proceeding concerns CHP's statutory certification decision. On April 9, 2026,

22   Petitioner submitted Form I-918, Supplement B and supporting materials asking CHP to

23   certify his helpfulness concerning unlawful restraint and false imprisonment during a

24   November 1, 2024 encounter. Captain Serrato's April 20 letter rejected qualifying and

25   substantially similar criminal activity. The surviving First Cause of Action asks whether that

26   determination was a lawful exercise of authority under Penal Code section 679.10. It is not a

27   claim that an officer's every unconstitutional act automatically qualifies for certification.

18                  III. THE GOVERNING DUTY AND STANDARD OF REVIEW

19

20          California expressly lists false imprisonment and unlawful criminal restraint among the

21   qualifying activities. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subds. (c)(18)–(19), (d).) Upon request, the

22   certifying official "shall certify victim helpfulness" when a person was the victim of

23   qualifying criminal activity and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful to its detection,

24   investigation, or prosecution. (Id., subd. (g)(1).) Subdivision (h) establishes a rebuttable

25   helpfulness presumption where the victim has not refused or failed to provide reasonably

26   requested assistance. The statute does not condition certification on an investigation remaining

27   open, charges, arrest of a suspect, prosecution, or conviction. (Id., subd. (k)(1).) Nor may an

28   official deny for reasons forbidden by subdivision (k)(3), including cooperation or refusal to

                                                    Page 3
                          MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES - 26CV191136
 1   cooperate in a separate case. These rules are concrete limits on certification discretion, not

 2   substitutes for proof of qualifying victimhood.

 3

 4          Under Code of Civil Procedure sections 1085 and 1086, traditional mandate may

 5   enforce a duty imposed by law and correct a material abuse of discretion when ordinary

 6   remedies are inadequate. A court may require an official to act under the governing law; it

 7   ordinarily does not select among genuinely lawful discretionary outcomes. (Common Cause v.

 8   Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442–443; Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d

 9   547, 560–562.) When only one outcome is lawfully reasonable after facts are established, the

10   writ may compel that result. (California Correctional Supervisors Organization, Inc. v.

11   Department of Corrections (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 824, 827.) Petitioner's direct personal

12   interest and the absence of another remedy that supplies the requested certification satisfy

13   sections 1085–1086.

14

15          IV. THE QUALIFYING CRIME IS UNLAWFUL PERSONAL RESTRAINT;

16                 THE RECORD REQUIRES ITS ELEMENTS TO BE DECIDED

17

18          False imprisonment is the unlawful violation of another's personal liberty. (Pen. Code,

19   § 236.) Penal Code section 146(a) separately identifies arrest or detention against a person's

20   will by a public officer acting under color of legal authority without lawful authority; to the

21   extent CHP evaluates section 146(a) as a substantially similar criminal restraint offense under

22   section 679.10(d), its actual elements and the alleged conduct must be assessed. Petitioner was

23   handcuffed, involuntarily transported and held in custody. (CHP Form 202, AGO

24   000031–000033; Answer ¶¶ 38–39; Newanforbi Decl. ¶¶ 2, 6.) That restraint was intentional;

25   the decisive issue is lawful authority for it. The criminal-activity theory does not turn on

26   whether the officers were later charged or prosecuted. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)(1).)

27

28          The contemporaneous recording, where properly authenticated and compared with the

                                                   Page 4
                          MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES - 26CV191136
 1   officer's report, bears materially on lawful authority. Before any sobriety investigation, the

 2   officers approached a stationary vehicle as a welfare inquiry. In the transcript, Officer Melano

 3   directed Petitioner to lower the window at approximately 00:56:40, then opened the passenger

 4   door three seconds later. The officers' inquiry thereafter developed into detention, tests,

 5   handcuffing and custody. (Pet., Ex. 5, 00:56:07–00:59:46, 01:10:14–01:11:33.) Petitioner asks

 6   the Court to distinguish lawful welfare assistance from subsequent compulsory restraint and to

 7   evaluate the justification existing at each relevant stage. The initial passenger-door opening is

 8   not asserted to be an independently enumerated U-visa offense; it is evidence bearing on the

 9   encounter's progression and the alleged restraint. The transcript first places the dispatch

10   discussion of parole at approximately 01:11:42, after the earlier opening of the front and rear

11   passenger doors. (Pet., Ex. 5, 00:56:43, 00:59:46, 01:11:42.) Later-acquired knowledge of a

12   search condition cannot itself retrospectively justify a prior search. (People v. Sanders (2003)

13   31 Cal.4th 318, 330–332.) This chronology is offered for its bearing on the authority asserted

14   at the time, not as a separate cause of action for a vehicle search.

15

16          The officers did not observe Petitioner driving. (Answer ¶ 34.) Vehicle Code section

17   40300.5 permits specified exceptions to the in-presence rule for warrantless misdemeanor DUI

18   arrests, but an arrest based on earlier driving must satisfy the applicable statutory

19   circumstances and reasonable-cause standard. The record identifies a stationary vehicle on a

20   freeway shoulder; it does not, simply by those words, establish roadway obstruction or another

21   statutory exception. (Veh. Code, § 40300.5; see Mercer v. Department of Motor Vehicles

22   (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753, 768–769 [driving requires volitional movement].) The agency must

23   identify the applicable facts supporting lawful authority rather than equate the encounter's DUI

24   label with an answer to the qualifying-restraint allegation.

20          VI. A VOLUNTARY PAS REFUSAL DOES NOT SUPPLY AN INFERENCE

21                                               OF GUILT

22

23          The transcript records Meza-Gonzales advising that the roadside preliminary alcohol

24   screening test was not required, Petitioner's refusal at approximately 01:11:15, and the

25   immediate direction to turn around. (Pet., Ex. 5, approximately 01:10:54–01:11:33;

26   Newanforbi Decl. ¶ 6.) Section 23612 expressly requires advice of the right to decline PAS.

27   (See Veh. Code, § 23612, subds. (h)–(i).)

28

                                                   Page 6
                          MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES - 26CV191136
 1             The First District held in People v. Jackson (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461 that it was

 2   error to admit refusal of a voluntary PAS as consciousness-of-guilt evidence at a DUI trial,

 3   because doing so would undermine the statutory right to refuse. The holding concerned

 4   evidentiary use at trial and did not itself decide every possible prearrest reasonable-cause

 5   application. Its controlling distinction nonetheless defeats treatment of exercising that

 6   legislatively protected choice as a substitute for objective evidence of criminal conduct. The

 7   Court should evaluate what information actually supported custody before the refusal, rather

 8   than deriving an adverse inference merely from the exercise of the statutory choice. (Id. at pp.

 9   1465–1470; Veh. Code, § 23612(i).)

11             VII. THE COURT SHOULD ASSESS THE TOTALITY ON THE RECORD

12               ACTUALLY PROVED, WITHOUT RETROSPECTIVE SUBSTITUTES

13

14             Petitioner does not ask for a finding of unlawful arrest from the failure to file charges.

15   Nor does he propose retroactive invalidation of prearrest probable cause from a blood draw at

16   02:38 a.m. The produced laboratory record reports 0.073 ± 0.004 g% ethanol at that later

17   collection time; it does not establish a scientifically defensible earlier concentration absent

18   additional foundation, nor does it decide impairment under Vehicle Code section 23152(a).

19   (AGO 000033–000034.) The subsequent judicial warrant authorized acquisition of evidence at

20   its time of issuance; its existence does not replace analysis of what was known at the earlier

21   arrest.

22

23             Similarly, the section 851.6 certificate is an official custody-classification document,

24   relevant to the incident and absence of a filed complaint but not a judicial judgment that the

25   officers lacked probable cause. (Pet., Ex. 7; Pen. Code, §§ 849.5, 851.6.) These limits prevent

26   later events from obscuring the concrete prearrest evidence. They also avoid confusing

27   certification with a criminal prosecution of the officers: Penal Code section 679.10 requires a

28   qualifying-activity finding, not a prior conviction.

13   If the factfinder credits the objective recording and determines that materially false report

16   679.10. Judge Whitman's August 29 order expressly preserved Count One on that premise.

18   and the statutory victimhood decision, not a demand that Serrato write a longer letter.

19

20           CHP's Verified Answer supplies admissions bearing on that evaluation: the vehicle

21   was stationary; the officers initially treated the cup as suspect but identified it as urine; they

23   Petitioner correctly identified his location and read signs. (Answer ¶¶ 34–37, 45–47.) These

24   admissions narrow the factual inquiry and permit the Court to test the officer narrative against

25   the recorded sequence. The additional walk-and-turn comparison provides a specific,

26   independently checkable inconsistency. Its significance is not that one discrepancy

27   automatically proves a crime, but that the certifying decision must not rest on a materially

28   inaccurate account of the confinement.

10              IX. THE HELPFULNESS REQUIREMENT IS SUPPORTED BY THE

11                  SUBMITTED COMPLAINT AND WILLINGNESS TO ASSIST

12

13           Petitioner made complaints to CHP and POST, provided a timeline and transcript of

14   the incident, identified the specific officers and supplied incident records with his certification

15   request. He remains willing to supply truthful information and reasonable assistance

16   concerning the alleged unlawful restraint. (Newanforbi Decl. ¶¶ 7–9; Pet., Exs. 2–3.) CHP's

17   Answer denies that an investigating entity requested information from Petitioner. (Answer ¶

18   57.) The statutory presumption of helpfulness applies where a victim has not refused or failed

19   to provide reasonably requested assistance. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (h).)

20

21           The relevant assistance concerns detection or investigation of the qualifying activity

22   alleged against the officers. Declining a voluntary PAS during a DUI investigation of

23   Petitioner is not, by itself, a refusal to aid investigation of the alleged unlawful restraint. Nor

24   may a separate-case cooperation decision become a forbidden reason for denying certification.

25   (Id., subd. (k)(3)(H).) Once qualifying victimhood is established, these facts independently

26   support helpfulness and the conditional certification obligation; they do not create qualifying

27   victimhood by themselves.

 1              X. JUDGMENT, REMAND, OR A FOCUSED DETERMINATION OF

 2                                          ESSENTIAL FACTS

 3

 4          The Court may enter judgment granting traditional mandate when the proven facts

 5   establish a material abuse of certification discretion. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1085–1086;

 6   Common Cause, supra, 49 Cal.3d at p. 442.) In the first instance, Petitioner requests that the

 7   Court set aside the April 20 refusal and direct CHP's authorized official to reconsider the

 8   request in accordance with the Court's actual findings, the enumerated offense definitions, and

 9   the nonprosecution/helpfulness provisions of Penal Code section 679.10. A remand would

10   leave genuinely discretionary determinations to CHP and require compliance with the material

11   finding that made the prior decision unlawful.

12

13          If the Court instead finds the alleged qualifying offense and helpfulness conclusively

14   established and no reasonable lawful disposition other than certification, it may direct

15   certification itself. (California Correctional Supervisors Organization, supra, 96 Cal.App.4th at

16   p. 827.) Direct certification is sought only on those findings, not simply from submission of

17   the request.

18

19          If the Court cannot resolve genuinely material factual disputes on this motion, it should

20   set an evidentiary procedure focused on the disputed grounds for restraint, the accuracy of the

21   report where material to the custody decision, and the statutory criteria. Code of Civil

22   Procedure section 1094 permits noticed-motion judgment when no triable factual issue

23   remains or decision rests solely on an administrative record. Section 1090 governs trial of

24   essential factual issues raised by a return. For informal administrative action subject to

25   traditional mandate, California law permits relevant extra-record evidence where facts are

26   contested. (Western States Petroleum Assn. v. Superior Court (1995) 9 Cal.4th 559, 575–576.)

27   Any presentation remains subject to authentication and the ordinary evidence rules. Petitioner

28   requests the narrowest procedure necessary to reach a final determination on Count One.

19          This matter came before the Court on __________________, on Petitioner's motion for

20   judgment on the First Cause of Action and issuance of a peremptory writ under Code of Civil

21   Procedure section 1085 and Penal Code section 679.10. The Court considered the admitted

22   evidence, arguments, pleadings and record.

24                                             FINDINGS

25

26   1. Petitioner requested Form I-918 Supplement B certification from CHP on April 9, 2026. On

27      April 20, 2026, CHP, acting through Captain G. Serrato, denied certification on the ground

28      that the November 1, 2024 incident did not involve qualifying or substantially similar

                                                  Page 1
                     [PROPOSED] JUDGMENT ON FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION - 26CV191136
 1      criminal activity.

 2

 3   2. California Penal Code section 679.10 includes false imprisonment and unlawful criminal

 4      restraint among the qualifying activities. Certification is required when the conditions

 5      specified in subdivision (g)(1), including victimhood and helpfulness, are established. The

 6      absence of a prosecution or conviction is not a statutory disqualification.

 7

 8   3. The Court finds that the challenged determination rests on a material error in the assessment

 9      of qualifying criminal activity. In particular, the Court finds that the factual basis used to

10      reject the alleged unlawful personal restraint cannot be sustained without correcting the

11      material conflict between CHP's statement that Petitioner chose not to perform the

12      walk-and-turn because it was difficult and the credited contemporaneous recording

13      showing that the officer declined to administer that test because the roadway shoulder was

14      unsuitable. This conflict materially affects the reliability of the report's stated arrest

15      rationale, in combination with the evidence concerning the conditions of custody. The

16      Court's reasoning and any additional factual findings are those stated on the record and

17      incorporated into this judgment.

18

19   4. Accordingly, CHP's April 20, 2026 decision constituted a material abuse of discretion

20      requiring vacatur and reconsideration under the correct evidentiary and statutory

21      framework. The Court does not order direct certification because lawful factual evaluation

22      remains for the authorized certifying official.

23

                                                    Page 2
