## 6. Litigation style — from the Serrato MTD brief (VERIFIED, read in full)

The August 26, 2026 brief is the best primary-source window into how he litigates, and it is a template with sharp elbows:

- **Kitchen-sink screening motion:** 12(b)(6) on every claim + 12(f) strike of the declaratory-relief cause of action, the injunction prayer, attorney's fees (*Kay v. Ehrler* — no fees for pro se litigants), and punitive damages + qualified immunity + opposition to leave to amend as futile. Everything pleaded gets a corresponding attack. Nothing is conceded for later.
- **Discretion-and-immunity framing:** the brief's theory of the case is "was the official's act authorized?" (U-visa certification is discretionary; no clearly established right) rather than "was the motive retaliatory?" On retaliation specifically, he argues *Ford v. City of Yakima* but-for causation and calls the plaintiff's motive allegations "not factual, but improper speculation" — "nonsensical," "improper speculation and innuendo."
- **The futility section is the weapon:** two full pages (pp. 16–17) reciting the plaintiff's litigation history — the E.D. Cal. R&R recommending dismissal with prejudice (2:25-CV-1460-DC-CSK PS, Apr. 20, 2025), the Alameda dismissal after the Fickes denial (26CV168913), the voluntary dismissals — to argue the plaintiff is "a litigant who has substantial experience with the legal process" running "a strategy of filing multiple lawsuits… only to strategically dismiss each suit after a court finds his claims to be without merit," closing with: **"It is now long past time for Plaintiff's meritless litigation against the CHP and its employees to end."** He litigates the plaintiff, not just the pleading.
- **Procedural posture toward pro se status:** he acknowledges the *Garity* rule (tell a pro se plaintiff of deficiencies, grant leave) and then argues around it via futility and the plaintiff's sophistication. He does not patronize; he uses the plaintiff's experience against him.
- **Briefing tells (observed):** the federal brief's footer carries the **state-court case number (26CV168913)** — template reuse across the Alameda and federal matters; the related-case cite gives Meza-Gonzalez as "3:26-cv-08271-PHK" against the user's 4:26-cv-08271-ASK; the 12(f) motion leans on unpublished district-court orders for the declaratory-relief strike. These are the marks of a busy deputy briefing from templates under caseload pressure — competent, not bespoke.

## 8. Tactics — the live matters

**The September 16 reply.** Expect him to double down on the two pillars: (a) discretion — no entitlement to U-visa certification, so no due-process interest; (b) qualified immunity — no clearly established right in these specific circumstances (*Wesby*'s anti-generality rule). On retaliation, expect a reply-shaped version of the MTD: but-for causation, "speculation and innuendo," and a renewed futility/serial-filer argument tuned to whatever the opposition's strongest retaliation authorities are. His weak zone remains retaliation-pleading doctrine (*Perry*, *Mt. Healthy*, the *Roth* entitlement framing) — the MTD engaged none of it, and a volume deputy is unlikely to brief it well in a reply. If the opposition forces him onto that ground, that is where the reply is thinnest.

**How he handles pro se plaintiffs.** This is his home turf — his docket is substantially pro se prisoner litigation. He does not underestimate them procedurally (he met-and-conferred, noticed properly, cited *Garity*), but his playbook converts a plaintiff's experience into a liability: the more you have filed, the less liberal construction buys you. Against this particular plaintiff, he has already committed to the "sophisticated serial filer" frame. Feeding that frame — more filings, more aggressive paper — is playing into his strongest argument. Starving it is the counter.

**The $250,000 offer from his side of the table.** Three structural facts: (1) **Line DAGs do not have settlement authority.** Any number moves through supervisory layers — at minimum Angres, likely higher, plus coordination with McMahon's chain on the Candelaria piece. (2) The offer spans **two deputies in (apparently) different chains** covering three cases; that is a coordination tax on their side and a reason a response may come late or ask for more time. (3) His office **lives and dies on 12(b)(6)** — a credible threat of surviving the pleading stage is worth more to them than the demand letter is. The offer's leverage peaks if the Serrato MTD is denied or converted; it troughs if the MTD is granted with prejudice, because Li has already asked for exactly that. Calendar-wise, the reply (Sept. 16) and the offer expiry (Sept. 25) mean he will be briefing dismissal-with-prejudice while the offer sits on his supervisor's desk — expect no engagement with the number until the MTD ruling, and expect any response to be routed through Angres, not from Li personally.
