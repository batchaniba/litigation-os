# Ross Discovery Set One — Objection and Protection Matrix

**Case:** *Brendan Ngwa Nforbi v. Ross Stores, Inc., et al.*, Santa Clara County Superior Court No. 26CV501995  
**Prepared:** October 16, 2026  
**Discovery reviewed:** Defendants' Form Interrogatories—General, Set One; Special Interrogatories, Set One (Nos. 1–27); and Requests for Production, Set One (Nos. 1–24), served October 15, 2026.  
**Status:** Internal work product for review and response drafting. Not a response set, not verified, not served, and not legal advice.

## Executive position

Plaintiff should not refuse the discovery wholesale. The strongest response is a disciplined mix of: (1) complete answers to ordinary identity and incident questions; (2) specific objections followed by narrow substantive answers where possible; (3) refusal to produce nonexistent material; and (4) a protective-order protocol before disclosing genuinely sensitive medical, employment, criminal-history, third-party, or confidential settlement material.

The highest-risk items are:

1. Form Interrogatories 2.8 and 2.13, and any request whose answer could create criminal exposure or waive a Fifth Amendment position;
2. Special Interrogatories 23–27 and RFPs 15–24, which reach recordings, prior claims, prior proceedings, public statements, and settlement-related material;
3. RFP 21, which refers to nonexistent RFP No. 26 and is unintelligible as written;
4. unrestricted medical-history requests, which should be limited to conditions genuinely put in controversy;
5. demands for home contact information, full social-media histories, whole-device access, passwords, or unrelated third-party information.

**Operating deadline:** November 17, 2026 is the conservative response date. PR #1009 identifies November 19 as the mail-based outside date, but the November 17 date should control absent a written stipulation or confirmed extension.

## Governing response rules

- Interrogatory objections must identify the specific ground and, when the objection is only partial, answer the unobjectionable portion. See Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.220 and 2030.240.
- An RFP response must state compliance, inability to comply after a diligent search and reasonable inquiry, or a specific objection. A partial objection should identify what is being withheld. See sections 2031.220–2031.240.
- Relevance is broad in discovery, but not unlimited. Discovery must concern a nonprivileged matter relevant to a claim or defense or reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence. Courts may restrict discovery whose burden, expense, or intrusiveness outweighs its likely benefit. See sections 2017.010 and 2017.020.
- Privacy objections require calibration, not slogans. Once Plaintiff puts a physical or emotional condition in controversy, Ross may obtain discovery reasonably tied to that condition, but it does not automatically receive Plaintiff's entire lifetime medical history. See *Britt v. Superior Court* (1978) 20 Cal.3d 844 and *Vinson v. Superior Court* (1987) 43 Cal.3d 833.
- Confidentiality is not automatically privilege. A settlement agreement may remain discoverable despite a confidentiality clause, subject to relevance, privacy, mediation privilege, third-party rights, redaction, and an appropriate protective order.
- Do not assume that a self-represented litigant's own notes, AI drafts, strategy documents, or damages worksheets qualify as statutory attorney work product. Code of Civil Procedure section 2018.030 is framed around attorney work product. Assert attorney-client privilege only for qualifying communications with counsel, and use separate, supportable objections for personal strategy material.
- Do not destroy, alter, overwrite, or “clean up” responsive evidence. Preserve native files, metadata, texts, posts, recordings, and devices. Production ordinarily means copies of responsive information—not surrendering a phone, passwords, or unrestricted account access.
- Interrogatory responses must be signed under oath after factual verification. Do not guess. A truthful “after reasonable inquiry, Plaintiff lacks sufficient information to answer further” is preferable to speculation.

## Special Interrogatories, Set One

### Global definition and instruction objections

- **Definition of “YOU”:** object to the extent it includes attorneys, insurers, agents, consultants, or others beyond Plaintiff's possession, custody, control, or personal knowledge; it also risks invading privilege and protected communications. State that responses are made for Plaintiff after a reasonable inquiry of reasonably available information.
- **Definition of “IDENTIFY”:** object to unnecessary disclosure of private home addresses, personal phone numbers, and personal email addresses. Provide business/service contact information where reasonably sufficient, and seek protective treatment for private third-party data.
- **Instruction 13:** preserve a specific objection under section 2030.060(d) to any unauthorized prefatory instruction that attempts to impose duties beyond the Civil Discovery Act.
- **Compound questions:** where a special interrogatory contains multiple discrete inquiries or subparts, object under section 2030.060(f), then answer each reasonably separable, understood portion so the response remains useful and defensible.

## Requests for Production, Set One

### Response format

For every request, use one of the statutory response forms:

1. **Compliance:** state that all responsive documents in Plaintiff's possession, custody, or control will be produced;
2. **Partial compliance/objection:** state the precise objection, the narrowed construction used, what will be produced, and whether responsive material is being withheld;
3. **Inability to comply:** after diligent search and reasonable inquiry, state why the item never existed, was lost/destroyed, or is not in Plaintiff's possession, custody, or control, and identify anyone believed to possess it if known.

Do not use a page of generic objections incorporated into every answer. Bates-label productions, keep an index mapping Bates ranges to request numbers, preserve originals/native files, and use a privilege log for documents withheld on privilege grounds.

## Confidential settlements and RFP 21

The proposed response should not say, “I cannot produce because the settlements are confidential.” A confidentiality clause ordinarily regulates disclosure between contracting parties; it does not necessarily create an evidentiary privilege against civil discovery.

The safer sequence is:

1. object because RFP 21 is facially defective—it references nonexistent RFP 26—and therefore does not identify which claim or proceeding it targets;
2. assert relevance, overbreadth, proportionality, privacy, third-party rights, and any applicable mediation privilege;
3. review each agreement's notice, subpoena, consent, and court-order provisions;
4. ask Ross to identify the specific proceeding and explain the claimed relevance;
5. propose disclosure, if ultimately warranted, only after notice to affected parties, redaction of immaterial terms, and entry of a suitable protective order;
6. seek a protective order before the response deadline if agreement cannot be reached and production would violate a real confidentiality duty.

Judgments and dismissals are not treated identically to settlement agreements: filed judgments and dismissals may already be public, while the agreement and negotiations may remain confidential or privileged under a separate doctrine.

## Protective-order protocol to propose

A stipulated order can materially reduce risk but should not become a device for Ross to designate everything confidential. Proposed terms:

- two tiers if justified: **Confidential** and narrowly defined **Highly Confidential—Attorneys' Eyes Only**; because Plaintiff is self-represented, any AEO tier must contain a workable mechanism allowing Plaintiff to review material needed to prosecute his case;
- use solely for this action;
- access limited to parties, counsel, retained experts/consultants, the court, court staff, reporters, vendors, and necessary witnesses who sign an acknowledgment;
- redaction of Social Security numbers, driver's-license numbers, complete birth dates, financial-account numbers, home addresses, personal contact information, unrelated medical information, and uninvolved third-party identifiers;
- no passwords, unrestricted device inspection, or whole-account production absent further agreement or court order;
- a designation-challenge procedure, with the designating party bearing the burden required by law;
- procedures for inadvertent production and clawback without conceding privilege;
- rules for filing under seal that comply with California Rules of Court 2.550 and 2.551—designation alone does not authorize sealing;
- return or destruction after final disposition, subject to one archival copy and legal preservation duties;
- no admission of relevance, admissibility, authenticity, or waiver;
- ability to use a document with its author, recipient, or other person who already possessed it;
- no restriction on lawful use of information independently obtained outside discovery.

## Proposed production workflow

1. **Freeze evidence now.** Preserve phones, cloud data, recordings, texts, posts, receipts, payment history, location data, notes, and original files with metadata.
2. **Create a factual inventory.** For each request, list responsive sources, custodian, date range, existence, location, confidentiality issue, and proposed treatment.
3. **Separate risk buckets.** Mark items as ordinary production; privacy/redaction; potential privilege; confidential settlement; or possible criminal exposure.
4. **Get targeted advice for criminal-exposure items.** Review FROGs 2.8 and 2.13, SROGs 23–24, and RFP 24 before verification or production.
5. **Prepare individual responses.** Avoid blanket objections. Give a usable substantive answer after an objection whenever possible.
6. **Build the production set.** Use sequential Bates numbers and a request-to-Bates index. Keep an unredacted preservation copy.
7. **Log withheld items.** Record date, author, recipients, general subject, request number, and protection claimed without revealing protected content.
8. **Meet and confer early.** Seek clarification of RFP 21 and agreement on medical, criminal-history, settlement, social-media, and third-party information before the deadline.
9. **Move for protection only if necessary.** Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.090 and 2031.060 permit protective orders, but a motion requires a good-faith meet and confer and should target a concrete dispute.
10. **Verify only after final fact check.** Ensure the interrogatory answers match the complaint, recordings, posts, medical records, prior filings, and documents produced.

## Open facts for Plaintiff to resolve before responses are drafted

1. Did Plaintiff affirmatively consent to electronic service in this action?
2. Is Plaintiff claiming wage loss, loss of earning capacity, property damage, medical expenses, psychotherapy expenses, or only noneconomic/statutory relief?
3. What physical or emotional conditions are attributed to the incident, and did Plaintiff seek treatment?
4. What convictions, if any, fall within the exact text and period of Form Interrogatory 2.8?
5. What substances or medications, if any, fall within the exact window in Form Interrogatory 2.13?
6. What recordings exist, who made each one, when and where was it made, has it been edited, and what metadata remains?
7. What prior claims or cases involve overlapping injuries, retail detention, public-accommodation discrimination, or damages of the same type?
8. Which prior matters ended in confidential settlement, mediation, judgment, dismissal, or release, and what do their confidentiality/notice provisions say?
9. What incident-related social-media posts, private messages, emails, texts, notes, receipts, payment records, and location records exist?
10. Are any responsive devices, accounts, or documents shared with third parties whose privacy rights need protection?
11. Which witnesses can be identified through business or counsel contact information rather than home contact details?
12. Are there responsive communications with a licensed attorney? If so, segregate them for privilege review; do not commingle them with nonprivileged AI or lay-assistance communications.

## Drafting cautions for later model review

- Do not use the same objection paragraph for every request.
- Do not say “irrelevant” without explaining the mismatch between the request and a pleaded claim, defense, injury, or damage category.
- Do not assert privacy and then provide no answer where a narrower answer is possible.
- Do not claim confidentiality as a standalone privilege.
- Do not assert attorney-client privilege over communications with AI, friends, media, or nonlawyer advocates.
- Do not claim all pro se litigation notes are attorney work product.
- Do not omit a known responsive document because it is embarrassing or harmful.
- Do not answer beyond the exact request, especially on criminal-history and substance-use questions.
- Do not produce original devices, passwords, or unrestricted account credentials.
- Do not waive objections by missing the response deadline.
