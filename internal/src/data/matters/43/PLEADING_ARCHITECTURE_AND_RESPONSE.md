## A. Verified-fact chronology for a future complaint (after rechecking source PDFs)
The following language deliberately avoids saying Hertz made a final adverse decision.

1. On or about October 22, 2026, **The Hertz Corporation** offered Plaintiff a conditional full-time Manager Trainee position assigned to Palo Alto, California, at $23.00 per hour, requisition 43711, identifying Kathleen Williams as supervisor. The offer was contingent on background verification and was at will.
2. Plaintiff accepted the conditional offer October 22. Hertz Careers separately confirmed acceptance, and the online application status showed "Offer Accepted."
3. On October 23, Hertz initiated preboarding tasks and a HireRight background check for this position. Plaintiff signed and submitted multiple screening disclosures, authorization documents, and acknowledgments and elected to receive a copy of any California investigative report.
4. On November 2 at approximately 2:29 a.m. Pacific, HireRight completed the report ordered for The Hertz Corporation. Its overall adjudication was "Client Review Required," not a final decision.
5. The report showed **two "In Registry" results for Newanforbi name forms** and a **"Not in Registry" result for the shortened Brendan Ngwa name form**. Researcher notes on the California hits described a Department of Justice search, expressly cited Penal Code §290.46, and warned that California registry information may not be used for employment except to protect a person at risk. **Source system/website is unverified.**
6. Separately, the report identified a California driver's-license suspension that the report described as having ended May 19, 2026, a commercial motor-vehicle disqualification described as having ended November 30, 2025, and a Santa Clara court conviction record. The report did not prove an active suspension on November 2, 2026.
7. On November 2 at 10:19 a.m. Pacific, HireRight transmitted Hertz's California preliminary pre-adverse notification and FCRA Summary of Rights. The notice identified `[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses-Does not meet` as the stated concern and said a final decision would not occur for at least five business days. The response-deadline field was uncompleted boilerplate in the captured letter. A report copy was also delivered to Plaintiff.
8. The November 2 notices did **not** disclose any final employment rescission or job-start loss. **Future fact addendum required** if a later event occurs: date, source, exact language, decision-maker, reason, delay/loss, mitigation.

### Defense-safe drafting conventions
* Do **not** allege "Hertz unlawfully used Megan's Law to revoke Plaintiff's offer" while the decision ground remains alcohol-related and there is no final withdrawal.
* Do not assert the report "wrongfully declares not in registry" or "falsely declares in registry" solely because separate name-form queries differ.
* If invoking public website information, explain whether the report's DOJ search meant NSOPW or a lawful restricted registry, when and how the data were obtained, and the actual employment decision to which they contributed.
* Don't claim a specific damages total absent actual loss, or state a guaranteed 12-month position from an at-will conditional offer.
* Do not allege employer never furnished an FCRA copy or summary: both appear in the October/November file.

## B. Claim-specific pleading gates for a later complaint
These are **templates for proof collection**, not paragraphs to paste in unconditionally.

### Count I — California Penal Code §290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A): Hertz only if supported
**Pleading gates:** (a) source is information *disclosed pursuant to §290.46* (California public website, possibly traced through NSOPW), (b) operating employer *used that data for employment*, (c) not use permitted by §290.46(j)(1) or (j)(3), (d) remedy, injury or statutory basis.  
**Facts still needed:** precise California origin, Hertz decision-maker knowledge/reliance, any final decision/conditional denial or other actionable actual employer use, explanation of alcohol rationale and whether it masked/combined other factors.  
**Counter:** *Mendoza* says screening company's republishing alone isn't forbidden employment use, and *Donaleski* permits a separate, genuinely independent court-conviction decision. Plead concrete employer conduct, not access or knowledge alone.  
**Example only after proof:** "Hertz's [identified decision maker], reviewing [identified California public registry-origin item] in HireRight's [exact report segment], marked Plaintiff [actual decision/reason] on [date]; [internal record or communication] connected the California public registry information to that action." **Do not use this sentence unless supported.**

### Count II — ICRAA §1786.16 and §1786.50: employer (potential current statutory damages)
**Pleading gates:** specific statutory required item missing from **exact standalone signed disclosure**, e.g., whether the required single investigating agency's name/address/phone is correctly identified; privacy website; nature/scope; and original electronic document boundaries, or a provable report-copy-election violation.  
**Current hurdle:** original evidence map says multiple forms *were* signed and report was provided at completion. No defect yet identified. A form's typo of the employer's name by itself may be trivial unless it caused material noncompliance.  
**If documented:** quote the actual defective clauses and describe how Hertz procured the report after providing that noncompliant disclosure. Analyze any liability on the employer versus HireRight. State alternative statutory remedy under §1786.50; do not multiply $10k by guessed violations. State court may be better than a federal case based solely on abstract informational defects.

### Count III — ICRAA §1786.28(a) (and §1786.18(c)/§1786.28(b) if verified): HireRight
**Pleading gates:** identify particular public-record item, where report omits required **source and initial-publication date**, when furnished, why reported item is public record; for §1786.18(c) prove no timely verification; for (b) prove inadequate strict procedures.  
**Current hurdle:** full report must be reviewed field-by-field; summary cannot verify an omission. Source-lineage request must be factually grounded and submitted to appropriate party.

### Count IV — Fair Chance Act, Gov. Code §12952(c)(2)(A), (C), (c)(3), (c)(4): employer
**Pleading gates:** preliminary or final disqualification was **in whole or part because of a conviction**, statute applies to employer/job, precise missing conviction identification and/or deadline, requested opportunity to respond, later response consideration, actual injury for damages, CRD filing/right-to-sue.  
**Current hurdle:** only identified ground is ambiguous `[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses`; MVR administrative action isn't itself conviction history. The statute does not demand a written individualized assessment. *McInerney* limits merits review and unsupported procedural-damages suits.
**Potential fact:** If Hertz asserts a particular conviction is disqualifying, yet never specifies it in the pre-adverse notice, §12952(c)(2)(A) becomes a powerful testable duty.

### Count V — FCRA §1681b(b)(2)(A) or §1681k(a), if a distinct proven violation
**Pleading gates:** defective federal standalone disclosure and procurement, or adverse public-record furnishing coupled with absence of **both** notice and adequate strict procedures. More federal procedural violations may be plausible only on exact document examination; subject matter jurisdiction and Article III injury must independently be pleaded.
**Avoid:** claiming FCRA §1681b(b)(3) breach when pre-adverse report and rights were furnished before final decision; claiming §1681k absolute contemporaneous-notice duty when it offers the strict-procedures option.
**Do not duplicate FCRA and ICRAA recovery over the identical act** without analyzing Civil Code §1786.52.

## C. Federal complaint Rule 12(b)(6) readiness test
1. Caption **The Hertz Corporation** as operating employer; **Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.** only with actual direct involvement/agency/alter ego; **HireRight, LLC** only on own reporting counts.
2. Jurisdiction independently justified: actual viable FCRA federal question, or complete diversity and non-speculative sum >$75,000; else state venue with demurrer and anti-SLAPP exposure analyzed.
3. Provide real positions/dates/offer terms/acceptance, what documents said, whose decision, which statute directly regulates the complained-of act.
4. Separate **(i) court-record conviction, (ii) DMV/MVR administration, (iii) registry/website-derived listing**; never collapse three fields into one adverse reason.
5. Plead true violations in each count with identified source documents, not blanket assertion that "background screening was illegal."
6. Fair Chance claim: preserve exact notice, whether any **particular conviction** triggered decision, the actual response and consideration, CRD exhaustion and damages.
7. Federal standing and damages: current employment harm is unproven; if alleging informational harm, plead concrete downstream impact, not a bare statutory infraction.
8. Exclude stale/unsupported UCL, seven-year, protected-class discrimination and contract counts; reserve if new evidence supports.
9. Keep all discovery/preservation communications private, accurate, and non-threatening. Avoid publicly posting full unredacted background records.
