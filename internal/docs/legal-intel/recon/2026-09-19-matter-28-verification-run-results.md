## LIST (2) — WHAT CUTS AGAINST PLAINTIFF

1. **§ 3574 took effect April 17, 2025 — after the February 20, 2025 grace-period revocation.** The regulation cannot be used to characterize that act, and if § 3574 was newly *adopted* rather than amended, there may have been **no written-good-cause retention requirement in force at all** when the earliest pleaded conduct occurred. The August 19, 2025 statements and the November 14, 2025 Form 1502-DR are covered; the February act is not. Any pleading that implies otherwise is vulnerable, and it is the kind of error a defense reply exploits.

2. **The general-demurrer rule is the rule; the technical-defect carve-out is the exception.** *Pollock* confirms a judgment after a **general** demurrer **is** on the merits. Van Dine's was almost certainly a general demurrer. *Goddard* gives the escape hatch, but it is an exception that has to be earned on the actual ground decided — not a default.

3. **CCP § 430.10 classification is unsupported so far.** No authority was located establishing that an exhaustion demurrer sounds in (a) rather than (e). If exhaustion is routinely raised under (e) in California practice, the (a)/(e) mismatch argument weakens considerably and should not lead.

4. ***Caviness* is adverse on its facts** — the Ninth Circuit held the private charter school was **not** a state actor for the conduct at issue. It supplies the conduct-specific framework, but a court can apply that framework and reach the defense's answer.

5. **The *Merrill* characterization may be wrong in the *other* direction too.** Until the docket is read, the portfolio does not actually know what *Merrill* held. Building an opposition around distinguishing a case nobody has read is a risk regardless of which way it cuts.

6. **Which § 3008 subdivision applies is unestablished.** If Plaintiff was paroled **before July 1, 2012**, the duty is "participate in," not "successfully complete" — and the completion-discretion theory that anchors the state-action and due-process sections changes shape. His parole date does not appear in any research file reviewed.

7. ***Meinhardt* is an appealability case.** Even read favorably, it does not hold anything about preclusion. Using it as a preclusion authority is an extension, and an opponent will say so.

8. **Two of the three headline authorities added on September 18 — *Estate of Esche* and *Wicklund* — remain unverified after two verification passes.** Neither should appear in a filed brief on the current record.
