## 3. Closed verification items

### 3.1 PLRA "prisoner" — a parolee at liberty is not inside § 1997e

**This was the highest-value open item.** UNIFIED and the survey both said: verify before briefing that *Patsy*'s no-exhaustion rule is not eaten by 42 U.S.C. § 1997e.

**Statute (PRIMARY, uscode.house.gov / GovInfo USCODE-2023).**

- § 1997e(a): "No action shall be brought with respect to prison conditions … by a **prisoner confined in any jail, prison, or other correctional facility** until such administrative remedies as are available are exhausted."
- § 1997e(h): "`prisoner` means any person **incarcerated or detained in any facility** who is accused of, convicted of, sentenced for, or adjudicated delinquent for, violations of criminal law **or the terms and conditions of parole**, probation, pretrial release, or diversionary program."

The parole clause modifies *why* the person is detained. It does not make every parolee a prisoner. The person must still be **incarcerated or detained in a facility**.

**Ninth Circuit (CL FOUND, holdings from opinion text / CDN quotations).**

| Case | Cite | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| *Page v. Torrey* | 201 F.3d 1136, 1139–40 (9th Cir. 2000) | PLRA "prisoner" requires **current** detention for a criminal accusation / conviction / sentence. A person "ceased being a 'prisoner' when he was released from the custody of the Department of Corrections." Quotes *LaFontant*: he "ceased being a 'prisoner' at the time he was released on parole." |
| *Talamantes v. Leyva* | 575 F.3d 1021, 1023–24 (9th Cir. 2009) | Exhaustion applies only to those who are § 1997e(h) prisoners **at the time they file**. A person not incarcerated or detained when the action is filed is not a prisoner. |
| *Jackson v. Fong* | 870 F.3d 928, 933–37 (9th Cir. 2017) | Status is measured at the **operative** complaint. (Confirm pin from reporter before filing.) |

**Sister-circuit one-liner, if needed.** *Kerr v. Puckett*, 138 F.3d 321, 323 (7th Cir. 1998) (CL FOUND): "a convict out on parole is not a 'person incarcerated or detained in any facility.'"

**Caveat, say it in the brief so they cannot.** Halfway-house or parole-violation **custody** can re-enter the definition (*Jackson v. Johnson*, 475 F.3d 261 (5th Cir. 2007); *Witzke v. Femal*, 376 F.3d 744 (7th Cir. 2004)). GPS / curfew / mandatory treatment is **not** detention in a facility. Plaintiff filed Matter 28 as a parolee at liberty. If he is later jailed on a parole hold, that does not retroactively make the July 20 filing a prisoner suit.

**Patsy itself.** 457 U.S. 496, 516 (1982): exhaustion of state administrative remedies is not a prerequisite to § 1983. The PLRA is the statutory carve-out. It does not apply here. Reopen the U.S. Reports PDF before quoting a pin other than 516.

**Opposition use.** One short paragraph under res judicata / issue preclusion: even if Matter 5 issue-precludes "he had not exhausted before the state FAC," that issue is not an element of the federal claims.

### 3.2 Penal Code § 3008(d)(2) — pinned

The Sept. 12 survey flagged exact (d)(2) text as unverified because leginfo was egress-blocked. CDCR's adopted-regulation package (Apr. 2025) quotes the subdivision twice, verbatim. FindLaw / california.public.law match.

> Persons placed on parole on or after July 1, 2012, shall successfully complete a sex offender management program, following the standards developed pursuant to Section 9003, as a condition of parole. The length of the period in the program shall be **not less than one year, up to the entire period of parole, as determined by the certified sex offender management professional in consultation with the parole officer and as approved by the court.**

**That clause is still the case.** A blanket no-completion-before-discharge rule removes the discretion the statute confers on the certified professional.

**15 C.C.R. § 3574 (PRIMARY, Cornell LII, current through the Apr. 17, 2025 Certificate of Compliance).** Post-*Macomber* paper now says what the complaint needs:

- (d): "Supervised persons **may complete** a sex offender management program **before discharge from parole**, as contemplated by … 3008(d)."
- (e)(2): the one-year CTM is "**the first review to determine** whether the supervised person's sex offender management program requirements have been satisfied."
- (f): retention after that review requires **written good cause**, signed by the treatment provider **and** the parole agent on Form 3043, delivered within 30 days.

**Use.** State action / *Monell* moving-force: HOPE's alleged blanket retention is not "clinical judgment under a discretionary statute." It is a policy that contradicts both the statute and the regulation CDCR now writes. Do not cite § 3574 as if it were in force in 2021; it is the 2024–25 regulatory fix. The **statute** has said "as determined by the certified professional" since 2012. *Dwyer* (Mar. 20, 2023) found the no-completion policy three years before this complaint.

### 3.3 *Gross v. Samudio* — retrieved and distinguished

*Gross v. Samudio*, 630 F. App'x 772 (10th Cir. Oct. 27, 2015) (unpublished). **PRIMARY:** Tenth Circuit CDN PDF (`14-1248`, 01019513908.pdf). Not binding in the Ninth Circuit (Fed. R. App. P. 32.1 / 10th Cir. R. 32.1 — persuasive only).

**What it actually held.** Affirmed 12(b)(6) dismissal of § 1983 claims against **private Colorado sex-offender treatment providers** for lack of state action. Gross pleaded that the providers had state contracts and that by refusing to **re-enroll / admit** him they "delegated" parole-revocation authority. The panel: contract + the ability to reject a referred parolee is not enough. He argued **only the public-function test** and did not plead coercive nexus, joint action, or symbiotic relationship. Sex-offender treatment, the court said, is not a traditional **and exclusive** state function.

**What it did *not* hold.** It did not decide a CASOMB vendor. It did not decide a CEO who allegedly **makes statewide policy**. It did not decide ongoing Form 3043 / no-show reporting into a parole-supervision pipeline. The challenged act was a **one-time admission refusal**, not a blanket retention policy and not retaliatory official reporting.

**Distinguishers for the *Merrill* footnote.**

| *Gross* / *Merrill* default | Matter 28 as pleaded |
|---|---|
| Admission / enrollment decision | Ongoing supervisory reporting (Form 3043, no-shows) that **feeds** parole sanctions |
| Contract + funding + referrals only | Willful joint participation with parole agents (the *Dennis* test *Merrill* said the plaintiffs there did not allege) |
| Public-function only | Close-nexus / joint action first (*Jensen* / *Rawson*); public function in the alternative |
| No statutory completion discretion | Pen. Code § 3008(d)(2) **assigns** the period-of-treatment determination to the certified professional |
| Unpublished 10th Cir. | *Jensen* and *Rawson* are published Ninth Circuit |

Do not hide *Gross*. *Merrill* already embedded it. Put the distinguisher in a footnote so Mag. Cisneros does not discover it first.

### 3.5 *Medical Marijuana, Inc. v. Horn* — verified, with a narrower holding than the Sept. 12 survey

Slip opinion (PRIMARY, supremecourt.gov `23-365_6k47.pdf`), argued Oct. 15, 2024, decided **April 2, 2025**. Barrett, J., 5–4 (Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Jackson). Cite as ***Medical Marijuana, Inc. v. Horn*, 604 U.S. ___ (2025)** until the U.S. Reports page lands. Do not invent a volume/page.

**Holding, one sentence:** § 1964(c) does **not** categorically bar recovery for business or property loss that **derives from** a personal injury. The "antecedent-personal-injury bar" used in the Sixth, Seventh, and Eleventh Circuits is rejected. The Ninth Circuit was already on the *Diaz v. Gates* side of that split; *Horn* confirms it.

**What the Court expressly did *not* decide (slip op. at 4–5).** Whether Horn suffered a personal injury; whether "`business`" includes "`employment`"; what "`injured in his … property`" covers. Do **not** brief *Horn* as "the Supreme Court held lost CDL wages are a RICO injury." Brief it as: if they run the personal-injury bar, *Horn* and *Diaz* shut that door. Proximate cause (*Anza*) is still the dangerous argument.

**CDL echo.** Horn was a commercial truck driver fired after a positive drug test. Useful color. Not a holding about this plaintiff.

### 3.6 *Sullivan v. Delta Air Lines* — exists; do not recruit it as a demurrer-as-judgment case

**Do not cite *Sullivan* as the lead finality case.** It is not a demurrer case. *Berri*, *Valvo*, and CCP § 581d still carry the "order sustaining a demurrer is not a judgment" point. *Valvo* existence reconfirmed (CL FOUND, 67 Cal. App. 3d 887 (1977)); full-text PDF still not pulled this pass — confirm the 891–92 n.3 pin from the reporter before filing.

**If they cite *Sullivan* / *Griset* to treat the formal order as a judgment** because it "leaves nothing to be determined": the formal order only sustains the demurrer; the minute order kept the October 13, 2026 CMC (even if later disposed administratively); the register has **no Judgment entry**; CCP § 581d requires a signed written **dismissal**.
