13                                        III. LEGAL STANDARD

14

15           Code of Civil Procedure section 1085 authorizes mandate to compel performance of an

16   act specially enjoined by law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1085, subd. (a).) Mandamus generally does

17   not control how an official exercises genuine discretion, but it may compel the official to

18   exercise discretion under the correct legal standard and may correct an abuse of administrative

19   discretion. (Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442-443.) Section

20   1088 further permits issuance of a peremptory writ in the first instance when the application is

21   made upon due notice; the notice must be at least ten days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1088.)

22

23           If a return raises a factual question essential to determination of the motion and

24   affecting the parties' substantial rights, Code of Civil Procedure section 1090 permits the

25   Court, in its discretion, to order that question tried before a jury. Petitioner requests a nonjury

26   merits determination and does not request a jury, while recognizing the Court's procedural

27   authority under section 1090.

28

                                                     Page 5
                      MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT - NEWANFORBI v. CHP, 26CV191136
 1           Penal Code section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1) provides that, upon the victim's request,

 2   a certifying official "shall certify victim helpfulness on the Form I-918 Supplement B

 3   certification" when the victim was a victim of qualifying criminal activity and has been, is

 4   being, or is likely to be helpful to detection, investigation, or prosecution. Section 679.10

 5   expressly includes false imprisonment, unlawful criminal restraint, perjury, and obstruction of

 6   justice among qualifying criminal activity and includes state offenses whose nature and

 7   elements are substantially similar to the enumerated activity. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subds. (c),

 8   (d).)

10                                            IV. ARGUMENT

11

12   A. The existing record permits a finding that probable cause for the DUI arrest was

13   lacking.

14

15           Probable cause exists when the facts known to the arresting officer would persuade a

16   person of reasonable caution that the person arrested committed a crime. (People v. Thompson

17   (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811, 818; Order at p. 3.) Here, the Court has already held that if it credits

18   Petitioner's factual showing concerning the absence of alcohol odor, the absence of

19   impairment in speech and field sobriety testing, and false statements in the report, it could

20   reasonably find probable cause lacking. (Order at p. 4.)

21

22           The recording is objective evidence bearing on that question. It contains no statement

23   by an officer that Petitioner's breath or person smelled of alcohol; the liquid examined in the

24   vehicle is identified on the recording as urine; and Petitioner's speech is recorded throughout

25   the encounter. (Pet. Ex. 5; Newanforbi Decl. ¶¶ 7-10.) Petitioner performed the field sobriety

26   tests requested of him. The preliminary alcohol screening test was described to him by the

27   officer as not required. (Pet. Ex. 5 at p. 10; Order at p. 3.) On the record as framed by the

28   Court, Petitioner asks the Court to find the arrest lacked probable cause.

                                                   Page 6
                     MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT - NEWANFORBI v. CHP, 26CV191136
 1

 2           CHP's section 851.6 certificate supplies additional contemporaneous evidence. Four

 3   days after the incident, CHP documented that there were "insufficient grounds for making a

 4   criminal complaint," and no criminal charge followed. (Pet. Ex. 7; Newanforbi Decl. ¶ 11.)

 5   Petitioner offers that record as corroborative evidence, not as a substitute for the Court's

 6   independent determination of the probable-cause question.

 7

 8   B. If the detention lacked lawful authority, the alleged conduct falls within or is

 9   substantially similar to qualifying criminal activity.

10

11           False imprisonment is the unlawful violation of the personal liberty of another. (Pen.

12   Code, § 236.) Penal Code section 146, subdivision (a) criminalizes a public officer's arrest or

13   detention of a person against that person's will under color of legal authority but without

14   lawful authority. Penal Code section 118.1 criminalizes a peace officer's knowing and

15   intentional material false statement in a peace officer report regarding the commission or

16   investigation of a crime. Penal Code section 679.10 expressly identifies false imprisonment,

17   unlawful criminal restraint, perjury, and obstruction of justice as qualifying activity and

18   extends to offenses substantially similar in nature and elements. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subds.

19   (c), (d).)

20

21           Petitioner does not ask this Court to convict any officer of a crime. He asks the Court

22   to determine whether the conduct shown by the record constitutes qualifying criminal activity

23   for the limited certification question. Section 679.10 does not require an investigation, charge,

24   apprehension, prosecution, or conviction as a condition of obtaining certification. (Pen. Code,

25   § 679.10, subd. (k)(1).)

 9   satisfied.

10

11   D. If the statutory predicates are established, the Court may command certification;

12   alternatively, it may command lawful reconsideration.

13

14             The primary rule is that mandate will not compel an official to exercise genuine

15   discretion in a particular manner. But Common Cause also recognizes that a particular result

16   may be compelled where the governmental entity has no discretion left to exercise because the

17   duty has become ministerial under the governing law. (Common Cause, supra, 49 Cal.3d at

18   pp. 442-443.) Here, section 679.10, subdivision (g)(1) states that the certifying official "shall

19   certify" when the statutory predicates are met. If the Court finds those predicates established

20   on the record, Petitioner respectfully submits that direct certification is the appropriate writ

21   relief.

 2   E. No plain, speedy, and adequate remedy exists in the ordinary course of law.

 3

 4          Penal Code section 679.10 provides no administrative appeal from CHP's certification

 5   denial. The certification component of a U nonimmigrant petition must come from an

 6   authorized certifying official or entity; an award of damages would not supply that

 7   certification. Traditional mandate therefore supplies the requested form of relief. (Code Civ.

 8   Proc., § 1086.)
