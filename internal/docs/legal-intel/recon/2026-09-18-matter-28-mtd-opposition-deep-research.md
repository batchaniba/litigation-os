# I. Executive strategy

The opposition should be built around one organizing proposition:

> **Defendants are attempting to convert a California state-court ruling expressly resting on unexhausted administrative remedies into a merits adjudication of federal constitutional claims that require no such exhaustion, while simultaneously asking the Court at Rule 12 to resolve factual questions concerning state action, policy/custom, retaliatory motive, falsity, intent, causation, and treatment necessity against the pleader.**

The strongest hierarchy is:

1. **Claim preclusion / issue preclusion**
2. **State action**
3. **Monell**
4. **First Amendment retaliation**
5. **Qualified immunity**
6. **Due process**
7. **Bane Act**
8. **Fraud / negligent misrepresentation**
9. **RICO**
10. **Alternative leave to amend**

The brief should not be enormous simply to look formidable. Judge Cisneros's recent Rule 12 practice favors a **claim-by-claim, allegation-driven paper**, not an evidentiary dump. The goal is to leave the Court with a narrow legal question at each stage and to force Defendants to seek factual findings they cannot obtain on a motion to dismiss.

# II. Claim preclusion: attack in layers

Do **not** make "there is no judgment" the only argument. It is useful but curable. The opposition should establish several independent defects.

## A. What Judge Abdallah actually adjudicated

The July 15, 2026 state-court minute order records that:

- exhaustion was a **"jurisdictional requirement"** to filing the FAC;
- Plaintiff failed to satisfy that requirement **before filing** the FAC;
- the demurrer was therefore sustained without leave to amend.

The defense-drafted formal order contains a broader recital that the FAC "fails to allege facts sufficient to state any cause of action." Do not ignore that wording. The narrower and stronger argument is that **preclusion follows the actual basis of the state adjudication**, read from the entire record, and the court's tentative + oral-argument minute order identify that basis as nonexhaustion/prematurity.

## B. No final judgment on the present record

The September 12 register audit found:

- an order sustaining the demurrer;
- an administrative "case disposed" notation;
- no separate judgment of dismissal.

Use *Berri v. Superior Court*, 43 Cal.2d 856, 860 (1955), *Valvo v. USC*, 67 Cal.App.3d 887, 891–92 n.3 (1977), CCP § 581d, and *Kay v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes*, 504 F.3d 803, 808 (9th Cir. 2007).

This is a current-motion defense, but it is **not the permanent kill** because Defendants can seek entry of a dismissal judgment.

## C. The permanent kill: exhaustion / prematurity is not merits adjudication

Lead with:

- ***Perez v. City of San Bruno*, 27 Cal.3d 875, 882–83 (1980)** — a disposition for prematurity is not a judgment on the merits.
- ***Heath v. Cleary*, 708 F.2d 1376, 1380 n.4 (9th Cir. 1983)** — California exhaustion dismissal does not claim-preclude the later § 1983 action.
- ***Costello v. United States*, 365 U.S. 265, 284–88 (1961)** — failure of a precondition to suit is not an adjudication of the underlying claim.
- Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2).

This section should directly quote Judge Abdallah's language: **"jurisdictional requirement"** and **"prior to the filing."**

That makes the mapping to *Perez* / *Heath* obvious.

## D. § 1983 has no state-administrative exhaustion requirement

***Patsy v. Board of Regents*, 457 U.S. 496, 516 (1982)** is the Supreme Court rule: state administrative exhaustion is generally not a prerequisite to § 1983.

The PLRA is not a substitute defense here. 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(h) defines "prisoner" as a person incarcerated or detained in a qualifying facility. The complaint concerns a parolee in the community, not a prisoner confined in a facility.

The opposition should therefore distinguish:

- **issue preclusion:** the state court may have conclusively determined that administrative remedies were unexhausted when the state FAC was filed;
- **claim preclusion:** that determination does not adjudicate the federal merits and does not supply an element Defendants can use to dismiss § 1983 under *Patsy*.

## E. Shannon Smith was not a Matter 5 party

HOPE was a defendant in Matter 5. Smith was not.

Use *DKN Holdings LLC v. Faerber*, 61 Cal.4th 813, 824–26 (2015), and *Taylor v. Sturgell*, 553 U.S. 880, 892–95 (2008). Do not overstate "CEO status can never create privity"; instead require Defendants to identify the recognized privity/nonparty-preclusion basis.

If they invoke employee/corporate derivative-privity cases, answer that those authorities presuppose a merits adjudication capable of preclusive effect. Matter 5's actual adjudicated ground was a filing prerequisite.

# III. State action: the new strongest formulation

## A. Do not argue "contract = state actor"

That is vulnerable to:

- *Merrill v. Mental Health Systems, Inc.*, S.D. Cal. No. 3:16-cv-01090-GPC-JMA (Sept. 13, 2016);
- *Gross v. Samudio*, 630 F. App'x 772 (10th Cir. 2015);
- other decisions finding that private treatment, funding, referrals, certification, and ordinary reporting do not automatically constitute state action.

Confront those cases.

## B. Ask the correct conduct-specific question

Under *Rawson*, *Jensen*, *Tsao*, and the joint-action line, the relevant inquiry is the **specific challenged conduct**, not the defendant's abstract corporate status.

Matter 28 should be framed as challenging HOPE's alleged participation in the **government-created parole-treatment retention machinery**, including:

1. mandatory SOMP participation under California law;
2. the certified provider's statutorily assigned role in treatment-duration / completion decisions;
3. joint CTMs with parole officials;
4. Form 3043 treatment-status / retention reporting;
5. provider + parole-agent signatures;
6. use of that reporting in continued compulsory treatment and supervision;
7. escalation to CDCR officials when the provider and parole side disagree.

That is not ordinary private psychotherapy.

## C. Penal Code § 3008(d)(2)

The statutory theory should be precise:

California requires covered parolees to successfully complete SOMP for a period **not less than one year and up to the entire parole period**, with duration determined by the **certified sex offender management professional in consultation with the parole officer and approved by the court**.

That provision embeds the private certified professional inside a state-created correctional decision.

## D. Permanent 15 C.C.R. § 3574

The permanent regulation took effect in 2025 and materially improves the pleading theory.

The regulation establishes a completion / retention review process and requires continued retention to be supported by **good cause** documented in writing. The Form 3043 process calls for treatment-provider and parole participation and identifies risk / supervision criteria relevant to retention.

Key pleading significance:

- State created the process.
- State requires the private provider's participation.
- The provider's professional judgment is used to determine whether the state-imposed treatment burden continues.
- The provider's official reporting enters the parole-supervision record.
- This is a far stronger joint-action / close-nexus configuration than mere referral to a private program.

Primary current regulation:
https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/15-CCR-3574

CDCR rulemaking:
https://www.cdcr.ca.gov/regulations/wp-content/uploads/sites/171/2025/01/NCR-25-01-Sex-Offender-Treatment.pdf

## E. Current CDCR operating procedures

The 2026 CDCR DOM materially strengthens the "integrated decisional machinery" framing.

The operating materials describe a process in which:

- the parole side and treatment provider participate in retention / completion decisions;
- retention documentation is formalized through Form 3043;
- disagreements are escalated through CDCR's Sex Offender Unit;
- a CDCR Parole Administrator / PA III can make the final determination.

Source:
https://www.cdcr.ca.gov/regulations/wp-content/uploads/sites/171/2026/03/Annual_DOM.pdf

**Use:** This is important for state action because it demonstrates that HOPE's role is not simply providing information to a state official who independently happens to use it. The private provider is a required institutional participant in a state-defined process whose output directly governs continuation of a compulsory parole condition.

## F. Plaintiff-side binding architecture

### *West v. Atkins*, 487 U.S. 42 (1988)
Private status does not end the state-action inquiry; function inside the state system controls.

### *Jensen v. Lane County*, 222 F.3d 570 (9th Cir. 2000)
Private clinician was a state actor where government and contractor participated in a **complex and deeply intertwined** statutory liberty-deprivation process.

### *Rawson v. Recovery Innovations, Inc.*, 975 F.3d 742 (9th Cir. 2020)
Private institutional mental-health provider and staff acted under color of state law where state statutory authority and private professional activity combined in an involuntary-liberty process.

### *Tsao v. Desert Palace, Inc.*, 698 F.3d 1128 (9th Cir. 2012)
A private corporation can be liable under § 1983 when state action exists, but *Monell* policy/custom requirements apply.

### *Dennis v. Sparks*, 449 U.S. 24 (1980)
Private actors jointly engaged with state officials in the challenged conduct act under color of state law.

## G. Distinguish *Merrill* and *Gross* on gravamen and institutional integration

The response should concede their general proposition: a private treatment provider is not automatically a state actor merely because it treats supervised people or transmits information.

Then identify the distinctions:

- Matter 28 attacks **retention / completion decisionmaking**, not ordinary treatment conditions.
- California expressly defines the provider as an institutional decision participant.
- The provider's Form 3043 activity is part of a formal state process.
- The provider and parole side jointly memorialize retention.
- CDCR resolves institutional disagreement.
- Complaint alleges a blanket policy applied through that process.
- Complaint alleges deliberate false information injected into the process, not passive truthful reporting.

This is the state-action section that should receive the most drafting attention.

# IV. Monell: keep it an express-policy / final-policymaker case

Once state action is established, the Monell theory is cleaner than the state-action gateway.

Do not drift into vague failure-to-train.

The alleged theory is:

- HOPE had an express / longstanding blanket retention policy;
- Smith, alleged CEO/founder/final policymaker, approved or enforced it;
- policy allegedly foreclosed individualized completion regardless of progress;
- policy was repeatedly implemented through CTMs and treatment-status decisions;
- the policy directly caused continued treatment / liberty consequences.

Use:

- *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658;
- *Tsao*, 698 F.3d 1128;
- *AE v. County of Tulare*, 666 F.3d 631;
- final-policymaker principles appropriate to the actual corporate governance facts pleaded.

The current factual record — operational manual language, completion practices, Smith role, discovery admissions, Form 3043 history — should be used as a **pleading map**, not dumped into the opposition as hundreds of pages.

# V. Dwyer: powerful, but characterize it correctly

The portfolio's later verification resolved the earlier attribution problem.

The *Dwyer* record supports the proposition that Dr. Cristina Loree of HOPE testified about HOPE's program structure and that the court found categorical treatment-phase practices inconsistent with an individualized exercise of § 3008 discretion.

Important guardrails:

1. HOPE was not a damages defendant in *Dwyer*.
2. *Dwyer* is not collateral estoppel against Smith.
3. The habeas petition itself was denied, while the court separately exercised § 3008(d)(2) authority to terminate further SOMP participation.
4. Use the decision for **notice, policy plausibility, historical context, and the nature of the discretion**, not as a binding liability judgment against Defendants.

The strongest role for *Dwyer* in opposition:
- it makes the alleged blanket policy plausible;
- it supplies historical notice that HOPE/DAPO's categorical approach had already been judicially scrutinized;
- it supports the argument that § 3008 calls for individualized judgment.

# VI. First Amendment retaliation: likely the cleanest merits theory

The opposition should not reduce the claim to the words "ringleader" or "abhorrent."

Those statements are **motive evidence**.

The adverse acts should be pled / defended as:

- allegedly false no-show records;
- adverse Form 3043 reporting;
- negative treatment-status recommendations;
- use of official parole/treatment reporting mechanisms to burden protected conduct.

## A. Element map

1. **Protected conduct:** complaints, grievances, litigation, petitioning, challenges to government-imposed treatment policy.
2. **Adverse action:** false official treatment reporting / adverse supervision input.
3. **Causation:** chronology, knowledge, hostile statements, repetition, departure from objective records.
4. **Chilling:** objective "ordinary firmness" test; actual persistence does not defeat the claim.
5. **No legitimate correctional goal:** truthful treatment reporting may serve a legitimate purpose; knowingly false treatment reporting in retaliation for protected activity does not.

## B. Key cases

### *Watison v. Carter*, 668 F.3d 1108 (9th Cir. 2012)
The portfolio identifies this as the closest published factual analogue: false disciplinary paperwork + false statements to parole authorities following protected grievances.

### *Brodheim v. Cry*, 584 F.3d 1262 (9th Cir. 2009)
Actual suppression is unnecessary. The inquiry is whether the challenged action would chill a person of ordinary firmness.

### *Pratt v. Rowland*, 65 F.3d 802 (9th Cir. 1995)
Retaliation prohibition clearly established; timing is relevant circumstantial evidence; legitimate-goal element remains important.

### *Wicklund v. Idaho Department of Correction*, 623 F. App'x 338 (9th Cir. 2015)
Unpublished but unusually useful. A supervised person engaged in litigation / speech outside treatment and was threatened with incarceration. The Ninth Circuit treated the external petitioning activity as protected and affirmed denial of QI on the retaliation theory.

The factual distinction to emphasize against defense treatment-regulation cases:

> Regulation of conduct genuinely necessary to therapy is different from using supervision or treatment machinery to punish external petitioning, litigation, grievances, or policy criticism.

# VII. Qualified immunity: force the threshold question first

Do **not** argue categorically that a private actor can never receive QI.

Instead use two steps.

## Step 1 — Is Smith entitled to invoke QI at all?

The most useful fresh Ninth Circuit authority is:

### *Estate of Esche v. Bunuel-Jordana*, Ninth Circuit (Sept. 29, 2025)

The court explains that most private-party § 1983 defendants do not automatically receive qualified immunity and collects the private-contractor cases. It distinguishes private firms / standing contractors from the temporary individual adjunct in *Filarsky*.

Source:
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/09/29/24-6244.pdf

Useful comparison:

- ***Richardson v. McKnight*, 521 U.S. 399 (1997):** standing private prison firm personnel — no QI.
- ***Jensen*:** private contract psychiatrist — no QI.
- ***Halvorsen v. Baird*, 146 F.3d 680:** private detox contractor — no QI.
- ***Ace Beverage Co. v. Lockheed Information Management Services*, 144 F.3d 1218:** private contractor — no QI.
- ***Filarsky v. Delia*, 566 U.S. 377 (2012):** temporarily retained individual assisting government — QI available.

The complaint's theory places Smith at the head of a standing, multi-site private contractor, much closer to the *Richardson* firm model than *Filarsky*'s one-off individual consultant.

## Step 2 — If QI is available, was the retaliatory right clearly established?

Use the combined line:

- *Pratt*
- *Brodheim*
- *Watison*
- *Wicklund*

Do not define the right at the hyper-specific level of "a California CASOMB CEO falsifying Form 3043 on a particular date."

A defensible level of specificity is:

> whether an actor participating in community supervision may knowingly use materially false official reporting and supervision consequences to punish a supervised person's protected petitioning / grievance activity.

## *Chavez v. Robinson*, 12 F.4th 978 (9th Cir. 2021)

Treat *Chavez* carefully.

- It ultimately **granted QI**.
- It assumed state action rather than deciding that every private sex-offender therapist is a state actor.
- It does not announce categorical QI for all private treatment contractors.
- Distinguish retaliation tied to genuine therapy-management conduct from retaliation for external protected petitioning plus false official reporting.

Never cite *Chavez* 2021 as a QI denial.

# VIII. Due process: narrower constitutional theory

Do **not** argue a freestanding federal right to "graduate SOMP after one year."

That overstates the law.

The stronger theory is:

> Once California establishes an annual treatment-completion / continuation process and says continued compulsory treatment must be supported by good cause and written reasons through an integrated CTM process, Defendants may not constitutionally perpetuate the liberty burden through a predetermined blanket rule or knowingly false information instead of the individualized process the State created.

## A. Why § 3574 matters

Current § 3574 uses mandatory procedural language for retention documentation and creates an individualized good-cause process.

This can support the due-process analysis in two ways:

1. identifying the state-created decisional framework;
2. showing the difference between legitimate individualized discretion and an alleged predetermined categorical result.

But **violation of a state regulation alone is not a federal constitutional violation**. The opposition must separately identify the liberty interest and process due.

## B. Authorities

### *Neal v. Shimoda*, 131 F.3d 818 (9th Cir. 1997)
Useful for stigma + compulsory sex-offender treatment + liberty consequences, but factually distinguish because Neal involved classification of a person who had not been convicted of a sex offense. Do not oversell.

### *Morrissey v. Brewer*, 408 U.S. 471
Parole carries a protected conditional liberty interest.

### *Vitek v. Jones*, 445 U.S. 480
Compelled treatment / stigma can implicate liberty.

### *Board of Pardons v. Allen*, 482 U.S. 369
Mandatory language + substantive predicates can create a protected expectation despite decisional discretion.

Potential amendment proffer if the complaint is too general:

- current § 3574 annual review process;
- exact CTM dates;
- the Form 3043 retention decisions;
- applicable risk scores and identified criteria;
- alleged false inputs;
- alleged blanket-rule substitution for individual assessment;
- resulting compulsory treatment / supervision burdens.

This is a strong fallback for leave to amend.

# IX. Bane Act: deliberate falsification is the key factual distinction

Defense preview: no threat / intimidation / coercion.

Response:

- ***Cornell v. City & County of San Francisco*, 17 Cal.App.5th 766 (2017)** rejects a categorical requirement that coercion be independent from the underlying constitutional violation.
- ***Reese v. County of Sacramento*, 888 F.3d 1030 (9th Cir. 2018)** follows *Cornell* and focuses on specific intent.
- ***Shoyoye*** remains defense ammunition when the challenged conduct looks like negligence, mistake, or ordinary administrative error.

Therefore the pleading must sharply distinguish:

> clerical mistake

from

> knowingly false reporting deliberately used as leverage through parole / treatment machinery to interfere with protected activity.

The Bane theory is strongest where the coercive mechanism is **the threat and use of liberty-affecting parole reporting**, not speech standing alone.

Do not request the § 52.1(b) $25,000 public-enforcement civil penalty as a private remedy.

| Intended reliance | |

If a row cannot be completed from existing pleadings / evidence, do not overclaim it.

Potentially stronger theory:
- concrete completion / graduation representations made to Plaintiff;
- Plaintiff acted in reliance by attending, completing required work, incurring burdens, etc.

Negligent misrepresentation remains weaker than intentional fraud unless a direct statement and duty to Plaintiff can be identified.

# XI. RICO after *Horn*: property injury improved; proximate cause did not

## A. What *Medical Marijuana, Inc. v. Horn* helps

In 2025, the Supreme Court rejected a categorical rule barring RICO recovery for business/property losses merely because those losses flow from personal injury.

Source:
https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-365_6k47.pdf

This helps against an argument that lost wages / employment losses are automatically noncognizable because the causal chain also contains liberty or personal injuries.

Pair with:

### *Diaz v. Gates*, 420 F.3d 897 (9th Cir. 2005) (en banc)
Lost employment / wages can constitute injury to business or property.

## B. Distinctness

***Cedric Kushner Promotions, Ltd. v. King*, 533 U.S. 158 (2001)** supports:

- Smith as RICO "person";
- HOPE corporation as distinct enterprise.

Source:
https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/533/158/USSC_PRO_533_158_00-549

So person/enterprise distinctness is not the best defense target if that is how the complaint is structured.

## C. Proximate cause remains the Achilles' heel

The current billing-centered theory appears to be:

> fraudulent billing to CDCR → continued treatment / revenue → continued supervision burden → lost trucking work / wages.

That is vulnerable because the most immediate victim of billing fraud is **CDCR**, not Plaintiff.

Use as warnings:

### *Anza v. Ideal Steel Supply Corp.*, 547 U.S. 451
RICO requires a direct causal relationship.

### *Canyon County v. Syngenta Seeds, Inc.*, 519 F.3d 969 (9th Cir. 2008)
Ninth Circuit applies the direct-injury requirement and rejects downstream public-cost injuries.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/06-35112/0635112-2011-02-25.html

### *Ratfield v. United States Drug Testing Laboratories*
Post-*Horn* warning: economic/employment consequences do not erase RICO's separate direct-causation requirement.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/24-1858/24-1858-2025-06-13.html

### *Painters & Allied Trades District Council 82 Health Care Fund v. Takeda*, 943 F.3d 1243 (9th Cir. 2019)
Useful contrast. The payor plaintiffs themselves spent money because the fraudulent scheme directly caused the purchases. The direct economic victim was before the court.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/18-55588/18-55588-2019-12-03.html

## D. Opposition posture

Do not let RICO control the credibility of the whole case.

Defend what can be defended:

- person/enterprise distinctness;
- pattern if specifically pleaded;
- predicate particulars actually contained in the complaint;
- cognizable property injury after *Horn* / *Diaz*.

On proximate cause, do not pretend *Horn* overruled *Anza*. If the filed complaint does not connect a predicate directly to Plaintiff's business/property loss, the serious fallback is **leave to amend**.

Research question for amendment:
Can any alleged mail/wire predicate be framed as directly obtaining Plaintiff's property, causing his employment loss, or directly inducing conduct producing the business loss — rather than merely defrauding CDCR?

If no, RICO should remain secondary.

# XII. Habeas / Heck defensive point

Current California rules separate SOMP completion from the ultimate controlling discharge date. Successful treatment completion does not automatically equal immediate discharge from parole.

That matters if Defendants later argue that any challenge belongs exclusively in habeas.

Under ***Wilkinson v. Dotson*, 544 U.S. 74 (2005)**, § 1983 remains available where success would produce a lawful procedure or remove an unlawful condition but would not necessarily produce immediate or speedier release.

Matter 28 should avoid pleading relief that necessarily implies invalidity of parole itself. The cleaner theory attacks unlawful treatment-retention / reporting practices and seeks damages / process consistent with the current statutory structure.

---

# XIII. Judge Cisneros: drafting implications

Recent Cisneros rulings reviewed in the research pass suggest:

1. she evaluates Rule 12 claims **individually**, not as an all-or-nothing case;
2. she gives meaningful leave to amend where a defect may be cured;
3. she does not allow an opposition to substitute thousands of pages of material for well-pled allegations;
4. a pro se plaintiff still must comply with Rule 8 / Rule 9(b), but curable defects ordinarily point toward amendment rather than prejudice.

Drafting consequence:

- No discovery dump.
- No 542-page production appendix.
- Quote or cite only the few record items necessary to address judicial notice / preclusion.
- Use discovery-produced facts primarily as a **Rule 15 amendment proffer**, unless they are already fairly incorporated into the complaint.
- Ask in the alternative for targeted leave and identify the exact facts that would cure each alleged deficiency.

# XV. Drafting posture / tone

Avoid:

- "everything is frivolous";
- oversized rhetoric;
- treating *Dwyer* as a judgment against HOPE/Smith;
- categorical "private defendants never receive QI";
- "state-law violation = due-process violation";
- "Horn solved RICO proximate cause";
- relying on facts absent from the complaint without a leave-to-amend fallback.

Prefer:

> The state court decided exhaustion, not the federal merits.

> Defendants' state-action argument abstracts the challenged conduct away from the statutory and regulatory machinery in which it occurs.

> The alleged adverse action is not criticism of Plaintiff; it is knowingly false official reporting to actors controlling compulsory treatment and parole conditions.

> Whether those reports were false, retaliatory, policy-driven, clinically justified, or causative presents merits questions that cannot be resolved against the pleader at Rule 12.

> If the Court concludes any count lacks sufficient detail, the existing documentary record demonstrates that amendment is not futile and identifies the specific curative allegations available.

# XVII. Immediate drafting checklist when MTD files

1. Capture the motion, RJN, exhibits, declaration, and proposed order.
2. Build a **defense-authority table**:
   - proposition claimed;
   - actual holding;
   - posture;
   - factual similarity;
   - whether binding;
   - answer / distinction.
3. Build a **complaint paragraph map** for each motion heading.
4. Separate:
   - facts already pleaded;
   - incorporated documents;
   - judicially noticeable public materials;
   - facts available only for amendment.
5. Run a cite verification pass on every load-bearing authority.
6. Build the Rule 15 cure chart simultaneously with the opposition.
7. Keep preclusion evidence narrow: state minute order, formal order, register / judgment status.
8. Do not make the opposition an evidentiary merits submission.
9. Preserve all claims worth preserving but do not let RICO / weak misrepresentation theories obscure the constitutional core.
10. Ask for denial; alternatively, targeted leave to amend with an identified cure.

# XIX. Bottom line

The deep-research pass materially changes the strategic center of Matter 28.

The strongest version of this lawsuit is **not** "a parolee suing a therapist over treatment disagreements."

It is:

> **a § 1983 action against a private corporate participant embedded in California's statutory parole-treatment decision machinery, alleging that the corporation and its chief policymaker substituted a categorical retention rule for the individualized determination that system requires and then used allegedly false official reporting to punish protected activity and perpetuate compulsory treatment / supervision consequences.**

That formulation strengthens state action, Monell, retaliation, Bane, and the answer to the *Merrill / Gross* line simultaneously.

The opposition should make Defendants win every gateway separately. Preclusion does not adjudicate the federal merits; private status does not defeat state action where the challenged conduct is integrated into state decisional machinery; *Monell* focuses the corporate claim on policy rather than respondeat superior; retaliation focuses on allegedly false official reporting rather than mere criticism; QI must first be available to this kind of standing private contractor before "clearly established" is reached; and any remaining pleading defects should be addressed through specific leave to amend rather than case-wide dismissal with prejudice.

RICO should remain secondary unless a direct-injury predicate theory can be honestly pleaded.
