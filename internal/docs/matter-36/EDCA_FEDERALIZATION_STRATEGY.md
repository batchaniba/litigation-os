
# Matter 36 — E.D. Cal. Federalization Strategy

## Executive assessment

Matter 36 is one of the cleaner employment-screening candidates for federal court because:
- the transaction occurred in May 2026;
- First Advantage is confirmed in the transaction itself;
- AT&T issued a formal written offer before the rescission;
- Plaintiff accepted and began onboarding;
- FCRA supplies original federal-question jurisdiction if the consumer-report use allegation is supportable.

The main weakness is factual, not jurisdictional:

**Did First Advantage actually furnish any report/result to AT&T before the 12:23 p.m. rescission?**

If no, the federal screening counts may fail, leaving only state contract/estoppel theories.

## Proposed opening counts

1. FCRA § 1681b(b)(3)(A)
2. ICRAA §§ 1786.40 / 1786.50
3. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) / (j)(4)(A)
4. Breach of Contract
5. Promissory Estoppel

## Claims deliberately omitted for now

### FCRA § 1681b(b)(2)(A)
Reserved until actual disclosure/authorization is inspected.

### ICRAA § 1786.16
Reserved until actual California investigative-report disclosure/authorization is inspected.

### CCRAA
Do not plead unless the actual product qualifies as a covered consumer credit report and the statutory theory is distinct from ICRAA/FCRA.

### UCL
Omitted to avoid a separate Article III/restitution/injunction fight in the opening federal complaint.

### Fraud / negligent misrepresentation
Omitted initially because they add Rule 9(b), reliance, knowledge, and economic-loss fights without improving the strongest federal theory.

### FEHA / Fair Chance Act
Reserved unless administrative prerequisites and underlying factual elements are satisfied.

## Federal jurisdiction

FCRA creates federal-question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331.

Supplemental jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1367 can carry the related California claims.

Do not rely on diversity unless the citizenship of every member of AT&T Mobility Services LLC is actually established.

## Venue

The offered job was in Lodi, San Joaquin County.

Current E.D. Cal. Local Rule 120 guidance places San Joaquin County civil actions on the Sacramento side.

## FCRA adverse-action theory

Section 1681b(b)(3)(A) requires a report copy and written Summary of Rights before adverse action based in whole or in part on a consumer report.

The complaint must allege:
1. procurement of a consumer report;
2. AT&T receipt/use;
3. adverse action;
4. report was a factor in whole or in part;
5. no pre-adverse report/rights package.

The present record strongly establishes adverse action and nonreceipt.

The unresolved elements are report creation/furnishing and causation.

## Article III standing — Dutta problem

Dutta is the defense case to expect.

The federal complaint should not allege only:
> AT&T failed to send a report.

It should allege concrete harm:
- accepted job lost;
- deprivation of the statutory opportunity to review the report before the decision;
- inability to identify and challenge any California-registry source;
- inability to tell AT&T that California law prohibited employment use of § 290.46-disclosed information before the position was withdrawn.

Still, if AT&T proves the Spanish assessment independently and unavoidably disqualified Plaintiff, Dutta becomes substantially more dangerous.

## Spanish explanation

This is the central defense fact.

Defense theory:
- role was bilingual;
- Plaintiff failed required Spanish test;
- failure alone disqualified;
- First Advantage had nothing to do with rescission.

Plaintiff-side response:
- LTI assessment occurred before the May 28 formal offer;
- LTI stated rating would go to AT&T recruiting system;
- three days elapsed before AT&T issued the offer;
- offer did not identify Spanish passage as an outstanding post-offer contingency;
- onboarding began;
- First Advantage screening then started;
- rescission followed approximately two hours later.

That sequence does not prove pretext.

It does make the following discovery essential:
- exact score;
- exact threshold;
- score receipt time;
- whether recruiter/manager knew score before 10:13;
- whether offer was automated despite a failing score;
- report result receipt time;
- every internal reason code.

## § 290.46 theory

Current statute prohibits use of information disclosed through California's Megan's Law website for employment, absent statutory authorization.

The claim must remain source-specific.

### Strong version
AT&T-specific First Advantage report shows:
- sex-offender component;
- California registry source;
- adverse/decisional status;
- AT&T access/use;
- rescission follows.

### Weak / losing version
Report shows:
- ordinary court-record criminal history as source;
- registry search clear/no-record;
- no registry information used.

Donaleski permits independent investigation and use of independently obtained information.

Lerma is the caution that an actual incorporated report can defeat a registry-source allegation when it contradicts the theory.

## ICRAA

ICRAA is attractive because California provides a statutory remedy under § 1786.50.

Parsonage v. Wal-Mart Associates (2026) confirms California statutory standing and the $10,000 remedy without requiring separate concrete injury for ICRAA under California law.

In federal court, however, Article III still applies. Matter 36 has concrete adverse employment loss, so it is stronger than a purely technical ICRAA disclosure case.

The initial federal count should focus on § 1786.40 adverse-action advice.

Reserve § 1786.16 until the actual form is reviewed.

## Contract / estoppel

These counts matter because they do not depend on First Advantage.

If screening counts fail because no report existed before 12:23, the federal case could lose original jurisdiction, but the underlying employment-offer dispute still exists.

Before filing:
- audit offer for at-will language;
- withdrawal rights;
- incorporated policy;
- arbitration;
- conditions precedent;
- integration;
- weekly-hours ambiguity.

Do not overstate expectancy damages where weekly hours are unspecified.

## Limitations

### FCRA
15 U.S.C. § 1681p generally uses the earlier of:
- two years after discovery of the violation; or
- five years after the violation.

The May 2026 transaction is facially recent, but the complaint should identify the truthful discovery event.

### ICRAA
Civil Code § 1786.52 provides two years from discovery.

Again, 2026 timing is favorable, but the discovery chronology should be recorded.

### § 290.46
No clearly settled published limitations rule located for this private civil remedy.

Do not make the penalty component the sole limitations theory.

### Contract / estoppel
No immediate limitations problem from a May 2026 transaction.

## Bottom line

Matter 36 is ready for a **federal review draft**, but not a filing recommendation until the report timing is pinned down.

The decisive question is not whether First Advantage was the vendor.

That is confirmed.

The decisive question is:

> **What, if anything, did First Advantage furnish to AT&T between 10:19 a.m. and 12:23 p.m. on May 28, 2026, and did AT&T use it?**
