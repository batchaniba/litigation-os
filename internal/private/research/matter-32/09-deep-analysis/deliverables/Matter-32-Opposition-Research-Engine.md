## Executive decision

### The principal strategic change

The existing narrative puts too much weight on four supposed contradictions, especially the urine cup and “beverage present” field. The production does not support treating those as four established falsehoods: the form field concerns breath odor, the actual warrant does not expressly contain two of the challenged propositions, initial confusion can coexist with later orientation, and silence about smell is not an audiovisual disproof of smell. Record basis: FAC ¶¶ 26–30, 40–46; R195 PDF pp. 2, 12, 20–21; R183, plaintiff-prepared transcript.

The replacement architecture should separate the earliest physical intrusion from later detention, arrest, warrant, parole, and licensing decisions. That matters because vehicle entry requires its own justification, later-acquired knowledge cannot retroactively supply an earlier justification, and a civil-rights damages action does not automatically exclude everything learned following an unlawful search. ([Ngumezi, 980 F.3d 1285](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf); [Estrella, No. 22-10027, June 6, 2023](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/06/06/22-10027.pdf); [Lingo, 832 F.3d 953](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/08/08/14-35344.pdf))

## The governing architecture

### Separate the decisions, not just the constitutional labels

Use a decision-time ledger rather than a single “encounter lacked probable cause” paragraph. Each decision has a different factual cutoff, legal rule, defendant, injury, and counterfactual.

| Decision | Relevant record cutoff | Required analytical question | Improper shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approach and observation | Before 00:56:43 | What could officers see from a lawful vantage, and was anyone seized? | Calling every approach a search or arrest. |
| Door opening | Allegedly 00:56:43 | Was there consent or an objectively reasonable exception at that instant? | Using post-entry odor or later parole information to justify the opening. |
| Cup handling | Allegedly 00:58:49–00:59:07 | What justified physical examination, and what was its reasonable scope? | Assuming lawful welfare access authorizes every evidentiary inspection. |
| Continued search/detention | After cup identification, split again at parole knowledge | What grounds remained, what new grounds arose, and what specifically continued? | “Urine means every suspicion vanished.” |
| Arrest | FAC ¶¶ 24, 50, 67; report says approximately 01:12 | What facts actually known then supported any arrestable offense? | Requiring new evidence during the last 18 seconds if adequate facts already existed. |
| Blood warrant | Declaration 02:30:22; signature 02:34:50 | Does the real affidavit, after defensible corrections, still establish probable cause? | Correcting an imaginary affidavit or importing later lab results. |
| Blood extraction | Reported 02:38 | Was the warrant validly obtained and executed, and what injury followed? | Treating McNeely as a ban on warrant-authorized blood tests. |
| Parole confinement | Requires hold and decision records | Which false fact, if any, caused an additional restraint beyond independently valid parole action? | Assigning all custody to one officer merely because arrest came first. |
| DMV action | DS-367 submission, review, stay, hearing and final order | Which finding, supported by which challenged information, caused suspension or CDL disqualification? | Treating a refusal suspension as necessarily based on BAC ≥ .08. |

The civil exclusionary-rule issue is especially important: Lingo rejected using the exclusionary rule to convert an arrest supported by probable cause into a § 1983 false-arrest claim merely because the probable cause was discovered through an allegedly unlawful search. Preserve the earlier search as its own injury, and analyze the later arrest on the information then known without asserting automatic suppression. ([Lingo, 832 F.3d at 958–60](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/08/08/14-35344.pdf))

### Apply pleading discipline in both directions

Khoja warns against incorporation by reference being used to resolve factual disputes against a complaint, while Grier explains why specific allegations are not automatically displaced by non-legally-operative documents. A report saying an officer smelled alcohol establishes that the officer made that report; where a sufficiently specific allegation disputes its truth, incorporation is not a license to find the officer truthful at Rule 12. ([Khoja, 899 F.3d 988](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf); [Grier, No. 21-16702, Jan. 20, 2023, n.1](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/01/20/21-16702.pdf))

That principle does not allow the plaintiff to misread a form, deny that the actual warrant contains a paragraph, or transform a report's silence into a sensor measurement. The opposition must distinguish the existence and wording of an instrument, disputed historical observations within it, and the legal materiality of a properly corrected version; Khoja and Grier support that distinction, not blanket exclusion of unfavorable documents. ([Khoja](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf); [Grier](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/01/20/21-16702.pdf))

The operative FAC has 21 pages and six counts; the exact current paragraphs, not earlier case numbering or prior drafts, must control every response. Extra-pleading production is chiefly an amendment and proof resource unless a proper consideration mechanism applies, and an opposition should not silently amend the FAC through an evidentiary narrative.

## Counts IV and V: maximize the independent vehicle-intrusion claims

### Count IV: door opening against Melano

The current claim is at FAC ¶¶ 54–58, against Melano and Does. Paragraph 58 mentions Meza's presence and nonintervention, but the count heading does not make him a named direct defendant to Count IV; any proposed expansion requires an express amendment and a supported personal-participation theory.

Ngumezi supplies the threshold proposition that opening the passenger door and leaning into a vehicle is a Fourth Amendment search, not merely a convenient method of conversation. It does not establish that every door opening to check a sleeping roadside occupant is unconstitutional or defeat every emergency-aid justification. ([Ngumezi, 980 F.3d at 1288–90](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf))

The FAC alleges ordinary resting posture, an intact vehicle, no visible crash, smoke or fire, and no consent; those are meaningful facts to apply to the need for immediate aid. But the absence of a collision is not the absence of a medical emergency, and officers need not wait for proof of serious injury before rendering objectively reasonable emergency aid. ([Bonivert, 883 F.3d 865](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/02/26/15-35292.pdf))

The most useful additional factual questions are the exact number and duration of knocks, observable breathing or movement, whether the occupant responded before the door opened, what the officers could actually see, and whether the door was opened before or after an apparent response. These are unresolved evidence questions, not facts this memorandum supplies.

### Reject the parking/emergency false binary without overclaiming

MTD printed pp. 8–9 argues, in substance, that the shoulder stop was either an emergency permitting entry or an unlawful stop supplying probable cause. Section 21718(a)(1) expressly excepts stopping “[w]hen necessary to avoid injury or damage to persons or property,” so the statute does not reduce every situation to a presently incapacitated occupant or unlawful parking. ([Cal. Veh. Code § 21718(a)(1)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=21718.))

The analytical answer is limited: a fatigue-related stop can be relevant to that exception without automatically creating an immediate medical emergency that authorizes physical entry. Necessity remains fact-dependent, and the FAC's CDL/HOS discussion does not establish a blanket federal right to park an ordinary rental Tesla on a freeway shoulder.

Even if an offense justified investigation or an arrest, the physical vehicle search still requires an applicable search exception, with facts fitting that exception. Ngumezi is the verified vehicle-entry anchor; the motion's bracketed expansion of Devenpeck to “[or search]” should be addressed as an unsupported analytical extension, not an accusation of misconduct or an attempt to nullify all automobile-exception doctrine. ([Ngumezi](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf))

### Count V: treat the cup examination and later searches separately

FAC ¶¶ 59–63 names both officers and alleges that Melano physically examined the cup while Meza directed plaintiff's attention and movements. The opposition should explain why the initial physical cup inspection allegedly exceeded any welfare-related access, while addressing the defense that a cup perceived as alcohol, surrounding circumstances, and any legitimately known odor supplied automobile-search probable cause.

Do not argue that discovery of urine retroactively invalidated an inspection supported by adequate facts before the inspection. Its strongest role is prospective: after the perceived source was identified as urine, what independent grounds justified the next specified intrusion or continuation? Sialoi supports attention to information that dissipates the basis for detention, but its toy-weapon facts do not establish that identifying this cup as urine necessarily eliminated all DUI suspicion. ([Sialoi, 823 F.3d 1223](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/05/24/14-55387.pdf))

The present Count V is less specific about what physical search occurred after the cup exchange. Amendment should identify each additional act, approximate time, officer, place or item, and the state of information at that moment; otherwise the “continuing search” theory risks remaining conclusory.

### The parole boundary is both an opportunity and a major defense

Estrella requires advance knowledge, supported by probable cause, that the person is on active parole and that an applicable condition authorizes the challenged search or seizure. It does not require certainty to the minute, and it affirms the search on its own facts. ([Estrella, slip op. pp. 4–5, 14–18](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/06/06/22-10027.pdf))

R193's 01:07:29 entry potentially strengthens the chronology for the earlier door/cup events while creating a substantial alternative justification for later searches. The correct argument is “later parole knowledge cannot retroactively justify an earlier search,” not “parole never matters because the officers initially announced a welfare check”; Payne also illustrates the breadth of a valid California parole-search condition. ([Estrella](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/06/06/22-10027.pdf); [Payne, No. 22-50262, Apr. 17, 2024](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/04/17/22-50262.pdf))

Obtain the applicable conditions, actual communication records, and exact receipt time before making an absolute no-knowledge allegation. A parole-search condition is not, without further analysis, a warrantless arrest authorization for every alleged offense or a license for material dishonesty in a subsequent affidavit.

## Count I: reconstruct the real warrant, not the report

### The current pleading and its repair

FAC ¶¶ 38–42 names Meza and Does. Paragraph 40 alleges on information and belief that the DS-367 and arrest report were submitted or caused to be submitted as the warrant's evidentiary basis, but the newly available production supplies an actual two-page affidavit that permits a more precise allegation.

Chism requires removing defensibly false statements and adding material omitted facts, then deciding whether the corrected affidavit still supports probable cause; materiality is a legal question. Its substantial-showing language arises at summary judgment and should not be imported as a requirement to prove the case in response to a Rule 12 motion. ([Chism, amended Nov. 7, 2011, 661 F.3d 380](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/10-35085/10-35085-2011-11-07.pdf?ts=1411064324))

Galbraith rejects an extra heightened motive-pleading regime but does not displace modern plausibility pleading. Benavidez supplies the safer contemporary response: plead the who, what, when, where, why false, and material consequence with enough specificity to satisfy even the particularity standard discussed there; do not claim that Benavidez categorically holds Rule 9(b) inapplicable. ([Galbraith, 307 F.3d 1119](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/307/1119/521840/); [Benavidez, 993 F.3d 1134, slip op. pp. 14–23](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2021/04/12/19-55274.pdf))

### Actual affidavit inventory

- **Discovery:** At approximately 00:55, a Tesla was parked on the I-580 shoulder; plaintiff was asleep, wearing an orange 21+ wristband.
- **Contact:** Meza says he knocked at the right-front window, plaintiff woke, and appeared confused and disoriented.
- **Odor inside:** Meza says he could smell a distinct alcoholic-beverage odor emitting from within the vehicle through the open passenger door.
- **Orientation and driving:** Plaintiff allegedly first said he was in a parking lot, had been parked about seven minutes, was alone, and had driven from a party in Oakland.
- **Denials:** Plaintiff's denial of alcohol before stopping and denial of alcohol after stopping are already included.
- **Personal odor and FSTs:** Meza alleges alcohol odor from breath and person and failure to perform FSTs as instructed or demonstrated.
- **After arrest:** Meza describes approximately 01:12 arrest, implied-consent advisement, choice of breath/blood/refusal, administrative consequences, and refusal of chemical testing.

The affidavit was declared at 02:30:22, and Judge Amy Sekany signed the warrant at 02:34:50. CHP 202 records blood collection at 02:38; these are document statements, not independent verification of clock synchronization or execution details.

### Candid materiality assessment

On the present production, deleting an imaginary cup assertion and adding urine/ground conditions does not demonstrate that the actual odor, initial confusion, recent-driving and FST narrative fails probable cause. The most valuable investigation is a test-by-test and statement-by-statement comparison with the original recording, not a stronger adjective applied to the current four-contradiction formula.

A viable amendment needs a particular material assertion that can truthfully be alleged false or misleading, facts permitting an inference that Meza knew or recklessly disregarded that, and a corrected narrative whose remaining facts actually change the warrant decision. Chism does not excuse materiality simply because the dispute is fact-sensitive. ([Chism](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/10-35085/10-35085-2011-11-07.pdf?ts=1411064324))

For QI, Chism's judicial-deception analysis is more focused than a generic “reasonable officers can disagree about probable cause” discussion: if material intentional or reckless judicial deception is established under the applicable rule, its QI analysis substantially overlaps that misconduct inquiry. Benavidez's majority and concurrence should not be blurred, especially the concurrence's qualification about reckless rather than affirmative knowing deception. ([Chism, n.15](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/10-35085/10-35085-2011-11-07.pdf?ts=1411064324); [Benavidez, majority slip op. pp. 28–31, concurrence pp. 39–45](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2021/04/12/19-55274.pdf))

## Count II: answer the no-charges argument without ignoring constitutional channeling

### What the defense actually argues

MTD printed p. 7 argues that the absence of criminal charges defeats fabrication and cites Caldwell and Ninth Circuit Model Instruction 9.38. The response should engage those authorities rather than incorrectly say the motion offers no authority or that any fabricated report, by itself, completes a constitutional tort.

Caldwell states that a conviction is unnecessary because being charged is sufficient, but its charged-and-convicted plaintiff did not present a no-charge civil-license claim. The holding should not be inverted from a sufficient injury into an exclusive list; it also requires real fabrication and a causal connection rather than liability for every inaccurate note. ([Caldwell, 889 F.3d 1105](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/05/11/16-15473.pdf))

The current instruction's opening refers to criminal charges, but its operative alternatives expressly include “[other deprivation of liberty],” and its comments discuss fabrication in civil dependency proceedings. That directly undermines treating the instruction as a universal rule that no indictment means no possible fabrication claim, although an instruction is not a substitute for controlling decisions or proof of this plaintiff's specific deprivation. ([Ninth Circuit Instruction 9.38, revised June 2025](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/jury-instructions/civil/9-38-particular-rights-fourteenth-amendment-due-process-deliberate-fabrication-of-evidence.pdf))

Costanich recognizes a due-process fabrication right in civil child-abuse proceedings involving protected liberty or property interests. It provides a substantial doctrinal answer to the categorical criminal-only premise, not a holding that all driver-license disputes or all inaccurate sworn forms necessarily satisfy § 1983. ([Costanich, 627 F.3d at 1108–16](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/180346/costanich-v-dept-of-social-and-health-services/))

### Use the distinct DMV injury already alleged

FAC ¶¶ 37 and 45 already allege a DS-367-based deprivation of commercial driving privileges. This is not a new invented claim introduced through the opposition; it is an existing alleged injury that needs better causal and procedural precision.

R181's DMV discovery packet states that the § 13353 suspension effective December 1, 2024 was stayed pending hearing, and a January 24, 2025 hearing was scheduled. R180's earlier driving record identifies a refusal-related APS entry and a separate commercial disqualification entry under § 15300(a)(9); it is a December 2024 snapshot, not the final 2025 determination.

Section 13353 concerns refusal of the required post-arrest chemical test and specifies the review findings, including reasonable cause, arrest, refusal, and warning. The causation theory therefore must identify which false statement affected which indispensable finding; it cannot assume that a sub-.08 later ethanol result independently eliminates a refusal suspension. ([Cal. Veh. Code § 13353](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=13353.); [Troppman v. Valverde, S132496, Apr. 26, 2007](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/troppman-v-valverde-33720/))

The 2024 Doe opinion describes APS as a separate administrative determination with procedural safeguards and recognizes the driver's property interest through the authorities it discusses. It does not decide individual-officer fabrication liability, and its holding on disclosure/privacy is not this case's cause of action. ([Doe v. Cal. DMV, A164843, June 21, 2024, published portions](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2024/a164843.html))

### Constitutional channel and causation

Galbraith, a defense-cited authority, is adverse to simply repackaging the same pretrial seizure injury as substantive due process where the Fourth Amendment supplies the specific protection. Preserve the independent license/property theory and identify any genuinely distinct deprivation rather than defend an undifferentiated Fourth/Fourteenth duplication. ([Galbraith, 307 F.3d at 1127–28](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/307/1119/521840/))

Spencer requires deliberate fabrication plus causation, rejects negligence or trivial discrepancy as sufficient, and explains why lack of probable cause is not automatically an additional element of its fabrication claim. It expressly limits what it decides about charging-only damages, so neither side should turn it into a universal causation shortcut. ([Spencer, 857 F.3d 789, slip op. pp. 14–24](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2017/05/18/14-35689.pdf))

Richards qualifies any assertion that strict but-for factual causation invariably governs every fabrication injury: in its criminal fair-trial setting, it applied a less demanding materiality test asking whether fabricated evidence could reasonably have affected the jury. Its limiting language concerns the circumstances needed to vindicate the fair-trial right, and it does not decide a no-charge administrative-license case; preserve a supported analogy if useful, but do not silently import a jury standard into DMV review or treat Spencer as the only possible formulation. ([Richards, 39 F.4th 562, slip op. pp. 17–21 & n.9](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/06/24/19-56205.pdf))

For this case, build separate chains: challenged statement → warrant decision → bodily intrusion; challenged statement → identified parole decision → added confinement; challenged statement → particular DMV finding → actual suspension/CDL disqualification → proven earnings loss. Each chain can fail independently, and a document's presence in a file is not proof it caused the decision.

The practical proffer should show concrete use and outcome significance under the strongest supported standard while preserving an alternative materiality argument rather than unnecessarily conceding an exclusive strict counterfactual requirement. Multiple contributing false facts cannot be dismissed merely because one additional cause was necessary, which is the error Richards identified in its own trial record. ([Richards, slip op. pp. 20–21](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/06/24/19-56205.pdf))

### QI and recent authority

FAC ¶ 44 overstates Costanich by saying QI is unavailable to officers who deliberately fabricate evidence without addressing context. Costanich actually granted immunity for 2001 conduct while recognizing the civil-context right going forward; the 2024 incident is later, but the DMV analogy and the specific clearly-established right still need to be argued, not assumed. ([Costanich, 627 F.3d at 1114–16](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/180346/costanich-v-dept-of-social-and-health-services/))

Ciria, decided June 5, 2026, is a useful current synthesis of fabrication and its preexisting roots, but causation was waived there and the case concerns coercively fabricated murder evidence, not an uncharged DUI/DMV proceeding. It cannot itself give officers notice in November 2024; rely on earlier holdings for that temporal inquiry. ([Ciria v. Gerrans, No. 24-3308, slip op. pp. 25–29, 38–42 & n.4](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/06/05/24-3308.pdf))

The best requested disposition is denial of a categorical criminal-charges bar, with claim-specific review of the existing independent license theory, or targeted leave to cure fabrication and causation specificity. That is more defensible than asserting Count II necessarily survives because the government wrote an inaccurate document.

## Count III: confront the full arrest record

### The current allegation

FAC ¶¶ 48–53 alleges the arrest against both officers, with Meza as arresting officer and Melano as an integral participant in the joint investigation. Paragraph 53 provides activities beyond mere presence, but amendment should specify Melano's actual communications and contribution to the allegedly unsupported arrest rather than rely on the label “integral participant.”

The relevant question is what facts were actually available at arrest under the totality, not whether each fact individually proves intoxication beyond a reasonable doubt. Wesby rejects a divide-and-conquer treatment of innocent explanations and separately demands specificity in the clearly-established-law analysis. ([District of Columbia v. Wesby, 583 U.S. 48](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/15-1485_1qm2.pdf))

### Improve the plaintiff's theory

The plaintiff's strongest formulation is that specific allegedly false observations cannot be accepted as established merely because the officer later wrote them down, while the actual known countervailing information must also be considered. It should identify which FST failures are disputed, what the recording can actually show, and what concrete facts negate the claimed impairment rather than rely exclusively on conversation being coherent.

Sialoi supports the proposition that officers cannot simply disregard information undermining the basis for a continued seizure. Its application here requires explaining why the remaining genuine facts did not independently sustain the seizure; the urine identification removes a perceived source, not necessarily odor from breath or person, eye observations, FST performance, or recent-driving evidence. ([Sialoi](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/05/24/14-55387.pdf))

The 18-second interval supports sequence, but “no new evidence” is not itself “no probable cause.” An officer may already possess adequate information, and a voluntary test might otherwise have supplied exculpatory information; the opposition must answer that defense rather than assume the timing resolves it.

### Preserve only defensible uses of later events

The § 851.6 certificate and no-charge decision do not retroactively decide what a reasonable officer knew earlier. FAC ¶ 33 already acknowledges the certificate is not independently dispositive; keep that concession, and do not let corroborative language swallow the time-of-arrest test.

The ethanol result was not available at arrest, and the report's 0.073 value cannot be called either a negative result or a definitive reconstruction of earlier driving-time BAC. Its proper immediate use here is accuracy control and honest assessment of the broader record, not retroactive justification of the arrest or an invented scientific exoneration.

No observed vehicle movement is relevant context but not a categorical defeat of reasonable cause. Troppman explains the modern refusal-suspension framework and the statutory developments after Mercer; reliance on Mercer's older arrest-presence formulation without that treatment would be vulnerable. ([Troppman](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/troppman-v-valverde-33720/); [Mercer, 53 Cal.3d 753](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/53/753.html))

## Count VI: protect credibility by recognizing the weakest bridge

### What is actually pleaded

FAC ¶¶ 64–70 and the count heading name both officers, not Meza alone. The factual retaliation narrative principally identifies Meza's PAS advisement and arrest; it does not plead a different protected criticism, request for a supervisor, or lawsuit-related retaliation during this encounter.

Section 23612(h)–(i) distinguishes a voluntary investigative PAS request from the separate chemical-testing obligation after lawful arrest. That distinction prevents conflating the earlier refusal with the later refusal recorded in the affidavit and DMV documents, but it does not by itself establish First Amendment protection for the conduct. ([Cal. Veh. Code § 23612(h)–(i), (a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=23612.&lawCode=VEH))

Ballentine concerns retaliation for actual protected expression and supports the general elements and causation framework on its facts. It does not decide that a statutory right to decline a PAS test is itself First Amendment activity; Moore's treatment of silence and retaliatory investigation is adverse enough that a protected-activity/QI argument should not be asserted at an abstract level. ([Ballentine, 28 F.4th 54](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/03/08/20-16805.pdf); [Moore v. Garnand, No. 22-16236, Sept. 29, 2023](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/09/29/22-16236.pdf))

### Separate the missing elements

- **Protected activity:** Identify a controlling pre-incident basis for treating the actual refusal as First Amendment conduct. A statutory permission, a Fourth Amendment consent issue, and protected speech are not interchangeable categories.
- **But-for retaliation:** Address the defense that the officer described a lawful decision based on existing information when a further voluntary test was declined. Proximity and conditional phrasing permit an argument but do not eliminate competing inferences.
- **Probable cause and Nieves:** If probable cause existed, identify the actual objective evidence needed for an exception. A general statement that compliant drivers are not arrested solely for compliance is not the same as evidence of differential enforcement.
- **QI:** Use pre-November 1, 2024 authorities with a factually meaningful analogy. Do not merely repeat “retaliation has long been unlawful.”
- **Melano:** Identify his own knowledge, conduct, and retaliatory participation if maintaining the claim against him. Joint presence alone is not a substitute.

Gonzalez clarifies that the Nieves exception does not demand virtually identical comparators, but it does not eliminate protected activity, the need for objective evidence, or causation. Treat it as a limit on an overly restrictive comparator requirement, not a universal exemption from probable-cause analysis. ([Gonzalez v. Trevino, 602 U.S. 653](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-1025_1a72.pdf))

The decision recommendation is to avoid leading with Count VI and to preserve any supportable statutory-refusal facts for the Fourth Amendment analysis even if a separate First Amendment claim cannot be cured. Do not add a new protected-speech story unless the original recording or other reliable evidence actually supports it.

## Damages, proof, and narrow relief

### Separate privacy injury from the largest economic number

The wage-loss model in FAC ¶¶ 36–37 and the prayer must not determine which constitutional theory is pleaded. The report-side records identify a stay, separate administrative entries, a medical-certificate expiration date, and a license-expiration date; any actual later renewal or extension must be obtained rather than assuming the December snapshot remains operative.

Build a damages timeline with days actually barred from commercial work, separate ordinary license/CDL restrictions, independent parole or medical restrictions, renewal records, pay records, work availability, mitigation, and the particular constitutional wrong alleged to have caused each loss. The point is not to abandon wages, but to avoid attributing every week since arrest to a document merely because it preceded the loss.

Early door or cup-search injuries should be preserved even if later arrest/warrant or economic causation fails. Lingo makes the need for this separation especially important in a civil action, and Keates illustrates why some claims can fail while others proceed rather than treating a complaint as an indivisible bundle. ([Lingo](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/08/08/14-35344.pdf); [Keates, 883 F.3d 1228](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/03/06/16-16568.pdf))

### Requested disposition hierarchy

- **Primary:** Deny dismissal of adequately pleaded, independently supported claims on the actual plaintiff-favorable facts, without using the disputed report as an adverse factual finding.
- **Alternative:** Limit any dismissal to the deficient claim, defendant, or damages theory instead of treating a weakness in the cup/form theory as a reason to eliminate unrelated conduct.
- **Further alternative:** Grant targeted leave to amend with the actual affidavit, correct form interpretation, refined temporal allegations, precise searches, and individualized causation, subject to truthful evidentiary support.
- **Credibility concession:** Correct the form-field and timeline errors expressly; acknowledge that no charges and the detention certificate do not independently resolve probable cause.
- **Remedies discipline:** Do not use Whittlestone's Rule 12(f) holding as if it creates punitive-damages entitlement or a pro se attorney-fee right. It addresses a procedural strike mechanism and must remain separate from substantive entitlement and prior concessions. ([Whittlestone, 618 F.3d 970](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/08/17/09-16353.pdf))

Keates both permits useful pleading-stage QI analysis and demonstrates that a judicial-deception claim can still fail for missing specific facts. The safe position is not “QI must await discovery,” but “this particular claim cannot be dismissed on a defense-favorable factual premise, while any purely legal deficiency should be identified and, if curable, amended.” ([Keates, 883 F.3d 1228](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/03/06/16-16568.pdf))

## What maximum-value engineering means here

The reusable unit is a claim module containing the defendant, decision time, governing rule, exact pleaded facts, source-supported correction, strongest defense, QI analysis, injury chain, requested disposition, and amendment alternative. The companion matrix and workbenches implement that structure so the eventual brief is assembled from checked components rather than generated from a generalized grievance narrative.

The decisive research investment is original-record verification at the narrow factual bottlenecks: door-opening response sequence, precise FST performance and reporting, actual warrant attachments, parole knowledge, DMV findings, and hold causation. No additional pile of opinions will substitute for those facts, and no missing fact should be filled with confident prose.

### Counterfactual chain worksheet

Every damages theory needs a controlled causal sequence. Fill it using source-backed facts, not a generic assertion that the entire chain was foreseeable; the counterfactual column is an investigation tool, not a concession that strict but-for factual causation is the only possible standard for every fabrication injury.

| Chain | Required false/unauthorized act | Decision and indispensable finding | Why outcome changes without it | Independent alternative to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Door privacy | Unjustified opening at the actual instant | Physical intrusion itself | Entry does not occur | Objective emergency / consent / preexisting other exception. |
| Cup privacy | Unjustified handling/examination | Search itself | Examination does not occur | Automobile exception / valid welfare scope / actual knowledge. |
| Arrest | Material false observation or lack of genuine adequate facts | Custodial arrest for identified offense | No valid arrest on remaining genuine facts | Genuine odor/FSTs/recent driving; alternative lawful offense; other detention authority. |
| Blood draw | Material intentional/reckless affidavit distortion | Magistrate's probable-cause determination | Corrected actual affidavit insufficient | Remaining affidavit facts; actual attachments; execution authority. |
| Parole custody | Identified false information transmitted by defendant | Specific hold / revocation finding | Added restraint would not occur | Independently valid conditions/violation/hold. |
| DMV license | Identified deliberate falsehood used in decision | Specific reasonable-cause/arrest/refusal/warning finding | Suspension/disqualification not imposed absent falsehood | Independent chemical refusal with valid predicates; separate CDL rule. |
| Wages | Above constitutional injury and actual status restriction | Employer inability/refusal to offer work | Lost eligible work would otherwise have occurred | Medical expiry, renewal, unrelated screening, availability, mitigation. |

The distinction between an unlawful search and later false-arrest damages follows Lingo; fabrication additionally requires actual injury and causation under Spencer. These are not interchangeable standards, and a valid independent basis must be assessed within the correct chain. ([Lingo](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/08/08/14-35344.pdf); [Spencer](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2017/05/18/14-35689.pdf))

Richards applies a less demanding factual-causation standard in its criminal fair-trial setting and rejects treating one necessary cause as the only possible cause. Its application to an uncharged administrative-license injury needs separate analysis, so preserve a supported materiality argument rather than substituting either “any use suffices” or “strict but-for always.” ([Richards](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/06/24/19-56205.pdf))

### Argument modules for adaptation

These paragraphs are proposed synthesis, not verbatim text from a court paper. They require final source, record, procedural and citation checks.

#### Separate intrusion from downstream arrest

> The motion collapses several decisions into one probable-cause conclusion. The alleged passenger-door opening and physical cup examination occurred before the later events on which defendants rely, and each intrusion requires its own legally sufficient justification at its own time. Plaintiff does not contend that an earlier unlawful search automatically excludes later facts from a civil false-arrest analysis; Lingo forecloses that shortcut. The earlier privacy claims nevertheless remain independently reviewable, and later information cannot retroactively supply an exception for an intrusion already completed.

The synthesis combines Ngumezi's search threshold, Estrella's advance-knowledge rule in the parole setting, and Lingo's civil damages limit. Do not use it to assume the emergency-aid facts have already been resolved for plaintiff. ([Ngumezi](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/11/20/19-10243.pdf); [Estrella](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/06/06/22-10027.pdf); [Lingo](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/08/08/14-35344.pdf))

#### No criminal case is not a universal fabrication answer

> The absence of criminal charges does not answer every deprivation alleged in Count II. Paragraphs 37 and 45 allege a separate administrative loss of commercial driving privileges through use of the challenged sworn report. Costanich recognizes deliberate-fabrication protection in a civil setting involving protected interests, and the operative alternatives in Model Instruction 9.38 include other deprivation of liberty. Plaintiff must still plead a protected injury, deliberate rather than careless falsity, and causation; the correct inquiry is whether the particular alleged administrative deprivation satisfies those requirements, not whether a prosecutor filed a criminal complaint.

This synthesis deliberately does not claim Costanich decided driver-license liability or that the model instruction defines a property-interest variant. The administrative findings and pre-incident QI analogy remain necessary work. ([Costanich](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/180346/costanich-v-dept-of-social-and-health-services/); [Instruction 9.38](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/jury-instructions/civil/9-38-particular-rights-fourteenth-amendment-due-process-deliberate-fabrication-of-evidence.pdf))

#### Correct the defect rather than defend it

> Plaintiff does not defend reading the produced breath-odor field as a representation that a physical alcoholic beverage was found. That characterization should be corrected, and the actual warrant affidavit should be analyzed on its own terms. The correction does not establish that the initial entry or cup inspection was lawful, nor does it eliminate a properly supported, independently material false-statement theory; it does mean that materiality must be demonstrated from the real application and truthful proposed corrections rather than from a misread form.

This is a strategic credibility concession grounded in R195, not a concession that every officer observation is true. The corrected-affidavit rule remains claim-specific under Chism. ([Chism](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/10-35085/10-35085-2011-11-07.pdf?ts=1411064324))

### Questions a skeptical judge will ask

- **Where is the false statement in the actual affidavit?** Answer with a precise location and supported difference, not a list of inaccuracies in a different document.
- **Can a coherent speaker still smell of alcohol or initially be confused?** Yes; identify the narrower disputed fact and avoid impossible sensor claims.
- **What makes a sleeping freeway occupant nonemergent?** Supply the actual observable sequence and response, not a rule that no crash means no possible emergency.
- **What happened after the cup was identified as urine?** Identify the next specific search or restraint and the information then available.
- **What is the DMV's actual reason for its final action?** Supply the order and finding; a refusal-based proceeding is not a BAC-threshold case by default.
- **Why is PAS refusal First Amendment activity?** A statutory right and temporal proximity are not answers by themselves.
- **What changed from Kim's analysis?** Point to true pleading/source differences and address unchanged adverse facts; do not evade finality questions.
- **What exact amendment avoids futility?** Offer truthful paragraph-level corrections, not an open-ended promise that discovery will reveal misconduct.

### Claims not to make

Do not assert negative alcohol/toxicology, that the recording can disprove odor, that no witnessed driving means no possible reasonable cause, that the cup retroactively invalidates all prior action, that every later injury is civil poisonous fruit, that March metadata alone proves fabrication, that Costanich universally denies QI, or that refusal alone is necessarily protected First Amendment expression. **Treatment:** Label them internal proof gaps. Separately test existing allegations under the actual pleading standard and Rule 11's support provisions; do not convert the project's quality gates into extra pleading elements. ([Rule 11(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_11))

#### A11: Replacing causation law with a more demanding self-imposed test

**Location:** Evidence matrix's causal-chain worksheet. **Verbatim:** “Why outcome changes without it”

A counterfactual is an investigation tool, not an admission that strict but-for causation is the only possible fabrication standard. The engine already includes a Richards qualification, but an isolated table export could erase it and hand the court an unnecessarily demanding test.

**Treatment:** Keep the qualification attached. Preserve the supported materiality argument and distinguish the administrative facts without silently conceding the broader causation question; Richards's fair-trial holding does not automatically decide the DMV variant either. ([Richards](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/06/24/19-56205.pdf))

#### A12: Giving the court dismissal-ready claim rankings

**Location:** Decision memorandum priority table and concurrent synthesis QI table. **Verbatim:** “QI likely”

An internal “Last,” “weakest,” “dismissal likely,” or “high risk” label is an assessment, not a pleading or legal concession. A filed version would hand the court its conclusion and could undermine a reasonably supportable request for denial.

**Treatment:** Keep rankings internal. Request claim-specific denial where supportable and alternative leave where appropriate, without predicting that the claim should lose.

### Additional places where the analysis goes too far

#### A13: Treating a count heading as a dispositive merits ruling

**Location:** Decision memorandum's Count IV defendant discussion. **Verbatim:** “any proposed expansion requires an express amendment”

The heading map should remain accurate, but the categorical statement risks deciding the legal significance of the entire complaint from the heading alone. Read the body and actual personal-participation allegations before conceding a defendant or theory is absent; conditional leave is not an admission that every contrary construction is unavailable.

**Treatment:** Replace in a filing with a precise description of pleaded conduct and, only if needed, an alternative request to clarify defendant attribution. Do not silently expand or withdraw a claim.

#### A14: Requiring the plaintiff to negate every hypothetical alternative offense

**Location:** Priority table, Count III. **Verbatim:** “address all genuinely available indicia and alternative offenses”

The wording can turn a useful totality check into an instruction to invent every arrest offense or exception the defense might have asserted. The current motion does put shoulder parking and DUI in issue, but that is not an invitation to produce an encyclopedia of unrelated offenses.

**Treatment:** Address the actual developed grounds and applicable legal framework. Keep other researched possibilities internal unless a concrete reason makes them necessary.

#### A15: Adopting the officer's innocent explanation for PAS timing

**Location:** Decision memorandum, arrest analysis. **Verbatim:** “a voluntary test might otherwise have supplied exculpatory information”

The motion already argues lawful discretion and inadequate retaliatory causation, so the response must engage that. The research nevertheless adds an officer-benefiting exculpatory-test hypothesis and risks making it the accepted reason for the arrest instead of a competing inference.

**Treatment:** Preserve the actual advisement, statement and temporal allegation. Argue the plaintiff-favorable inference from the facts without saying a lawful motive has been established or that timing alone conclusively proves liability.

#### A16: Announcing “missing elements” before testing the existing pleading

**Location:** Decision memorandum, Count VI. **Verbatim heading:** “Separate the missing elements”

The heading prejudges the result and encourages copying an adjudication rather than an issue list. The same problem occurs when a table calls the existing joint-investigation allegations “conclusory” without first considering the specific conduct already pleaded.

**Treatment:** Use “Elements to evaluate” internally and argue actual sufficiency in the brief. Do not invent facts to fill a gap, but do not concede a gap merely because more detail could improve an amendment.

#### A17: Overbuilding the government's search exception

**Location:** Decision memorandum's cup analysis. **Verbatim:** “any legitimately known odor supplied automobile-search probable cause”

Emergency aid and purported crime-related entry are actually raised, so some exception analysis is responsive. The leakage is supplying additional cases, reconstructed combinations of contested facts, or a complete doctrinal fit the motion does not articulate, especially when the research uses “legitimately known” as if truth and timing had been decided.

**Treatment:** Identify the motion's asserted justification and its missing factual or legal link. Avoid accepting disputed odor, consent, visibility, lawful vantage, or scope; research alternative fits privately.

#### A18: Leading with an inventory of plaintiff errors

**Location:** Decision memorandum's relief hierarchy and proposed modules. **Verbatim:** “Credibility concession”

Narrow correction is necessary where the brief would otherwise perpetuate a known mistake. A broad opening concession that the FAC is defective, followed by a catalog of every immaterial duration, transcript or form problem, does unnecessary persuasion for the other side.

**Treatment:** Correct the specific proposition where relevant, then state the surviving argument. Do not equate a form-label correction with a concession of lawful entry, truthful odor observations, valid warrant, no retaliation, or futility.

#### A19: Supplying withdrawal or abandonment language without authorization

**Location:** Concurrent synthesis, warrant decision tree. **Verbatim:** “abandon or substantially narrow Count 1”

That can be a conditional internal decision option after the facts and law are checked, not a drafting instruction or present authorization to surrender a claim. Similar “drop,” “omit,” or voluntary-dismissal language in the historical drafts must not migrate into a filed document by default.

**Treatment:** Preserve the option internally with a decision gate. No actual withdrawal, dismissal, amended omission, stipulation, or waiver occurs without an informed choice and procedural review.

#### A20: Narrowing punitive damages by claim label rather than alleged conduct

**Location:** Concurrent synthesis, remedies. **Verbatim:** “preserve punitive damages only against an officer for whom deliberate deception, deliberate fabrication, or intentional retaliation remains plausibly alleged”

The motion actually challenges punitive damages, so a response is necessary. The quoted formulation may unnecessarily restrict the available conduct theories to three labels and concede relief for other sufficiently culpable conduct before that issue has been analyzed.

**Treatment:** Respond using the actual punitive standard and supported conduct, not a claim-label limitation. Do not turn the Rule 12(f) response into a concession about compensatory damages or treat a prior withdrawal reservation as automatic present entitlement.

#### A21: Making binding-law research more restrictive than the law requires

**Location:** Count VI element list. **Verbatim:** “Identify a controlling pre-incident basis”

Pre-incident specificity is an important internal research check, but the phrase can become a demand for an indistinguishable case, or a statement that an argument for a nonfrivolous legal extension is forbidden. The merits question, clearly-established-law question, and factual-support question must remain distinct.

**Treatment:** Analyze the applicable QI test without announcing that absence of an identical PAS case necessarily defeats every relevant theory. Preserve only legally supportable arguments; Rule 11 expressly permits nonfrivolous extensions of existing law, which is not the same as saying such an extension defeats QI. ([Rule 11(b)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_11))

#### A22: Treating an internal audit as an unlimited permission to omit

**Location:** Concurrent synthesis's existing leakage table. **Verbatim:** “Do not mention toxicology or adverse transcript passages unless Defendants properly place them before the Court or the Court converts the motion under Rule 12(d)”

The anti-volunteering objective is sound, but the instruction is too categorical if read as a legal rule. A plaintiff's own necessary reliance, proper incorporation, an actual disclosure order or an obligation to avoid a misleading presentation can matter; proper consideration does not invariably require conversion, and “not raised” is not a universal withholding entitlement. ([Khoja](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf); [Rule 11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_11))

**Treatment:** Use a relevance/record/duty gate, not a blanket ban. Do not continue advocating a known false proposition while waiting for amendment authorization, and do not describe private repository storage or an “internal” label as a privilege determination.

### What should not be removed as supposed leakage

- **The actual form wording:** Correct the physical-beverage misreading. That correction does not concede the truth of reported odor or the legality of any intrusion.
- **The actual warrant identity:** Do not knowingly call one document another. Determine proper court use before making an evidentiary submission.
- **The current FAC map:** Maintain accurate paragraphs and defendant attribution. Accuracy does not require admitting a disputed legal interpretation of the whole pleading.
- **The response to the no-charges premise:** Costanich and the operative Instruction 9.38 alternatives are plaintiff-serving analysis, not a concession that Count II must be abandoned. Respond to the motion's actual premise while identifying the distinct alleged deprivation. ([Costanich](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/180346/costanich-v-dept-of-social-and-health-services/); [Instruction 9.38](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/jury-instructions/civil/9-38-particular-rights-fourteenth-amendment-due-process-deliberate-fabrication-of-evidence.pdf))
- **Necessary materiality and QI analysis:** Both are raised. Avoid adverse factual assumptions and unneeded defense construction, not the governing standards themselves.
- **The narrow pro se fee correction:** Do not seek compensation for the plaintiff's own pro se time as though it were retained-counsel fees. Preserve other properly available relief without a gratuitous merits concession.
- **A truthful amendment alternative:** “If the Court finds a defect, leave would permit the following supported clarification” is not the same as “the Court should dismiss because we admit the defect.”
