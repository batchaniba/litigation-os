# Protective, criminal, and victim-relief track

## Protective order

A current or former spouse is within Maryland's protective-order framework.
Maryland defines qualifying abuse by statute, including specified acts such as
serious bodily harm, placing an eligible person in fear of imminent serious
bodily harm, assault, and other listed conduct. The facts must be matched to the
current statute; labels such as “emotional abuse” alone do not substitute for a
statutory act.

A petition may be filed in District or circuit court. When courts are closed,
a District Court commissioner can address interim relief. Temporary and final
orders have different procedures and proof. Available final relief can include
no-contact/stay-away terms and firearm surrender, among other statutory relief.

If danger is immediate, safety and emergency services take priority over
document completion. Do not alert the alleged aggressor to a safety plan.

## Criminal route

Njiubu may report alleged crimes to law enforcement. If police do not initiate
charges, Maryland Judiciary materials describe an Application for Statement of
Charges submitted to a District Court commissioner. The applicant must provide
truthful, first-hand or properly sourced facts. Once a case is initiated, the
State—not Brendan or Njiubu—controls prosecution and charging decisions.

Limitations and offense classification are incident-specific. Maryland
generally imposes a one-year prosecution period for misdemeanors unless another
rule applies. Second-degree assault is classified as a misdemeanor, but conduct
may implicate different or more serious offenses depending on verified facts.
Do not classify a 2012 event, promise prosecution, or submit a charge
application without Maryland criminal-law review.

## Related safety tools

- Screen for an Extreme Risk Protective Order only if concrete present facts
  show firearm danger and the petitioner is statutorily eligible.
- Screen for Adult Protective Services only if Njiubu meets vulnerable-adult
  criteria.
- Preserve stalking, location tracking, account intrusion, impersonation,
  threats, and coercive-control evidence without unauthorized access.
- A Maryland domestic-violence advocate can assist with safety planning and
  court accompaniment.

## Compensation and restitution

The Maryland Criminal Injuries Compensation Board may assist qualifying crime
victims with eligible losses. Restitution or compensation is not guaranteed,
does not replace a civil limitations analysis, and must not duplicate the same
loss. Preserve bills, wage records, insurance payments, and program decisions.
