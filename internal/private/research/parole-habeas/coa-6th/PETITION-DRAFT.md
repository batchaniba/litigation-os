# MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

## Introduction

This is a delay writ. Respondent has had Petitioner’s habeas corpus petition since April 3, 2026. That petition attacks how CDCR ran a December 2025 discharge review. It prays, in the alternative, for immediate discharge on a futility theory or for a new lawful individualized review within a set time. This Court is not asked to grant either prayer. The Rules of Court told Respondent when to rule. Respondent used its one extension. Petitioner used the one notice the Rules give him. The calendar the Rules then required has passed. Mandate is the way to obtain a **ruling** — not a second habeas petition in this Court, and not a request that this Court discharge Petitioner from parole.

## A. Respondent has a duty to rule on a pending habeas petition

Rule 4.551(a)(5)(A) is mandatory: “The court must rule on a petition for writ of habeas corpus within 60 days after the petition is filed.” The court “rules” by issuing an order to show cause, denying the petition, or requesting an informal response. (Rule 4.551(a)(8).) After an informal response, the court must issue an OSC or deny within 45 days of receiving it. (Rule 4.551(a)(9).) Time may be shortened or extended “for good cause stated in the order.” (Rule 4.551(i).)

If the court misses the 60-day mark, the petitioner “may file a notice and request for ruling.” (Rule 4.551(a)(6).) When that notice is complete and the court has still failed to rule, “the presiding judge or their designee must assign the petition to a judge and calendar the matter for a decision without appearances within 30 days of the filing of the notice and request for ruling.” (Rule 4.551(a)(6)(B).)

Those are duties. They are not hortatory. They exist so that a person restrained of liberty is not left without a decision. Petitioner filed on April 3, 2026; used the 60-day period; received a July 2 extension to August 1; filed the HC-004 in person on September 1; and watched October 1 pass. That is the entire sequence this writ needs.

A denial, when it comes, “must contain a brief statement of the reasons for the denial. An order only declaring the petition to be ‘denied’ is insufficient.” (Rule 4.551(h)(1).) Petitioner is entitled to a ruling that the Rules recognize, not to silence.

## B. Mandate lies to compel that duty

“A writ of mandate may be issued by any court to any inferior tribunal … to compel the performance of an act which the law specially enjoins, as a duty resulting from an office, trust, or station ….” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1085, subd. (a).) “The writ must be issued in all cases where there is not a plain, speedy, and adequate remedy, in the ordinary course of law. It must be issued upon the verified petition of the party beneficially interested.” (Id., § 1086.)

Rule 4.551 is the law that specially enjoins the act. Respondent is the inferior tribunal. Petitioner is beneficially interested. There is no appeal from a failure to rule. There is no second HC-004. Ordinary motion practice on a habeas file is the HC-004, already used.

This Court is the proper court. Rule 8.486(a)(1) requires an explanation when the petition could have been filed first in a lower court. A writ **to** the superior court **from** the superior court is not a remedy. Original mandate in the Court of Appeal is the ordinary vehicle.

The petition is verified. (Rule 8.486(a)(4).) It is accompanied by this memorandum. (Rule 8.486(a)(5).) Supporting documents are the filed habeas petition, the extension order, the HC-004, the current docket printout, and the Form 1502-DR that identifies what is pending. (Rule 8.486(b).) The “ruling from which the petition seeks relief” does not exist; rule 8.486(b)(2) permits that showing by declaration, which the verification and paragraphs 22–25 supply.

Service is made as rule 8.486(e)(1) requires: petition and supporting documents on the real parties; petition only on Respondent. The Attorney General is served under rule 8.29 because this proceeding arises from a criminal judgment and names a state officer in official capacity. The cover so states.

No filing fee is due if the underlying matter is criminal. (Sixth Appellate District fee schedule, citing Gov. Code, § 68926: “there is no fee for filing a petition if the underlying matter is criminal or juvenile in nature.”) The underlying matter is a Penal Code section 1473 habeas on a criminal judgment. If a fee is nonetheless assessed, Petitioner tenders a request to waive court fees on form FW-001. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.26.)

## C. Original habeas in this Court is not the remedy sought, and would be the wrong next paper

This Court has original habeas jurisdiction. (Cal. Const., art. VI, § 10.) It also has discretion not to use it when the petitioner has not first obtained a superior-court determination. “There is no question but that this court has jurisdiction to issue the writ of habeas corpus …. But this court has discretion to refuse to issue the writ as an exercise of original jurisdiction on the ground that application has not been made therefor in a lower court in the first instance.” (*In re Hillery, supra,* 202 Cal.App.2d at p. 294.) A higher court “has discretion to deny without prejudice a habeas corpus petition that was not filed first in a proper lower court.” (*In re Steele, supra,* 32 Cal.4th at p. 692.) A parole-related habeas is to be filed in the first instance in the superior court of the county of conviction. (*In re Roberts, supra,* 36 Cal.4th 575; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 4.552(b)(2)(C).)

Petitioner **did** file first in the proper superior court. That petition is still there. An original habeas in this Court would not compel a ruling; it would duplicate the case and invite a discretionary denial. The Rules of Court themselves keep habeas out of the mandate chapter except as rule 8.384 provides. (Rule 8.485(b).) Unrepresented original habeas in a reviewing court is filed on form HC-001. (Rule 8.380(a).) This paper is not that petition.

If this Court were to treat the present filing as a habeas petition, Petitioner asks that it not do so, and that the clerk file it as a petition for writ of mandate. The prayer is a ruling by Respondent, not immediate discharge and not an order that CDCR convene a new review. Those asks remain in `C2604791`.

## D. Peremptory writ in the first instance

The Court may, without waiting for a preliminary opposition, deny this petition, issue an alternative writ or order to show cause, or notify the parties that it is considering a peremptory writ in the first instance. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.487(a)(4).)

A peremptory writ in the first instance is proper only with due notice to the parties adversely affected. (*Palma v. U.S. Industrial Fasteners, Inc., supra,* 36 Cal.3d 171.) Petitioner hereby requests that procedure if the Court finds the record undisputed: the dates of filing, extension, HC-004, and non-rule are documentary. There is no factual dispute this Court needs an evidentiary hearing to resolve. Real parties may file a preliminary opposition within 10 days. (Rule 8.487(a)(1).)

The relief is narrow. Respondent retains full discretion over the **content** of the ruling — OSC, reasoned denial, or informal response — including whether to grant immediate discharge, to order a new review on a date certain, or to deny. Mandate does not direct Respondent to grant the habeas petition.

## Conclusion

The Rules of Court already decided when a superior court must rule on a habeas petition. Petitioner followed those Rules. Respondent has not. This Court should issue the writ and set a date certain for a ruling. What that ruling says — discharge, a new review, or a reasoned denial — is Respondent’s to decide.
