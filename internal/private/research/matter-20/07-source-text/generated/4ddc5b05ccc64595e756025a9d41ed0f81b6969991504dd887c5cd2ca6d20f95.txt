11   III. THE GOVERNING STATUTE IMPOSES A CONDITIONAL, ENFORCEABLE CERTIFICATION DUTY

12   CHP is a law enforcement certifying entity, and Serrato issued the decision for that entity. (Pen.
13   Code, § 679.10, subds. (a)–(b); Pet., Ex. 1.) Penal Code section 679.10(c) expressly lists
14 “[u]nlawful criminal restraint” and “[f]alse imprisonment” as qualifying criminal activity;

15   subdivision (d) includes crimes whose nature and elements are substantially similar to listed
16   criminal activity. Most importantly, subdivision (g)(1) provides that, upon request, an authorized
17   official “shall certify victim helpfulness” when the person was the victim of qualifying
18   criminal activity and “has been helpful, is being helpful, or is likely to be helpful” in
19 detection, investigation or prosecution of it.

20   The statute does not require the victim to procure a criminal complaint, officer arrest, conviction
21   or open investigation. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)(1).) The statutory victim is also not
22   rendered ineligible merely because the person was investigated as a potential suspect in another
23   offense. The statute protects against specified impermissible grounds, including use of the
24   victim's criminal history or cooperation in a separate case; Petitioner does not assert that Serrato
25   actually employed those prohibited factors absent proof. (Id., subd. (k)(3)(A), (H).)

26   Subdivision (h) creates a rebuttable presumption of helpfulness where the victim did not
27   refuse or fail to provide reasonably requested assistance. It does not eliminate the qualifying-
28   activity element. Certification, moreover, is evidence for federal immigration proceedings, not a

 1 grant of immigration status. The Legislature placed the certification decision with defined

 2 officials but bounded it by the mandatory conditions it enacted.

 3 Traditional mandate corrects a public official's legally erroneous exercise of duty or abuse of

 4 discretion; it ordinarily cannot dictate a discretionary judgment unless one lawful result remains.

 5 (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1085–1086; Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547, 562; Common

 7 requested certification and the alternative request for remand.

 8 IV. THE REQUEST PRESENTED THE ELEMENTS OF A QUALIFYING RESTRAINT OFFENSE; CHP'S

 9 “NO QUALIFYING ACTIVITY” CONCLUSION MUST BE TESTED AGAINST THE ACTUAL

10   AUTHORITY FOR CONFINEMENT

11   The offense is unlawful restraint of a person. Penal Code section 236 defines false
12   imprisonment as the unlawful violation of another's personal liberty. Penal Code section 146(a)
13   additionally proscribes a public officer's arrest or detention against a person's will under color of
14 authority without lawful authority. Petitioner does not claim that section 146(a) automatically

15   qualifies by name under subdivision (c); it supplies a closely related state criminal-law
16   description of an unlawful detention by an officer whose nature and elements must be
17   considered under subdivision (d), while section 236 directly matches the listed offense. Whether
18   every criminal element and absence of legal privilege is established is the factual dispute—not a
19 reason to categorically treat a law enforcement detention as incapable of qualifying.

20   Intentional restraint is not disputed. CHP's own Form 202 records handcuffing, custody,
21   transport, a warrant-authorized blood draw, and jail booking arising from the November 1
22   incident. (Pet., Ex. 6; AGO 000029, 000032–000033.) The material question is lawful privilege
23   at the point the custody became compulsory. The officers did not witness the Tesla moving and
24   the DS-367 treats driving as admitted rather than observed. (CHP Form 202/DS-367, AGO
25   000029–000053 [exact DS-367 Bates to insert]; Verified Answer ¶34 [verify filed text].) Under
26   Vehicle Code section 40300.5, arrest without witnessed driving requires both reasonable cause to
27   believe prior DUI and one of the statute's specified circumstances; the officers observed the car
28   on a right shoulder, not necessarily obstructing a traveled roadway. The Court must determine
29   the relevant statutory circumstance on the evidence actually available when arrest occurred, not

26   V. SERRATO'S REJECTION OF BOTH LISTED AND SUBSTANTIALLY SIMILAR ACTIVITY REQUIRES
27   A SUBSTANTIVELY LAWFUL BASIS

28   The decision's determinative sentences deny listed qualifying activity and substantially similar
29   activity. Because section 679.10(c)(18)–(19) expressly identifies criminal restraint and false

                                                                               MATTER 20 | 26CV191136 | Page 7
                                                              REVISED REVIEW DRAFT — NOT FILED OR SERVED


 1 imprisonment, a rejection can stand only if it represents a legally permissible assessment that the

 2 claimed underlying conduct fails the elements of qualifying victimhood on a supported record—

 3 not a proposition that police detention, an absence of prosecution, or CHP's unilateral

 4 characterization of the encounter necessarily excludes such victimhood. The latter propositions

 5 would conflict with subdivisions (c), (d) and (k). The current letter does not expressly endorse

 6 those incorrect propositions, and Petitioner does not pretend that it does.

 7 The demonstrable question is the factual and legal basis of CHP's actual finding. On April 9 the

 8 agency was presented with the same report and timestamped transcript identifying disputed

 9 descriptions of impairment and claimed restraint; on April 20 it stated categorically there was no

10   qualifying or similar activity. Petitioner submits that, if the Court credits the evidence that the
11   arrest lacked lawful authority and that the resulting custody constituted criminal false
12   imprisonment, the denial's dispositive premise cannot stand. In that event, it is no answer that the
13   officer was acting during an ostensible DUI investigation: state law explicitly recognizes
14 detention without lawful authority as criminal misconduct under section 146(a), and section

15   236 recognizes unlawful confinement.

16   The alternative agency-error route is narrower and evidence-dependent. If a contemporaneous
17   review file reveals that CHP treated absence of a filed criminal case as determinative;
18   categorically excluded criminal misconduct by peace officers; assessed only whether the officers
19 asserted DUI probable cause rather than evaluating contradictory evidence bearing on it; or

20   employed another legally impermissible rule, the denial must be reconsidered under the correct
21   statutory standard. This motion does not claim any such specific internal criterion was used
22   before the relevant April records establish it. The later May 9, 2026 internal review/interview
23   memoranda postdate the denial and are not evidence of what Serrato actually reviewed before
24   April 20.

25   Under traditional mandate the Court need not infer deception, animus, or sham solely from a
26   five-sentence decision. The proposed finding instead is either (1) a substantively untenable
27   qualifying-activity ruling after the Court finds the necessary facts, or (2) an actual,
28   established misapplication of the governing law or demonstrable abuse of discretion,
29   resulting in a writ tailored to the finding. This is the claim that survived demurrer.
