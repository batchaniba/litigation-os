# Serrato decision-error analysis and verified authority audit
**Private work product; not a court filing or an assertion that the court has adjudicated these points.**

## What Serrato actually wrote
Signed denial copied into original petition Ex.1, R05 p.45; Judge Whitman's R01 p.2 reproduces key text: after asserting “A thorough review of this incident was conducted,” Serrato says the incident “does not meet the standard of qualifying criminal activity identified in Section 679.10” or federal INA §101(a)(15)(U)(iii), and finds no substantially similar qualifying activity under §679.10(d). **He did not identify helpfulness, criminal history, parole or absence of charges as the reason**. The claim that the refusal necessarily rested on such reasons is not grounded.

The exact challenge is: did the agency *substantively* apply the criminal-restraint definition and the established incident facts in a legally tenable fashion? A claimed review may be imperfect, but a terse letter and later dated reviews alone do not prove sham, categorical exclusion, missing consideration, or abuse. R01 dismissed standalone §679.10(i)(2) reasons and helpfulness causes; a rebranded reasons-only count is not permissible. The independent Count One survived because the court expressly allowed factfinding about the arrest and report at a merits trial.

## Most promising distinct lines
**1. Arrest authority, not just alcohol impairment.** The factual predicate for §40300.5 must be tied to (a) crash, (b) observed in/about obstructing vehicle, (c) would not be apprehended unless immediately arrested, (d) immediate safety/property risk or (e) evidence destruction/concealment. Stationary freeway shoulder and prior admission of driving alone do not conclusively establish *any one* enumerated exception. This is a conditional plaintiff-favorable legal argument; if an exception is proven, assess objective probable cause separately.

**2. The 'walk-and-turn' is an independently testable report misattribution.** If native audio confirms the test was canceled because of uneven ground, no lawful custody rationale should count it as a refusal or an unsuccessful test. But HGN, Romberg, empty can and other claimed clues remain; a single corrected item is not automatically dispositive. Connect materiality to totality, then identify evidence that Serrato used the mistaken premise.

**3. Optional PAS inference.** *People v. Jackson* (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461 held trial admission of **voluntary PAS** refusal to prove consciousness of guilt was error; the panel held error harmless. It did **not** establish that officers may never note such refusal when making a prearrest decision. Avoid arguing an absolute proposition it does not hold.

**4. Helpfulness.** Under §679.10(h), cooperation is rebuttably presumed absent a refused or failed reasonable information/assistance request about the *qualifying offense*. CHP's Answer ¶57 contests whether an investigating entity requested information. The April letter rejects victimhood instead. It is useful as a conditional, separate predicate, not grounds that secretly infected denial.

**5. Decision-review file.** Original request and transcribed material existed before decision; a 2024 recording produced in 2026 is evidence of earlier conduct but its production date does not show pre-April review. RFP No. 8 still critical. The May 9 memoranda and May 20 interview postdate denial and should not be used as proof of pre-denial knowledge. Do not infer a hidden categorical policy from privilege.

### Internal red-team only, not volunteered in court-facing brief
R01 p.3 itself cites *People v. Wolterman* and *Espinoza v. Shiomoto*. The court's prior identification of these cases is a reason to prepare a well-supported response privately—not a reason to manufacture unasserted alternative arrest grounds in the opening memorandum. *Wolterman* was fact-sensitive on objectively supportable public-intoxication grounds; *Espinoza* weighs impairment totality. **Do not misdescribe either as rejected or silently assume it cannot apply.**

**Exclude invalid authority from any filed document:** *People v. Brock* (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 1266 is not a police false-imprisonment holding. *California Correctional Peace Officers Assn. v. State* (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 1454 does not establish an unqualified no-post-hoc-rationalization rule.
