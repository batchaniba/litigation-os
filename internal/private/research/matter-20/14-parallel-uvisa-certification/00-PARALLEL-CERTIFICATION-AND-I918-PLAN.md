## Executive decision

A new U petition **cannot** bypass a signed authorized Form I-918 Supplement B; supporting incident, custody and court records are not a substitute. See 8 CFR §214.14(c)(2)(i), USCIS official I-918 instructions and State Dept. FAM 9 FAM 402.6-6(D). The certifier can be any authorized law-enforcement official, prosecutor, judge, or jurisdictionally competent detecting/investigating authority; CHP's rejection does not create exclusive jurisdiction. But every alternative must have a real relationship to *detection, investigation, prosecution, conviction or sentencing of the alleged qualifying crime*. Judge involvement in related civil proceedings may qualify under USCIS official U-Visa Law Enforcement Resource Guide (2026). The immigration petition's independent **substantial physical/mental abuse** and admissibility standards remain separate.

**Recommended concurrent tracks:** (A) preserve the existing CHP/Serrato §1085 writ, (B) formal written criminal-incident referral and I-918B certification inquiry to Alameda DA's appropriate intake/supervisor, (C) explore available administrative judicial-certification protocol in Alameda Superior Court *after* verifying the previous June 18 Fickes denial and material new evidence, (D) if local resources fail, seek proper DOJ investigative review, (E) keep POST's decertification investigation as corroborating evidence and potential officer-accountability process, **not assumed certifying authority for criminal false imprisonment**.

## The independent federal petition: build now, **file only after Supplement B**

**Core federal assembly:** (1) current USCIS Form I-918 signed and properly completed; (2) properly signed I-918 Supplement B dated within **six months** before USCIS receives I-918; (3) original, credible petitioner personal statement covering victimhood, information, help, specific physical/mental impact; (4) lawfully obtained reports and court materials; (5) corroboration of substantial physical/mental abuse causally attributable to qualifying activity; (6) full immigration-history and inadmissibility review and, if required, signed Form I-192 with supporting waiver evidence; (7) biometrics/address confidentiality preparation, accurate filing address and any EAD forms indicated by current USCIS rules.

**Substantial abuse is a separate test USCIS decides.** 8 CFR §214.14(b)(1) evaluates nature/severity/duration/permanent or serious effects of harm and aggravation of existing conditions; no single factor controls. Actual jail records, clinical/therapy records, prescriptions, symptoms and functional effects contemporaneous or later, workplace consequences, contemporaneous contacts, and a truthful chronology could help *only if they exist and causally relate to the qualifying crime*. Do not infer psychiatric diagnoses from civil litigation or make a medical professional testify without consent.

**Immigration screening is essential:** Criminal history and any prior detention, proceedings, removal or entry history require specialized legal analysis of inadmissibility, eligibility for a possible I-192 waiver, and discretionary/background-screening issues. A Supplement B signature does not override USCIS scrutiny; a bona fide determination-based EAD/deferred action is discretionary and distinct from actual U status. USCIS I-192 page https://www.uscis.gov/i-192 ; 8 CFR §212.17; policy manual. Do **not** file an unsigned or unaccompanied I-918 merely to secure an earlier queue date; 8 CFR §214.14(c)(2)(i) requires a certification with initial filing.
