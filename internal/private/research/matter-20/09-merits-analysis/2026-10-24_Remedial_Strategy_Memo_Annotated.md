## TL;DR

- **Section 679.10(g)(1) is mandatory once its conditions are met. The problem is that CHP decides whether they are met, and a court will review that decision for abuse of discretion.**
  - The statute says a certifying official "shall certify" when the requester "was a victim of a qualifying criminal activity" and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful.
  - The California Attorney General has said that, "unlike federal law," the statute mandates certification "when certain conditions are met."
  - No California appellate decision construing § 679.10 was found. Direct certification is therefore a first-impression request. It depends on showing the arrest was unlawful on undisputed facts, and the current record does not show that.

## 1. Recommended Remedial Theory

**One request in two tiers, built on shared findings.**

- **Tier B-1 (primary): direct certification.** Available only if the court also finds that, on facts not reasonably disputed, the restraint at 01:11:16–01:11:33 lacked reasonable cause. In that case only one lawful result remains under § 679.10(g)(1).
- **Tier B-2 (alternative): vacatur and controlled reconsideration.** Available on the shared Tier A findings alone.

**Tier A findings (common to both):**
1. CHP is a certifying entity and Serrato is a certifying official. Both points are admitted.
2. The April 9, 2026 request triggered the § 679.10(g)(1) duty.
3. "False imprisonment" and "unlawful criminal restraint" are enumerated qualifying crimes, and an arrest by a peace officer is not exempt from that category as a matter of law.
4. Arrest, prosecution, conviction, and officer discipline are not prerequisites (§ 679.10(k)(1)).
5. The helpfulness presumption (§ 679.10(h)) is unrebutted. CHP admits that no investigating entity ever asked petitioner for information (Answer ¶ 57). *[superseded as a ground for relief]*
6. The denial lacks the explanation required by § 679.10(i)(2) and does not show that the correct standard was applied to an accurate record. *[superseded]*
7. The arrest documentation contains a specific, material factual error: the walk-and-turn entry, the surface description, and the "poor performance on the FST's" arrest basis.

This structure lets the court grant Tier B-2 without deciding probable cause. That is the most likely choice for a cautious judge facing a first-impression statute and a state agency. *[superseded — the order makes probable cause the question]*

**Why direct certification is harder:**
- **Who decides.** The statute does not say who determines "victim of a qualifying criminal activity" or by what standard. A court will likely treat that as the certifier's call, subject to review.
- **The sworn attestation.** Federal regulations require the Supplement B to state that "the applicant has been a victim of qualifying criminal activity that the certifying official's agency is investigating or prosecuting" (8 C.F.R. § 214.14(c)(2)(i)). The AG's 2015 bulletin notes that the certifier signs "under penalty of perjury." A court will hesitate to order a sworn statement the official disputes unless its own findings leave nothing to dispute.
- **The blood result.** The 0.073 ± 0.004 g% reading at 02:38 cannot justify the arrest after the fact. But it undercuts any claim that the officers' observations were fabricated, and it undercuts petitioner's roadside statement that he drank "zero."

## 2. Best Route to Direct Certification

**Legal premise.**
- Mandamus will not control discretion "unless the court's discretion can be exercised in only one way." San Gabriel Tribune v. City of West Covina (1983) (Ct. App.; https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1764266.html), applying Hurtado v. Superior Court (1974) 11 Cal.3d 574, 579 (binding).
- AG Information Bulletin DLE-2015-04 (Oct. 28, 2015): "Unlike federal law, which provides certifying state and local agencies and officials with discretion in determining whether to complete the certification, California's new law mandates that state and local agencies and officials submit certifications when certain conditions are met" (persuasive; https://www.oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press_releases/dle-2015-04.pdf).
- CHP's own 2023 Report to the Legislature (https://www.chp.ca.gov/siteassets/files/2023-victims-of-crime---u-nonimmigrant-status-report.pdf):
  - CHP "provides certification if the U Visa application meets the criteria set forth in Section 679.10";
  - denied requests are those that "did not meet statutory requirements";
  - in FY 2022–23, CHP received 91 requests, certified 49, and denied 42.

  This is CHP's own admission that certification turns on criteria rather than unfettered discretion.

**Narrowest findings needed to order certification:**
1. Intentional restraint: handcuffing at about 01:11:33, transport, and booking at 03:25. Admitted (Answer ¶¶ 38–39, 41).
2. The restraint was against petitioner's will.
3. At 01:11:16, the facts known to the officers did not give reasonable cause to believe he had been driving under the influence. This is the only contested element.
4. The restraint was therefore unlawful, satisfying Pen. Code §§ 236 and 237(a).
5. Petitioner is a direct victim (§ 679.10(o)(2)) and is presumptively helpful (§ 679.10(h)).
6. No lawful alternative to certification remains.

**Candid assessment of finding 3.** Once the misreported walk-and-turn is removed, CHP's remaining grounds are:
- HGN (four clues claimed);
- a Romberg estimate of about 38–43 seconds against a 30-second target;
- red, watery eyes;
- a "strong" odor;
- "slurred" speech;
- an empty Mike's Hard Lemonade can;
- the admission that he drove from Oakland;
- the seat adjusted to his size;
- the location on a freeway shoulder.

A court will likely find that these could support reasonable cause. **On this record, direct certification is a low-probability outcome.**

**What would make direct certification viable:**
- a qualified SFST expert, working from the native video, attacking how the HGN and Romberg were administered;
- a frame-accurate showing that his speech was not slurred;
- proof that no officer voiced an odor, together with Melano's on-camera identification of the cup as urine;
- evidence from Serrato that the denial rested on the Form 202 without anyone reviewing the video.

**Leave out of this theory:** the 00:56:43 door opening, the 00:59:46 rear-seat search, parole status, Serrato's on-call status, THC metabolites, and any claim that he was "not driving."

## 3. Best Route to Vacatur and Mandatory Reconsideration

**Authority for remand with directions:**
- *Keeler v. Superior Court* (1956) 46 Cal.2d 596, 600–601 (binding): courts have "inherent power[]" to "remand a cause in mandamus for further proceedings which are deemed necessary for a proper determination" (https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/keeler-v-superior-court-32756).
- *Voices of the Wetlands v. State Water Resources Control Bd.* (2011) 52 Cal.4th 499 (binding as to CCP § 1094.5; https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2011/s160211/): "in the final judgment itself, the court may direct the agency's attention to specific portions of its decision that need attention, and need not necessarily require the agency to reconsider, de novo, the entirety of its prior action." It applies here by analogy and through inherent authority.
- *Hollman v. Warren* (1948) 32 Cal.2d 351, 355 (https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/hollman-v-warren-29397): mandamus "does lie to command the exercise of discretion—to compel some action upon the subject involved," even where it cannot dictate the result.

**Minimum findings for remand:**
1. **Pure law, reviewed de novo:**
   - the § 679.10(g)(1) duty exists;
   - federal "discretionary" guidance does not displace it;
   - false imprisonment can occur under color of an arrest that lacks lawful authority;
   - § 679.10(k)(1) makes charges and conviction irrelevant;
   - § 679.10(k)(3) bars reliance on criminal history, including parole status.
2. **Inadequate explanation.** The letter states conclusions ("no qualifying criminal activity," "not substantially similar") without identifying elements, facts, or evidence. *[superseded]*
3. **Material factual error and failure to consider evidence.** The Form 202 says petitioner "chose not to perform" the walk-and-turn. The recording shows he agreed and Officer Melano declined to give the test because of "uneven ground." The report also calls the surface "Clear, cool, and dry" and relies on "poor performance on the FST's." The denial does not address this, and the formal video review is dated May 9, 2026, after the denial.
4. **Result:** vacatur and reconsideration under the directives in Section 15.

**Answering the § 1094.5(f) objection.** CCP § 1094.5(f) says the judgment "shall not limit or control in any way the discretion legally vested in the respondent" (https://www.law.berkeley.edu/archive/files/Cal._Code_Civ._Proc._1094.5.pdf). The answer turns on "legally." Discretion exercised on a misreading of the statute, or on materially inaccurate facts, is not "legally vested." Directing CHP to apply the correct standard, consider specified evidence, avoid rejected grounds, and explain element by element removes unlawful discretion. It does not dictate the outcome.

## 5. Element-by-Element False-Imprisonment Analysis

**The law:**
- Pen. Code § 236: "False imprisonment is the unlawful violation of the personal liberty of another." Misdemeanor false imprisonment is § 237(a).
- CALCRIM No. 1242 (https://justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/1200/1242): "1. The defendant intentionally [and unlawfully] (restrained[,]/ [or] detained[,]/ [or] confined) a person; AND 2. The defendant's act made that person stay or go somewhere against that person's will."
- The bench notes add "and unlawfully" "if there is evidence that the defendant acted lawfully," and state: "False imprisonment is a general-intent crime."
- Felony false imprisonment (CALCRIM 1240) requires "violence, menace, fraud, or deceit." Section 679.10(c)(19) is not limited to felonies. **Use the misdemeanor elements.** The report was written after the restraint, so it cannot be the "fraud or deceit."

**Federal overlay** (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.14; confirmed through LII and govinfo mirrors because ecfr.gov returned an error):
- 8 C.F.R. § 214.14(a)(9) lists "unlawful criminal restraint; false imprisonment" and defines "any similar activity" as offenses whose "nature and elements… are substantially similar."
- § 214.14(a)(14) defines a victim as one "who has suffered direct and proximate harm as a result of the commission of qualifying criminal activity."

**Phase analysis: challenge only Phase 3.**
- **Phase 1 (00:55:37–00:59:12): approach and door opening.** Likely community caretaking, with no confinement. Remove this as an independent theory.
- **Phase 2 (00:59:12–01:11:15): detention and field sobriety tests.** Asleep in the driver's seat on a freeway shoulder, a cup of urine, an empty alcoholic beverage can, and coming from a party add up to reasonable suspicion. Concede this phase. Doing so strengthens the challenge to Phase 3.
- **Phase 3 (01:11:15–01:11:33): the arrest.**
  - Meza-Gonzales said that if petitioner declined the voluntary preliminary alcohol screening (PAS) test, "it'll be up to my discretion whether I put you under arrest for DUI or not." Petitioner said "I don't consent to that." About one second later he was told to turn around.
  - The PAS is voluntary, with a required advisement of the right to refuse (Veh. Code § 23612(h)–(i)).
  - *People v. Jackson* (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1461 (1st Dist., Div. 3) is a real case. It holds that admitting a PAS refusal as consciousness of guilt was error (harmless there) because the statute gives a right to refuse (https://capcentral.org/case_summaries/people-v-jackson-9/; https://www.michaelrehm.com/people-v-jackson-189-ca4th-1461-2010).
  - **Accuracy note:** *Jackson* is about trial admissibility, not probable cause. Use it only by analogy. *[narrowed — see status table]*
- **Phase 4: jail custody, November 1–5.** The record does not show whether the hold was CHP's or a CDCR parole hold. Do not attribute it to CHP without booking records. The § 849 certificate corroborates that no complaint was filed. It is not a finding that the arrest was unlawful.

**Timing.**
- Probable cause is judged on facts known at the time of arrest (*Beck v. Ohio* (1964) 379 U.S. 89, 91).
- By analogy, *People v. Sanders* (2003) 31 Cal.4th 318, 330–332 (binding) holds that an unknown parole condition cannot justify a search.
- The 02:34 warrant and the 0.073 result therefore cannot retroactively justify the 01:11 arrest.

**Vehicle Code § 40300.5** (https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/vehicle-code/veh-sect-40300-5/):
- It requires "reasonable cause to believe that the person had been driving while under the influence" plus one of five circumstances, including "(b)… a vehicle that is obstructing a roadway" and "(e) The person may destroy or conceal evidence of the crime unless immediately arrested."
- *People v. Thompson* (2006) 38 Cal.4th 811 (binding; https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1197922.html) treats dissipation of blood alcohol as a potential exigency. Subdivision (e) will almost always be met when reasonable cause exists.
- Subdivision (b) is doubtful for a shoulder stop. Check Veh. Code § 530 and § 21718 against CHP's cited § 22500(h) [verify].
- Limit the § 40300.5 discussion to one paragraph. The real issue is reasonable cause.

**"Driving."** Petitioner told officers "I was in the driver's seat," and the Form 202 records "Were you driving the vehicle? Yes." *Mercer v. DMV* (1991) 53 Cal.3d 753, 768–769 predates the expansion of § 40300.5. **Drop the "no driving observed" theory.**

**Best one-sentence theory:** At 01:11:16, the officer turned a lawful investigation into a custodial arrest immediately after, and expressly because of, petitioner's exercise of his statutory right to decline the PAS. At that moment, the "poor performance on the FST's" CHP later relied on included a walk-and-turn the officers themselves called off, and no officer had mentioned an odor of alcohol.

## 6. Material-Factual-Error Analysis (Walk-and-Turn)

**What the Form 202 says:**
- "Walk and Turn: Newanforbi chose not to perform this test and related it was difficult."
- Arrest basis (5): "poor performance on the FST's."
- Surface: "Dirt shoulder: Clear, cool, and dry."
- The DS-367 marks "Poor FSTs."
- The field notes were "thrown out upon completion of the report."

**What the recording shows:**
- 01:10:23, petitioner: "I mean I think we can do that."
- 01:10:25, Melano: "It's kind of sketch here bro."
- 01:10:27, Melano: "it's on the side of the freeway and it's like uneven ground. I wouldn't do that one."
- 01:10:30, Meza: "Alright then."

**Why this is material to the certification decision** (even though, standing alone, it does not prove the arrest unlawful):
1. **It goes to the stated arrest basis.** The one-leg stand was declined with the officer's approval, and the record arguably supports that entry ("You don't have to"). The walk-and-turn was cancelled by the officers. That leaves "poor FSTs" resting only on HGN and a Romberg time estimate.
2. **The documents are internally unreliable.** The report calls the surface suitable; on camera the officer called it uneven.
   - Answer ¶ 38 says the arrest came "shortly after Mr. Newanforbi refused to complete a chemical alcohol test."
   - If that refers to the post-arrest refusal, the sequence is wrong: handcuffing was about 01:11:33 and the admonition began at 01:15:54.
   - If it refers to the PAS, it concedes the arrest followed a protected refusal. Argue both readings in the alternative.
3. **It likely affected the decision.** The formal video review (May 9, 2026) and the officer interviews (May 9 and May 20) post-date the April 20 denial. That supports, but does not prove, an inference that the video was not reviewed first. Petitioner's April 9 transcript put CHP on notice of the conflict.
4. **The court can resolve it from the video.** The officer's own recorded words control. No credibility contest and no evidentiary hearing are needed.

**Finding to request:**
> "The CHP 202 entry stating that Petitioner 'chose not to perform' the Walk and Turn test 'and related it was difficult' is contradicted by the native dashcam recording (file 7659@20241101005536-0.mp4, at approximately 01:10:14–01:10:30), which shows that Petitioner stated 'I think we can do that' and that Officer Melano advised against administering the test because of 'uneven ground.' The CHP 202 likewise describes the test location as 'clear, cool, and dry' and relies on 'poor performance on the FST's' as a basis for arrest. The April 20, 2026 denial does not address this discrepancy. Because the existence of qualifying criminal activity depends on whether the restraint was lawful, and the lawfulness of the restraint was assessed in part on these entries, the discrepancy is material to the certification decision."

**To support direct certification, add:** expert critique of the HGN and Romberg, a video-based showing that speech was not slurred, the absence of any voiced odor, and the "up to my discretion" statement followed by an arrest one second later.

## 7. Traditional-Mandate Analysis (CCP §§ 1085, 1086)

| Issue | Rule | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Duty | A writ issues "to compel the performance of an act which the law specially enjoins, as a duty resulting from an office" (§ 1085(a)) | § 679.10(g)(1) "shall certify" |
| Beneficial interest | Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442–443 [verify pinpoint] | Petitioner is the requester and alleged victim |
| No adequate remedy (§ 1086) | — | No administrative appeal exists; USCIS cannot compel certification |

| Standard of review | Statutory meaning de novo; application reviewed for arbitrary or capricious action, lack of evidentiary support, or legal or procedural error | Legal error and reliance on a misreported fact both qualify |
| Extra-record evidence | Western States Petroleum Assn. v. Superior Court (1995) 9 Cal.4th 559 barred it for quasi-legislative decisions. It remains admissible "in traditional mandamus actions challenging ministerial or informal administrative actions if the facts are in dispute" (Stockton Citizens for Sensible Planning v. City of Stockton (2010) 48 Cal.4th 481) | A denial letter issued without a hearing is informal action, so the video and declarations are admissible |
| Evidentiary hearing | CCP § 1090 [verify] | Request only in the alternative |
| Remand | Keeler; Voices | Directive-laden remand |
| Return | CCP § 1097 [verify] | Verified return; retained jurisdiction |
| Discovery | CCP § 1109 [verify] | Requests for admission; narrow Serrato deposition |

**What the court may and may not do:**
- Compelling the duty is proper only if the predicate facts are established.
- Ordering reconsideration is proper once the decision is legally defective.
- Directing the legal rules to be applied is proper.
- Resolving disputed facts is permitted for informal actions, though remand is preferred.
- Substituting the court's judgment for the certifier's is improper.

## 9. Likely CHP Opposition and Responses

1. **"Certification is discretionary."**
   - *CHP's authority:* *Ordonez Orosco v. Napolitano* (5th Cir. 2010) 598 F.3d 222, 226–227 ("the decision to issue a law enforcement certification is a discretionary one"); the DHS Resource Guide ("completely discretionary").
   - *Response:* Both construe federal law. California added "shall certify." The AG says California "mandates" certification "unlike federal law," and the Commission on State Mandates (Test Claim 17-TC-01) found a state-mandated duty.
   - *Briefing line:* "Ordonez Orosco construed 8 U.S.C. § 1184(p). California chose differently." **(Both tiers.)**
2. **"The court cannot order certification."** Agreed, unless only one lawful result remains, which *Hurtado* permits. **(Direct.)**
3. **"There is no qualifying crime because this was a lawful DUI arrest"** (Pen. Code § 836; Veh. Code § 40300.5; *Thompson*). "An arrest's label does not establish its lawfulness," and the denial never analyzed the elements. **(Remand.)**
4. **"It is at most a disputed DUI investigation."** The dispute is the reason for remand, because it was resolved on a misreported record. **(Remand.)**
5. **"Probable cause existed"** (HGN, Romberg, odor, 0.073). Later evidence cannot justify an earlier arrest (*Beck*; *Sanders* by analogy). What remains is a credibility question for a properly informed certifier. **(Remand.)**
6. **"The walk-and-turn issue is immaterial."** It is arrest basis (5), repeated in the DS-367; the surface entry is also contradicted; and the notes were destroyed. **(Remand.)**
7. **"The PAS refusal supports the arrest"** (§ 23612). *Jackson* by analogy, plus the officer's own "up to my discretion" statement. *[narrowed]*
8. **"The court cannot resolve credibility in a writ proceeding."** This is § 1085 review of informal action (*Western States*; *Stockton*), and the recording is not a credibility contest.
9. **"The recording is unauthenticated."** CHP produced the files itself; add the requests for admission and the hash declaration.
10. **"The denial letter was sufficient."** It identifies no facts, elements, evidence, or failure to cooperate (§ 679.10(i)(2)). *[superseded — the court so held]*
11. **"CHP need not accept petitioner's characterization."** Agreed. It must, however, apply the statute to accurate facts.
12. **"Helpfulness is not shown."** No request for cooperation was ever made (Answer ¶ 57), the denial did not rely on helpfulness, and the presumption stands.
13. **"No extra-record evidence."** The informal-action exception applies.
14. **"Direct certification would compel a false attestation"** (8 C.F.R. § 214.14(c)(2)(i)). This is CHP's strongest point. Response: judicial findings supply the factual basis for the attestation. Otherwise, accept remand. **(Direct.)**
15. **"The writ would control future discretion."** The directives set legal limits, not outcomes (*Voices*).
16. **"Unclean hands."** Argue that equitable defenses cannot cancel a statutory duty owed to the protected class [no direct authority found]. Do not repeat the "zero drinks" claim.
17. **"The DMV APS suspension shows the arrest was lawful."**
    - § 13353.2(e) makes the DMV determination "a civil matter that is independent of the determination of the person's guilt or innocence" (https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/vehicle-code/veh-sect-13353-2/).
    - Collateral estoppel requires an issue that was actually litigated; if no hearing was held, nothing was litigated.
    - *Gikas v. Zolin* (1993) 6 Cal.4th 841 addresses this statutory scheme [verify holding before citing].

## 13. Proposed Findings for Direct Certification

1. CHP is a "certifying entity" (§ 679.10(a)(1)), and Serrato is a "certifying official" (§ 679.10(b)).
2. On April 9, 2026, Petitioner requested a Form I-918 Supplement B certification under § 679.10(g)(1).
3. Section 679.10(g)(1) requires certification when the requester was a victim of qualifying criminal activity and has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful. Once those conditions are established, certification is not discretionary.
4. False imprisonment (§ 679.10(c)(19); Pen. Code §§ 236, 237(a)) is qualifying criminal activity. A restraint under color of arrest is not excluded where the arrest lacked lawful authority.
5. On November 1, 2024, at approximately 01:11, CHP officers intentionally restrained Petitioner against his will by handcuffing, transporting, and booking him.
6. On the facts known at 01:11:16, as shown by the native recording and CHP's records, reasonable cause to believe Petitioner had been driving under the influence did not exist. In particular:
   - (a) the Walk and Turn test was not performed because Officer Melano declined to administer it, not because Petitioner refused;
   - (b) declining the PAS was the exercise of a statutory right;
   - (c) [further findings supported by expert or other admissible evidence].
7. The restraint was unlawful, and Petitioner was a direct victim of false imprisonment (§ 679.10(o)(2)).
8. No law enforcement agency requested information or assistance that Petitioner refused or failed to provide. The § 679.10(h) presumption is unrebutted.
9. Charges, prosecution, conviction, and officer discipline are not required (§ 679.10(k)(1)).
10. The certifying official's discretion can be exercised in only one way.

## 14. Proposed Findings for Remand

1. Findings 1–4 and 8–9 above.
2. The April 20, 2026 denial does not identify the facts, evidence, or elements relied on, and does not provide the explanation required by § 679.10(i)(2). *[superseded]*
3. The CHP 202 and DS-367 rely on "poor performance on the FST's" and report that Petitioner "chose not to perform" the Walk and Turn test. The native recording shows Petitioner agreed to attempt it and Officer Melano advised against it because of "uneven ground." The CHP 202 describes the surface as "clear, cool, and dry."
4. The record does not show that the certifying official considered the recording, or the conflict described in Finding 3, before denying.
5. The discrepancy is material, because qualifying criminal activity depends on whether the restraint was lawful, and that was assessed in part on the inaccurate entries.
6. The denial was not made in the manner required by law and cannot stand. The Court does not decide whether the arrest was lawful.
