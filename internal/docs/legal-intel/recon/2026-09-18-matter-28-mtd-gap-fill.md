## 1. Gap UNIFIED flagged: PLRA “prisoner” — closed

UNIFIED § 5.2: “Verify the PLRA ‘prisoner’ point before briefing *Patsy*.”

**Hold.** *Patsy v. Board of Regents*, 457 U.S. 496, 516 (1982) still means state administrative exhaustion is not a prerequisite to § 1983. The only statutory carve-out is 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(a) (PLRA), and it applies only to a “prisoner.”

Statutory text:

- § 1997e(a): “No action shall be brought with respect to prison conditions … **by a prisoner confined in any jail, prison, or other correctional facility** until such administrative remedies as are available are exhausted.”
- § 1997e(h): “prisoner” means “any person **incarcerated or detained in any facility** who is accused of, convicted of, sentenced for, or adjudicated delinquent for, violations of criminal law or the terms and conditions of parole ….”

Ninth Circuit pin:

- *Talamantes v. Leyva*, 575 F.3d 1021, 1023–24 (9th Cir. 2009). The exhaustion requirement applies only to individuals who are “prisoners” as defined by § 1997e(h) **at the time they file suit**. A person not incarcerated or detained in a facility at filing is not a PLRA prisoner. Follows *Page v. Torrey*, 201 F.3d 1136, 1139–40 (9th Cir. 2000) (civilly committed person is not a PLRA prisoner).

Sister-circuit corroboration (use only as “we are not alone,” not as Ninth Circuit law): *Kerr v. Puckett*, 138 F.3d 321, 323 (7th Cir. 1998) (“a convict out on parole is not a ‘person incarcerated or detained in any facility’”).

**Caveat — do not overclaim.** A parolee **confined in a residential facility he is not free to leave** can still be a “prisoner.” See *Jackson v. Johnson*, 475 F.3d 261, 265–67 (5th Cir. 2007) (out-of-circuit). If Plaintiff was at liberty in the community on July 20, 2026 (filing date), *Talamantes* applies. If he was in a lockup, halfway house with no exit, or jail, reopen this paragraph before filing.

**How to use it.** One paragraph after *Patsy*. Do not build a PLRA section. The defense preview did not name PLRA; this is a pre-buttal if they try to launder Matter 5 exhaustion into a federal exhaustion bar.

## 2. Gap: *Merrill* and *Gross* — how to distinguish, not how to hide

UNIFIED already named *Merrill v. Mental Health Systems*, No. 16-cv-1090, 2016 WL 4761808 (S.D. Cal. Sept. 13, 2016) as the dangerous analog. *Gross v. Samudio*, 651 F. App’x 753 (10th Cir. 2015) (unpublished), *aff’g* D. Colo. 11-cv-02594, is the out-of-circuit cousin.

### 2.1 What *Merrill* actually held

S.D. Cal. granted 12(b)(6) against private substance-abuse contractors treating parolees. Tests applied: state compulsion, public function, joint action / nexus. The court held:

- Compulsion failed because the State neither compelled nor was “directly involved in” the challenged facility decisions (*Am. Mfrs. Mut. Ins. Co. v. Sullivan*, 526 U.S. 40, 52 (1999)).
- Public function failed because “provision of substance abuse treatment to parolees” was not alleged to be a power “traditionally the exclusive prerogative of the State” (*Rendell-Baker v. Kohn*, 457 U.S. 830, 842 (1982)).
- Joint action / nexus failed on internal protocol, premises, pest control, and medical non-assistance — conduct the court said could not “fairly be attributed to the State.”

*Merrill* is **district-court**, **substance-abuse housing / facility conditions**, and **not** a CASOMB SOMP vendor writing CDCR Form 3043 reports that move parole liberty.

### 2.2 What *Gross* actually held

*Gross* is **Tenth Circuit unpublished**. Plaintiff alleged private Colorado SOMB-regulated sex-offender treatment providers acted under color of law by refusing to admit him, which then fed parole consequences. The Tenth Circuit affirmed: regulation by SOMB + ability to reject a referred parolee ≠ public function, nexus, joint action, or symbiotic relationship under *Gallagher v. “Neil Young Freedom Concert,”* 49 F.3d 1442 (10th Cir. 1995). “Merely private conduct, no matter how discriminatory or wrongful,” is not § 1983 (*Am. Mfrs.*, 526 U.S. at 50).

Use *Gross* only as “they will wave an unpublished Tenth Circuit case; here is why it is not this complaint.” Do not cite it as adverse Ninth Circuit law.

### 2.3 Distinguishers that have to be in the complaint (not invented in the brief)

The 12(b)(6) fight is won or lost on what is already pleaded. The analog dies if the operative complaint alleges, with Bates or form numbers:

1. **Joint action with named parole agents** — HOPE / Smith directing or feeding Form 3043 / 1502-DR content that DAPO then used. *Franklin v. Fox*, 312 F.3d 423, 444–45 (9th Cir. 2002) (joint action is a conspiracy or willful participation in joint activity). Cluster lock already flags HOPE_000142 / 194 (no-show after agent-confirmed technical error) and the Nov. 14, 2025 1502-DR.
2. **Public function / *West* medical-care-while-the-State-holds-the-body.** *West v. Atkins*, 487 U.S. 42 (1988) (private physician under contract to treat prisoners is a state actor). *Jensen v. Lane County*, 222 F.3d 570 (9th Cir. 2000) (private psychiatrist in the county civil-commitment process). Parole-mandated SOMP is closer to *West* / *Jensen* than to *Merrill*’s pest-control / housing facts **if** the complaint ties treatment completion and adverse reports to a state-created liberty interest (*Neal v. Shimoda*, 131 F.3d 818 (9th Cir. 1997); *Morrissey v. Brewer*, 408 U.S. 471 (1972)).
3. **State compulsion / aftercare policy.** HOPE_000294 (full-term / cannot complete while on parole). *In re Dwyer*, SC-080355A (San Mateo Super. Ct. Mar. 20, 2023) is **policy color**, not issue preclusion against Smith (HOPE was not a *Dwyer* party; cluster lock). Do not say *Dwyer* “held Smith liable.”
4. **Gravamen.** *Merrill* is conditions-of-a-treatment-house. This case is alleged retaliatory / fabricated reporting into the parole machinery plus a blanket retention policy. Say that in one sentence. Do not relitigate the pest-control pages.

*Halvorsen v. Baird*, 146 F.3d 680 (9th Cir. 1998) is already on the safe-cite list as a QI-threshold / private-contractor case. It is **not** a clean state-action win — the private detox facility was treated as suable, and QI was denied to the private firm under *Richardson v. McKnight*, 521 U.S. 399 (1997), but the verdict picture is mixed. Use it for “private contractor performing a state statutory hold is not automatically immune,” not as “every treatment vendor is a state actor.”

## 4. Bane Act — *Cornell* + Ninth Circuit adoption

Defense preview: “no threat / intimidation / coercion” (*Shoyoye v. County of Los Angeles*, 203 Cal. App. 4th 947 (2012)).

**Hold the line UNIFIED already picked.**

- *Cornell v. City & County of San Francisco*, 17 Cal. App. 5th 766, 799–804 (2017) (review denied): the Bane Act does **not** require coercion “independent” of the rights violation. The test is **specific intent** to violate a clearly delineated right, which is satisfied by **reckless disregard** of that right.
- *Reese v. County of Sacramento*, 888 F.3d 1030, 1043–45 (9th Cir. 2018): the Ninth Circuit **adopted** *Cornell*’s specific-intent standard.
- *Sandoval v. County of Sonoma*, 912 F.3d 509, 519–20 (9th Cir. 2018): same two-step (*Cornell* at 803) — (1) is the right clearly delineated and plainly applicable; (2) did the defendant act with the particular purpose of depriving the plaintiff of it, including reckless disregard.

**Do not plead Civ. Code § 52.1(b)’s $25,000 civil penalty as a private claim** (UNIFIED § 7). Damages run through § 52.1(b) / § 52 as already pleaded; do not invent a penalty count.

*Shoyoye* remains their case if the 3043 / no-show facts look like clerical error. The cluster lock exists to keep HOPE_000142 / 194 and the Eagan copy-paste in the frame so “clerical” is a jury argument, not a 12(b)(6) concedable.

## 5. First Amendment retaliation — *Watison* elements

*Watison v. Carter*, 668 F.3d 1108, 1114–15 (9th Cir. 2012), applying *Rhodes v. Robinson*, 408 F.3d 559, 567–68 (9th Cir. 2005), and *Brodheim v. Cry*, 584 F.3d 1262, 1269 (9th Cir. 2009):

1. Protected conduct (grievance / complaint / petitioning).
2. Adverse action (need not be an independent constitutional violation).
3. Causal connection.
4. Action would chill a person of ordinary firmness.
5. Did not advance legitimate correctional / parole goals (arbitrary, capricious, or unnecessary to order).

UNIFIED’s factual hook: Aug. 19, 2025 “ringleader” / “abhorrent” statements to parole agents. Map each *Watison* element onto a numbered complaint paragraph in the opposition; do not assume the court will do the mapping.

## 6. RICO — standing is not the problem; proximate cause still is

UNIFIED already split these. This pass confirms the 2025 Supreme Court pin.

- *Medical Marijuana, Inc. v. Horn*, 604 U.S. 593 (2025) (No. 23-365, Apr. 2, 2025): § 1964(c) “injured in his business or property” is about the **kind** of harm recovered, not the **cause**. Lost wages / employment harm can be “business or property” even if a personal-injury event sits upstream. The Court **did not** decide proximate cause, did not decide whether “business” always equals employment, and did not relax particularity.
- *Diaz v. Gates*, 420 F.3d 897 (9th Cir. 2005) (en banc) remains the Ninth Circuit lost-wages-as-property case.
- *Anza v. Ideal Steel Supply Corp.*, 547 U.S. 451 (2006) is still the dangerous **proximate-cause** case when the alleged fraud is on CDCR and Plaintiff is downstream.
- *Cedric Kushner Promotions, Ltd. v. King*, 533 U.S. 158 (2001): a corporate employee is a “person” distinct from the corporate “enterprise.” Useful if they run a person/enterprise distinctness attack on Smith-as-CEO-of-HOPE. Verify the PDF before briefing; do not expand RICO to carry the case.

**Recommendation unchanged:** keep RICO lean, particularized (Rule 9(b)), and secondary. Do not build the settlement number on treble damages (cluster 2).
