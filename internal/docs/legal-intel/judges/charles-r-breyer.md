# Dossier: Senior U.S. District Judge Charles R. Breyer — CourtListener Docket Audit

**Who:** Charles Roberts Breyer, Senior U.S. District Judge, Northern District of California,
San Francisco. Nominated by President William J. Clinton (July 24, 1997) to the seat vacated by
D. Lowell Jensen; confirmed by voice vote (Nov. 8, 1997); commission Nov. 12, 1997. **Assumed
senior status Dec. 31, 2011** but continues to carry a full, high-profile docket. Harvard College
A.B. 1963; UC Berkeley Law J.D. 1966; clerked for Judge Oliver J. Carter (N.D. Cal.).
Professional background: Legal Aid Society of San Francisco (1967); Assistant DA, City and
County of San Francisco (1967–1973; chief assistant 1979); **Assistant Special Prosecutor,
Watergate Special Prosecution Force (1973–1974)**; private practice, San Francisco (1974–1979,
1980–1997). Judicial governance: Judicial Conference of the United States (2006–2010); U.S.
Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (2011–2018); U.S. Sentencing Commission (2013–2022;
vice chair 2013–2017, acting chair 2021–2022). Younger brother of Justice Stephen Breyer.
Sources: [FJC bio](https://www.fjc.gov/node/1390436),
[Ninth Circuit announcement](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/uploads/ce9/590-Breyer_Confirmed_to_USSC.pdf),
[MetNews profile](http://www.metnews.com/articles/2013/brey061013.htm)

**Structural context:** A senior judge with a reduced-but-selective docket who nonetheless
draws the district's biggest MDLs and politically charged government cases. His current docket
is dominated by the ~5,000-case Uber passenger sexual-assault MDL (centralized Oct. 2023) and,
in 2025, the *Newsom v. Trump* National Guard litigation. His standing orders were refreshed
in July 2025 ([standing orders page](https://cand.uscourts.gov/judges/standing-orders)):
Standing Order (July 16, 2025) plus separate trial guidelines for civil bench cases (July 29,
2025) and civil jury cases (July 16, 2025). **Read the current standing order before filing
anything in a Breyer case** — it governs motion practice, not local rules alone.

**Method honesty:** Sampled audit, not a census. Built from (a) the full CourtListener opinion
corpus for Breyer in N.D. Cal. — **212 authored opinions, 1998–2019** (CourtListener's N.D.
Cal. district-opinion coverage ends mid-2019; the court has no active opinion scraper, so
nothing later is in that corpus); (b) CourtListener RECAP docket search, which proved too

(c) verified 2024–2026 news and Justia records for his two headline matters. Individual
opinion outcomes below 2019 were categorized from case names and docket metadata; full texts
were not re-read for every entry.

## Case-type breakdown (observed activity)

**1. Mass-tort / MDL leadership — the defining assignment.** Sits as an MDL transferee judge
by reputation and JPML experience (he sat on the Panel 2011–2018):
- *In re Volkswagen "Clean Diesel" Mktg., Sales Practices, & Prods. Liab. Litig.*, MDL 2672
  (2016–2018 opinions) — steered the landmark consumer settlement.
- *In re German Auto. Mfrs. Antitrust Litig.*, MDL 2796 (2019).
- *In re Bextra & Celebrex Mktg. Sales Practices & Prod. Liab. Litig.*, MDL 1699 (2007).
- *In re Transpacific Passenger Air Transportation Antitrust Litig.* (2014).
- *In re Air Crash Over Mid-Atlantic* (2010–2011).
- **Uber passenger sexual-assault MDL (2023–present):** ~79 actions centralized Oct. 2023;
  roughly 5,000 cases by Sept. 2026. Bellwether program with trials (Arizona jury: $8.5M;
  North Carolina: $5,000; third trial set Sept. 30, 2026).

**2. Employment cases — deep bench.** A dozen-plus employment opinions in the corpus:
*Dukes v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.* (2013 — the remanded nationwide class action, class
certification on remand); *Cuevas v. SkyWest Airlines* (2014); *Pyke v. Arcadis, US Inc.*
(2014); *Hendricks v. AT&T Mobility* (2011); *Prather v. AT&T Inc.* (2013); *Phillips v.
Kaiser Foundation Health Plan* (2011); *Schlegel v. Wells Fargo Bank* (2011); *Moreno v.
Autozone* (2008); *Chowdhury v. Northwest Airlines Corp.* (2002, 2004); *Farac v. Permanente
Medical Group* (2002); *Leung v. Skidmore, Owings & Merrill LLP* (2002); *Torres v. AT&T
Broadband* (2001); *Kucharczyk v. Regents of the Univ. of Cal.* (1999); *Salazar v. Golden
State Warriors* (2000); ADA testing-accommodations case *Enyart v. Nat'l Conference of Bar
Examiners* (2011). **Directly on point for the portfolio:** *Soo v. United Parcel Service,
Inc.*, 73 F. Supp. 2d 1126 (N.D. Cal. 1999) — a published Breyer opinion in a UPS case
(existence and citation verified; holding not re-reviewed for this audit).

**3. Criminal — independent, government-skeptical sentencer.** *United States v. Rosenthal*
(2003, medical-marijuana prosecution); *United States v. Cannabis Cultivators Club* (1998);
*United States v. Lazarenko* (2008–2009, Ukrainian ex-PM money laundering); *United States
v. Bogucki* (2018 — **acquitted** the HSBC forex trader at bench trial); *United States v.
Giraudo* (2016); *United States v. Arteaga-Centeno* (2019). His Sentencing Commission
tenure (vice chair, then acting chair) makes him one of the federal bench's leading
sentencing-policy voices.

**4. Civil rights / First Amendment.** *Hoye v. City of Oakland* (2009, abortion-clinic
buffer zone); *Citizens for Free Speech v. County of Alameda* (2014–2016, billboard
ordinance); *Cal. Parents for Equalization of Educ. Materials v. Torlakson* (2017, 2019);
*Levin v. City & County of San Francisco* (2014, 2017).

**5. Pro se prisoner habeas (§2254) — steady volume.** Regular adjudication of pro se
prisoner petitions across the corpus (*Rush v. Curry*, *Ruvalcaba v. Curry*, *Delgado v.
Yates*, *Glenn v. Berndt*, *Smith v. Pliler*, *Woodard v. Mayberg*, *Travasso v. Clark*,
*Chin v. Runnels*, *Galle v. Clark*, *McCollum v. California*, *Serna v. Lizarraga*).
Individual outcomes were not reviewed for this audit.

**6. Commercial / IP / securities.** *Craigslist v. 3taps* (2013); *In re Tesla Motors
Securities Litig.* (2014); *Blizzard v. Lilith Games* (2015); *SEC v. Reyes* (2007, stock-options
backdating); *Depomed v. Ivax* (2006); *Hitachi v. Tatung* (2006); *SanDisk v. Lexar* (2000);
*Heller Ehrman* bankruptcy adversary matters (2011, 2014).

## Notable 2024–2026 rulings

- ***Newsom v. Trump*, 3:25-cv-04870-CRB:** June 2025 TRO, then preliminary injunction, then
  a **52-page Sept. 2, 2025 order** holding the Trump administration violated the Posse
  Comitatus Act by deploying National Guard troops and Marines to Los Angeles — enjoining
  further domestic-law military deployments in California; **renewed preliminary injunction
  granted Dec. 10, 2025**. Drew a House impeachment resolution (H.Res. 556, introduced June
  27, 2025, referred to the Judiciary Committee — no further action verified).
  ([9/2/25 order](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2025cv04870/450934/176/0.pdf),
  [12/10/25 order](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2025cv04870/450934/225/0.pdf),
  [H.Res. 556](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-resolution/556/text))
- **Uber MDL — July 2025 37-page bellwether dismissal order:** dismissed fraud claims
  (the "Don't drink and drive, call an Uber" ads were "merely encouragement") and
  product-liability claims (app not defective for failing to prevent high-risk
  driver–passenger pairings), **but preserved** the deceptive-notification theory (app
  notifications "could form a deceptive scheme to obfuscate the risk of serious harm") and
  **denied dismissal of vicarious-liability claims** under California law — one bellwether
  case dismissed entirely, others narrowed.
  ([Reuters/SRNNews](https://srnnews.com/us-judge-dismisses-some-claims-in-uber-sexual-assault-lawsuits/))
- **Uber MDL — contract vs. MDL:** held Uber's terms-of-use "non-consolidation clause"
  unenforceable — it would "nullify the judiciary's ability to manage litigation" and
  "substantially interferes with the public interests that Congress sought to advance" in
  creating the MDL; denied Uber's stay pending Ninth Circuit review of centralization.
  ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/class-action/ubers-terms-of-use-fails-to-split-up-mass-sexual-assault-suits),
  [stay denial](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/uber-stay-request-denied-in-federal-sexual-harassment-cases))
- **Uber MDL — settlement pressure (Sept. 8, 2026):** publicly told Uber to "change its
  attitude" and pursue a universal settlement — "It's not working" — with ~5,000 cases
  pending and bellwether verdicts not producing convergence.
  ([Bloomberg Gov](https://news.bgov.com/texas-brief/ubers-assault-case-plan-not-working-judge-urges-resolution),
  [Law.com 7/22/26](https://www.law.com/2026/07/22/im-trying-to-help-both-sides-judge-in-uber-sex-assault-trials-wants-bellwether-plan-replenished/))

## Observable patterns — for a pro se employment civil-rights plaintiff

1. **MTD style: surgical trimming, not wholesale dismissal.** The July 2025 Uber bellwether
   order is the template — fraud and product-liability theories cut, negligence and
   vicarious-liability theories preserved. Plead each count so the strongest theory survives
   even if adjacent counts fall; do not bundle everything into one fraud-adjacent count.
2. **Not intimidated by powerful defendants.** Ruled against a sitting President's military
   deployment; voided Uber's contract clause as an interference with the MDL statute. A
   well-briefed statutory claim against a large corporate defendant gets a real hearing here.
3. **Pro-settlement and hands-on.** The Sept. 2026 "change its attitude" hearing, routine
   class-settlement approvals in the corpus, and MDL bellwether management all point the
   same way: he will push parties toward resolution, and a settlement-conference proposal
   fits his practice. Expect pressure on *both* sides, not just the defense.
4. **Employment-pleading fluency.** With *Dukes* on remand plus a dozen employment opinions,
   he has seen every variant of the 12(b)(6)/summary-judgment employment fight. Conclusory
   discrimination allegations will not survive; fact-pleaded comparator and pretext
   allegations will get their day.
5. **Reads the papers, holds the hearing.** The Newsom pretrial order (July 19, 2025) shows
   tight, detailed case management with live testimony — he engages deeply rather than
   ruling from chambers alone. Briefing quality matters; oral argument is real.
6. **Criminal-side independence cuts both ways on credibility.** A judge who acquits at
   bench trial (*Bogucki*) and spars with the executive branch is persuadable by evidence
   and statute, not by authority or volume. Cite the record, not the outrage.
7. **Check the standing order first.** Refreshed July 2025; his motion-practice rules live
   there as much as in the local rules.
8. **Senior-status docket reality:** he takes fewer cases but bigger ones. If a portfolio
   matter lands before him, expect a slower, more deliberate pace than a busy active-status
   docket — and a judge with ample time to read everything.
