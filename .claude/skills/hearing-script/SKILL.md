# Hearing cribs

The user speaks for himself, in propria persona. A page of "If they say… / Your Honor, the Attorney General asks…" is a brief with stage directions. If he reads it, the Court hears a script.

## Two artifacts, never one

When they ask what to say in court, produce **both**:

1. **Night-before memo** — labeled `DO NOT TAKE TO THE LECTERN`. Full argument, cites, traps, AG attacks. They read this at home. Headings like "If the AG contests count one" belong only here.
2. **Lectern card** — one page, 8–12 bullets. This is the only page that may be in front of them while they talk.

If they are already in the hearing or about to walk in, send **only the lectern card** in the chat. No memo. No "If they say."

## Lectern card rules

- No heading that is a cue to the other side: never "If they say," "Q. / A.," "Point A," "Script," "Read this."
- No complete "Your Honor, …" paragraphs. Those get read aloud and sound recited.
- Opening: at most four short sentences they can say without looking down.
- Body: exhibit numbers, statute subdivisions, and three-to-five-word cues (`851.6 certificate`, `conflict of exhibits`, `679.10(k)`).
- One case name only if they already know it cold. No string cites.
- Closing: one sentence (the ask) they can memorize.
- First person. Their voice. Not a filed opposition.

## Chat while a hearing is live

Give two or three spoken sentences and stop. No labels. No menu of hypotheticals.

## After the hearing

Do not invent the ruling. Retire the calendar entry as held. Record "minute order not yet in hand" until they supply the order or a specific holding.
