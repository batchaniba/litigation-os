# 14 — Post-draft assessment: is Matter 20 a winning writ as written?
**2026-10-23 | Open-record, candid prefiling audit | No filing/service authorized.**
**Verdict: NOT YET.** The current #1027 motion in `10-PEREMPTORY-WRIT-MOTION-DRAFT.md` is a fair *incident evidence* brief but a comparatively weak *Serrato decision abuse-of-discretion* brief. The other model's strategic criticism is substantially correct. A remand is more plausible than direct certification on existing proof, but neither is established merely by a short letter or an erroneous officer report.

## The precise error of focus
- Section III–IV of the draft (~first half of its MPA) presents potentially useful officer-entry, search and probable-cause questions.
- Section V on the actual **April 20 denial** is the shortest critical section and asserts an unproved absence of lawful agency review.
- To get a writ, Petitioner must **prove (not simply suspect)** that the underlying qualifying-criminal-activity threshold and helpfulness requirements are met **or** prove identifiable agency legal error/abuse justifying vacatur. An unconstitutional vehicle **search** alone is not automatically criminal false imprisonment or PC 679.10 qualifying victimhood.
- Section 679.10(g)(1)'s "shall" is **conditional**, not an automatic order upon submitting information. Mandamus cannot displace genuine lawful evaluative discretion absent proven predicates leaving only one option.

## Exact denial: quoted in the filed federal FAC, not yet independently checked against original signed letter
An existing sourced filing, N.D. Cal. Serrato FAC Dkt.10, filed July 15, 2026, ¶29, reproduces this as Serrato's five sentences:
1. "Recently, you requested that the California Highway Patrol (CHP) Dublin Area certify your Form I-918 Supplement B, U Nonimmigrant Status Certification, stemming from an incident that occurred on November 1, 2024."
2. "A thorough review of this incident was conducted."
3. "Unfortunately, this incident does not meet the standard of qualifying criminal activity identified in Section 679.10 of the California Penal Code, nor does it meet the standards of the Immigration and Nationality Act Section 101(a)(15)(U)(iii)."
4. "Additionally, this review found no substantially similar qualifying criminal activity as defined in Section 679.10(d) PC."
5. "Therefore, your request to certify your Form I-918, Supplement B, U Nonimmigrant Status Certification, was denied."

**IMPORTANT:** The prior internal `10-skeleton.md` incorrectly characterizes sentence 3 as *recognizing general victim assistance*; sentence 3 quoted in the federal FAC is a substantive qualifying-activity rejection. Do not rely on that old sentence map. Before court use, copy directly from Petition Exhibit 1 scanned original PDF page ~45 and retain exact punctuation. The letter expressly addresses **qualifying** and **substantially similar** crime, and claims a completed review. It does **not** facially invoke no charges, applicant history, immigration background, PAS refusal, or lack of helpfulness. Merely not naming PC §236 or not discussing each exhibit is not sufficient proof of unlawful decision; Count 2 separate explanation duty was dismissed. Eleven days to decide is timely and **does not prove** it was impossible to review.

## The strongest possible opposition order *on this draft*
"The Court preserved Count One at pleading, but does not find a legal error in Serrato's qualifying-activity analysis or an established criminal false-imprisonment offense on these disputed facts. CHP identified the statutory victimhood criterion in its denial. Alleged unreasonable vehicle searches do not themselves prove qualifying criminal confinement. A nonverbalized odor observation does not negate that observation, a sub-0.08 sample 86 minutes later does not disprove reasonable cause under §23152(a) at arrest, absence of charges is not an adjudication, and §851.6 is a detention classification, not judicial officer guilt. Without a proven legal-standard error, direct certification and remand are unwarranted." THIS IS A RED-TEAM TEST, NOT A PREDICTION OF WHAT WHITMAN WILL DO.

## Prioritize the revised brief in this order
1. **A. Exact decision + legal question (~15–20%)**: five-sentence letter, proof of April 9 full packet received, posture Count One, evidence CHP had, Serrato's precise qualifying-activity findings and factual/legal basis if disclosed.
2. **B. Statutory threshold (~20%)**: §679.10(c),(d),(g),(h),(k); Penal Code §236 unlawful criminal restraint and possibly §146 as an alternative offense with overlapping elements, without assuming any officer criminal liability. **Tie each element to admitted facts and precise contradictory evidence**.
3. **C. Source-pinned incident record (~25%)**: produce *narrow factual grid*, not extensive §1983 car-entry argument. Pre-entry "I think it's booze" and 00:56:40/43 door opening are important context; explain exactly whether/how either establishes **criminal personal restraint**, or keep it for separate federal action. Include CHP's reported odor, HGN, Romberg, eye signs, can, admitted prior driving and VC §40300.5 possibilities; don't hide them. Conceded parole timing from Plaintiff and 01:11 dispatch is significant for federal *search*, but not automatically a qualifying crime.
4. **D. Prove decisional error/abuse (~25–30%)**: link each finding to what was submitted before Apr 20 and, if known, what Serrato actually reviewed. Do not infer sham solely from 11 days, five sentences, or no expressly described exhibits. Address alternative lawful evaluation rather than demand de novo substitute for CHP.
5. **E. Helpfulness and requested relief (~10–15%)**: support §679.10(h) on complaints/cooperation; clarify that PAS refusal is separate; direct certification only with full finding, alternative vacatur/remand only on proved abuse.

**Do not overemphasize sentence-by-sentence rhetorical demolition**: Sentence 1 acknowledges request, 2 asserts review, 3 legally identifies threshold, 4 denies substantially similar activity, 5 gives disposition. What matters is whether **sentences 3–4 are substantively or procedurally unlawful**, not whether sentences 1,2,5 lack detailed exhibit discussion.
