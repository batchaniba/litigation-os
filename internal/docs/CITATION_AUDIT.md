## Part 1 — The "AI hallucination" accusation, resolved

Two separate quoted-authority claims are at issue in Matter 5's demurrer reply, and they resolve differently on independent verification:

**8 C.F.R. § 214.14(a)(14)(ii)** (Matter 2's U-visa certification motion) — **this one is real.** The litigant's motion quoted this subsection as saying "the absence of criminal charges... is not disqualifying." That language does not appear in (a)(14)(ii), which actually addresses witness-tampering/obstruction/perjury victims — a different topic entirely. Opposing counsel's characterization here is accurate. To the litigant's credit, his own Reply partially catches and corrects the error, redirecting to the actually-supportive subsection (a)(12) and USCIS guidance.

> Reply ISO Motion for U-Visa Certification: *"...the proposition...rests on 8 C.F.R. section 214.14(a)(12) and the USCIS guidance quoted above—not on subsection (a)(14)(ii). To the extent the Motion's citation to (a)(14)(ii) caused confusion, Plaintiff respectfully clarifies the controlling authority."*

**15 CCR § 3574(f)(1)** ("[t]reatment completion and parole discharge are legally separate decisions") — **opposing counsel's "hallucination" framing appears to overstate the problem.** CDCR's own NCR 25-01 regulatory notice, issued in January 2025 to implement the real ACSOL v. Macomber settlement, amended § 3574 to state that program-completion review is "independent of whether the supervised person should be discharged from parole" — the same substantive concept. Available evidence suggests the language sits at a different subsection (likely (e), not (f)(1)) — a pinpoint-cite error, not a fabrication. Notably, defense counsel's own reply cites yet another version of the same regulation with a different subsection letter and date, suggesting both sides may be working from different regulatory snapshots of an evolving rule. **Recommendation: rebut the hallucination characterization directly, but correct the subsection citation before further briefing.**

## Part 2 — Fabricated or seriously mis-attributed authority

These are the citations that would not survive a judge's or opposing counsel's own cite-check, ranked by severity.

### Likely fabricated (no such case exists)

**"In re Leman (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 200"** — Matters 9 and 19. Extensive searching across CourtListener, Google Scholar, Justia, and Leagle's own volume index for 66 Cal.App.5th found no trace of a case by this name at this or any citation. It is reused with an **identical invented quote** — "one-size-fits-all... individualized consideration of current risk" — in two separate filings (Matter 9's opening brief and Matter 19's habeas petition), cited as reaffirming *In re Gadlin*'s "wholesale exclusion" holding. This is the single most serious finding in the audit and should be removed from both filings immediately.

**"Friends of the Trinity River v. North Coast Regional Water Quality Control Bd. (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1466"** — Matter 14. No matching case exists at this citation or under this name anywhere searched. It's cited for a routine, black-letter CCP §1085 mandate-relief proposition that never needed an obscure water-quality-board case to support it in the first place — a red flag in itself. Should be pulled or replaced with a standard mandate-relief citation (e.g., *AIDS Healthcare Foundation v. L.A. County*).

### Real case, wrong court — resolved by cross-referencing two audit groups

**Alliance for Constitutional Sex Offense Laws (ACSOL) v. Macomber** is cited in two different places in this portfolio, and comparing the two independent audit passes resolves what looked at first like a fabricated case:

- Matters 5 and 6 cite it as **No. 23STCP02378 (L.A. County Superior Court, demurrer overruled Dec. 13, 2023, Beckloff, J.)** — confirmed real via ACSOL's own public statements. This citation is correct.
- Matters 9 and 19 cite the **same case number and date** but call it a **Sacramento County Superior Court** case — it isn't. The real Sacramento ACSOL case is a different, later filing (No. 25WM000131, filed July 16, 2025); the case-number format "23STCP..." itself matches L.A. County's convention, not Sacramento's.

**Net finding: the underlying case is real and Matters 5/6 cite it correctly. Matters 9 and 19 need the court corrected from "Sacramento Superior Court" to "Los Angeles County Superior Court."** As currently written, anyone checking the Sacramento docket for this case number would find nothing — exactly the kind of error a motivated reader would flag, and the litigant relies on it for venue argument, as claimed "precedent," and as an attached exhibit in the habeas petition.

### Real case, wrong judge — also resolved by cross-referencing

**In re Dwyer**'s presiding judge is cited as "(Hill, J.)" in Matters 3, 5, and 6. Matter 9/19's independent reading of the actual attached exhibit **confirms this is correct** — the real judge is Hon. Elizabeth M. Hill. However, Matter 5's own Request for Judicial Notice describes the same attached exhibit as being from judges "Hon. Susan Jakubowski / Hon. Lisa Novak" — a name that appears nowhere else and conflicts with the independently-confirmed correct judge. **The FAC's citation is right; Matter 5's RJN has the error.** This is an unforced, easily-checked internal inconsistency that should be corrected immediately given the pending hallucination accusation in this exact matter — a mistake like this invites the very scrutiny that led to the accusation in the first place.

**Soranno's Gasco, Inc. v. Morgan Hill Unified School District, 874 F.2d 1310 (9th Cir. 1989)** — Matter 24. The reporter citation is real, but the case name is wrong. The actual case is *Soranno's Gasco, Inc. v. Morgan* — a petroleum dealer's First Amendment retaliation suit against individual air-pollution-control-district officers. "Morgan Hill Unified School District" is a real, unrelated Santa Clara County school district; the individual defendant's surname ("Morgan") appears to have been expanded into a fabricated institutional party. Correct to *Soranno's Gasco, Inc. v. Morgan* before further use.

## Part 3 — Real cases cited for holdings they don't support

- **Caldwell v. Montoya (1995) 10 Cal.4th 972** (Matter 14) — cited backwards. The complaint uses it to argue discretionary-act immunity "does not shield officials who act outside the scope of their legal authority." Caldwell actually held the opposite on its facts, upholding immunity for school board members even against alleged improper-motive claims. The general ministerial/discretionary principle asserted is sound California law; Caldwell just doesn't support it.
- **People v. Superior Court (Laff) (2001) 25 Cal.4th 703** (Matter 9) — real case, but it concerns attorney-client privilege review and special-master costs, not parole discretionary/ministerial duty determinations.
- **County of Contra Costa v. State of California (1986) 177 Cal.App.3d 62** (Matter 9) — real case, but it's a state-mandate-reimbursement dispute, not a general "pure questions of law" doctrine case.
- **Aryeh v. Canon Business Solutions, Inc.** (Matter 12) — cited as "(2013) 1 Cal.5th 1185"; the real citation is **55 Cal.4th 1185**. The California Reports 5th series didn't begin until 2016 — a Cal.5th cite for a 2013 case is chronologically impossible. The underlying discovery-rule doctrine is otherwise correctly described.
- **In re Dwyer** itself (Matter 9's FAC) — one quote attributed to CDCR testimony ("cannot successfully 'complete'... while on parole") is presented as verbatim but the real order reads "...short of being discharged from parole." Similar substance, wrong wording presented in quotation marks.
