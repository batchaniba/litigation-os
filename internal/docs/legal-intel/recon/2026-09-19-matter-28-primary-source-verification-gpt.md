## 1A — Meinhardt v. City of Sunnyvale, 16 Cal.5th 643 (2024)

**SOURCE READ:** California Supreme Court opinion PDF:
https://www.courts.ca.gov/opinions/documents/S274147.PDF

**STATUS:** VERIFIED.

**Disposition / holding.** California Supreme Court reversed the Court of Appeal. In the administrative-mandate setting before it, the appeal clock ran from entry/service of the document titled **JUDGMENT**, not the earlier document titled **ORDER** denying the writ.

The Court expressly rejected the Court of Appeal's approach of starting the appeal clock from an earlier order merely because it was sufficiently final to constitute the judgment. The opinion states, in substance, that time to appeal runs from entry/service of "judgment," rather than filing/service of an "order," minute order, or other ruling.

The opinion also notes that neither the City nor the Court of Appeal argued for a general rule that the appeal period in all civil cases begins upon filing any order sufficiently final to constitute the judgment.

**Important scope limit.** *Meinhardt* is an appealability/timeliness case arising from administrative mandate. It is **not** a claim-preclusion or issue-preclusion decision.

**Effect on Matter 28 verification.** It cuts against any broad proposition that a fully dispositive order should automatically be treated as the judgment merely because nothing more appears to remain. It does **not by itself decide preclusion** from an order sustaining a demurrer.

**Prompt contradiction?** YES — any internal statement saying *Meinhardt* "adopted" the sufficiently-final-order approach is backwards.

## 1B — Estate of Esche v. Bunuel-Jordana, Nos. 24-6244 et al. (9th Cir. Sept. 29, 2025)

**SOURCE READ:** Ninth Circuit published opinion path previously verified in this research cycle:
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/09/29/24-6244.pdf

**STATUS:** VERIFIED as to disposition/characterization.

**Disposition.** The Ninth Circuit dismissed for lack of appellate jurisdiction. The appeal concerned denial of a private defendant's good-faith defense / asserted protection from § 1983 liability. The panel concluded the denial was not immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine.

**Qualified-immunity discussion.** The opinion discusses the line of cases limiting qualified immunity for private parties, including *Richardson*, *Jensen*, *Clement*, *Ace Beverage*, and *Halvorsen*, while recognizing *Filarsky* as a different contractor setting.

**Critical characterization.** That private-party immunity discussion is **reasoning supporting the jurisdictional disposition**, not a holding that private medical/treatment contractors categorically may never invoke qualified immunity.

**Prompt contradiction?** YES if *Esche* is cited as a merits holding "denying QI to private providers." The actual holding is appellate jurisdiction.

## 2C — Goddard v. Security Title Ins. & Guar. Co., 14 Cal.2d 47 (1939)

**STATUS:** VERIFIED through California cases reproducing and applying *Goddard*; direct historical reporter text still should be preserved in the final pin audit.

Later California authority repeatedly states *Goddard*'s rule: a judgment following a demurrer for **technical or formal defects** is not on the merits. A general-demurrer judgment may be on the merits when the facts alleged fail to establish a cause of action, but that is not an absolute rule where the defect is technical/formal.

Useful corroborating published California source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2017/b279246.html
(*Boyd v. Freeman* quoting *Goddard*, 14 Cal.2d at 52.)

**Finding.** The internal proposition that even a general-demurrer judgment may fail to be "on the merits" where the defect is technical/formal is substantively sound.

**Pin confidence:** PARTIAL pending direct historical opinion/reporter confirmation of the exact 52–53 wording.

## 2B — Pollock v. University of Southern California, 112 Cal.App.4th 1416 (2003)

**SOURCE READ:** Published California Court of Appeal opinion:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2003/b159352.html

**STATUS:** CONTRADICTED as to the requested proposition.

The requested verification prompt assumes *Pollock* contains a passage distinguishing **general** from **special** demurrers for preclusion purposes. The opened opinion does not appear to contain that requested passage.

A closer source for the general/special-demurrer distinction is **Kanarek v. Bugliosi**, 108 Cal.App.3d 327 (1980), which states that a judgment on a **special demurrer for technical or formal defect** is not on the merits, while a demurrer sustained because pleaded facts fail to establish a cause of action is generally on the merits, subject to the defects/facts actually pleaded.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/108/327.html

**Finding.** Do not attribute the general-vs-special demurrer passage to *Pollock* without a separate pinpoint showing it is actually there.

## 2E — Lee v. City of Los Angeles, 250 F.3d 668

**STATUS:** VERIFIED.

**Year:** **2001**, not 2000.

**Rule.** *Lee* permits judicial notice of undisputed matters of public record but not judicial notice of **disputed facts stated in public records**. Later Ninth Circuit and district-court decisions consistently cite *Lee*, 250 F.3d at 689–90, for that proposition.

Corroborating public text:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-15929/17-15929-2018-09-19.html

**Finding.** The Rule 12 / RJN use in PR #880 is sound: existence/content of a court record may be noticed; a disputed factual recital does not become true merely because it appears in a public record.

## 3A — 15 Cal. Code Regs. § 3574 / NCR 25-01

**SOURCES READ:**
CDCR Notice / Initial Statement of Reasons:
https://www.cdcr.ca.gov/regulations/wp-content/uploads/sites/171/2025/01/NCR-25-01-Sex-Offender-Treatment.pdf

Current regulatory text:
https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/15-CCR-3574

**STATUS:** VERIFIED as to the regulatory architecture and 2024–2025 sequence already identified in the Shannon research stack.

**Regulatory purpose.** CDCR's own Initial Statement of Reasons says the regulation was adopted because Title 15 previously lacked criteria allowing a supervised person to complete treatment between the one-year minimum and the full parole period, and it expressly contrasts individualized completion with a blanket requirement to attend for the entire parole period.

**Date sequence confirmed in the existing source audit:**
- emergency § 3574 operative **Oct. 14, 2024**;
- regular-rulemaking notice NCR 25-01 dated Jan. 3, 2025 (notice date is not operative date);
- emergency refile **Mar. 24, 2025**;
- certificate of compliance / permanent rulemaking **Apr. 17, 2025**.

**Substantive architecture confirmed from current § 3574 / rulemaking record:**
- supervised persons may complete SOMP before parole discharge;
- treatment completion is evaluated separately from parole discharge;
- CTM review is built into the completion/retention process;
- retention beyond the completion point requires written **good cause**;
- the retention determination is documented through CDCR Form 3043 and requires treatment-provider and parole-agent participation/signatures.

**Additional high-value source fact.** CDCR's ISOR expressly states the Department sought individualized treatment criteria because individualized treatment "affords the supervised person the opportunity to complete their treatment prior to discharging parole, as opposed to a blanket requirement to attend treatment for the entire parole period."

**Temporal caution.** Do not use § 3574 as though it governed 2021–2023 conduct. The emergency regulation began in October 2024.

# Findings that cut against Plaintiff

1. *Palomar* confirms that a **general-demurrer dismissal that becomes a final state judgment and is affirmed** can have ordinary claim-preclusive effect. The factual posture therefore matters enormously.
2. *Estate of Esche* is not the categorical private-contractor QI merits authority an aggressive reading might suggest; *Filarsky* remains potentially useful to a government contractor.
3. *Wicklund* is unpublished and therefore cannot carry a clearly-established-law argument by itself.
4. The current § 3574 framework did not exist for early 2021–2023 conduct; relying on it for those years would be anachronistic.
5. *Pollock* does not presently support the proposition for which the verification prompt sought to use it.
6. *Goddard/Kanarek* also recognize that a demurrer for failure of pleaded facts to establish a cause of action can be a merits judgment in the appropriate final-judgment posture. The characterization of the actual Matter 5 ruling therefore remains central.
