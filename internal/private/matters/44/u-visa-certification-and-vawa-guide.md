# MODULE 1 — U-Visa Certification + VAWA Self-Petition in Maryland

## 1. Maryland statute: Criminal Procedure § 11-931 (full text pulled from 2026 code)

**Citation:** Md. Code Ann., Crim. Proc. § 11-931 (enacted 2019; current text verified at https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Statute_Web/gcp/11-931.pdf)

**Who may request certification** — § 11-931(a)(1): the victim **or** the victim's parent, guardian, next friend, **attorney, victim advocate, or other representative** may request that a certifying official of a certifying entity certify victim helpfulness on Form I-918, Supplement B, if the victim:

1. (i) was a victim of a **qualifying criminal activity** and has been helpful, is being helpful, or is **likely to be helpful** in the detection, investigation, or prosecution of that qualifying criminal activity; **or**
2. (ii) was under 16 when an act constituting an element of qualifying criminal activity first occurred, and the victim's parent, guardian, or next friend has been helpful; **or**
3. (iii) is incapacitated or incompetent, and the victim's parent, guardian, or next friend has been helpful.

§ 11-931(a)(2): a victim of a qualifying crime **or** qualifying criminal activity is eligible; if multiple crimes, each must be listed on the certification.

**Helpfulness standard** — §§ 11-931(b)-(c): an individual is "helpful" if, since the initiation of helpfulness, the individual has **not unreasonably refused** to cooperate or unreasonably failed to provide information/assistance reasonably requested. If the victim (or parent/guardian/next friend) is assisting, has assisted, or is likely to assist law enforcement in detection, investigation, or prosecution, they **shall be considered** helpful / to have been helpful / likely to be helpful.

**What the certification must contain** — § 11-931(d): if criteria met, the certifying official **shall** fully complete and sign Supplement B, including: (1) specific details about the nature of the crime; (2) a detailed description of the victim's past/present/likely helpfulness; (3) copies of documents in the official's possession evincing the harm.

**Timelines** — § 11-931(e): the certifying entity **shall certify or decline within 45 days** of receiving the request; **within 7 days** if the noncitizen victim is in removal/exclusion/deportation proceedings or subject to a final order.

**No criminal case required** — § 11-931(f): a current investigation, filing of charges, prosecution, or conviction is **not** required. § 11-931(g): **no statute of limitations** — no time limit between the qualifying criminal activity and the certification request.

**Denial/explanation and withdrawal** — § 11-931(h): if the official cannot determine qualifying-victim status or determines the applicant doesn't qualify, a **written explanation** must be provided. § 11-931(i): certification may be denied or withdrawn **only** if, considering the totality of the circumstances, the person unreasonably refused to cooperate or unreasonably failed to provide reasonably requested information/assistance.

**Certification ≠ visa** — § 11-931(j): the official's completion of the form "may not be considered sufficient evidence that an applicant for U Nonimmigrant Status has met all eligibility requirements" and "may not be construed to guarantee" immigration relief; it serves only as verification of factual information for federal adjudication; attestation is to the best of the official's knowledge; the manner of describing helpfulness or providing additional relevant information is not limited.

**LEP access** — § 11-931(k): certifying entities must develop protocols to assist petitioners with limited English proficiency.

**Which agencies may certify:** § 11-931 itself uses "certifying entity"/"certifying official" **without defining them** (the 2025 amendment bills HB 579/SB 608 would have added federal-law-mirror definitions — see Unverified below). The federal framework governs: under 8 C.F.R. § 214.14(a)(2)-(3), a "certifying agency" includes Federal, State, or local **law enforcement agencies, prosecutors, judges**, and other authorities having responsibility for the detection, investigation, or prosecution of qualifying criminal activity — and a "certifying official" is the agency head, a designee in a supervisory role, or a Federal, State, or local judge. In practice in Maryland: municipal/county police, Maryland State Police, State's Attorney's offices, courts (judges), and other qualifying authorities (e.g., MDH, DLLR for labor crimes; AG's office where it investigates).

**Maryland Attorney General guidance / standard forms: NOT FOUND.** A targeted search of the Maryland AG's office produced no U-visa certification guidance memo, standard form, or protocol. The operative form is the **federal Form I-918, Supplement B, U Nonimmigrant Status Certification** (USCIS/DHS).

## 2. Federal U-visa basics (context only)

- **Statutory basis:** INA § 101(a)(15)(U), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U); eligibility standards at 8 C.F.R. § 214.14.
- **Elements** (per DHS, http://dhs.gov/immigration-options-victims-crime): (1) victim of **qualifying criminal activity** occurring in the U.S. or violating U.S. law; (2) **substantial physical or mental abuse** as a result; (3) possesses credible and reliable information about the criminal activity; (4) has been, is being, or is likely to be helpful to law enforcement/prosecution/judges/other certifying officials in detection, investigation, or prosecution; (5) **certification** — Form I-918, Supplement B signed by a certifying agency official; (6) admissible or qualifies for a waiver (Form I-192). Principal petition: **Form I-918**; derivatives (qualifying family): **Form I-918, Supplement A**.
- **Qualifying criminal activities** include rape, torture, trafficking, incest, domestic violence, sexual assault, abusive sexual contact, prostitution, sexual exploitation, female genital mutilation, hostage-taking, peonage, involuntary servitude, slave trade, kidnapping, abduction, unlawful criminal restraint, false imprisonment, blackmail, extortion, manslaughter, murder, felonious assault, witness tampering, obstruction of justice, perjury, fraud in foreign labor contracting. Note: **stalking is not on the federal list** (it is an ACP-qualifying harm in Maryland but not an enumerated U-visa qualifying crime — 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U)(iii); "any similar activity" where elements are substantially similar; attempt/conspiracy/solicitation included).
- **DHS emphasizes:** "signing a certification does not grant immigration protections — only USCIS has the authority to grant or deny a U visa petition." Confidentiality: 8 U.S.C. § 1367.
- **Cap/backlog reality:** INA § 214(p), 8 U.S.C. § 1184(p)(2) caps **principal U visas at 10,000 per fiscal year**; the cap has been reached every year since FY 2010, producing a substantial multi-year backlog. Interim relief: USCIS's **Bona Fide Determination** process (since 2021) can grant deferred action + employment authorization to petitioners with bona fide pending petitions. U status is valid 4 years (extendable in limited circumstances); after 3 years of continuous U status, adjustment to LPR is possible under INA § 245(m), 8 U.S.C. § 1255(m).
- **Certification freshness:** per Form I-918 instructions, USCIS generally requires the petition + initial evidence (including the signed Supplement B) be filed within **6 months** of the certifying official's signature — timing the request matters even though Maryland imposes no statute of limitations.

## 3. VAWA self-petition basics (context only)

- **Statutory basis:** INA § 204(a)(1)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1154(a)(1)(A); form **I-360**; strict confidentiality under 8 U.S.C. § 1367.
- **Who may self-petition** (per USCIS): (1) **abused spouse** (or former/intended spouse, including within 2 years of divorce/death tied to abuse) of a U.S. citizen or LPR; (2) **abused child** (unmarried, under 21, with late-filing up to 25 if abuse caused delay) of a U.S. citizen or LPR parent; (3) **abused parent** of a U.S. citizen son or daughter aged 21+. Requirements: qualifying relationship, battery or extreme cruelty, good moral character, resided with the abuser. **No police report or conviction is required.** USCIS will not contact the abuser.

## 4. Honest eligibility map for the AUNT

- **VAWA self-petition: not available to an aunt qua aunt.** The qualifying relationships are closed categories — spouse, child, or parent of the *abuser*. An aunt is not the spouse, child, or parent of the alleged abuser. The only aunt-adjacent VAWA path would require the aunt herself to have been abused by her own USC/LPR spouse, USC/LPR parent, or USC adult child — none alleged here. → VAWA is off the table on these facts.
- **U-visa: only as a DIRECT victim of qualifying criminal activity herself** (or, strictly, as an "indirect victim," which federal regs define narrowly: for a direct victim 21+, only the victim's spouse and unmarried children under 21 qualify as indirect victims; for a direct victim under 21, spouse, children, parents, and unmarried siblings under 18 — 8 C.F.R. § 214.14(a)(14)(ii)). An adult **aunt is not an indirect victim** of a niece/nephew's victimization under the regulation. So the aunt must have been **herself the victim** of a qualifying crime (e.g., domestic violence, felonious assault, kidnapping, sexual assault) **and** helpful/likely helpful to law enforcement **and** suffered substantial physical or mental abuse. Derivative status (Supplement A) also runs only from a principal petitioner within the standard derivative set (spouse, children, and for principals under 21, parents and siblings — 8 C.F.R. § 214.14(f)(2)); she cannot ride someone else's petition as a derivative either.
- **Certification is the gate, not the destination.** Even with a signed Supplement B, USCIS adjudicates eligibility, substantial abuse, admissibility/waivers — and the backlog means relief, if any, is years out (with possible Bona Fide Determination interim relief).
- **Needs qualified immigration counsel — non-negotiable.** Federal immigration is fact-sensitive; Maryland counsel should refer to an immigration attorney before any representation about eligibility is made to the family member. Also note the **advisability of reporting**: helpfulness requires cooperation with law enforcement, which is a strategic decision for the family, not an automatic one.
