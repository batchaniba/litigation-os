## Executive assessment

The Westlaw report **materially improves the authority research**, especially the controlling framework for false arrest/false imprisonment and California courts' ability to order an agency to correct its exercise of discretion. It records pinpoints, full-text-versus-headnote read status, and KeyCite information rather than casually treating every citation as verified.

**But its central asserted facial statutory error is not supported by the actual denial.** Do **not** incorporate the repeated claim that Captain Serrato stated, interpreted, or ruled that *false imprisonment is not a qualifying criminal activity*. He said the **November 1 incident** did not involve qualifying or substantially similar criminal activity. That is an adverse **application of the statutory threshold to the incident**, not necessarily a rejection of §679.10(c)(19)'s enumerated offense.

The principal opportunity is to use the report's better authorities to prove that *unlawful arrest can satisfy a qualifying false-imprisonment theory* **after the necessary absence-of-lawful-authority findings**; the remaining obstacle is proving both (a) criminal unlawfulness of this compulsory custody and (b) an actual abuse or error in Serrato's April 20 substantive determination.

## 1. Non-negotiable correction: what Serrato actually decided

**Westlaw report's repeated characterization:** p. 2, takeaway 1 says “Captain Serrato's April 20, 2026 letter said false imprisonment was ‘not qualifying criminal activity.’” The statutory table at pp. 3–4 and paragraph beginning “The denial's stated ground” at p. 4 repeat or depend on this characterization and label a direct statutory contradiction.

**Court-record source:** Judge Whitman's signed August 29 order, p. 2, quoting the denial: “A thorough review of this incident was conducted. Unfortunately, **this incident does not meet the standard of qualifying criminal activity** identified in Section 679.10 ... Additionally, this review found **no substantially similar qualifying criminal activity** as defined in Section 679.10(d) ...”

**Distinguish three propositions:**

| Proposition | Evidentiary status | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Penal Code §679.10(c)(19) enumerates false imprisonment | Statutory text; supported | CHP cannot lawfully refuse certification on a categorical theory that false imprisonment is not qualifying |
| Serrato **expressly** said false imprisonment is categorically unqualified | **Not supported by the signed denial or Whitman's quotation** | **DO NOT CLAIM** this as a proven facial legal error |
| Serrato determined **this incident** did not satisfy qualifying or substantially similar criminal activity | Supported by original denial and judicial quotation | The writ must test whether the agency's particular finding rested on a *materially erroneous fact, incorrect offense-elements standard, impermissible criterion, or a result outside lawful discretion* |

To allege a categorical misinterpretation, first produce independent proof (actual decision file, contemporaneous written policy, admission or sworn testimony). Neither a generic denial nor a post-denial May review memo establishes it.

**Correction scope:** All subsequent passages invoking a “textbook interpretation error,” “on that ground alone the denial cannot stand,” or “the denial's error was a statutory-definition error” must be rewritten conditionally, not pasted into any court-facing filing. This audit **does not** conclude Serrato applied the correct standard; it concludes the *asserted proof of an incorrect one is missing*.

## 3. Claims and doctrines to correct or narrow

1. **False-imprisonment elements remain disputed on this record.** The restraint and involuntary custody are supported. Their *criminal unlawfulness* depends on objective legal authority. The fact officers did not witness driving should focus the inquiry on the actual **Vehicle Code §40300.5** statutory exception(s), as well as contemporary DUI evidence; this is **not** conclusively settled by *Hagberg* or *Asgari*.
2. **Distinguish §679.10(g), (h) and (i)(2).** “Shall certify” is conditional; helpfulness rebuttably presumed if its conditions are met; written notice is subdivision **(i)(2)**. The denial **did not** cite noncooperation. Do not revive dismissed explanation/helpfulness counts by repackaging them as the only proven abuse.
3. **Do not equate civil false-arrest immunity with criminal elements.** Penal Code §847(b) and Government Code §820.4 concern civil liability. An officer's lack of immunity does not, without more, adjudicate guilt of Penal Code §236 or §146(a).
4. **“CV Amalgamated models direct relief” must be precise.** It models orders requiring an agency to perform the correct procedural step or re-score absent an unlawful criterion, not guaranteed issuance of a particular certification or other requested ultimate license.
5. **Federal USCIS cases are not controlling here.** *Perez Perez v. Wolf* and *Chairez v. Mayorkas* distinguish statutory threshold and discretionary immigration decisions in federal contexts. They have internal red-team value; do not crowd out California's own §679.10 and CCP §1085 framework in the court-facing brief.
6. **Citator flags require proposition-specific review.** The report's “READ IN FULL” or “yellow” labels reflect its author's research account; this audit did not independently access their Westlaw account, capture the underlying KeyCite history, or re-run all 2026 updates. Before filing, inspect holdings, adverse treatment and unpublished-citation restrictions.
7. **Do not use speculative Serrato motive.** Letter alone establishes rejection of qualifying and substantially similar *incident activity*, not prohibited reliance on parole, criminal history, lack of charges or applicant's noncooperation.
8. **Keep event chronology distinct from agency review.** Officer report discrepancies or October 2026 CHP production may establish 2024 events, but do not show which items Serrato received or relied on in April 2026. Check RFP No.8 and April 9 document/transcript versions.
9. **Do not overclaim earlier federal screening order.** June 29 order accepted allegations as true under §1915 screening, not a ruling of criminal misconduct or entitlement to state certification.

## 4. Precise recommendations for the replacement memorandum, without editing it now

- **Introduction:** Preserve the decision-focused framing and drop any claim that Serrato wrote “false imprisonment is not qualifying.” Explicitly say §679.10(c)(19) makes false imprisonment *a legally qualifying category*, then argue why this **specific custody** meets its elements.
- **§236 criminal activity subsection:** Introduce *Hagberg* (2004) 32 Cal.4th 350, 372 fn.7 and 372–373, then *Asgari* (1997) 15 Cal.4th 744, 752–753; distinguish case-specific facts and civil liability from conditional criminal qualification.
- **§40300.5 arrest-authority subsection:** Keep as principal source-to-law question; force a finding on the applicable warrantless misdemeanor exception, not simply subjective good faith or “the car was parked.”
- **Serrato nexus subsection:** Move beyond “five sentences” or a corrected walk-and-turn. Demand *actual evidence* of an error that infected the qualifying-activity determination, or explain how a court's dispositive legal/factual findings themselves make the denial untenable even if subjective agency deliberations are unknown.
- **Remedies:** Cite *CV Amalgamated* accurately for a court-directed correction of the agency decision process (pp. 283,286), and *Saleeby* for correction and remand, not categorical issuance of form. Direct certification still requires every statutory predicate and no remaining lawful option.
- **Proposed judgment/writ:** Distinguish (A) completed §679.10(g)(1) criteria requiring signing from (B) proven material agency error requiring re-evaluation. Do not prewrite a finding that Serrato relied on an inaccurate walk-and-turn fact.
- **Private proof audit:** Add one task: check the Westlaw author's unreviewed Common Cause negative treatments, flagged 2025/2026 cases, and exact *Asgari* pinpoint. Do not state a comprehensive KeyCite audit is complete until accomplished.

Its false premise about Serrato categorically rejecting false imprisonment must not migrate into any signed court paper. *The report makes our available legal arguments more authoritative; establishing the factual and decisional predicates remains the immediate litigation task.*
