# Matter 40 — Best-Case Pleading and Discovery Strategy

**Matter:** Brendan Ngwa Nforbi v. TaskUs, LLC  
**Status:** Prospective / pre-dispute monitoring  
**Prepared:** September 14, 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify the strongest fact pattern, pleading architecture, and discovery posture if the pending TaskUs / First Advantage screening produces the same registry-source pattern already documented elsewhere in the portfolio.

---

## Executive assessment

Matter 40 has the potential to become one of the cleanest Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E) matters in the portfolio because the evidentiary record is being preserved **prospectively**, before any adverse decision occurs.

The key advantage is not simply that the portfolio has seen a recurring background-screening pattern. The advantage is that Matter 40 can avoid the principal weakness that existed at filing in several earlier matters: having to plead the contents, source, or use of the employer's background report on information and belief.

The strongest version of Matter 40 would combine the best features of:

- **Matter 22 / Pilot** — employer-side documentation identifying registry status as the reason for adverse action;
- **Matter 26 / UPS** — an actual First Advantage report whose adverse component expressly states `SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY`;
- **Matter 27 / Randstad / RK** — a preserved report, tightly sequenced adverse-action process, and termination shortly after screening activity.

If the pending TaskUs screen produces that combination, the complaint can plead the decisive chronology as known facts supported by documents rather than asking the court to infer the critical source-and-causation link.

## Best-case factual sequence

The strongest possible sequence is:

### 1. First Advantage completes the TaskUs report

Preserve the **complete report PDF**, not merely an email, portal landing page, or pre-adverse notice.

The most important question is whether the TaskUs report reproduces the architecture now documented in Matter 26.

The ideal report would contain a sex-offender-registry component that:

- is marked `Decisional`, `INELIGIBLE`, `Consider`, or equivalent;
- identifies the California registry as the source; and
- expressly states language materially equivalent to:

> SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY

If that language appears in the TaskUs report, the source question is documented before suit.

### 2. Other screening components do not independently explain the decision

The cleanest record would show:

- drug screen passed;
- ordinary criminal database components clear, eligible, or non-disqualifying;
- employment / identity checks complete;
- no independent job-related disqualifier;
- registry component is the only adverse or ineligible item.

This matters because it prevents the principal problem exposed by *Lerma v. Amazon*: an actual report can defeat a registry-sourcing allegation where the disqualifying information came from ordinary court records and the sex-offender database itself returned no record.

Matter 40 should be built around the actual TaskUs report, not around the recurring pattern alone.

### 3. The pre-adverse packet is preserved in full

If First Advantage / TaskUs starts a pre-adverse process, preserve:

- the email;
- complete report;
- Summary of Rights;
- California-specific notices;
- any adjudication status;
- portal screenshots;
- timestamps;
- any dispute link or candidate-response window;
- sender domain and employer/account identifier.

The goal is to know exactly what TaskUs possessed before any employment decision.

### 4. TaskUs then takes adverse action

A final adverse-action notice stating that TaskUs acted based in whole or in part on the First Advantage report materially strengthens causation.

The record becomes stronger still if:

- TaskUs expressly identifies the registry entry;
- the report's registry component is the only adverse component;
- an adjudication field links that component to `INELIGIBLE`;
- internal or candidate-facing records identify registry status as the reason.

The Pilot matter remains the gold-standard comparator because Pilot's own Post-Adverse Action Notice identified `REGISTRATION IN CA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY` as the reason.

### 5. Highest-value variant: Plaintiff starts work before screening is complete

Matter 40's offer expressly contemplates employment beginning while screening remains pending.

If Plaintiff begins work September 23, performs the job, receives wages, and is then terminated after the First Advantage registry result, Matter 40 changes materially.

That sequence provides:

- actual employment rather than a mere lost opportunity;
- a defined wage rate;
- a concrete termination date;
- a temporal link between the report and termination;
- cleaner economic damages;
- a stronger wrongful-termination-in-public-policy posture than a pure failure-to-hire theory.

The ideal sequence would therefore be:

> accepted offer → onboarding → begins work → report completes → registry component is identified as adverse → TaskUs receives / adjudicates report → pre-adverse process → termination.

## Why Matter 26 / UPS is the immediate blueprint

The UPS litigation has developed beyond the evidentiary posture that existed when its complaint was originally filed.

The portfolio now contains UPS Exhibit A, lodged with UPS's Rule 12(b)(6) papers. The cross-matter record describes the critical component as:

- `Decisional`;
- `INELIGIBLE`;
- adjudicated by the Centralized Adjudication Team; and
- containing the sentence:

> SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY

That matters because the strongest defense to a § 290.46(j) claim is not merely “we did not use registry information.” It is also “the information came from an independent lawful source.”

The actual source field narrows that issue dramatically.

For Matter 40, the objective should be to possess the equivalent TaskUs report **before drafting the complaint**.

If the document itself establishes the source, the complaint does not need to plead:

> On information and belief, First Advantage probably obtained or furnished California registry information.

It can instead plead:

> On [date], First Advantage generated TaskUs screening report [identifier]. The report's sex-offender component was marked [status] and expressly identified “SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY.” TaskUs received the report and thereafter took [specified adverse action].

That is a materially stronger pleading posture.

## Comparator ranking

### Matter 22 — Pilot

**Current benchmark for direct causation.**

Pilot's own notice identified registry status as the adverse basis. The matter settled for $35,000 before service was completed.

Strength:
- direct employer statement;
- registry explicitly identified;
- active employment and wages before termination;
- minimal inference.

### Matter 26 — UPS

**Potential benchmark for source tracing.**

The First Advantage report is now in the court record, and the only Decisional / INELIGIBLE component is described in the portfolio as expressly sourcing the California registry.

Strength:
- actual report;
- source language;
- adjudication status;
- adverse-action sequence.

Remaining dispute:
- statutory construction and whether the employer can characterize the information as national-registry information despite the report's California source field.

### Matter 27 — Randstad / RK

**Strong circumstantial and documentary comparator.**

The InfoMart report is preserved, the registry is the adverse flag, and the employment decision followed the report / pre-adverse chronology closely.

### Matter 40 — TaskUs, best-case version

Matter 40 can potentially exceed the earlier matters in pleading cleanliness because the record is being built before the adverse event.

If it produces:

1. actual First Advantage report;
2. express California-registry source;
3. sole or decisive adverse flag;
4. employer receipt / adjudication;
5. final adverse action;
6. preserved notices; and
7. ideally post-start termination;

then it could become the cleanest newly filed § 290.46 matter in the portfolio.

### Matters 36, 37, and 39

AT&T, Home Depot, and Best Buy remain useful pleading comparators, but their § 290 theories presently rely more heavily on information-and-belief allegations because the employer-specific report or registry-use record is not yet in hand.

Matter 40 should not voluntarily inherit that evidentiary weakness if the relevant documents become available before filing.

### Matter 29 — Amazon

Amazon / *Lerma* is the cautionary comparator.

The lesson is not that a recurring screening pattern is irrelevant. The lesson is that the actual report controls the source question.

Pattern supports context, notice, willfulness, and discovery. It should not substitute for the TaskUs report where the report can be obtained.

## Pleading architecture if the best-case facts occur

The strongest complaint should be narrower and more document-driven than some earlier portfolio complaints.

### Core factual chain

The complaint should plead, with dates and document identifiers:

1. TaskUs issued the written employment offer.
2. Plaintiff accepted it.
3. TaskUs initiated screening through First Advantage.
4. First Advantage generated the identified report.
5. The report expressly identified the California Sex Offender Registry as the source of the adverse component.
6. That component was marked adverse / decisional / ineligible.
7. TaskUs received or adjudicated the report.
8. TaskUs began the adverse-action process.
9. TaskUs rescinded the offer or terminated Plaintiff.
10. The adverse-action documentation ties the decision to the report.

If those facts exist, the § 290 count should not depend on an inferential paragraph to establish report contents or source.

## Potential causes of action

Final causes should be selected from the actual TaskUs record rather than copied mechanically from prior matters.

### 1. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E)

This is the core claim if the TaskUs / First Advantage record establishes employment use of information disclosed through the California registry.

The statutory remedy framework should be pleaded accurately under § 290.46(j)(4).

### 2. FCRA adverse-action claim

If TaskUs takes adverse action based in whole or in part on a consumer report without first providing the required report / rights material, the FCRA provides an independent theory.

This claim should depend on the actual timing and documents.

### 3. ICRAA

If the First Advantage product qualifies as an investigative consumer report and the statutory prerequisites are met, ICRAA provides another independent path.

The current TaskUs packet should not be treated as resolving the exact ICRAA disclosure question. The complete Profile Advantage authorization and report package should be preserved and audited.

### 4. UCL

The UCL can borrow established statutory violations, subject to its separate standing and remedy limitations.

### 5. Wrongful termination in violation of public policy

This becomes significantly cleaner if Plaintiff actually begins working before TaskUs terminates him.

A post-start termination would avoid importing the same failure-to-hire / Tameny issue litigated in Matter 26.

### 6. Fair Chance Act

Evaluate separately from the registry-use theory based on TaskUs's actual individualized-assessment process, notices, conviction-history use, and any required administrative exhaustion.

Do not add the claim simply because the offer references a Fair Chance Act process.

## Pleading objective: eliminate unnecessary inference

The ideal Matter 40 complaint should make the Rule 12 / demurrer question simple:

> Taking the attached documents and pleaded chronology as true, did TaskUs use employment-screening information expressly sourced to the California Sex Offender Registry and then take adverse employment action?

The complaint should avoid asking the court to resolve factual questions that belong in discovery, including:

- what TaskUs's adjudication matrix instructed;
- which TaskUs entity received the report;
- which employee or automated system rendered the decision;
- whether First Advantage's source label reflects direct California DOJ sourcing or an upstream database;
- how the relevant entities configured Profile Advantage;
- what internal policy governed California sex-offender results.

Those are discovery questions.

The pleading should establish enough known facts that the defense cannot defeat the case merely by offering an alternative factual explanation outside the complaint.

## Arbitration complication

Matter 40 has a signed Mutual Arbitration Agreement, and the current record states that covered claims include the “background check form or process.”

Accordingly, the defense may move first to compel arbitration rather than litigating Rule 12 merits in court.

That changes the forum, not the importance of the evidentiary record.

The factual objective remains the same:

- complete report;
- source identification;
- adverse component;
- employer receipt;
- decision;
- damages.

Any eventual complaint should plead the case cleanly enough that a motion to compel is the principal threshold fight rather than a plausibility attack on whether registry use occurred at all.

## Matter 31 spillover — First Advantage pattern

Matter 40 may materially strengthen Matter 31 if First Advantage again furnishes a registry-sourced employment report.

The progression would then be:

- UPS — First Advantage employment screen;
- FedEx — First Advantage employment screen;
- TaskUs — First Advantage employment screen.

If the TaskUs report reproduces the same California-registry source / adjudication architecture, Matter 31 becomes more than a two-instance historical vendor theory.

The third instance would support investigation into whether First Advantage has a repeatable California employment-screening workflow that:

1. queries or receives sex-offender information;
2. identifies the California registry as source;
3. transmits the information to employers;
4. assigns or facilitates adverse adjudication; and
5. repeats the process across unrelated employers.

That pattern is relevant to willfulness, procedures, compliance architecture, and discovery. It still does not replace proof of each individual furnishing or use.

## Litigation trigger matrix

### Scenario A — hired and retained

If Plaintiff starts and remains employed, there may be no § 290 adverse-use claim. Preserve the report and close or downgrade the monitoring file based on what actually occurred.

### Scenario B — no hire / rescission, report unavailable

This resembles the weaker information-and-belief matters. Preserve all notices and pursue statutory file-inspection mechanisms before drafting if practicable.

### Scenario C — no hire / rescission, report shows registry hit

Strong claim. Plead the report contents as known fact.

### Scenario D — report shows California registry source + sole adverse flag

Very strong source-and-causation posture. Use UPS / Pilot as the primary internal comparators.

### Scenario E — Plaintiff begins work, then TaskUs terminates after registry report

Strongest economic and wrongful-termination variant.

### Scenario F — report shows ordinary court-record source and registry search clear

The § 290 theory may fail under the same source problem identified in *Lerma*. Reassess FCRA, ICRAA, Fair Chance Act, contract, and other independent theories based on the actual documents.

## Bottom line

Matter 40 should be treated as a chance to perfect the litigation model developed across the earlier employment-screening matters.

The recurring pattern is useful because it tells the portfolio exactly what evidence to preserve and exactly where prior pleadings were vulnerable.

But the goal is not to plead “the pattern happened again.”

The goal is to be able to plead:

> **Here is the offer. Here is the First Advantage report. Here is the source field. Here is the adverse adjudication. Here is TaskUs's receipt/use. Here is the adverse action. Here are the damages.**

If those documents exist, Matter 40 can be drafted so that the central factual questions are discovery questions rather than plausibility gaps.

That would make it one of the strongest § 290.46 employment-use matters in the portfolio and potentially the cleanest newly filed complaint in the cluster.
