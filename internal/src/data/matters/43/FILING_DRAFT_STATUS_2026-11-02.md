**Two proposed complaint documents were prepared in ChatGPT as downloadable DOCX and PDF documents, not pushed to this repository. Neither was filed or served. The California draft is the preferred current path; the federal complaint is a conditional alternative only.**

## Current proposed California complaint (8 pages)
**Court:** Superior Court of California, County of Santa Clara.  
**Defendants:** The Hertz Corporation (employer); HireRight, LLC (reporting agency). **Do not caption the parent holding company merely because ADP WOTC named Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.**  
**Count 1:** California Civil Code §§ 1786.28(a), 1786.50 against HireRight, relying on **specific public-record source / initial-publication-date omissions** in the registry portions of the investigative employment report. The October 31 forensic report in the user's Library records the exact omissions (registry pages 8–9); confirm the same omissions in the November 2 **completed** report that was furnished to Hertz. This theory is based on content of the actual report, not the mere fact of unfavorable records. *Parsonage v. Wal-Mart Associates, Inc.*, D083831 (Cal. Ct. App. Feb. 4, 2026), holds California ICRAA's $10,000 individual statutory remedy does not need proof of a separately concrete injury.
**Count 2:** Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A) against Hertz for employment *use* of California **public website-derived** registry information. Source and internal use expressly pleaded on information and belief, grounded in the DOJ-source notes and employer-chosen registry screening with client review. **This remains the principal contested element.** See *Mendoza v. ADP Screening & Selection Services*, B214653, and *Donaleski v. Wal-Mart*, 2:08-cv-00219 (E.D. Cal. 2009) (independent employer investigation defense).
**Remedies:** ICRAA greater of actual damages or $10,000 on a qualifying individual claim; §290.46(j)(4)(A) minimum $250 and additional relief as statutory text allows upon proof. No invented wages or final rejection; the 2026 report describes Class C driver's license as **VALID** (Oct. 31 forensic review), prior suspension reinstated and disqualification cleared.

## Federal complaint (10 pages): CONDITIONAL — attribution not established
Proposed 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b) count against HireRight, with supplemental § 290.46 count against Hertz. Federal jurisdiction depends on showing HireRight **furnished or generated** the misleading '[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses-Does not meet' *criminal classification*, not merely correct MVR raw data that Hertz independently classified. **Important adverse fact:** the earlier Oct. 31 HireRight version labels an adjudication ruleset **'Excl. MVR'**, with MVR subrequest adjudication 'Undefined'; this cuts against an inference that HireRight's managed adjudication generated a criminal label from the MVR. The Nov. 2 notice came from Hertz. **Do not file the federal draft without verifying attribution and concrete injury; otherwise the FCRA count may fail Rule 12(b)(6), imperiling supplemental jurisdiction.** *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), recognizes third-party dissemination of materially misleading reporting as concrete reputational injury, but only if the allegedly misleading communication is properly attributed to the reporting defendant.
