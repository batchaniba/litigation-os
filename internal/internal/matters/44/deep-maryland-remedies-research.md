## 2. Immediate safety and protective relief

### 2.1 Domestic-violence protective order

A current or former spouse is a “person eligible for relief.” Family Law § 4-501 defines “abuse” through listed acts, including serious bodily harm, placing a person in fear of imminent serious bodily harm, assault, rape or another listed sexual offense, false imprisonment, stalking, and revenge porn. Vulnerable-adult abuse may also qualify.

**Critical limit:** emotional or psychological torment, cruelty, financial control, or “coercive control” is not, standing alone, a current § 4-501 ground. Those facts matter if they prove a listed act, establish reasonable fear, explain delay, or corroborate a pattern. The 2026 coercive-control proposal, HB 1586, remained in the House after a March 13 hearing; its proposed October 1 effective date did not make it law.

A petition may be filed in Maryland District or circuit court. When court is closed, a District Court commissioner may issue interim relief. A temporary order ordinarily bridges the case to a final hearing. A final order requires proof by a preponderance of the evidence.

Potential final-order terms under Family Law § 4-506 include:

- no abuse, threats, contact, harassment, or entry;
- stay-away relief and exclusive use or possession of the home;
- temporary custody and structured or denied visitation when children are involved;
- emergency family maintenance and earnings withholding;
- temporary use and possession of a jointly titled vehicle;
- counseling or an abuse-intervention program;
- temporary pet possession and protection;
- filing costs and other relief necessary to protect an eligible person; and
- mandatory firearm surrender in a final order, with temporary-order firearm relief where the statutory criteria are met.

Most final orders are time-limited. Extension and permanent-order provisions have separate criteria. A violation can support enforcement, contempt, arrest, or criminal prosecution. The correct Maryland Judiciary forms include the petition and supplemental description; an address-withholding form should be considered when disclosure creates danger.

### 2.2 Injunction in a divorce case

After an alimony, annulment, or divorce action exists, Family Law § 1-203 permits equitable injunctive relief against threatened or actual physical harm or harassment. This is not a substitute for emergency protective-order practice. It can, however, coordinate safety with a pending family case. Filing a divorce alone is not an automatic asset freeze or lis pendens; specific preservation relief must be requested and supported.

### 2.3 Extreme Risk Protective Order

An ERPO is a firearm-risk remedy, not a general domestic-abuse order. It belongs in the plan only if present, specific facts show that Patrick possesses or may acquire firearms and poses a danger of injury. Petitioner eligibility, the evidentiary showing, firearm descriptions, recent threats or acts, and any law-enforcement or health-professional evidence must be confirmed.

### 2.4 Safety execution

If danger is immediate, call emergency services and use an advocate-assisted safety plan; do not warn the alleged aggressor. Preserve evidence outside shared accounts or devices. Do not use unauthorized tracking, account access, impersonation, deletion, or secret interception. Maryland generally requires prior consent of all parties to record a private wire, oral, or electronic communication when the recorder is a party, subject only to statutory exceptions.

## 3. Divorce, property, support, and economic disentanglement

### 3.1 Divorce grounds and forum

Family Law § 7-103 authorizes absolute divorce for:

- six-month separation without interruption before filing;
- irreconcilable differences based on the complainant's stated reasons for permanently ending the marriage; or
- mutual consent through a signed agreement resolving alimony, property, and all child issues.

Spouses may pursue separate lives while living under the same roof. Venue is available where the plaintiff resides under Courts and Judicial Proceedings § 6-202(1). Residency and subject-matter jurisdiction must be confirmed, particularly if marriage, conduct, property, or a spouse has connections outside Maryland or the United States.

### 3.2 Interim relief

Depending on verified facts and requested relief, a complaint or motion may seek:

- pendente lite alimony under Family Law § 11-102;
- suit money, reasonable counsel fees, and costs under § 11-110;
- family-home or family-use-property relief under § 8-208, especially where a child-related statutory purpose exists;
- temporary custody, access, and child support if there are minor or dependent children;
- discovery and narrowly supported orders preserving identified assets or records; and
- an injunction under § 1-203 against harm or harassment.

### 3.3 Final financial relief

The family court can identify and value marital property, determine ownership disputes, order partition or sale where authorized, transfer certain interests, divide retirement interests, and make a monetary award under §§ 8-201 through 8-205. The § 8-205 factors include each party's contributions, economic circumstances, duration of the marriage, age and condition, how and when property was acquired, and circumstances contributing to estrangement. Abuse is therefore potentially relevant to the equities even when a tort claim is time-barred.

Alimony under § 11-106 is prospective from no earlier than the pleading requesting it. Relevant factors include the ability to become self-supporting, time for training, standard of living, marriage duration, monetary and nonmonetary contributions, circumstances of estrangement, age, health, income, needs, and ability to pay. Indefinite alimony has additional demanding criteria. Delay in pleading can lose recoverable months.

The inventory must cover real estate, bank and investment accounts, retirement, business interests, vehicles, tax refunds and liabilities, insurance, debts, transfers, gifts, beneficiary designations, digital assets, and foreign property. No asset should be moved, hidden, drained, or accused of being dissipated without proof and counsel review.

## 4. Civil damages and property remedies

Maryland abolished interspousal tort immunity in *Bozman v. Bozman*, 376 Md. 461 (2003). Marriage therefore is not a categorical defense. Each theory still needs timely, admissible proof of every element, causation, injury, and damages.

### 4.1 Common-law claims to screen

- **Assault:** intentional conduct creating legally sufficient apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact. Civil assault has a one-year limitations period under CJP § 5-105.
- **Battery:** intentional harmful or offensive contact without consent. Counsel should apply the general three-year period unless a more specific rule controls.
- **False imprisonment:** deprivation of liberty without consent and without legal justification.
- **Intentional infliction of emotional distress:** an exacting, rarely satisfied claim requiring intentional or reckless, extreme and outrageous conduct, causal connection, and severe distress.
- **Conversion, detinue, or replevin:** wrongful dominion over personal property; recovery of property or its value. Replevin/detinue venue may lie where the property is located.
- **Trespass and property damage:** unauthorized entry, interference, or damage, subject to ownership, possessory rights, consent, and privilege.
- **Fraud or concealment:** a specific material misrepresentation or concealment, knowledge, intended and reasonable reliance, and resulting loss. Fraudulent concealment may toll accrual under CJP § 5-203, but the concealment and diligence must be proved.
- **Defamation or invasion of privacy:** publication or intrusion facts, privileges, damages, and the one-year libel/slander bar must be analyzed precisely.
- **Malicious prosecution or abuse of process:** only if the special elements—including favorable termination where required and misuse of process—exist. They are not labels for every false report or hostile lawsuit.
- **Civil conspiracy:** not a standalone tort; it depends on an underlying actionable wrong and agreement.

### 4.2 Maryland SAFE Act — financial exploitation

Estates and Trusts §§ 13-601 through 13-607 create a significant route if Njiubu is:

- an **older adult**, currently defined as at least 68; or
- a **susceptible adult** whose ability to perform activities of daily living, protect rights, or exercise executive functioning is impaired by a qualifying condition.

The statute reaches knowing unfair use or attempted use of funds, assets, or property by a person in a position of trust and confidence, and includes fiduciary breach, unauthorized taking, misuse of individual or joint accounts, deception, coercion, isolation, and excessive persuasion within the statutory definitions.

A qualifying plaintiff may seek compensatory damages; up to treble damages; prejudgment interest; injunction, rescission, restitution, accounting, unjust enrichment, declaratory relief, constructive trust, and emergency asset-preservation relief. Fee shifting is possible, including a risk of fees for a bad-faith claim. The special period is generally five years after discovery or when the exploitation should have been discovered, with criminal-prosecution tolling specified by § 13-607.

This route cannot be selected until Njiubu's age, health/capacity, the asset, title, transaction, authority, benefit, discovery date, and tracing records are known.

### 4.3 Express digital, privacy, and image-based remedies

- **Maryland Wiretap Act, CJP §§ 10-402 and 10-410:** unlawful interception, disclosure, or use can support actual damages or statutory liquidated damages of $100 per day or $1,000 minimum, punitive damages, fees, and costs. The communication, privacy expectation, interception method, consent, participant status, disclosure, and exception must be analyzed. This statute is also a warning against self-help recording.
- **Revenge porn, Criminal Law § 3-809:** qualifying nonconsensual disclosure of an intimate visual representation can be prosecuted and can support civil relief through defamation per se or invasion-of-privacy provisions and attorney's fees.
- **Visual surveillance, Criminal Law § 3-902:** qualifying prurient, surreptitious observation or recording of private areas in a private place can support actual damages and reasonable attorney's fees, in addition to criminal exposure.
- **Computer crime, Criminal Law § 7-302:** a directly injured person may seek actual damages and reasonable fees and costs for qualifying unauthorized access, copying, alteration, damage, or related conduct; a criminal conviction is not a prerequisite to the civil action.
- **Identity fraud, Criminal Law § 8-301:** primarily a criminal route. Restitution can include specified costs of clearing credit, records, or debts. Do not plead an implied private civil action from the criminal section without authority.

### 4.4 Damages and forum

District Court generally handles contract or tort claims up to $30,000, with small-claims procedure at $5,000 or less; circuit court is the ordinary forum above $30,000 and for broader equitable relief. Forum, jury demand, joinder, family-case coordination, and claim preclusion require counsel review.

Maryland's CJP § 11-108 cap on noneconomic damages changes annually and is keyed to when the cause of action arose. It is not one blanket number for a course of conduct beginning in 2012. Punitive damages generally require clear and convincing proof of actual malice or specific intent to injure and may be uncollectible or uninsured.

## 5. Criminal and public-enforcement routes

Njiubu may make a truthful report to police. If police do not initiate charges, Maryland procedure also permits an Application for Statement of Charges to a District Court commissioner. The applicant must distinguish firsthand knowledge from information supplied by others. Once proceedings begin, prosecutors—not the family—control charging, plea, dismissal, and trial decisions.

Depending on facts, authorities may screen:

- first-degree assault, including intentionally causing or attempting serious physical injury and qualifying strangulation (§ 3-202);
- second-degree assault (§ 3-203) and reckless endangerment (§ 3-204);
- stalking, including qualifying repeated electronic or location-tracking conduct (§ 3-802);
- harassment, misuse of telephone facilities, and misuse of electronic communications (§§ 3-803 through 3-805);
- false imprisonment at common law, kidnapping (§ 3-502), or related restraint offenses;
- revenge porn (§ 3-809), visual surveillance (§§ 3-901 through 3-903), wiretap violations, and computer crime (§ 7-302);
- malicious destruction of property (§ 6-301), theft (§ 7-104), identity fraud (§ 8-301), and extortion (§ 3-701);
- abuse or neglect of a vulnerable adult (§§ 3-604 and 3-605), including specified severe emotional distress inflicted by a family or household member;
- sexual offenses, sexual extortion (§ 3-709), forced marriage (§ 3-1103), or trafficking/labor offenses (§ 3-1202), only if their specific predicates exist;
- obstruction, witness intimidation, or retaliation (§§ 9-302, 9-303, 9-305, and 9-306) if later conduct targets reporting, testimony, or process; and
- allowing a child at least age two to be present during a crime of violence or assaultive conduct under § 3-601.1, if applicable.

**Spousal-property caution:** Criminal Law § 7-110 limits theft prosecution between spouses, with an exception tied to spouses living in separate residences. Property conduct must be dated against marital and residence status.

**Limitations caution:** Maryland generally uses a one-year prosecution limit for misdemeanors unless a statutory exception applies. Different periods apply to specified crimes; stalking has a longer special period, and certain offenses may be prosecuted at any time. Felony status and the applicable version of CJP § 5-106 must be classified by a Maryland prosecutor or criminal lawyer. Do not promise that a 2012 event is chargeable.

If a criminal case results, Criminal Procedure § 11-603 permits restitution for direct property loss, personal injury expenses, medical costs, and qualifying lost earnings. Restitution does not bar a civil action, but duplicate recovery must be credited.

## 6. Vulnerable-adult, victim, housing, work, and confidentiality relief

### 6.1 Adult Protective Services and vulnerable-adult offenses

If Njiubu lacks the physical or mental capacity to provide for daily needs, Family Law Title 14 may permit an Adult Protective Services report. Any person may report suspected abuse, neglect, self-neglect, or exploitation to the local department; emergency investigations have accelerated timing. This is not triggered merely by being an older adult or experiencing marital cruelty. Capacity, dependency, injury, caretaker, and risk facts control.

### 6.2 Criminal Injuries Compensation and restitution

The Criminal Injuries Compensation Board can potentially cover a qualifying victim's medical or mental-health care, lost earnings, limited property loss, relocation or security, transportation, childcare, funeral, or other authorized losses, subject to eligibility, proof, caps, offsets, and filing requirements. Psychological injury requiring counseling can qualify under the current framework. Cohabitation or family relationship is not by itself a disqualification. Maintain bills, wage proof, insurance explanations of benefits, police/court records, and proof of payment.

### 6.3 Employment and housing stabilization

- Labor and Employment § 3-1305 allows earned sick and safe leave for specified domestic-violence, sexual-assault, or stalking needs, including obtaining services and legal relief; § 3-1309 addresses prohibited adverse action.
- Unemployment eligibility may remain available where domestic violence forced a separation from work and there was no reasonable alternative, if statutory documentation and agency standards are satisfied.
- Real Property §§ 8-5A-02 through 8-5A-05 provide qualifying victims routes to terminate future residential-lease liability, address eviction consequences, or obtain a lock change using the required documentation and notice.
- Maryland's Address Confidentiality Program can provide a substitute address and restrict disclosure for eligible victims. It does not erase existing public records.

### 6.4 Immigration certification

Criminal Procedure § 11-931 supplies a Maryland process for requesting law-enforcement certification associated with a potential federal U-visa matter, without a Maryland limitations bar on requesting certification. Certification does not establish federal eligibility. VAWA self-petitioning and all other immigration relief are federal, fact-sensitive matters for qualified immigration counsel; no contact or filing should occur without Njiubu's authorization.
