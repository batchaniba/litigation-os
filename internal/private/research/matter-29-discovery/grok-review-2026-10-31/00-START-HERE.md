## Bottom line

Set One is worth keeping. The defects are narrow clauses inside otherwise useful requests, plus claims the complaint does not yet plead. Do not amend the served requests by a silent rewrite. Serve the broader language as Set Two after November 6.

The foundation ratings are right on RFPs 1–4, 6–8, 12–17, 19, and 20. They are off on four points:

1. **RFP 5 is Strong**, not Moderate. The string `NEWANSORBI` was searched on July 7 and was the principal first name on the Santa Clara vendor request. That is a fact of the pleaded screening, not a side issue that waits on an amended complaint.
2. **RFP 9 is not largely satisfied.** The text extract has the outbound Santa Clara request and then header-only pages. The court return is not readable text.
3. **RFP 11's factual premise is wrong.** San Joaquin search 496644200 returned **three** cases, all date-of-birth only, not two. Two are from 2020 and one is from 2025. The case list is partly in hand. The exclusion decision is not.
4. **RFP 18 is Strong** for the non-privileged dispute file. The privilege carve-out is already in the served request. The October 28 letter is a result notice, not the activity log.

Three record corrections belong in every later draft:

- **National Criminal Database Search 496623727** has a formatted name list and **no raw output** anywhere in the extract.
- The revised report **drops `NEWANSORBI`** from the alias block and from the name-searched lists. Component completion dates stay July 7, September 3, and October 14. The header still says `updated on 10/14/2026`. Sex-offender search 496623723 still lists **no names**. Inference: the alias was deleted from the displayed report and the searches were not re-run. That inference is not yet an admission.
- The October 27 **text extract contains** the October 28 letter, starting at extract page 179, after a long run of image-only pages. The original 34 MB PDF is not in the repo. Do not tell the Court the October 27 disclosure "contains" the October 28 letter until that PDF is checked.

## Amendment, in one paragraph

Add **15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b)** and **Civil Code § 1786.20(b)** now, limited to the `NEWANSORBI` alias and its use as a search name. Hold a separate § 1681i count: the October 28 letter is inside the 30-day window. Hold § 1681k: the statute is disjunctive (notice **or** strict procedures), and an October 14 public-record notice PDF already exists in `documents/matter-29/`. Also plead **§ 1786.20(a)** with the existing certification theory. It is the written-agreement duty, and the foundation memo skipped it. Civil Code § 1786.50(c) bars a claim whose only effect was a **more favorable** report, so do not plead "they failed to report the San Joaquin hits" or "the sex-offender search should have hit" as the accuracy violation.
