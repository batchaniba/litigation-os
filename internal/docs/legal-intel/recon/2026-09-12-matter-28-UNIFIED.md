## 2. One-sentence answer on res judicata

Claim preclusion needs a **final judgment on the merits** between the **same parties** on the **same primary right**. Matter 5 has no judgment of dismissal. What the court decided was a **jurisdictional precondition**, not the claim. Shannon Smith was not a party.

The kill that survives a later CCP § 581d judgment is not “no judgment yet.” It is: **the state court decided a jurisdictional precondition, not the claim.** Ask Mag. Cisneros to write that down.

## 5. How each element dies — and which ones stay dead

### 5.1 No final judgment — wins today’s motion; does not stay dead

An order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend is **not a final judgment** and is **not appealable**. The appeal is from the ensuing judgment of dismissal. Until that judgment is entered, the ruling is not res judicata and the trial court may still change it.

- *Berri v. Superior Court*, 43 Cal.2d 856, 860 (1955). CourtListener: FOUND.
- *Valvo v. University of Southern California*, 67 Cal.App.3d 887, 891–92 & n.3 (1977) — “Until entry of judgment, a ruling on a demurrer is not res judicata.” Confirm PDF before filing.
- *Beazell v. Schrader*, 59 Cal.2d 577, 579–80 (1963).
- CCP § 581d — a **dismissal** ordered by the court, in a signed written order filed in the action, constitutes a judgment. An order that only **sustains a demurrer** is not a dismissal.

*Kay v. City of Rancho Palos Verdes*, 504 F.3d 803, 808 (9th Cir. **2007**) (not 2008), quoting *Franklin & Franklin*, 85 Cal.App.4th 1168, 1174 (2000): California finality does not attach “until an appeal from the trial court judgment has been exhausted or the time to appeal has expired.” CourtListener: FOUND.

Two consequences the earlier memos blurred:

1. **CRC 8.104’s 60-day clock runs from a judgment**, not from an order sustaining a demurrer. If there is no judgment, Monday, September 14 is not an appeal deadline. Do not file a premature appeal of a non-appealable order just to keep *Kay* open.
2. **Finality can ripen while Matter 28 is pending.** Filed July 20 — five days after the July 15 order. If they later obtain a judgment and appeal time expires, *Kay* can attach mid-case.

**Nunc pro tunc risk.** Van Dine can move in Dept. 10A for entry of a judgment of dismissal. Do **not** request that judgment. If they move, oppose any recitation that converts the “jurisdictional requirement” holding into a merits dismissal, and tell Mag. Cisneros that a later 581d judgment still would not be on the merits (§ 5.2).

### 5.2 Not on the merits — this is the once-and-for-all kill

**Best California case.** *Perez v. City of San Bruno*, 27 Cal.3d 875, 882–83 (1980). Caption is *City of San Bruno*, not a bare “Perez” and not *City of Bruno*. A dismissal for **prematurity** is not on the merits. After the missing fact exists, a later action is not barred. CourtListener: FOUND. Primary text read (SCOCAL / Justia).

Judge Abdallah’s words map onto *Perez* without translation: a **jurisdictional requirement**, unmet **prior to the filing of** the FAC.

**Best Ninth Circuit case.** *Heath v. Cleary*, 708 F.2d 1376, **1380 n.4** (9th Cir. 1983). Pin is 1380 n.4, not 1379 n.4. A California mandamus denial **for failure to exhaust** does not claim-preclude a later § 1983 action. Cites *Perez* and Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2). Also applies *Patsy* as “a flat rule without exception.” Primary F2 text. CourtListener: FOUND.

Caveat: *Heath* n.4 is a footnote prediction of California law, on mandamus facts. It has never been contradicted by the California Supreme Court. *Perez* is the California holding; *Heath* is the Ninth Circuit applying it to exhaustion.

**Supreme Court / Restatement.** *Costello v. United States*, 365 U.S. 265, 284–88 (1961) — failure to satisfy a precondition is not an adjudication on the merits. CourtListener: FOUND.

Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2): a judgment that “rests on the prematurity of the action or on the plaintiff's failure to satisfy a precondition to suit” does not bar a later action after the precondition is satisfied.

**Why *Goddard* does not save them.** *Goddard v. Security Title Ins. & Guar. Co.*, 14 Cal.2d 47, 52–53 (1939) says a judgment after a general demurrer *may* be on the merits. Two answers: *Goddard* itself admits even a general-demurrer judgment “may not be on the merits” when the defect is technical; and courts look to the **actual basis of decision**, not a defense-drafted § 430.10(e) recital. Exhaustion as a jurisdictional precondition is that class of defect.

**Why *Keidatz* does not travel.** *Keidatz v. Albany*, 39 Cal.2d 826 (1952): a demurrer judgment bars a second action only if the new complaint does not cure the defect, or if the ground is “equally applicable.” The Matter 5 ground is temporally capped (“prior to the filing of his FAC”). It is not equally applicable to a federal complaint that does not require exhaustion of state administrative remedies.

***Patsy v. Board of Regents*, 457 U.S. 496, 516 (1982):** exhaustion of state administrative remedies is not a prerequisite to § 1983. The only statutory carve-out is 42 U.S.C. § 1997e (PLRA), for **adult prisoners**. Plaintiff is a parolee at liberty. **Verify the PLRA “prisoner” point before briefing it** (#765 flagged this). Reopen the *Patsy* U.S. Reports PDF before filing.

### 5.3 Smith is not a party — permanent as to her, unless they prove control

*DKN Holdings*, 61 Cal.4th at 824–26: claim preclusion runs only against the same party or a privy. Joint-and-several liability, partnership, and cosigner status do not create privity. CourtListener: FOUND.

*Taylor v. Sturgell*, 553 U.S. 880, 892–95 (2008) disapproved “virtual representation.” Six categories only. CEO is not one of them. CourtListener: FOUND.

**Honest caveat.** *DKN* collected California cases that treat derivative liability (corporation and its employees) as privity for some purposes: *Sartor v. Superior Court*, 136 Cal.App.3d 322, 328 (1982); *Lippert v. Bailey*, 241 Cal.App.2d 376, 382 (1966). Distinguish: those cases need a **merits** judgment against the corporation. Matter 5 has none. The only *Taylor* category that could still grow is **(4) assumption of control**. HOPE_000189 is notice of the Matter 5 complaint. Notice is not control.

For **HOPE**, the party element is gone. That is why § 5.2 has to be in the federal order.

### 5.4 Same claim (*Boeken*) — do not fight this as the lead

*Boeken v. Philip Morris USA, Inc.*, 48 Cal.4th 788, 797–98 (2010): the cause of action is the harm suffered, regardless of legal theory. CourtListener: FOUND. *Gonzales*, 739 F.3d 1226, 1233–34 (9th Cir. 2014), and *Furnace*, 838 F.3d 1019, 1024 (9th Cir. 2016), reject “§ 1983 = different primary right.” If the other elements were ever met, *Migra* would carry the never-litigated federal theories.

The Bane-on-Bane and fraud-on-fraud overlap against **HOPE** is the most exposed (#765 § 1.6). Win on judgment, merits, and (for Smith) parties.

### 5.5 Issue preclusion is a different, smaller weapon

The only issue actually litigated is whether plaintiff exhausted **before filing the state FAC**. That issue is not an element of the federal claims (*Patsy*; *Heath*). Concede the narrow issue; it does not move a federal claim.

Matter 6 (E.D. Cal. 2:26-cv-00195, first Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) dismissal without prejudice, June 1, 2026) is a preclusion nullity. *Semtek*, 531 U.S. 497, 505–09 (2001). It becomes a merits bar only if this action is voluntarily dismissed. **Never voluntarily dismiss the N.D. Cal. case.** Disclose Matter 6. It is still missing from this docket.

## 6. The holding that kills it once and for all

Ask for a written finding in the alternative, in this order:

1. **No final judgment.** The July 15 order sustains a demurrer. It is not a CCP § 581d judgment of dismissal. Under *Berri* and *Valvo*, it is not res judicata.
2. **Even if a judgment of dismissal is later entered in STK-CV-UF-2026-0001032**, the state court’s stated ground — failure to satisfy a jurisdictional precondition unmet “prior to the filing of” the FAC — is a dismissal for prematurity / failure of a precondition. Under *Perez*, *Heath* n.4, *Costello*, and Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 20(2), that is **not** a decision on the merits and **does not bar** this action. The formal order’s generic § 430.10(e) recital, on a defense-drafted “[PROPOSED] ORDER,” does not recharacterize the court’s own words.
3. **Shannon Smith was not a Matter 5 party** and is not in privity by CEO title (*DKN*; *Taylor*).
4. **Issue preclusion**, if reached, is limited to whether administrative remedies were exhausted before the state FAC, which is not an element of the federal claims (*Patsy*).

Paragraph 2 survives nunc pro tunc. Paragraph 3 survives as to Smith even if paragraph 2 were ever lost. Paragraph 1 wins the September motion.
