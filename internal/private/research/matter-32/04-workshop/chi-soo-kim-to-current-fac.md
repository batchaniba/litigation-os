## What Kim actually reasoned

Kim's April 20, 2026 findings and recommendations recount sleep in the driver's seat, refusal of some tests, a compelled blood draw, bloodshot/watery eyes, strong alcohol odor, slurred speech, and HGN findings. The recommendation concluded that those report-described observations supported reasonable suspicion and probable cause, treated the attached report as controlling over inconsistent allegations under then-cited exhibit principles, and found no separate due-process fabrication injury from the allegations presented. [Public reproduction of Kim's recommendation](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10913465/brendan-ngehsi-newanforbi-v-state-of-california-et-al/).

The recommendation also proposed denying further amendment based on the allegations and prior opportunity at that stage. It is a substantive adverse analysis that must be confronted; it is not itself proof that a district judge entered a final merits judgment adopting every proposition. [Public reproduction of Kim's recommendation](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10913465/brendan-ngehsi-newanforbi-v-state-of-california-et-al/).

## What changed, and what has not

- **Express challenge:** The current FAC contains paragraph-level challenges to alleged impairment observations and describes records as fabricated. Compare the specific allegations to the exact attached documents; merely adding the word “fabricated” does not establish a plausible fact or materiality.
- **Warrant identified:** Current FAC ¶¶ 16, 22, and 30–32 identify warrant-related injury. The workshop now contains the actual produced warrant/affidavit, which allows an honest corrected-affidavit exercise rather than speculation about the CHP 202 alone.
- **Serious factual repair:** The FAC's physical-beverage checkbox premise is contradicted by the visually reviewed produced form label. That requires a proposed correction, not another repetition of the same premise.
- **Exhibit doctrine:** Grier's footnote 1 and Khoja provide a focused way to argue that disputed non-legally-operative factual recitations should not trump specific contrary allegations. They do not establish that every exhibit contradiction must be resolved for plaintiff. [Grier](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/01/20/21-16702.pdf); [Khoja](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2018/08/13/16-56069.pdf).
- **Procedural distinction:** Do not call Kim's recommendation “vacated,” “overruled,” or “nonbinding because venue was wrong” without the orders. Determine which recommendations were adopted, which motions were denied/transferred, and how the case actually ended.
- **Prior concessions:** R087 pp. 17–18 withdrew punitive damages without prejudice and withdrew pro se fees, with reservations. A new prayer may require analysis, but the earlier concession is part of the record and should not be denied.
