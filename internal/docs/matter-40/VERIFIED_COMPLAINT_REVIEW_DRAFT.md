# I. NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. This is an employment-screening case. Defendants used information disclosed pursuant to California Penal Code § 290.46 — Plaintiff's California sex offender registry information — for a purpose relating to employment, in violation of Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

2. On September 14, 2026, TaskUs, LLC offered Plaintiff temporary employment as a Teammate on the **Uber AV Teammate (SJ: 09/23/2026)** campaign at $22.00 per hour, with an anticipated start of September 23, 2026. Plaintiff accepted the same day.

3. The same day, First Advantage Background Services Corp. ("First Advantage") opened Profile Advantage Order **WPS-361214148** for employment screening on that hire.

4. At 11:24 a.m. on September 14, 2026 — while Order WPS-361214148 was still open and its national sex-offender component (PRINSOS) was still In Progress — TaskUs Talent Acquisition Operations placed Plaintiff's start date on a "standard administrative hold" expressly **"including the processing of your background screening report."** That is employer writing that the First Advantage report is what stopped the accepted start.

5. On September 15, 2026, First Advantage issued a consumer disclosure letter stating that it was reporting public-record information concerning Plaintiff to **TASKUS HOLDINGS INC USA**. The enclosed national sex offender registry component states:

> SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY. SENTENCE: REGISTERED SEX OFFENDER

6. Defendants never restored the September 23 start. They never issued a Fair Chance individualized assessment restoring the offer. They never told Plaintiff the hold was lifted. The accepted Uber AV Teammate assignment was withheld and effectively rescinded.

7. Uber Technologies, Inc. is named because the only assignment offered was Uber's AV Teammate campaign, branded and requisitioned as Uber's. TaskUs staffed that campaign for Uber's benefit. Uber is pleaded as end client and joint employer. TaskUs entities are pleaded as the staffing / offer-letter / report-recipient employers. The § 290.46(j) prohibition reaches each person who used the registry-disclosed information for an employment purpose.

8. A separate saliva drug-test schedule (First Advantage Schedule ID 96558696) was ordered the same day. That product is analytically separate from the consumer report. The September 14 hold named the **background screening report**, not the saliva test. Saliva confirmation later failed on September 16. Defendants may attempt to recast the non-hire as a drug-screen miss. The chronology defeats that as the Sep. 14 hold basis.

## Unauthorized Employment Use of Information Disclosed Pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46
### Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A)
### Against All Defendants

64. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 63.

65. Plaintiff is a person as to whom information is disclosed through California's Megan's Law internet website.

66. Section 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits use of information disclosed pursuant to § 290.46 for purposes relating to employment except where statutorily authorized.

67. First Advantage furnished to TaskUs Holdings Inc USA, on Order WPS-361214148, information concerning Plaintiff whose sex-offender component expressly states SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY.

68. That information is information disclosed pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46.

69. Defendants used that information for purposes relating to employment, including by placing and maintaining the September 14 administrative hold pending the background screening report, refusing to restore the September 23 Uber AV Teammate start, and withholding or rescinding Plaintiff's accepted assignment.

70. The California-registry-sourced information was a substantial factor in those employment actions.

71. Uber used, caused the use of, or is jointly liable for the use of that information for purposes relating to employment on its Uber AV Teammate campaign, as end client and joint employer.

72. Plaintiff is unaware of another law authorizing use of § 290.46-disclosed information for this assignment.

73. The use occurred on and after September 14–15, 2026. A September 2026 filing is timely under either a potentially applicable one-year penalty characterization or a three-year statutory-liability characterization.

74. Defendants thereby violated Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E).

75. Plaintiff seeks the remedies authorized by § 290.46(j)(4)(A), including actual damages or the statutory minimum, treble damages according to proof, exemplary damages according to proof, and fees and costs as authorized — without duplicative recovery.

# XIII. COUNT TWO
## Fair Credit Reporting Act — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A)
### Against All Defendants

77. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 63.

78. Plaintiff was a consumer. Defendants procured or caused a consumer report to be procured for employment purposes, and were persons using the report within the meaning of the FCRA.

79. Before taking adverse action based in whole or in part on an employment consumer report, § 1681b(b)(3)(A) requires the person intending to take the action to provide the consumer a copy of the report and a written description of FCRA rights.

82. Defendants thereby violated § 1681b(b)(3)(A).

83. The count is timely. The conduct occurred September 14–17, 2026.

84. Noncompliance was negligent and, on proof, willful. Willfulness is pleaded as a damages theory under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n, not as a separate count.

85. Plaintiff seeks relief authorized by 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681n and 1681o according to proof.
