## PART 1 — CRIMINAL INJURIES COMPENSATION BOARD (CICB)
### Crim. Proc. §§ 11-801 – 11-819

### 1.1 What it is
The Criminal Injuries Compensation Board sits in the Governor's Office of Crime Prevention and Policy (GOCPP). Since 7/1/2025 it has **seven** members (up from five), appointed by the Executive Director with the Governor's approval and Senate advice and consent; membership must reflect the state's racial, ethnic, geographic, and gender diversity, and must include one family member of a homicide victim, at least one survivor of violence from a community experiencing disproportionately high rates of violence and incarceration, and at least one representative of an organization that assists victims applying for compensation. § 11-804.

### 1.2 Who is eligible — § 11-808
Eligible: (1) **a victim**; (2) a dependent of a victim who died as a direct result of the crime/delinquent act (or of trying to prevent/apprehend it, or of helping law enforcement/fire/EMS); (3) anyone who paid or assumed responsibility for the funeral expenses of such a victim; (4) a parent, child, **domestic partner**, dependent minor, adult under legal guardianship, or spouse of the victim who **resides with** the victim — or of an individual incarcerated for "abuse" as defined in Fam. Law § 4-501 who, before incarceration, resided with and financially supported them.
**Excluded:** a person who committed the crime/delinquent act, or an accomplice. § 11-808(a)(2).

Key definition changes (7/1/2025), § 11-801:
- **"Victim"** now means a person who suffers **physical or psychological injury or death** as a result of a crime or delinquent act (the old version restricted psychological-injury-only claims to sexual offenses, felonies, or injuries flowing from another's physical injury/death). A pure psychological-injury claim is now cognizable if the injury "necessitated mental health counseling."
- **"Dependent"** now expressly includes a **domestic partner**.
- New **"qualified third party"** category: licensed physician/dentist/psychologist; social worker or caseworker of any public/private health or social services agency or provider; or a DV/sexual-assault advocate or victim service provider.

**"Crime"** covers offenses under state, federal, or common law committed in Maryland **or in another state against a Maryland resident** (plus international terrorism against a MD resident abroad). § 11-801(d). A Maryland resident victimized in another state with no/inadequate program may also claim. § 11-808(b).

### 1.3 Filing deadline — § 11-809 (THE CRITICAL NUMBER)
Official text (mgaleg StatuteText, current):
> "Except as provided in paragraphs (2), (3), and (4) of this subsection, **a claimant shall file a claim within 4 years after the later of: (i) the discovery of the occurrence of the crime or delinquent act or the death of the victim; or (ii)** the earlier of: 1. the date the claimant discovered an attempt to obtain a reversal of a conviction, a sentence, or an adjudication for the crime or delinquent act; or 2. the date the claimant, exercising ordinary diligence, should have discovered [the same]."

Exceptions:
- **Child abuse: file at any time** (no deadline). § 11-809(a)(2).
- DNA-testing delay in sexual-offense cases: 4 years after notification of test/match results. § 11-809(a)(3).
- **Good cause: file at any time** if the Board finds good cause for missing the deadline. § 11-809(a)(4).

⚠️ **COMAR 12.01.01.04 still says "within 3 years of the date of the crime" (child abuse to age 25). It is stale** — pre-dates Ch. 705 of 2024 (effective 7/1/2025). **The 4-year statute controls.** Diarize 4 years from *discovery*, and cite the statute, not the regulation.

Filing mechanics: in person, by mail, or electronically. § 11-809(b). The Board must notify the claimant within **10 days** of receiving a claim. § 11-809(b) (old subsection (c) renumbered; substance: 10-day notice).

**Debt-collection freeze — § 11-809(c):** a health-care provider or funeral establishment that has **notice of a pending CICB claim** must **refrain from debt-collection activity** until the Board issues a final decision; on request the court shall stay a collection action; and the claimant gets the protections of the Maryland Consumer Debt Collection Act. Practical tip: once a claim is filed, give written notice to every medical/funeral creditor.

### 1.4 How to apply
- The Board must make a form/online application portal publicly available on its website (emergency awards, § 11-813(d); the same portal serves regular claims).
- Per COMAR 12.01.01.04 and the Board's application instructions: the claim form, a signed authorization for release of medical/police records, and documentation of each claimed loss (bills, receipts, proof of earnings). Claims examiners investigate; the Board may request information from the State's Attorney, State/local police, or a qualified third party. § 11-805(a)(3).
- Law-enforcement units must give every violent-crime victim **written information about CICB** (supplied by the Board) when a report is filed. § 11-807(b).

### 1.5 Conditions for an award — § 11-810
The Board may award only if it finds: (1) a crime or delinquent act was committed; **and** (2) it directly resulted in **physical injury to or death of the victim, OR psychological injury that necessitated mental health counseling**. § 11-810(a).

**The two biggest historical barriers are gone (7/1/2025):** the old 48-hour police-reporting rule and the full-cooperation-with-law-enforcement requirement were **repealed outright** (not merely waivable). The Board also may no longer reduce/deny an award based on the victim's contributory conduct — the entire old § 11-810(d) ("victim initiated, consented to, provoked…") was stricken.

**Evidence the Board SHALL accept**, § 11-810(b): (1) a law-enforcement report; (2) medical records documenting an injury consistent with the alleged crime; (3) a **sworn statement from a qualified third party**; (4) a peace order (Cts. & Jud. Proc. Title 3, Subt. 15); (5) a protective order (Fam. Law Title 4, Subt. 5); (6) any other evidence the Board considers probative. → A police report is now *one option among six*, and a DV/sexual-assault advocate's sworn statement suffices.

**$100 minimum:** the claimant must have incurred at least $100 in unreimbursed/unreimbursable expenses/indebtedness for: medical care (incl. supplies), eyeglasses/corrective lenses, mental health counseling, funeral expenses, property repair/replacement/cleaning, disability/dependent claim (incl. related assistive technology), **one-time relocation (incl. security deposit)**, child care incurred to seek medical/psychological care, transportation to seek medical/psychological care, or other necessary services — **or** lost at least $100 in earnings or support. § 11-810(d).

**Relative-of-offender rule — § 11-810(f):** a victim or dependent may **not** be denied compensation because the victim is a relative of the offender or was living with the offender as a family/household member — if the Board can reasonably determine the offender will not receive economic benefit or undue enrichment, it may award. (Directly relevant to intra-family abuse facts.)

### 1.6 Loss categories and caps — § 11-811 (current, via Ch. 705)
- **Disability/dependency:** $25,000; **+$25,000 more** if the injury caused permanent total disability. § 11-811(b)(1).
- **Medical:** $45,000. § 11-811(b)(2).
- **Counseling (victim's own, or after death of victim / for psychological injury):** **$45,000 per claimant**. § 11-811(a)(5), (b)(3). (Was $10,000.)
- **Counseling (parent, child, domestic partner, dependent, or spouse residing with an injured victim):** $10,000 per claimant; $20,000 per incident. § 11-811(a)(6), (b)(6).
- **Funeral expenses:** $10,000. § 11-811(a)(4). (Was $7,500.)
- **Property repair/replacement/cleaning** (incl. crime-scene cleanup and **replacing locks**): **$2,000 per claimant**. § 11-811(b)(5). (Was $250.)
- **Lost earnings/support:** up to **two-thirds of the victim's gross average wage** (floor per LE Art. Title 9, Subt. 6 schedule as of 1/1/2001; tips/gratuities/undeclared wages excluded; if no average weekly wage, award = average of max/min in the applicable schedule). § 11-811(a)(1)–(2).
- **Parent/guardian of a child victim** residing with the victim: up to **30 days of lost earnings** for caring for the victim. § 11-811(a)(3).
- **Parent, child, domestic partner, dependent, or spouse of a victim who died:** up to **2 weeks of lost average weekly wages**, capped at $2,000. § 11-811(a)(7), (b)(7).
- **Overall cap:** $45,000 total per claim, including subsequent/supplemental awards (except the extra $25,000 for permanent total disability). § 11-811(b)(4).

**Offsets — § 11-811(c)** (secondary source: CaseMine excerpt of current text; subsection not amended by Ch. 705): awards are reduced by (i) amounts the offender paid; (ii) payments from any other public or private source, **including workers' compensation**; (iii) life-insurance amounts over $25,000. Emergency awards are deducted from the final award.

### 1.7 Emergency awards — § 11-813
- Standard: award likely + undue hardship without immediate payment → up to **$10,000**, deducted from final award. § 11-813(a)–(b).
- **Mandatory:** unless the Board finds by **clear and convincing evidence** the application is without merit, it **shall** make emergency awards for **funeral expenses, crime-scene cleanup, and emergency relocation**. § 11-813(c). (The old repayment obligation for emergency awards was repealed.)
- The Board must maintain a public form/online portal for emergency award applications. § 11-813(d).

### 1.8 Processing timeline and decisions — § 11-814
- **Within 30 days after receipt of a claim**, the Board must notify the claimant (by email, phone, or other immediate means — and document date/time) if additional material is required. § 11-814(a).
- **Within 30 days after receipt of the claim *and all necessary supporting material***, the Board **or its employees** must complete review/evaluation and file a **written report** (decision + reasons) with the Executive Director. For good cause the Board may extend up to **1 year**. § 11-814(b).
- The claimant gets a copy of **any** report issued by the Board or an employee. § 11-814(d).
- Accountability: the Board's annual report to the Governor/Executive Director/General Assembly must include the **average time between receiving an application, the eligibility determination, and the first disbursement**. § 11-805(c)(2)(vii). (FY2023: 905 claims processed, 351 approved (~39%), 554 denied (~61%), most denials for technical reasons — late filing, missing information. Per Baltimore Banner reporting on the reform bill.)

### 1.9 Reconsideration and judicial review — §§ 11-814(c), 11-815
1. **Reconsideration:** within **30 days after receipt of the written report**, the claimant may **petition the Board for reconsideration** of a decision made by the Board or an employee. The Board must issue a decision **within 30 days** of the petition and file a written report with the Executive Director. § 11-814(c)(1)–(2).
2. **Judicial review:** the claimant may appeal the Board's reconsideration decision **by filing a petition for judicial review in accordance with Title 7 of the Maryland Rules** (i.e., circuit-court review of agency action). § 11-814(c)(3).
3. **§ 11-815 nuance (flag):** § 11-815 still provides that APA contested-case procedures apply, a hearing is available after proposed findings, and judicial review lies within 30 days of "the final decision of the **Secretary**" under State Gov't §§ 10-222/10-223. Ch. 705 **eliminated** the Secretary/Executive-Director review step and substituted Board reconsideration + Md. Rules Title 7 review, but did not amend § 11-815. Treat § 11-814(c) as the operative path; expect the Board/regulations to harmonize, and confirm with counsel before relying on either route's deadline.

### 1.10 Payment, protection, and penalties
- Awards are paid as the Board specifies; **may be converted to a partial or total lump sum without discount**. § 11-816(a), (c).
- **An award is not subject to execution or attachment** except for expenses resulting from the injury underlying the claim. § 11-816(b). (Functions like the state analogue of 11 U.S.C. § 522(d)(11)(A); see *In re Ludwig*-type bankruptcy treatment.)
- **False claim:** misdemeanor; fine **not less than $500**; forfeiture of benefits received + reimbursement to the State. § 11-818.
- **Subrogation:** the State is subrogated to the claimant's rights against the offender to the extent of the award. § 11-817. (Section cited; current text not re-verified from an official source in this pass — confirm before relying.)
- **Confidentiality:** a non-victim claimant may not include narrative describing the victim or a photograph; the Board **may not disclose the identity** of (or identifying information about) a victim of sexual assault, **child abuse**, stalking, or elder abuse; records protected by law/regulation stay confidential. § 11-805(d).
- **Forensic examinations:** § 11-816.1 provides reimbursement for forensic examinations and other expenses (section not text-verified in this pass — confirm before relying).

## PART 2 — RESTITUTION
### Crim. Proc. Title 11, Subtitle 6, Part I (§§ 11-601 – 11-619)

### 2.1 When a court may order restitution — § 11-603
Official text (mgaleg StatuteText, current): a court **may** enter a judgment of restitution ordering a defendant or child respondent to make restitution **in addition to any other penalty** for the commission of a crime or delinquent act, if:
- (a)(1) as a direct result of the crime/delinquent act, the victim's property was stolen, damaged, destroyed, converted, unlawfully obtained, or substantially decreased in value; **or**
- (a)(2) the victim suffered: **actual medical, dental, hospital, counseling, funeral, or burial expenses or losses**; **direct out-of-pocket loss**; **loss of earnings**; expenses incurred with **rehabilitation**; medical expenses paid by DHMH or another governmental unit; **CICB benefits paid to a victim**; or expenses paid by DHMH/another governmental unit under Subtitle 1, Part II.

**Presumption:** a victim is **presumed to have a right to restitution** if the **victim or the State requests** restitution and the court is presented with **competent evidence** of the loss. § 11-603(b).

**Civil action preserved:** a judgment of restitution does **not** preclude the victim from bringing a civil action against the obligor — but a **civil verdict must be reduced by the amount of restitution already paid**. § 11-603(c).

### 2.2 How and when to request it
- Request restitution at sentencing (victim or State's Attorney). The presumption in § 11-603(b) attaches on request + competent evidence (bills, receipts, pay records, expert testimony as needed).
- **Safety valve — § 11-103(e)(4)** (added by Ch. 363 of 2013, HB 250): "In any court proceeding involving a crime against a victim, the court shall ensure that the victim is in fact afforded the rights provided to victims by law." A victim who alleges the right to restitution under § 11-603 "was not considered or was improperly denied" may **file a motion requesting relief within 30 days of the denial or alleged failure to consider**; if the court agrees, it **may enter a judgment of restitution**. Other victim-rights violations have the same 30-day motion window (no remedy that modifies incarceration/commitment unless timely requested, and no double-jeopardy violation).
- Restitution is a **condition-sensitive** part of sentencing/probation; keep documentation of every expense from day one.

### 2.3 To whom restitution may be paid — § 11-606
The court may order payment to:
1. **the victim**;
2. DHMH, **the Criminal Injuries Compensation Board**, or any other governmental unit (i.e., the court can order the defendant to **reimburse CICB** for benefits it paid);
3. a **third-party payor** (insurer, or anyone who compensated the victim for property/pecuniary loss or paid an expense on the victim's behalf);
4. any person for whom restitution is authorized by law; or
5. a person who provided goods, property, or services to/for the victim for which restitution is authorized under § 11-603 (e.g., the counselor, the hospital).

**Priority:** payment to the **victim has priority** over payments to any other person or governmental unit — subject to the third-party-payor exception (if the victim was already fully compensated by a third-party payor, the court may direct payment to that payor) and to § 11-617(b)'s earnings-withholding priority (Family Law § 10-128 support orders first, restitution withholding second, other liens last). § 11-606(b); § 11-617(b).

### 2.4 Enforcement of a restitution judgment
- A judgment of restitution is **recorded and indexed as a money judgment**, **free of charge**, in the issuing court; on the victim's written request the District Court clerk also forwards **notices of lien to the circuit courts** (of that county and any other county specified), also free — including filing a notice of satisfaction. §§ 11-610/11-611 (see Ch. 512/2005 as amended by HB 591, Ch. 108 of 2023: "issued BY ANY COURT IN THE STATE"). The District Court clerk must send the payee written notice explaining the judgment is not a money judgment **until recorded and indexed**, and how to get it recorded. (HB 786 of 2005, Ch. 512.)
- Once a money judgment: standard civil-enforcement remedies — **wage garnishment, bank-account levy, property liens, writ of execution**. (Maryland DJS "Victim's Guide to Restitution," rev. Sept. 2025.) Per that guide, a restitution judgment is **valid for 12 years and renewable** — consistent with Maryland money-judgment law, but confirm with counsel for a circuit-court adult-criminal judgment.
- **No costs** may be assessed against the restitution payee for recording/indexing or lien notices. § 11-610 (HB 591/2023).
- Restitution compliance is typically a **condition of probation/supervision**; nonpayment can support a violation proceeding (separate from civil enforcement).

### 2.5 Restitution ↔ CICB ↔ civil suit: how they interact
- CICB pays first (or concurrently); the criminal court can then order the defendant to **reimburse CICB** via restitution (§§ 11-603(a)(2)(vi), 11-606(a)(2)); the State is subrogated to the claimant's recovery rights to the extent of the CICB award (§ 11-817).
- A later **civil verdict is reduced by restitution already paid** (§ 11-603(c)) — restitution does not double-compensate, but it does not bar the civil case either.
- **Juvenile note:** if the offender were a child respondent, § 11-604 caps restitution at **$10,000 per child per incident** and requires the parent be given a reasonable opportunity to be heard before a judgment against the parent. (Likely N/A on Matter 44's facts; included for completeness.)

### 2.6 Victim rights in the criminal process (Subtitle 1, Part I — overview)
- **Notification:** law enforcement and the State's Attorney must inform victims of their rights, including the right to request restitution and to seek CICB compensation. §§ 11-102, 11-103 (rights enumerated include notice of proceedings, right to attend, right to be heard at sentencing, victim-impact statement).
- **Enforcement of rights:** § 11-103(e) (Ch. 363/2013) — the court must ensure victims are "in fact afforded" their rights; 30-day motion for relief if a right (including restitution) was not considered or was denied.
- **Victim-impact statement** at sentencing is the vehicle for documenting losses for both the court's restitution decision and the record generally.

## PART 3 — PRACTICAL SEQUENCING NOTES (Matter 44, family-support posture)

1. **CICB does not require a criminal conviction, an arrest, or even a police report anymore.** The claim can proceed on medical records and/or a sworn statement from a qualified third party (physician, social worker/caseworker, DV or sexual-assault advocate). For a claimant reluctant to engage law enforcement, the advocate-sworn-statement path (§ 11-810(b)(3)) is the designed alternative.
2. **Intra-family offender is not a bar.** § 11-810(f): no denial because the victim is related to or lived with the offender, so long as the offender won't economically benefit.
3. **Psychological-injury-only claims are now cognizable** (counseling necessitated), with counseling caps up to $45,000/claimant.
4. **Diarize the deadline as 4 years from discovery** (not the stale COMAR 3-year figure). Child-abuse claims: no deadline. Good-cause escape hatch exists but should not be relied on.
5. **Document from day one:** every medical bill, counseling invoice, receipt for relocation/locks/transportation/child care, pay stubs showing lost earnings — the $100-per-category minimum is trivially met, but unreimbursed/unreimbursable status must be shown, and **offsets apply** for insurance, workers' comp, offender payments, and life insurance over $25,000.
6. **Use the debt-collection freeze:** once the CICB claim is filed, give written notice to medical/funeral creditors to trigger § 11-809(c).
7. **Emergency relocation** is one of the three **mandatory** emergency-award categories (funeral, crime-scene cleanup, emergency relocation) — up to $10,000, clear-and-convincing burden on the Board to deny.
8. **Restitution requires a criminal case and conviction/adjudication** — it is a sentencing-phase remedy. Preserve the option by (a) making the request on the record, and (b) remembering the 30-day § 11-103(e)(4) motion if the court overlooks it.
9. **Confidentiality is statutory** for sexual-assault/child-abuse/stalking/elder-abuse victims (§ 11-805(d)) — the Board may not disclose identifying information.
10. **No double recovery, but no election of remedies either:** CICB award → restitution can reimburse CICB → civil suit still available with verdict offset by restitution paid.
