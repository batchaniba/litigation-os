## 2A — Palomar Mobilehome Park Ass'n v. City of San Marcos, 989 F.2d 362 (9th Cir. 1993)

**SOURCE READ:** https://openjurist.org/989/f2d/362

**STATUS:** VERIFIED (rule sentence). PARTIAL (star page “364” — OpenJurist did not display reporter pagination).

**Holding.** Ninth Circuit affirmed a federal res judicata dismissal. California treats a judgment after a *general* demurrer as a judgment on the merits to the extent it holds the alleged facts do not state a cause of action, and that judgment bars a second action on the same facts and on claims that could have been litigated.

**Exact quote:** “In California, a judgment entered after the sustaining of a general demurrer is a judgment on the merits, and, to the extent that it adjudicates that the facts alleged do not establish a cause of action, it will bar a second action on the same facts.”

**Scope.** *Palomar* involved an entered state dismissal that was appealed and affirmed. It is not authority that an order sustaining a demurrer, standing alone and without a judgment, is preclusive.

**Published?** Yes.

## 2D — Valvo v. University of Southern California, 67 Cal.App.3d 887 (1977)

**SOURCE READ:** CaseMine / vLex extracts of the published opinion.

**STATUS:** VERIFIED (rule sentence). PARTIAL (footnote 3 text not isolated as a standalone official-page image).

**Exact quote:** “Until entry of judgment, a ruling on a demurrer is not res judicata and may be reconsidered and changed by the trial court.” (67 Cal.App.3d at 891–92, citing *Berri v. Superior Court* (1955) 43 Cal.2d 856, 860, and *Collins v. Marvel Land Co.* (1970) 13 Cal.App.3d 34, 45.)

**Published?** Yes.

**Use.** This is the California sentence for “no judgment, no res judicata.” *Meinhardt* does not replace it; *Meinhardt* is an appeal-clock case.

## 2F — CCP § 430.10 and exhaustion demurrers

**SOURCE READ:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&title=6.&part=2.&chapter=3.&article=1 and Justia § 430.10.

**STATUS:** VERIFIED (statute). PARTIAL (no California Supreme Court case opened that locks exhaustion to only (a) or only (e)).

**Exact quote:**

> (a) The court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the pleading.
>
> (e) The pleading does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

**Finding.** The Code lists both grounds. Exhaustion is argued both as “jurisdictional” and as failure to state a claim. A court calling exhaustion jurisdictional while a proposed order recites (e) is not a statutory contradiction. The Code does not assign exhaustion to one subdivision.

## 3B — Penal Code § 3008(d)(1)–(2)

**SOURCE READ:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=3008

**STATUS:** VERIFIED.

**Yes — two verbs.**

**(d)(1)** persons placed on parole *prior to* July 1, 2012 “shall **participate in** an approved sex offender management program … for a period of not less than one year or the remaining term of parole if it is less than one year.”

**(d)(2)** persons placed on parole *on or after* July 1, 2012 “shall **successfully complete** a sex offender management program … The length of the period in the program shall be not less than one year, up to the entire period of parole, as determined by the certified sex offender management professional in consultation with the parole officer and as approved by the court.”

Both subdivisions apply “without regard to when” the crime was committed. Leginfo notes amendment by Stats. 2014, ch. 611 (AB 2411), effective Sept. 26, 2014. The July 1, 2012 line is in the statute itself.

**Adverse point.** (d)(2) is a statutory ceiling of “up to the entire period of parole.” Retention past one year is not *ultra vires* as such.

### Caviness v. Horizon Cmty. Learning Ctr., Inc., 590 F.3d 806 (9th Cir. 2010)

**SOURCE READ:** https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/01/04/08-15245.pdf

**STATUS:** VERIFIED (outcome). PARTIAL (interior “conduct-specific” pin not isolated as a star page).

**Holding.** Affirmed. A private nonprofit running an Arizona charter school was not a state actor for the employment / post-termination conduct at issue. Statutory label “public school” is not enough. The inquiry is conduct-specific.

**Adverse point.** Cuts against treating a regulated contractor as a state actor for all purposes.

### Richardson v. McKnight, 521 U.S. 399 (1997)

**SOURCE READ:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/521/399

**STATUS:** VERIFIED.

**Holding.** Private prison guards employed by a systematically organized private firm are not entitled to qualified immunity.

**Exact quote:** “The firm is systematically organized to perform a major administrative task for profit.” (opinion body; syllabus uses the same “systematically organized” formulation.)

**Expressly not decided.** Whether private defendants have a good-faith defense (left open).

### Medical Marijuana, Inc. v. Horn, 604 U.S. 593 (2025)

**SOURCE READ:** CourtListener syllabus/opinion extract (No. 23-365, decided Apr. 2, 2025). Bound U.S. Reports PDF not opened; “604 U.S. 593” is from secondary official-cite tables.

**STATUS:** VERIFIED (holding and “declined to decide”). PARTIAL (bound page).

**Holding.** 5–4, Barrett, J. Affirmed and remanded. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1964(c), a plaintiff may seek treble damages for business or property loss even if that loss resulted from a personal injury.

**Expressly not decided:** whether Horn suffered a personal injury when he ingested THC; whether “business” covers employment; what “injured in his property” means. The majority flagged proximate cause / direct relation as a possible later obstacle.

**Use.** Do not treat *Horn* as a RICO-standing win. RICO stays off the Matter 28 opposition unless the safe-cite list is updated.

## Findings that cut against Plaintiff

1. *Palomar* and the general-demurrer line: if a **judgment** of dismissal was entered on a general demurrer, federal preclusion is an uphill fight.
2. *Meinhardt* is not a preclusion holding. Citing it as “no judgment = no res judicata” is an overread. *Valvo* is the actual sentence.
3. *Esche* does not hold that private SOMP vendors lack QI. Holding = no interlocutory appeal from denial of a good-faith defense. *Filarsky* remains in the landscape.
4. *Caviness*: Ninth Circuit refused to treat a heavily regulated private “public” contractor as a state actor for the conduct at issue.
5. No found damages judgment against a private sex-offender treatment vendor under § 1983 / Bane / RICO. Expect a novelty / clearly-established-law argument.
6. Penal Code § 3008(d)(2) authorizes program length “up to the entire period of parole.” Retention past one year is not automatically unlawful.
7. 15 CCR § 3574 lists concrete retention grounds (LS/CMI ≥ 11, Stable-2007 ≥ 4, deceptive polygraph, recent sex-offense-related violations) and did not exist for 2021–23 conduct (emergency operative Oct. 14, 2024).
8. *Horn* left proximate cause and “business or property” open. It is not a RICO-standing win. Keep RICO off the next opposition unless the whitelist is updated.
9. *Wicklund* is unpublished (9th Cir. R. 36-3) even if the memo is later useful as an analogue.
10. If the state court called exhaustion jurisdictional and then entered a **judgment**, defendants will argue *Palomar* plus issue preclusion on exhaustion, regardless of whether a proposed order recited § 430.10(e).

This is source-checking, not advice.
