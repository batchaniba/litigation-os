## 1. Common Rule 45 mechanics

Proceed after the actual Rule 26(f) conference absent an order authorizing earlier discovery. Ask Accurate first for its own records and contractual retrieval rights; a vendor label does not prove that the vendor has all the missing information or that Accurate lacks control. Parallel narrow vendor discovery is justified where a separate system preserves unique query/source evidence or deletion is imminent; do not wait indefinitely for the first party response if unique logs may disappear. [RFP 6–11; FD pp. 1–2,28–31; FRCP 26(d),34(a),45(d)(1).]

For each vendor:

1. Verify the current legal entity, registered/service agent, business address, transaction cross-reference and physical compliance location. The disclosure provides **leads**, not verified present service information.
2. As a pro se party, obtain an issued/signed subpoena from the N.D. Cal. clerk under Rule 45(a)(3); do not sign as an attorney authorized to practice. A document-only AO88B form is a starting point. The issuing court is this action's court, even when compliance is elsewhere.
3. Serve notice and a copy on all parties **before** serving the vendor, Rule 45(a)(4). A protective order or party agreement does not dispense with notice.
4. Have a nonparty adult serve the subpoena. Choose a lawful compliance place within Rule 45(c)(2)(A)'s100-mile boundary relative to where the vendor resides, is employed or regularly transacts business in person; remote electronic production can be permitted without demanding unlawful travel to Oakland. Verify the appropriate district for enforcement/quashing, normally the compliance district under 45(d), subject to45(f).
5. Allow reasonable time—propose twenty-one days after actual service for this narrow document-only production, adjusted to burden—not an invented fixed November date. Written objections are due before the earlier of the compliance date or fourteen days after service. Resolve objections before seeking an order compelling production; do not treat objection as contempt.
6. Request reasonably usable structured/native query records, meaningful timestamps/time zones, original attachments and a custodian declaration if voluntarily available. A subpoena for documents does not require a new narrative report or an unnotified deposition. If testimony becomes necessary, separately notice a limited Rule 30(b)(6) deposition and tender applicable attendance/mileage fees; no attendance fees are ordinarily due for a purely document-only demand with no appearance.
7. Offer appropriate protection of consumer identifiers and trade secrets, and avoid significant nonparty expense. Use a secure transaction reference or separately transmitted identifier if needed; do not spread a full SSN in a public subpoena filing. Apply Rule 45(d)(2)(B)(ii)'s expense protection when seeking compelled compliance.

Preserve now; produce under a meaningful confidentiality protocol when needed. Lack of a protective order is not an automatic discovery stay. Narrow non-sensitive metadata may be produced while disputed confidentiality terms are resolved. No party gets an automatic right to source code, unrelated customer files or every historical vendor contract. [FRCP 26(c),45(d).]

### Privilege protocol to propose

The 2025 amendments expressly place the timing and method of Rule 26(b)(5)(A) compliance on the discovery-plan agenda. Propose a first log with each production or within an expressly agreed short interval, periodic updates, and a final date early enough to resolve disputes before cutoff. Include date, author, recipients/roles, type, nonrevealing subject, privilege basis and production linkage; tailor categories if volume justifies them. Krishnan requires prompt, informative logs, not generalized claims. [FRCP 26(f)(3)(D); ASK § 5,p. 3.]

A limited logging exclusion may cover counsel-client communications **solely about litigating this action** after a defined date, only by express agreement/order and with enough category information to assess the exclusion. It must not eliminate logs for ordinary-course screening, vendor contacts, the October dispute, correction propagation or client business instructions. The suit already existed when these October operations occurred, so a blanket “all post-suit communications” cutoff would swallow the very evidence at issue. Copying counsel does not automatically establish privilege; neither does this protocol automatically waive real privilege. [C filed complaint; CL p. 2; ASK § 5; FRCP 26(b)(3),(5).]
