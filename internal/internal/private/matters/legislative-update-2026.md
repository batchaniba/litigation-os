## Part (a) — HB 1586 (Protective Orders – Coercive Control)

**FINAL STATUS: DIED — NOT ENACTED.** Per its official MGA bill page (https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/hb1586?ys=2026rs): introduced 2/13/2026; rereferred to House Judiciary 3/10/2026; hearing 3/13/2026; **no further action, no chapter law, no Governor's signature** (2026 session ended sine die April 2026). Sponsors: Delegates McComas (lead) et al. Prior version: HB 1290 (2024).

**Substance:** Would have amended Family Law § 4-501 to add "coercive control" to the definition of "abuse" for protective orders **only where the person seeking relief is an adult**, defined as a pattern of emotional/psychological manipulation, maltreatment, threat of force, or intimidation to compel conduct (bill enumerated: isolation, deprivation of basic necessities, monitoring, immigration-status threats, reproductive coercion). Notably opposed by the Maryland Judiciary (as drafted), MNADV, the Women's Law Center, and TurnAround, Inc.

## HB 497 (Ch. 791) transition — independently verified against the enrolled chapter text (2026-09-26)

The module above was researched from the MGA bill page. The following details were **additionally verified against the enrolled chapter PDF** (https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026rs/Chapters_noln/CH_791_hb0497e.pdf), approved by the Governor May 26, 2026, effective **October 1, 2026**:

- **FL § 4-505(c)(1):** a temporary protective order is effective for not more than **14 days after ISSUANCE** (the enrolled text changed "7 days after service" to "14 days after issuance" — measure from issuance, not service).
- **FL § 4-506(b)(1)(ii):** the final protective order hearing shall be held no later than **14 days after the temporary protective order is ISSUED** (was 7 days after service), unless continued for good cause.
- **New FL § 4-506(d)(14):** a final protective order may order the respondent to pay, by a date fixed by the court, any losses or expenses incurred as a direct result of the abuse — including (i) medical, dental, or mental health treatment costs; (ii) costs to repair or replace damaged property; (iii) food, temporary shelter, or relocation costs; (iv) transportation costs; (v) filing fees and court costs; and (vi) reasonable attorney's fees.
- **New FL § 4-506(l):** a monetary award under § 4-506(d)(14) does **not** preclude the petitioner from bringing a subsequent claim for additional losses or expenses not awarded in the final protective order.
