10          I. THIS CASE CHALLENGES SERRATO'S APRIL 20 DECISION, NOT AN

11             INDEPENDENT CLAIM FOR DAMAGES ARISING FROM THE DUI

12                                           INVESTIGATION

13

14          The challenged administrative act is Captain G. Serrato's April 20, 2026 rejection of a

15   certification request that specifically identified false imprisonment and unlawful criminal

16   restraint by CHP personnel. The statutory issue is whether that rejection was a lawful

17   application of Penal Code section 679.10(c), (d), (g) and (h), or an abuse of discretion

18   correctable under Code of Civil Procedure section 1085. What the officers did on November

19   1, 2024 matters here as evidence of the qualifying-criminal-activity determination, not as an

20   invitation to adjudicate six collateral Fourth Amendment claims.

21

22          In overruling the demurrer to the First Cause of Action, this Court recognized that a

23   credible evidentiary showing undermining the officers' asserted intoxication observations

24   could support the conclusion that the detention lacked probable cause and that the certification

25   denial was abusive. (Order Sustaining Demurrer In Part, Aug. 29, 2026, at p. 4.) That pleading

26   ruling did not establish the facts. This motion asks for the resulting merits determination on

27   the competent record. Counts Two and Three were dismissed without leave, and Count Four

28   was not amended within the allowed period. The motion neither revives a standalone

14                III. THE GOVERNING STATUTE IMPOSES A CONDITIONAL,

15                             ENFORCEABLE CERTIFICATION DUTY

16

17          CHP is a law enforcement certifying entity, and Serrato issued the decision for that

18   entity. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subds. (a)–(b); Pet., Ex. 1.) Penal Code section 679.10(c)

19   expressly lists “[u]nlawful criminal restraint” and “[f]alse imprisonment” as qualifying

20   criminal activity; subdivision (d) includes crimes whose nature and elements are substantially

21   similar to listed criminal activity. Most importantly, subdivision (g)(1) provides that, upon

22   request, an authorized official “shall certify victim helpfulness” when the person was the

23   victim of qualifying criminal activity and “has been helpful, is being helpful, or is likely to be

24   helpful” in detection, investigation or prosecution of it.

25

26          The statute does not require the victim to procure a criminal complaint, officer arrest,

27   conviction or open investigation. (Pen. Code, § 679.10, subd. (k)(1).) The statutory victim is

28   also not rendered ineligible merely because the person was investigated as a potential suspect

                                                    Page 5
     PETITIONER'S REVISED NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT OF MANDATE - 26CV191136
 1   in another offense. The statute protects against specified impermissible grounds, including use

 2   of the victim's criminal history or cooperation in a separate case; Petitioner does not assert that

 3   Serrato actually employed those prohibited factors absent proof. (Id., subd. (k)(3)(A), (H).)

 4

 5          Subdivision (h) creates a rebuttable presumption of helpfulness where the victim did

 6   not refuse or fail to provide reasonably requested assistance. It does not eliminate the

 7   qualifying-activity element. Certification, moreover, is evidence for federal immigration

 8   proceedings, not a grant of immigration status. The Legislature placed the certification

 9   decision with defined officials but bounded it by the mandatory conditions it enacted.

10

11          Traditional mandate corrects a public official's legally erroneous exercise of duty or

12   abuse of discretion; it ordinarily cannot dictate a discretionary judgment unless one lawful

13   result remains. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1085–1086; Saleeby v. State Bar (1985) 39 Cal.3d 547,

14   562; Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors (1989) 49 Cal.3d 432, 442–446.) These

15   principles govern both the requested certification and the alternative request for remand.

16

17           IV. THE REQUEST PRESENTED THE ELEMENTS OF A QUALIFYING

18                RESTRAINT OFFENSE; CHP'S “NO QUALIFYING ACTIVITY”

19          CONCLUSION MUST BE TESTED AGAINST THE ACTUAL AUTHORITY

20                                         FOR CONFINEMENT

21

22          The offense is unlawful restraint of a person. Penal Code section 236 defines false

23   imprisonment as the unlawful violation of another's personal liberty. Penal Code section

24   146(a) additionally proscribes a public officer's arrest or detention against a person's will

25   under color of authority without lawful authority. Petitioner does not claim that section 146(a)

26   automatically qualifies by name under subdivision (c); it supplies a closely related state

27   criminal-law description of an unlawful detention by an officer whose nature and elements

28   must be considered under subdivision (d), while section 236 directly matches the listed

     PETITIONER'S REVISED NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT OF MANDATE - 26CV191136
 1   offense. Whether every criminal element and absence of legal privilege is established is the

 2   factual dispute—not a reason to categorically treat a law enforcement detention as incapable

 3   of qualifying.

 4

 5          Intentional restraint is not disputed. CHP's own Form 202 records handcuffing,

 6   custody, transport, a warrant-authorized blood draw, and jail booking arising from the

 7   November 1 incident. (Pet., Ex. 6; AGO 000029, 000032–000033.) The material question is

 8   lawful privilege at the point the custody became compulsory. The officers did not witness the

 9   Tesla moving and the DS-367 treats driving as admitted rather than observed. (CHP Form

10   202/DS-367, AGO 000029–000053 [exact DS-367 Bates to insert]; Verified Answer ¶34

11   [verify filed text].) Under Vehicle Code section 40300.5, arrest without witnessed driving

12   requires both reasonable cause to believe prior DUI and one of the statute's specified

13   circumstances; the officers observed the car on a right shoulder, not necessarily obstructing a

14   traveled roadway. The Court must determine the relevant statutory circumstance on the

15   evidence actually available when arrest occurred, not treat the absence of observed movement

16   as dispositive or ignore available alternatives under subsections (c)–(e).

17

18          The record contains an affirmative discrepancy in a purported arrest factor. CHP Form

19   202 records that Petitioner chose not to perform the walk-and-turn because of difficulty. (Pet.,

20   Ex. 6; AGO 000031.) Yet at 01:10:23 Petitioner told officers he thought he could attempt that

21   test; at 01:10:25–30 Melano objected to the freeway shoulder's uneven ground and told

22   Meza-Gonzalez he would not perform that test there; Meza-Gonzalez agreed. (Pet., Ex. 5,

23   timestamped transcript at p. 11.) If the native video authenticates that exchange, the test's

24   absence cannot fairly be attributed exclusively to Petitioner's refusal or inability. That matters

25   because Form 202 invokes field-test performance as an actual factor in the arrest decision. It is

26   a narrow, testable issue of factual reliability, not a demand that the Court assume every other

27   observation was false.

24           V. SERRATO'S REJECTION OF BOTH LISTED AND SUBSTANTIALLY

25           SIMILAR ACTIVITY REQUIRES A SUBSTANTIVELY LAWFUL BASIS

26

27          The decision's determinative sentences deny listed qualifying activity and substantially

28   similar activity. Because section 679.10(c)(18)–(19) expressly identifies criminal restraint and

                                                    Page 9
     PETITIONER'S REVISED NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT OF MANDATE - 26CV191136
 1   false imprisonment, a rejection can stand only if it represents a legally permissible assessment

 2   that the claimed underlying conduct fails the elements of qualifying victimhood on a

 3   supported record— not a proposition that police detention, an absence of prosecution, or

 4   CHP's unilateral characterization of the encounter necessarily excludes such victimhood. The

 5   latter propositions would conflict with subdivisions (c), (d) and (k). The current letter does not

 6   expressly endorse those incorrect propositions, and Petitioner does not pretend that it does.

 7

 8          The demonstrable question is the factual and legal basis of CHP's actual finding. On

 9   April 9 the agency was presented with the same report and timestamped transcript identifying

10   disputed descriptions of impairment and claimed restraint; on April 20 it stated categorically

11   there was no qualifying or similar activity. Petitioner submits that, if the Court credits the

12   evidence that the arrest lacked lawful authority and that the resulting custody constituted

13   criminal false imprisonment, the denial's dispositive premise cannot stand. In that event, it is

14   no answer that the officer was acting during an ostensible DUI investigation: state law

15   explicitly recognizes detention without lawful authority as criminal misconduct under section

16   146(a), and section 236 recognizes unlawful confinement.

17

18          The alternative agency-error route is narrower and evidence-dependent. If a

19   contemporaneous review file reveals that CHP treated absence of a filed criminal case as

20   determinative; categorically excluded criminal misconduct by peace officers; assessed only

21   whether the officers asserted DUI probable cause rather than evaluating contradictory

22   evidence bearing on it; or employed another legally impermissible rule, the denial must be

23   reconsidered under the correct statutory standard. This motion does not claim any such

24   specific internal criterion was used before the relevant April records establish it. The later May

25   9, 2026 internal review/interview memoranda postdate the denial and are not evidence of what

26   Serrato actually reviewed before April 20.

27

28          Under traditional mandate the Court need not infer deception, animus, or sham solely

     PETITIONER'S REVISED NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR PEREMPTORY WRIT OF MANDATE - 26CV191136
 1   from a five-sentence decision. The proposed finding instead is either (1) a substantively

 2   untenable qualifying-activity ruling after the Court finds the necessary facts, or (2) an actual,

 3   established misapplication of the governing law or demonstrable abuse of discretion, resulting

 4   in a writ tailored to the finding. This is the claim that survived demurrer.

20          Subdivision (h) presumes helpfulness if the qualifying victim has not refused or failed

25   does not allege that the presumption supplies missing proof of qualifying victimhood; it bears

26   on the second statutory predicate, which must be decided separately.
