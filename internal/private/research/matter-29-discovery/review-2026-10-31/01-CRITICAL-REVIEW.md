## 1. The discovery is better than the existing liability analysis

**Assessment:** Keep the first wave. Its core is tightly tied to the pending screening dispute. But do not tell the Court that the disclosure proves a false criminal match, registry-based employment denial, willfulness, or an untimely reinvestigation. None is established by these materials. The decisive missing link is the content, timing, and use of Accurate's communications to Amazon, particularly before the alleged July 27 loss of the training shift. [C ¶¶22–29, PDF pp. 8–10, lines 353–475; RFP Nos. 1–7, pp. 3–4; CL p. 2, lines 17–44; FD pp. 17–19,28–31.]

### Pleadings actually at issue

The complaint has seven counts, but they are not all pleaded identically against both defendants. Count Two is the employer-facing Fair Chance claim; Count Four is captioned primarily against Accurate. Accurate's specifically identified FCRA conduct is in ¶61, under § 1681b(b)(1), followed by willfulness/negligence remedies in ¶¶62–63. The complaint does not articulate the October alias, reinvestigation, or § 1681k events as distinct violations; those events postdate the complaint. [C Count Two, PDF pp. 14–16; Count Three ¶¶54–63, PDF pp. 16–18, lines 812–945; Count Four ¶¶64–70, PDF pp. 19–20, lines 965–1036.]

Amazon's Dkt. 19 seeks dismissal of Counts Five–Seven. It is not a motion by Accurate and does not seek dismissal of Counts One–Four. Plaintiff's opposition declines to oppose dismissal of the UCL and IIED counts **against Amazon only**, subject to the qualifications stated there. Accurate answered with twelve defenses. Do not describe the unchallenged counts as judicially approved, or dismiss claims against Accurate by implication. [MTD notice p. 2, lines 130–153; OPP lines 807–877,935–943; A pp. 38–39, lines 2287–2388.]

## 4. Willfulness and comparator discipline

Section 1681n covers knowing and reckless noncompliance, not every error. Under *Safeco*, 551 U.S.47,68–70 & n. 20, the objective legal interpretation matters. A deleted alias or lawsuit title is not proof of reckless procedures. Ask who knew of a particular recurring defect, what was audited, and what operating correction was implemented before Plaintiff's report. A later fix is not automatically an admission; distinguish discoverability from admissibility. [CL p. 2; authority SAFECO.]

*Rangel* is a closer **subject-matter** comparator for criminal identity matching: it alleges another person's records, mismatched identifying data, disputes, and eventual deletion. These remain allegations. *Varela* alleges criminal-history reporting-age problems, while *Santee* concerns a driver's-license restriction and repeated disputed reporting. Do not group them as adjudicated findings of the same matching defect. [RAN-C ¶¶20–30, lines 200–270; VAR-C ¶¶11–12, lines 133–146; SAN-C ¶¶10–14, lines 125–142.] Accurate's answers contest liability; the Varela joint26(f) report describes policies/procedures discovery but is neither an order governing this case nor permission for unlimited companywide production. [RAN-A defenses; VAR-A defenses; SAN-A defenses; VAR-26F p. 7, lines 315–334.]

*Lerma* is **adverse pleading guidance**, not plaintiff-side proof of an identical settled registry violation. Its order treated the county conviction result and NSOR no-hit as insufficient to infer Megan's Law sourcing/use and dismissed the derivative Accurate FCRA theory. It did not establish that every NSOR search is unlawful, and it did not adjudicate compliance with every certification requirement. [LERMA36 pp. 7–10, especially pp. 8–10; fn.1 p. 9.] Use this case to isolate a genuinely different source/use record and independent CRA duties. No settlement terms are necessary for this review.
