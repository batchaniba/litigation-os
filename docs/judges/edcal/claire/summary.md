## Chambers and calendar (S1, S2)
- Courtroom 26, 8th floor. CRD Jonathan Anderson, janderson@caed.uscourts.gov, (916) 930-4199.
- Civil law and motion is Wednesdays at 10:00 am. Dates are not reserved.
- **Pro se hearing preference:** "Where a party is proceeding in pro se, that party's preference for in-person or video appearance will be given significant weight." Contact the CRD at least a week before the hearing. Another party who wants to appear by phone at an in-person hearing must first tell the pro se party.
- Telephonic appearances are otherwise generally fine; tell the CRD at least 2 days ahead. Settlement conferences are the exception: phone appearance there only in the "rarest of circumstances."
- She may submit motions without a hearing. A request to let an attorney four or fewer years out of law school argue guarantees a hearing.

## Rules (S2, S3)
- Non-discovery motions and oppositions are limited to 20 pages and replies to 10. Over-length papers filed without leave "will be stricken without further notice."
- Courtesy copies are generally unnecessary, **except discovery Joint Statements.** Those require a **mandatory tabbed courtesy copy delivered to the Clerk at least 14 days before the hearing.**
- **Discovery:**
  - The Joint Statement is due 14 days before the hearing, **strictly enforced**; the motion is removed from calendar if it is late.
  - Limited to 25 pages. It must reproduce each request and objection in full. File exhibits as attachments, not separate docket entries.
  - Meet and confer is "strictly" enforced. Email or other written correspondence is insufficient; confer in person, by phone, or by video.
  - Informal telephonic conference (S3): give dates 3 to 14 days out. Email a 2-page joint letter, including any exhibits, in 12-pt type to acorders@caed.uscourts.gov at least 24 hours before.
- Proposed orders in Word to acorders@ with /s/ and the date.
- On consent (S2): "the trial calendars of magistrate judges are generally less congested and more flexible than those of district judges."
