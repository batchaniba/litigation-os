THE LOOK ASSESSMENT:

The LOOK Assessment is a computer-based idiographic assessment of sexual interests,
including pedophilic sexual interest, which generates data for the preferences of different age and
gender categories. LOOK combines a participant’s opportunity to report his sexual preferences
along with having three (3) covert viewing time validity measures. LOOK incorporates an anti-
feigning tool that requires participants to look at pictures and make a decision concerning sexual
attractiveness. As with any single assessment, the results of the LOOK should not be used in
isolation to determine the presence or absence of any specific sexual interest. Click here

https://sites.google.com/a/almaxconsulting.com/hope/frequentl y-asked-questions/assessment-

process/look-assessment for YouTube tutorials on how to administer and score the LOOK.

SARATSO AUTHORIZED RISK ASSESSMENT TOOLS:

HOPE is responsible for ensuring all clients convicted of committing a sexual offense are

evaluated in accordance with SARATSO policy.

Static and Dynamic Risk Factors (DRFs) are factors statistically associated with increased

recidivism risk and thus are targets for treatment.

STATIC-99R

Registered Sex Offenders (PC 290)— who are on formal supervision will have a completed
Static-99R (provided by probation or parole) that will be reviewed and verified during the
individual’s onboarding. The Static-99R is an actuarial instrument designed to assist in the
prediction of sexual and violent recidivism for clients. The Static-99R has shown moderate
accuracy in ranking clients according to their relative risk for sexual recidivism. Furthermore, its
accuracy in assessing relative risk has been consistent across a wide variety of samples,

countries, and unique settings.

The STABLE-2007 is an empirically informed, cross-validated, risk assessment instrument that
relies on changeable factors to estimate risk of sexual recidivism. Hanson and Harris (2000,
2007) developed this risk assessment instrument based on a large prospective study from Canada

and the states of Alaska and Iowa with a total sample size of 997 sexual offenders. The presence

DISCHARGE REPORTS

Discharge (DC) reports are to be completed for every client (CLT) who has entered treatment
and subsequently is no longer participating in treatment at HOPE.

This includes but is not limited to clients who:
® Client completes their required parole/probation period
® Return to Prison
® At the request of supervising officer/agent
® Transfer to another contract/county

® Deceased

All DC summaries are to be completed in TA within five (5) calendar days of the Controlling
Discharge Date (CDD). DC summaries must be approved by the supervisor on day seven (7) and
received by our Stakeholders on day ten (10). If a client was never seen by HOPE’s clinical team
or if the client was referred for assessment only, the local OC or OM are to create an
administrative discharge report.

The clinical Discharge Summary should include an accurate representation of the CLT’s entire
experience in treatment, not just the current clinician’s experience. Clinicians are to perform a
thorough chart review and consult with other clinicians to obtain an accurate picture of a
particular client’s journey in treatment. DC summaries require an updated risk profile as assessed
by utilizing validated assessment tools. All known risk factors or high-risk behaviors must be
reflected in the discharge summary, as well as the most recent polygraph results, reason for
conclusion or termination of contracted services, a Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental
Disorders (DSM) diagnosis, progress in treatment goals or lack thereof; familial or prosocial
supports at time of discharge, and recommendations for continued therapy and management of

sexual deviancies or risky behaviors.

PROOF OF PRACTICE (POP)— APPOINTMENT SLIPS

Per policy, all clients receive an appointment slip for every appointment they are scheduled to
attend. These are commonly known as Proof of Practice slips. Clients receive one appointment
slip for their group schedule. Repeating group appointments only require one original
appointment slip. The client only needs to receive a new appointment slip if their group schedule

changes and/or if they request a new one.

A client is to receive a new appointment slip for every individual session, unless the client is
being seen weekly, on the same/day time (i.e., recurrent sessions). In this case, the client only

receives one appointment slip that states this is a repeating appointment.

It is imperative that every new appointment is accompanied with an appointment slip. The client
receives the white copy and HOPE keeps the yellow copy. Proof of practice slips are used as
reminders for upcoming appointments and proof of documentation that is often referenced for
verification that the client was notified. Once both copies are signed by the client and
clinician/operation member providing the slip, the POP is scanned and uploaded in the client’s

chart under documents in TA.

Policy

Individual appointments should be scheduled in a consistent fashion. If a participant is to be seen
the 3 Friday of every month, it should not change for the most part, unless rescheduled due to
the appointment falling on a holiday. If a participant needs to move their appointment due to
work or school change, the request MUST come from their supervising authority if the assigned
clinician is unable to accommodate their schedule. In general, clients should attempt to schedule
external appointments around their mandated treatment, which demonstrates their ability to

problem solve and practice assertive communication skills with their employer.

COLLECTING PAYMENT

HOPE Program is committed to providing accessible and affordable services to all our clients.
Following the service agreement outlined in the opening paperwork packet, all self-pay clients
are obligated to make full payments for services at the time of service. Self-pay clients are
defined as individuals who receive voluntary or mandatory services and who are required to pay
at least a portion (i.e., up to 100%) of their treatment service fees. CDCR clients do not pay for

their treatment and Federal Probation clients occasionally pay a co-pay for their treatment.

The process of collecting payment involves collaboration between our clinical and operations
teams. Either before or at the time of the initial appointment, HOPE’s operation team shall
describe the fees for services to the client. If the client is a minor or lacks the capacity for
consent, HOPE’s operation team shall inform the parent or legal guardian of all fees. In the case
of an emergency, the clinician shall inform the client of any fees as soon as is practical after

rendering the service.

A variety of payment options are accepted, including Credit Cards (CC), debit cards, certified
checks, money orders, and cash. Clients without CCs or bank accounts will have the option to
pay via Check, Cashier’s Check, and Cash. If paying via CC, the client is required to provide a
signed Credit Card Authorization Form for auto payments, which will be processed when the
client provides the Office Coordinator (OC) or Office Manager (OM) with the CC information.
If the client does not possess a CC, they must pay dues before entering each session. Before
sessions, the assigned or covering clinician will coordinate with the front desk (CC or OM) to
confirm receipt of the client's payment. The messaging to clients when requesting that they pay
their balance, in full, is to promote accountability and prevent the financial burden associated

with accruing significant balances at HOPE.

If a client does not pay for services as agreed and if HOPE wishes to use a collection agency to
collect the fees, HOPE Program will first give written notice to the client that a collection agency
will be employed, but that the client will first be allowed a reasonable opportunity to make
prompt payment. The written notice shall specify a date by which the collection agency will be
employed if prompt payment is not received. HOPE shall limit information provided to the

collection agency to that which is essential to recover the fees.

When a client enters the HOPE Program, unless otherwise noted, they are expected to pay the

zero balance for three (3) months prior to the scholarship being awarded. It generally takes

To ensure that we are providing quality care to our clients, All HOPE providers are responsible
for documenting each client encounter completely, accurately, and on time. Clear and concise
documentation is critical to forensic and clinical report writing as it vital to risk management and
community safety. Accurate and timely documentation of all HOPE artifacts (e.g., client
attendance, progress notes, alerts, and reports) will also help providers evaluate and plan the
client’s treatment, mitigate malpractice risks, maintain contractual compliance, and ensure
payment for the services rendered. The practice of good risk management should build on the old
adage of four Cs: compassion, communication, competence, and charting. In cases where a
HOPE employee struggles with completing their documentation within HOPE’s deadlines, staff

will be placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP).

POLYGRAPH EXAMINER QUALIFICATIONS REQUIREMENTS

HOPE Program works only with certified polygraphists. HOPE Program requires that the
examiners performing the Clinical Polygraph are properly trained and adhere to the established
ethics, standards, and practices of ATSA, and meet the certification standards adopted by the
CASOMB, the American Polygraph Association, and the California Association of Polygraph

Examiners.

Our examiners are required to complete a typed clinical polygraph report within 10 business days
of conducting the examination. Clients undergo examinations at HOPE offices where they will

meet with our subcontractor.

POLYGRAPH PROTOCOL

We require that all clients sign a specialized consent form stating that they agree to the
Polygraph Examination and acknowledge that they understand the examination procedure.

The Office Manager (OM) is responsible for coordinating the schedule with the polygraph
examiner. Clinicians are responsible for tracking and ensuring that their clients are being
polygraphed in a timely manner. The following types of polygraphs can be administered: An
Instant Offense (IO) polygraph, a Sexual History polygraph, or a Maintenance/Monitor
polygraph examination. However, due to an offenders elevated risk upon transition from prison
or local custody into the community, a maintenance examination is recommended within the first
six (3) months of treatment. Maintenance polygraphs will allow the polygraphist to ask questions
that will focus on the offender’s ability to comply with his or her conditions and restrictions of

community supervision.

contains information as to what the client did or did not do during his or her instant or qualifying
offense(s) and includes statements made by the client and the victim(s) to the police. Operations
will follow up with probation or parole to ensure the required information is in their file. A

TYPES OF POLYGRAPH EXAMINATIONS

SEXUAL HISTORY POLYGRAPH: Evaluates the truthfulness of the client’s answers to their

MAINTENANCE POLYGRAPH: Used to determine the client’s commitment to treatment by

SPECIFIC INCIDENT POLYGRAPH: Addresses the instant/qualifying offense(s) only. This test is

issues, victimization issues related to patient’s offense. State relevance of request; state referral
