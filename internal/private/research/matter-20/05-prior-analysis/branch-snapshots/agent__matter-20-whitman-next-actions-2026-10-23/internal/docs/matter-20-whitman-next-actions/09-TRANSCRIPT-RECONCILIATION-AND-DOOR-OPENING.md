## Legal significance: a precise and defensible question
**"Once officers knocked, instructed the awake occupant to lower the window, and opened the door approximately three seconds later, what objective safety or emergency-aid facts necessitated physical access to the car instead of allowing compliance with their own instruction?"**

That is a **particularized constitutional reasonableness challenge**, not a claim that a law prescribes a minimum wait of 3 seconds. *United States v. Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d 1285 (9th Cir. 2020), recognizes opening a passenger door and leaning into the car as a search requiring lawful independent justification. The opinion's **opening-plus-leaning** fact pattern is not automatically identical to opening alone, and subsequent physical inspection of cup should be separately analyzed. Looking through glass from outside using flashlights is generally permissible (*Texas v. Brown*, 460 U.S. 730).

**CHP's best account:** highway shoulder at night, occupant asleep or not immediately responsive, potential medical distress and dangerous position, possible alcoholic open container. *Mardis v. Superior Court* (1963) 218 Cal.App.2d 70 allows opening sleeping person's car door in highway police context; distinguish only after admitting its adverse holding and material extra facts: officer in *Mardis* had prior official radio dispatch of an apparently intoxicated driver, found subject asleep in rear seat, and opened before waking subject. Under objective Fourth Amendment reasonableness (*Brigham City v. Stuart*; *Whren*), officer uttering "booze" does not itself invalidate a plausible welfare-aid justification. Whether the occupant was visibly responsive **before 00:56:43** matters.

**Agency causal distinction:** an arguably unconstitutional *privacy intrusion* at 00:56:43 is distinct from intentional *restriction of bodily liberty* under Penal Code §236 and qualifying criminal false imprisonment under §679.10(c). To win Matter 20's writ, identify first involuntary restriction, the applicable reasonable suspicion/probable cause authority, and evidence of qualifying criminal restraint (not merely privacy invasion), plus statutory abuse in Serrato's April 20 decision. For Matter 32, the door-opening and cup-handling may support separate Fourth Amendment claims without necessarily proving Count One in Whitman.

**Key questions for Serrato April 2026 file:** Did April 9 request include specific details of the 00:56:40 instruction and 00:56:43 opening? What did the original decisionmaker review? Did he distinguish the suspected unlawful search from the separate alleged criminal detention and use correct §679.10 classification? Short denial alone does not prove misapplication. ## Additional critical chronology: parole-search justification cannot be backdated without proof of earlier officer knowledge
- The full Exhibit A transcript describes a **rear passenger-door search at 00:59:46**. It next records a radio-dispatch communication at **01:11:42** stating Petitioner was on parole. Later, at **01:18:34**, Melano reportedly explained an ongoing search as needing to look for guns because "He's on parole."
- Under **People v. Sanders** (2003) 31 Cal.4th 318, 330, an otherwise unlawful search cannot be justified post hoc by an unknown parole-search condition. **People v. Schmitz** (2012) 55 Cal.4th 909 recognizes parole-condition authority for vehicle searches when the officer actually knew of the applicable status, subject to reasonable scope.
- **This is a conditional argument only:** the 01:11:42 dispatch establishes a recorded confirmation by that time; it DOES NOT prove the officers had no earlier knowledge through another database, prior information, or a statement outside the selected transcript. Nor does it eliminate a separate lawful basis for an earlier search such as probable cause plus an applicable automobile exception, true emergency, or consent if proved.
- Specific discovery: all MDT/CLETS/GGCC/RMS lookups and timestamps, the original dispatch audio, identities of operators, officer reports recording first knowledge of parole and search conditions, the actual search sequences at 00:56:43 and 00:59:46, and precise source/time of the empty can. Compare **what officers knew before each search**—do not treat later confirmation as proof they first learned it then.
- The parole-search sequence may be stronger in **federal Matter 32's separate unlawful-search issue** than as independent proof of Penal Code §236 victimhood in Matter 20. Qualifying criminal restraint remains a distinct element.
- Opinions: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2003/s094088.html and https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2012/s186707.html
